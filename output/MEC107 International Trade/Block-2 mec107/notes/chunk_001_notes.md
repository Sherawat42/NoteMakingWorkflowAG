## Section: Introduction to Intra-Industry Trade (IIT) 🟢

### Core Idea
International trade involves gains from division of labor and specialization. A significant portion of modern trade is intra-industry trade (IIT), where countries simultaneously import and export similar products. To explain this behavior, economists have developed new theories focused on economies of scale, product differentiation, and imperfect competition.

> **In Simple Terms:** Instead of one country making only cars and another making only clothes, both countries make cars and trade different models with each other.

### Key Concepts

#### Horizontal vs. Vertical IIT
Horizontal IIT refers to the trade of products that are differentiated but of similar quality (e.g., different features or styles). It typically occurs between countries with similar resource endowments seeking to exploit internal scale economies. Vertical IIT involves trading products of varying quality levels, which usually happens between countries with different factor endowments (e.g., one country exports high-quality goods, the other exports low-quality goods).

| Aspect | Horizontal IIT | Vertical IIT |
|--------|----------------|--------------|
| Quality | Similar | Different |
| Country Endowments | Similar factor endowment ratios | Different factor endowment ratios |
| Specialization Basis | Internal scale economies | Quality differentiation based on factor abundance |

### Definitions
- **Intra-Industry Trade (IIT)**: The simultaneous export and import of products which are very close substitutes for each other in terms of factor inputs and consumption. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Horizontal IIT**: The simultaneous export and import of differentiated products within an industry of a similar quality. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Vertical IIT**: The exchange of differentiated products that are of different qualities. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Smooth Adjustment Hypothesis**: The idea that a high share of IIT will be associated with relatively low adjustment costs during trade liberalization, since workers move within industries rather than between them.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - IIT involves simultaneous imports and exports within the same industry
> - Horizontal IIT = similar quality, similar endowments
> - Vertical IIT = different quality, different endowments
> - Smooth adjustment hypothesis reduces fear of de-industrialization

## Section: Theory of Intra-Industry Trade 🔴

### Core Idea
Traditional trade models like Heckscher-Ohlin-Samuelson (H-O-S), which rely on perfect competition and factor endowment differences, fail to explain IIT, especially among similar industrial nations. New theories emerged in the 1970s incorporating imperfect competition, economies of scale, and product differentiation.

> **In Simple Terms:** The old theories worked well when explaining why a tropical country exports bananas and imports cars, but they couldn't explain why two wealthy countries trade different brands of cars with each other. We needed a new theory based on consumers wanting variety and companies lowering costs by mass-producing specific models.

### Key Concepts

#### Approaches to Product Differentiation
Two main approaches explain consumer demand for horizontal product differentiation. The "love-of-variety approach" assumes every consumer intrinsically values having many different options to choose from. The "ideal variety approach" assumes each consumer has one specific preferred version of a product they desire most.

#### Strands of Theoretical Literature
IIT theories fall into four main categories:
1. Horizontal IIT based on economies of scale and imperfect competition
2. Vertical IIT based on factor endowment differences
3. IIT in differentiated intermediate products
4. IIT in identical commodities based on strategic oligopolistic interactions

### Definitions
- **Love-of-Variety Approach**: An approach considering that each consumer has a taste for many different varieties, leading them to maximize utility by increasing the number of varieties consumed. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Ideal Variety Approach**: An approach considering that each consumer has a desire for the most preferred variety rather than many varieties.

### Mechanisms / Processes
**Krugman's Model of Horizontal IIT**
1. Consumers have a utility function where all varieties enter symmetrically (love of variety).
2. Diminishing marginal utility implies that utility is maximized by consuming many varieties rather than spending income on just a few.
3. A large number of firms operate under monopolistic competition.
4. Each firm produces a unique variety to exploit economies of scale (average cost falls with output, while marginal cost remains constant).
5. No two firms have the incentive to produce the exact same variety.

# Running Context

## Key Concepts Introduced
- **Rule of 70** (Chunk 001): 70 divided by growth rate equals the number of years for an economy to double.
- **Static vs Dynamic gains in Solow Model** (Chunk 001): Trade provides immediate reallocation gains and medium-run capital accumulation gains.
- **Productivity Channels of Trade** (Chunk 001): Scale economies, market competition, resource reallocation, and knowledge transmission.
- **Infant-Industry Argument** (Chunk 002): Young domestic industries need temporary protection to mature before competing globally.
- **Three Stages of ISI** (Chunk 002): Consumer non-durables, then intermediate/capital goods, lastly complex industrial goods.
- **Pathways of Environmental Impact** (Chunk 003): Scale effect, Composition effect, Technique effect.

## Definitions (⭐ exam-important)
- **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)** (Chunk 001): The total output produced for final consumption in a particular period. ⭐
- **Steady State** (Chunk 001): A condition in which key economic variables are constant and do not deviate. ⭐
- **Golden Rule Level of Capital** (Chunk 001): The steady-state level of capital stock that maximizes consumption. ⭐
- **Static Gains from Trade** (Chunk 001): Immediate increase in per-worker output/welfare from efficient resource reallocation. ⭐
- **X-inefficiency** (Chunk 001): Managerial slackness/inefficiency due to lack of market competition. ⭐
- **Exogenous Technological Change** (Chunk 001): External technology progress assumed to simply increase labor efficiency.
- **Spillover Effects** (Chunk 001): Externalities from R&D where non-rival technological knowledge diffuses.
- **Engel's law** (Chunk 002): As income rises, proportion spent on primary goods falls while manufactured goods demand rises. ⭐
- **Prebisch-Singer Thesis** (Chunk 002): Long-run prices of primary exports fall relative to manufactured imports. ⭐
- **Import Substitution Industrialisation (ISI)** (Chunk 002): Trade policy isolating domestic markets via tariffs to create local industries. ⭐
- **Infant-industry argument** (Chunk 002): Rationale for protecting new industries until they become internationally competitive. ⭐
- **High-Performance Asian Economies (HPAE)** (Chunk 002): Asian nations that achieved massive growth via outward-oriented policies and social investment. ⭐
- **Open General Licensing (OGL)** (Chunk 002): Restrictive government trade licenses used heavily during India's inward-oriented regime. ⭐
- **Scale Effect** (Chunk 003): Environmental impact from simply expanding aggregate output. ⭐
- **Composition Effect** (Chunk 003): Environmental impact from shifting the structure/industry mix of an economy. ⭐
- **Technique Effect** (Chunk 003): Reduction in environmental impact through adoption of cleaner technologies. ⭐
- **Externalities** (Chunk 003): Unpriced side effects of production/consumption borne by third parties. ⭐
- **Race to the Bottom** (Chunk 003): Competition between jurisdictions to attract investment by lowering environmental regulations. ⭐
- **Border Trade Adjustments (BTAs)** (Chunk 004): Trade taxes on imports meant to equal the domestic carbon tax, preventing emission leakage. ⭐
- **Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) Measures** (Chunk 004): WTO provisions to protect human/animal/plant health without creating unjustified trade barriers. ⭐
- **Technical Barriers to Trade (TBT)** (Chunk 004): WTO rules ensuring quality/safety standards don't act as unfair trade obstacles. ⭐

## Named Models / Laws / Theories
- **Classical Growth Theory / Solow Model** (Chunk 001): Growth determined by exogenous technological progress since capital has diminishing returns.
- **Endogenous Growth Theory** (Chunk 001): Growth determined internally by purposeful investments in R&D and resulting spillovers (Paul Romer, Robert Lucas).
- **Primary-Export-Led Development Strategy** (Chunk 001, 002): Strategy of exploiting natural comparative advantages to trade for manufactured goods.
- **Prebisch-Singer Thesis** (Chunk 002): Explains structural deterioration of terms of trade for primary goods exporters.
- **Environmental Kuznets Curve Hypothesis (EKCH)** (Chunk 003): Environmental degradation rises, then falls, as national income increases.
- **Pollution Haven Hypothesis (PHH)** (Chunk 003): Dirty industries relocate to developing countries with lax environmental laws.
- **Factor Endowment Hypothesis (FEH)** (Chunk 003): Environmental degradation is dictated by a country's natural resource endowments and specialization.
- **Natural Resource Curse Hypothesis (NRCH)** (Chunk 003, 004): Heavy reliance on abundant resources leads to policy myopia and stunted broader growth. Does not necessarily mean environmental collapse on its own.
- **Multilateral Environment Agreements (MEAs)** (Chunk 004): Group of international treaties (e.g., CITES, Montreal/Kyoto protocols) that often impact cross-border trade.

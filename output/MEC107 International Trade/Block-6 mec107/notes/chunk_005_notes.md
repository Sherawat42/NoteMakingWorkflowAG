## Section: Introduction to India's Trade Agreements 🟢

### Core Idea
India's engagement in global trade features a mix of broad multilateral agreements and specific regional trade agreements. While India joined GATT in 1948, it did not actively start signing localized Regional Trading Agreements (RTAs) until the 2000s, reflecting its historically diverse, non-neighborhood-dependent trade portfolio.

> **In Simple Terms:** India has traded all over the world for a long time, so for decades it didn't bother making specialized local "trade clubs" until the 2000s. Now, it has agreements stretching from local neighbors like Bhutan all the way to Chile in South America.

## Section: India’s Regional & Multilateral Trade Agreements 🔴

### Core Idea
India's recent shift toward regionalism has resulted in an array of Preferential Scope Agreements (PSAs), Free Trade Areas (FTAs), and Economic Integration Agreements (EIAs). These span across the Asia-Pacific, South-East Asia, South Asia, and even South America. 

### Key Concepts
#### Asia-Pacific Trade Agreement (APTA)
The oldest preferential trade agreement in the Asia-Pacific region, originally signed in 1975 as the Bangkok Agreement. After China joined in 2001 (and renamed APTA in 2005), India began experiencing massive trade deficits with the bloc, driven primarily by an enormous deficit with China (US$ 44.76 billion in 2014).

#### ASEAN-India Free Trade Area (FTA)
Signed in 2009 and enacted in 2010. Before this, India’s only South-East Asian FTA was with Singapore (2005). The agreement significantly boosted trade across 13 core sectors ranging from mineral fuels and chemicals to electronics and transport equipment.

#### South Asian Free Trade Area (SAFTA)
Formed in 2006 (replacing the previous SAPTA of 1995) among SAARC nations. The goal is to ultimately achieve a South Asia Economic Union. While it aimed for zero customs duty on all internal trade, political tensions (notably between India and Pakistan) constantly stall progress. India maintains a heavy and increasing trade surplus with SAFTA members.

#### India-MERCOSUR PSA
A classic example of an agreement completely devoid of geographical proximity. Signed conceptually in 2003 and fully as a PSA in 2009. Gives India preferential access for chemicals and machinery in exchange for lowering duties on meat, hides, and wool. India's trade balance heavily fluctuates based on its trade with Brazil.

#### Global System of Trade Preferences among Developing Countries (GSTP)
Established in 1988 under UNCTAD to increase trade specifically between developing countries. During the Sao Paulo Round (2004), India unilaterally offered a massive 25% tariff reduction on 77% of its tariff lines for Least Developed Countries (LDCs).

### Definitions
- **APTA**: Asia-Pacific Trade Agreement, the oldest RTA in the Asia-Pacific region, heavily heavily impacting India's trade deficit due to China's inclusion. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **SAFTA**: South Asian Free Trade Area, replacing SAPTA to enhance local economic cooperation among SAARC nations despite political hurdles. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **MERCOSUR PSA**: India's preferential scope agreement with South American nations (Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay). ⭐ (exam-important)

> **Quick Recall:**
> - APTA = oldest, causes huge trade deficit due to China.
> - SAFTA = South Asian, blocked by India-Pakistan tensions, massive surplus for India.
> - MERCOSUR = Trans-continental agreement with South America.

## Section: India’s Other Strategic Groups 🟡

### Core Idea
Beyond strict trade agreements, India secures its diplomatic and economic voice by participating in strategic macro-level groups. These provide a platform to champion developing-world interests and secure global sea routes.

### Key Concepts
- **G20 (Group of Twenty)**: Represents 85% of global GDP. Originally focused on macroeconomic issues, it now covers climate and sustainable development. India assumed its Presidency in Dec 2022.
- **BRICS**: Formed between Brazil, Russia, India, China, and later South Africa. Focuses on political, security, and economic cooperation. 
- **The Quad (Quadrilateral Security Dialogue)**: An informal strategic dialogue between Australia, India, Japan, and the USA aimed primarily at keeping Indo-Pacific sea routes free of military/political influence and securing a unified liberal trading system.

## Section: From GATT to WTO: India’s Transformation 🔴

### Core Idea
During the GATT era, the "Quad" (US, EU, Canada, Japan) dominated negotiations. India was a passive bystander, simply enjoying the protective benefits of "Special and Differential Treatment". However, the rigid binding commitments enforced by the WTO forced India into becoming an aggressive, highly vocal leader advocating for the rights of developing nations.

> **In Simple Terms:** During the old days (GATT), the rich countries made the rules and let poor countries take a free ride. When the WTO was created, everyone had to follow stricter rules. Realizing the new rules favored the rich, India stepped up and became the outspoken leader defending developing nations.

### Key Concepts
#### The Uruguay Round Assumption
The initial belief was that developing countries would gain from lowered agricultural subsidies in the West, while developed countries would gain from IP (TRIPS) and Services (GATS) liberalization. This theoretical "win-win" quickly broke down. 

#### The Shift in Power
As exports from emerging economies (India, Brazil, China) boomed, the decision-making balance shifted. During the WTO rounds, particularly starting at the 1998 Geneva Ministerial, India led the block of developing nations in refusing new negotiation rounds until developed countries fulfilled their past agricultural subsidy reduction promises. This fundamentally shifted global trade from North-North negotiations to highly contentious North-South negotiations.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Believing India was always an aggressive negotiator in global trade. → ✅ Correct: India was a bystander during the GATT era and only became aggressive upon the formation of the WTO and the Doha rounds due to forced binding commitments.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Under GATT: India was a passive beneficiary of Special and Differential Treatment.
> - Under WTO: India transformed into the leader of the developing-nations bloc.
> - Key triggering conflict: Developed nations failing to cut agricultural subsidies.

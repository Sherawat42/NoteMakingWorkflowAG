## Section: Equilibrium Structure of Regional Trading Agreements 🔴

### Core Idea
Regional Trading Agreements (RTAs) are not static; they are highly dynamic entities that constantly change in structure over time. They can expand by taking in new members (horizontal expansion), deepening the economic commitments among current members (vertical expansion), or doing both at the same time. The ultimate size and structure of an RTA depends on whether it requires unanimous approval for new members and the timing of nations deciding to join.

> **In Simple Terms:** Trade clubs are always evolving. Sometimes they invite more countries to join (getting wider), sometimes the existing members agree to cooperate even more closely (getting deeper), and sometimes they do both. The rules of the club decide who gets in and how big it can grow.

### Key Concepts
#### Horizontal Expansion
An increase in the number of members in an RTA. The rules governing horizontal expansion fall into two theoretical categories:
1. **Open Regionalism**: Any country can join without needing the consent of existing members (assuming they meet base criteria). Though frequently claimed (e.g., EU Copenhagen criteria, US template FTAs), true unconditional open regionalism is rarely practiced.
2. **Unanimous Regionalism**: A potential member can only join if *all* existing members agree to grant accession. E.g., The ASEAN group sequentially added Vietnam, Myanmar, Cambodia, etc.

#### Vertical Expansion (Consolidation)
Deepening of economic cooperation among the existing members of an RTA.
- **Example**: Moving from a Preferential Trading Arrangement (PTA) to a Free Trade Area (FTA), or an FTA to a Customs Union over time. E.g., NAFTA slowly cross-phasing out investment barriers over decades.

#### Simultaneous Expansion
When an RTA both deepens economic cooperation and accepts new members at the same time.
- **Example**: The European Union is the classic example, growing from the BENELUX customs union to the massive Economic and Monetary Union (EMU) with a common currency, while simultaneously expanding from 6 to 28 members.

#### The Address Game
A theoretical game theory framework explaining RTA formation choices among four hypothetical countries. 
- Setting: Countries can either stay single {1,1,1,1}, pair up {2,2}, form a 3-member RTA {3,1}, or form global free trade {4}.
- **Simultaneous move**: If all countries decide at the exact same moment, Global Free Trade {4} emerges as the Nash Equilibrium.
- **Sequential move**: If countries decide one by one (under unanimous regionalism rules), a 3-member RTA with 1 non-member {3,1} emerges as the Sub-game Perfect Nash Equilibrium. Why? The 3-member RTA has no incentive to invite the fourth member because their individual payoffs would drop (from 23 to 20). 
- **Conclusion**: The number of members in an RTA relies heavily on the sequence of moves and the type of regionalism practiced.

### Definitions
- **Horizontal Expansion**: Expansion of an RTA through an increase in the number of member countries. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Vertical Expansion**: Deepening of economic cooperation among the existing members of an RTA. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Open Regionalism**: A system where any country can join an existing RTA without requiring the consent of the existing members. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Unanimous Regionalism**: A system where an applicant country needs consent from *all* existing members of an RTA to join. ⭐ (exam-important)

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Horizontal = wider (more countries).
> - Vertical = deeper (tighter economic rules).
> - Simultaneous = European Union model (wider + deeper).
> - The Address Game proves that sequential decision-making often leads to regional blocs rather than Global Free Trade.

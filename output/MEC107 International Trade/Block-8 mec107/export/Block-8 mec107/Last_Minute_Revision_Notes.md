## Topics

- **Trade Policy**: A national framework of laws, regulations, decisions, practices, and negotiating positions that governments adopt to regulate the exchange of goods and services. ⭐ (exam-important)

### Basic Tools of Trade Policy 🔴

**Tariff vs. Non-Tariff Measures**
| Aspect | Tariff Measures | Non-Tariff Measures |
|--------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Structure | Direct taxes (Import/Export duties) | Quantitative limits or technical standards |
| Visibility | Transparent and explicit | Often complex and administrative |
| Primary use | Revenue generation & price-based protection | Controlling specific volumes or safety standards |
- **Quantitative Restrictions (QRs)**: Strict limits on the quantity of a specific good that can be imported or exported, implemented via quotas or licensing policies. ⭐ (exam-important)
1. **Tariff measures**: Export taxes and import tariffs.
2. **Quantitative Restrictions (QRs)**: Quotas, import licensing, and export restrictions.
3. **Non-tariff measures**: 
    - Antidumping measures (India is the second-highest user globally).
    - Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) measures.
    - Technical Barriers to Trade (TBT).

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Believing SPS and TBT measures are primarily designed for trade restriction. → ✅ Correct: SPS and TBT are generally health, safety, and compatibility standards; they become a trade policy concern only when applied defensively as protectionist tools.

**Quick Recall:**
- Tools of trade policy: Tariffs, QRs, Antidumping, SPS, TBT.
- India globally ranks as the second-highest user of anti-dumping duties.
- SPS and TBT are collectively referred to as "standards".
- **Import Substitution**: An economic theory and practice aimed at replacing foreign imports with domestic production to foster a self-reliant economy. ⭐ (exam-important)

### Evolution of Trade Policy (1991–2014) 🔴

**Structural Trade Liberalization**

**Export Promotion Schemes**
- **Agri Export Zones (AEZs) (2002–07)**: Provided end-to-end development for exporting specific agricultural products from contiguous regions.
- **ASIDE Scheme (2002)**: Assistance to States for Development of Export Infrastructure to improve state-level supply capabilities.
- **Market Access Initiative (MAI) / Focus Product & Market Schemes (FPS & FMS)**: Aimed at offsetting high freight costs and strategically expanding India's footprint in new international markets.
1. Devalued the Indian Rupee → 2. Transitioned toward a flexible exchange rate → 3. Implemented current account convertibility → 4. Opened the economy to foreign investment.

**Quick Recall:**
- Result of tariff cuts: Average tariffs dropped to one-tenth of their 1990-91 levels.
- 1999 WTO ruling: Forced India to drop remaining QRs on consumer goods.
- Major schemes: AEZ (Agri Export Zones), ASIDE (export infrastructure), FMS (Focus Market Scheme).
- 2013/2014 steps: Commitment to WTO Trade Facilitation Agreement (TFA) and initiation of the Indian Customs Single Window Project.

### Foreign Trade Policy of 2015-20 🔴

**MEIS and SEIS Integration**

**Ease of Doing Business**
- **EPCG Export Obligation**: Reduced from 90% to 75% for capital goods sourced locally.
- **Single Window Interface**: Allowed traders to electronically lodge documents with customs at a single point, bypassing multiple physical agencies.
- **24*7 Customs Clearance**: Implemented at 19 seaports and 17 air cargo complexes.
1. **WTO Dispute**: In 2019, a WTO panel ruled that India's export subsidies (under MEIS and SEIS) violate WTO rules and must be withdrawn, forcing a rethink of subsidy-based promotion.
2. **FTA Skepticism**: There's a prevailing belief that Free Trade Agreements (FTAs) have disadvantaged India, highlighted by India's November 2020 exit from the RCEP.
3. **GST Bottlenecks**: Implementational delays regarding input duty credit and refunds harmed exporter liquidity.
- **Status Holders**: Entrepreneurs certified by the DGFT as major export players who are allowed to self-certify their manufactured goods as originating from India. ⭐ (exam-important)

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Believing reforms instantly boosted all sectors equally. → ✅ Correct: The pace varied widely. Protection for capital/intermediate goods was slashed rapidly, whilst consumer goods stayed protected for nearly a decade post-1991.

### Foreign Trade Policy 2023 🔴

**Four Pillars of FTP 2023**
1. **Incentive to Remission**: Moving away from flat subsidies to remitting duties.
2. **Export Promotion via Collaboration**: Strengthening synergy between Exporters, States, Districts, and Indian Missions.
3. **Ease of Doing Business**: Process re-engineering, IT-based schemes, and transaction cost reduction.
4. **Emerging Areas**: Facilitating export of dual-use technology items (SCOMET) and developing e-commerce export hubs.
- **One-time Amnesty Scheme**: To close old pending authorizations and allow exporters to start afresh.
- **Towns of Export Excellence**: Recognizing new towns for localized export promotion.

**Quick Recall:**
- FTP 2015-20 brought MEIS, SEIS, and a 2.1% to 3.5% trade share target. 2019 WTO ruling forced their withdrawal.
- FTP 2023 focuses on the "Incentive to Remission" shift, e-commerce, SCOMET items, and the Amnesty Scheme.
- RCEP: India withdrew in Nov 2020 over FTA concerns.
- Prior to 1991, India experienced the "Hindu rate of growth" of just 3 to 4 percent.

### Challenges Faced by India's Foreign Trade 🔴
1. **Tariff and Non-Tariff Barriers**: Growing protectionism worldwide blocking Indian market access.
2. **Global Crises**: Volatility (like the 2008 US and 2009 EU crises) directly crashes demand for Indian gems, readymade garments, and handicrafts.
3. **Decrease in Export Incentives**: Government withdrawal of income tax benefits and reductions in Duty Drawback (DBK) rates make Indian products costlier overall.
4. **Product Standards Requirement**: Exporters must pass intensive, expensive laboratory tests for SPS and TBT to sell in developed markets.
5. **Anti-Dumping Duties**: Developed nations (like the USA on Indian steel flanges) increasingly target Indian goods with punitive anti-dumping tariffs.
6. **Subsidies in Developed Nations**: The US and EU heavily subsidize their own producers (like fertilizers), undercutting Indian competitiveness artificially.
7. **Sea Pirates**: High physical risk to cargo transiting the Gulf of Aden.
8. **Documentation Burden**: Exporters historically face around 25 documents per transaction, defeating ease-of-business goals.

### Key Definitions from Unit 19 🔴
- **Anti-Dumping Duty**: A protectionist tariff that a domestic government imposes on foreign imports which it believes are priced artificially low or below fair market value. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade)**: A historic legal agreement ensuring the reduction or elimination of trade barriers, formally replaced by the WTO in 1995.
- **SPS and TBT Agreements**: Key categories of non-tariff barriers under the WTO. **SPS** (Sanitary and Phytosanitary) regulates food safety and animal/plant health. **TBT** (Technical Barriers to Trade) dictates labeling, quality, and environmental standards. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **WTO (World Trade Organization)**: The primary intergovernmental organization that regulates international trade, officially commencing on January 1, 1995, under the Marrakesh Agreement. ⭐ (exam-important)

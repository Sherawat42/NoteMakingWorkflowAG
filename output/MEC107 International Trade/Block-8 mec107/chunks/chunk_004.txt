19.5 CHALLENGES FACED BY FOREIGN TRADE 
OF INDIA 
The Indian government is keen to stimulate foreign trade of India in recent years. 
Indian government has come up with numerous reforms and schemes to support 
foreign trade of India. Despites of these efforts, India faces significant challenges 
and rejections in its foreign trade from the world market. One of the important 
challenge and concern is India’s share in the global market is only 1.94% in 2016 
which is significantly low. Currently, Increment in tariff and non-tariff barriers, 
the global economic slowdown, increasing protectionism, the stalled Free -trade 
and multilateral agreements e.g., Free trade agreement with European Union is 
still not finalized and unfavourable foreign trade policies are major obst acles for 
External Sector of India. Manufacturing sector in India is failed to develop and 
India relay mostly on services trade which is volatile in nature. Thus, 
manufacturing sector in India must prepare for opportunities and greater 
engagement in an evolving multilateral trade arena. India’s main concern is most 
of the traders are not able to meet global standards developed by World Trade 
Organization (WTO). In recent years, India has observed a growing trend

barriers which includes increase in import duties accordance with Article II of 
the GATT or non-tariff barriers, which are any trade barriers other than tariff 
barriers like, import licensing, Standards, testing, labelling & certification, 
Anti-dumping and countervailing measures, Export subsidies and domestic 
support. Taking the help of non-tariff barriers most of the countries are 
putting restrictions on India’s Goods in their country.

sheet of exporters.

export incentives such as reduction in DBK rates, withdrawal of income tax 
benefits for majority of exporters etc. These reduction in the export incentives 
decrease the exports from India as competitiveness of Indian product 
decrease in the world market due to increment in their prices. 
4) Product Standards requirement: In recent years, developed countries imposes

developing countries need to undertake various laboratory tests addressed by 
importing countries. This increases the cost of product from India in 
International market and leads to decrease in export from India.

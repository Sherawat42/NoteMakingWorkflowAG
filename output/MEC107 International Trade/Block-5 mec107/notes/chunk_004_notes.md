## Section: Explaining Financial Crises 🔴

### Core Idea
A financial crisis occurs when the value of financial assets plunges, liquidity dries up, and entities default on their debts. These crises can be broadly divided into systemic crises, which threaten the entire financial ecosystem, and non-systemic crises, which are isolated to a specific sector. They are often rooted in macroeconomic imbalances, irrational market behavior, and regulatory deficiencies.

> **In Simple Terms:** A financial crisis is like a massive heart attack for the economy. Suddenly, banks don't have enough money, people and companies can't pay their debts, prices for things like houses or stocks crash, and panic spreads across the global system.

### Key Concepts

#### Systemic vs. Non-Systemic Crises
- **Systemic Crisis**: The risk of cascading failure that breaks down the entire financial system due to deep vertical and horizontal integration. Examples include the Great Depression (1930s) and the 2008 Global Financial Crisis.
- **Non-Systemic Crisis**: Isolated failures in one or a few markets or sectors, such as the Savings and Loan Crisis.

#### Causes of Crises
- **Fundamental Macroeconomic Imbalances**: Shocks in money supply, inflation, large fiscal/current account deficits.
- **Irrational Factors**: Bank runs (panic withdrawals), financial contagion/spillovers, limit to arbitrage, and the bursting of asset bubbles.
- **Structural/Regulatory Mismatches**: As noted by Schumpeter, bank credit drives innovation and growth, but a lack of regulation creates a dangerous mismatch between banks' short-term liabilities (deposits) and long-term investments (loans).

### Definitions
- **Financial Crisis**: A severe situation where financial asset prices plummet, widespread defaults occur, and liquidity vanishes. ⭐
- **Systemic Crisis**: The risk of a breakdown of an entire financial system caused by cascading failures. ⭐
- **Non-Systemic Crisis**: A crisis limited to one or a few isolated markets or sectors.

## Section: The 2007-2008 Global Financial Crisis 🔴

### Core Idea
The 2008 global financial crisis originated in the US housing market due to heavily encouraged subprime lending. These high-risk mortgages were repackaged into complex credit derivatives (like RMBS and CDOs) that hid the true risk. When housing prices fell and interest rates rose, borrowers defaulted en masse, causing these complex financial products to fail and sparking a global liquidity crisis through financial contagion.

> **In Simple Terms:** The US government pushed banks to give home loans to people with bad credit. Banks then bundled these risky loans together, disguised them as safe investments, and sold them globally. When people stopped paying their loans, the whole house of cards collapsed worldwide.

### Key Concepts

#### Origins: Subprime Lending & Deregulation
A political mandate to increase US homeownership forced lenders—encouraged by the Federal Reserve—to relax strict lending criteria. Commercial banks began aggressively issuing "subprime loans" to borrowers with poor credit histories.

#### The Role of Credit Derivatives
To get these risky loans off their balance sheets, commercial banks sold their mortgage portfolios to investment banks. Investment banks used **Credit Derivatives** to transfer this risk to third parties. 
- **Unfunded Credit Derivatives**: Contingent instruments (like Credit Default Swaps) where the protection seller only pays out if a default occurs, with no initial payment from the protection seller.
- **Funded Credit Derivatives**: Instruments (like CDOs or RMBS) where the counterparty pays upfront to cover potential default.

#### Residential Mortgage-Backed Securities (RMBS) Structure
1. **Origination**: Commercial bank approves risky home loans.
2. **Transfer**: Bank sells the portfolio to an Investment Bank to free up capital.
3. **Special Purpose Entity (Trustee)**: The investment bank creates a trustee to manage the portfolio.
4. **Tranching**: The trustee pools the loans and issues new securities backed by them (RMBS). These are split into tranches based on risk:
   - **Senior notes**: Lowest risk, lowest return. (Given high investment grades by rating agencies).
   - **Mezzanine / Junior notes**: Medium risk/return.
   - **Equity tranches**: Highest risk, highest return (often bought by the investment bank itself because no one else would).
5. **Insurance**: The trustee buys credit protection from a seller (often the same investment bank) to cover potential defaults.

#### The Bubble Burst & Contagion
When US interest rates rose between 2004-2006, borrowers could no longer afford their subprime mortgage payments. Housing prices then plummeted in 2007. The RMBS and CDO securities lost their value overnight, becoming highly illiquid. Banks backing them faced bankruptcy. Due to deep global financial integration, this US-centric crisis quickly infected Europe and the broader real economy worldwide.

### Definitions
- **Subprime Lending**: Making loans to people who may have difficulty maintaining the repayment schedule, reflecting lower creditworthiness. ⭐
- **Credit Derivatives (CDs)**: Off-balance-sheet financial contracts that allow a lender to transfer the credit risk of a financial instrument to a third party for a fee. ⭐
- **Residential Mortgage-Backed Securities (RMBS)**: A type of funded credit derivative formed by pooling various residential mortgages. ⭐
- **Special Purpose Entity (Trustee)**: An entity created to isolate financial risk, used in securitisation to manage pooled assets and issue securities.

### Mechanisms / Processes
1. Subprime loans issued → 2. Loans pooled and sold to Investment Banks → 3. Repackaged into structured tranches (RMBS/CDOs) → 4. Sold to global investors → 5. Interest rates rise / Housing prices fall → 6. Widespread mortgage defaults → 7. RMBS lose value → 8. Global banking liquidity crisis.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Believing the 2008 crisis was solely caused by greedy homeowners. → ✅ Correct: It was a systemic failure caused by political mandates, relaxed lending standards, misleading credit ratings, and opaque financial reengineering by investment banks.

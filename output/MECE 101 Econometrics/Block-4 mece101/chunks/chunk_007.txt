16.7IDENTIFICATION CONDITIONS 
There are two conditions that must be fulfilled for an equation to be 
identified.  
16.7.1Order Condition of Identification 
This condition is based on a counting rule of the variables included and 
excluded from a particular equation.It is a necessary but not sufficient 
condition for identification of an equation.The order condition may be stated 
as follows: 
For an equation to be identified the total number of variables (endogenous 
and exogenous) excluded from it must be equal to or greater than the number 
of endogenous variables in the model less one.Remember that in a 
simultaneous equation system, the number of endogenous variables is equal to 
the number of equations of the model. Thus, the order condition can be stated 
as follow: 
For an equation to be identified the total number of variables excluded from it 
but included in other equations must be at least as large as the number of 
equations of the system less one. 
Suppose, 
G = Total number of equations (=total number of endogenous variables) 
K = Total number of variables in the model (endogenous and 
predetermined) 
M
 = Total number of variables, endogenous and exogenous, included in 
a particular equation 
Then the order condition for identification may be symbolically expressed as  
 
(𝑲𝑴)≥(𝑮1) 
[Excluded variables] ≥[Total number of equations 1]

Thus, the order condition is satisfied for the second equation. 
The order condition for identification is necessary for a relation to be 
identified, but it is not sufficient. Therefore, order condition may be fulfilled 
in an equation and yet the relation may not be identified.  
16.7.2 Rank Condition of Identification  
The rank condition sates that: in a system of 𝐺 equations an equation is 
identified if and only if it is possible to construct at least one non-zero 
determinant of order ( 𝐺1) from the coefficients of the variables excluded 
from that equation but contained in other equations of the model. 
This condition is called the rank condition because it refers to the rank of the 
matrix of parameters of excluded variables. The rank of a matrix is the order 
of the largest non-zero determinant which can be formed from the matrix. In 
our case the relevant matrix is the sub-matrix of coefficients of the excluded 
variables. Hence, the rank condition may be also stated as follows: a sufficient 
condition for identification of a relationship is that the rank of the matrix of 
parameters of all the excluded variables (endogenous and predetermined) 
from that equation be equal to ( G 1)

16.8LET US SUM UP 
Unlike the single equation model, in simultaneous equations models, we get 
more than one dependent, or endogenous variable. This endogenous variable 
may appear in one equation as explained variable and, in another equation, as 
explanatory variable. Due to the presence of endogenous variables as 
explanatory variables in some of the equations, the regressors are stochastic in 
nature.In such situations the classical OLS regression method is not 
applicable as it may result in inconsistent estimators, and there is simultaneity 
bias.  
Identification of an equation is necessary estimation of the equation. The 
objective of the identification problem is toobtain unique estimators of the 
structural parameters from the reduced form parameters. An equation in a 


--- Page 69 ---
 
 
Extensions of Regression 
Models 
312 
system of simultaneous equations is identified only when we can resolve this 
issue. If not, the equation remains in unidentified or under-identified situation.  
An identified equation can be just-identified or over-identified. In the case of 
an exactly identified equation, unique estimators of structural parameters can 
be obtained. In the case of an over identified equation, on the other hand, 
more than one estimator for certain structural parameters is obtained.  
There are two conditions for evaluation of identification status of an equation 
in a simultaneous equation system, viz., order condition, and rank condition. 
The order condition is a necessary condition for identification. On the other 
hand, the rank condition is a necessary and sufficient condition for 
identification. If the rank condition is satisfied, the order condition is satisfied 
too, although the reverse is not true.

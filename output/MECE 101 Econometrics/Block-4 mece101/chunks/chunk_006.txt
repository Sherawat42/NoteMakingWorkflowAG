16.6 CONCEPT OF IDENTIFICATION 
In the previous section we presented the reduced form of an equation. In the 
reduced form an endogenous variable is explained in terms of the pre-
determined variables in the simultaneous equations system and the stochastic 
error term. Thus, we can apply OLS to the reduced form and obtain estimators 
of the reduced form parameters.  
Our objective, however, is to find out the structural form parameters. The 
concept of identification is used to denote the possibility (or impossibility) of 
deducing the values of the structural parameters from the reduced form 
parameters.  
The identification issue arises prior to estimation of the model. If more than 
one theory is consistent with the same data (we illustrate this in Example 16.4 
below), then they are said to be observationally equivalent, and we cannot 
distinguish them. In such cases the structure of the econometric model is said 
to be unidentified and there is a need to modify the model. 
Identification is a problem of model formulation, rather than of model 
estimation or model appraisal. A model is said to be ‘identified’ if it is in a 
unique statistical form, where we can obtain unique estimator for each of its 
structural parameters. If a model is ‘not identified’, then we cannot say 
exactly what relationship we are estimating. 
Example 16.4 
Identification Problem in the Demand-Supply Model 
To measure the coefficients of the demand equation, normally the published 
time series reporting the quantity bought of the commodity is used.However, 
the quantity bought is identical with the quantity sold at the given 
price.Market data register points of interaction of equilibrium supply and 
demand at the price prevailing in the market at a certain point of time. A 
sample of time-series observations shows simultaneously the quantity 
demanded, and the quantity supplied, at the prevailing market price.That is, it 
only shows the points of interactions of demand and supply. If we use these 
data for estimation, we measure the coefficients of a function of the form 
𝑄 = 𝑓(𝑝).This equation may be either the demand function or the supply 
function. How can we be sure whether this equation represents demand 
function or supply function? If person A is interested in estimation of the 
demand function, then (s)he can use the market data. Similarly, if person B is 
interested in estimation of the supply equation, (s)he will also be using the 
same market data! Obviously, weneed some criteria, which will enable us to 
verify that the estimated coefficients belong to a particular relationship. Such 
criteria are known as ‘identification conditions’ of a function. We will explain 
these criteria in Section 16.6.

16.6.1 Paradox of Identification 
Discussion in the preceding section suggests that if we want to measure a 
given function belonging to a simultaneous-equations model, the function 
must be fairly stable over the sample period, that is, it must shift within a 
smaller range as compared with other relationships of the same model. In 
Example 16.4 we have shown that we can measure the supply function when 
it is fairly stable and the demand function is shifting. Similarly, we can 
measure the demand function if it is fairly stable while the supply function 
shows adequate variability. 
It can happen when the factor causing a shift in a function is absent in another 
function.In other words, to identify the supply function (see Fig. 16.2(b)), 
some factors absent from it but included in the demand function.So, to 
identify an equation in a simultaneous equations model, we have to look for 
the variables absent from it (but present in other equations of the model). This 
is called the paradox of identification. In identification problem, the relevant 
variables are the ones that are absent from the equation!

16.6.2 Identification Status of an Equation 
As mentioned earlier, identification refers to the possibility of finding out 
estimators of all the parameters of an equation. Thus, we have to move 
equation-wise. There are two possibilities: whether the equation under 
consideration is identified or not identified. 
1. Equation is under-identified 
If the structure of the equation is not unique or there is not sufficient 
information to uniquely distinguish the equation, then the equation is 
under-identified. 
 


--- Page 61 ---

Models 
304 
2. Equation is identified 
(a) Exactly identified: If there is sufficient information available to 
distinguish the equation then it is called just identified or exactly 
identified equation 
(b) Over-identified: If there is more than sufficient information 
regarding the equation then the equation is called over-identified. 
Identification is closely related to the estimation of the model. 
(1) If an equation is identified, its coefficient can, in general, be statistically 
estimated.In particular:  
(a) If the equation is exactly identified, the appropriate method to be 
used for its estimation is the method of indirect least 
squares(ILS). 
(b) If the equation is over-identified, indirect least squares cannot be 
applied, because it will not yield unique estimates of the 
structural parameters.There are various methods which can be 
used in this case, for example two-stage least squares (2SLS), or 
maximum likelihood methods.These methods will be developed 
in Unit 15.  
Identification may be established either by the examination of the 
specification of the structural model, or by the examination of the reduced 
form of the model. Reduced form approach is conceptually confusing and 
computationally more difficult than the structural model approach, because it 
requires the derivation of the reduced form first and then examination of the 
values of the determinant formed from some of the reduced form coefficients. 
The structural form approach is simpler and more useful. In this unit we will 
only discuss the structural form approach of identification. 
In applying the identification rules, we should ignore the intercept term if it is 
present in each equation. If intercept term is present in some equations of the 
model, not in others, then we should treat it like a separate variable(say 𝑋଴), 
that takes the value 1 for presence of the intercept term, and 0 otherwise.

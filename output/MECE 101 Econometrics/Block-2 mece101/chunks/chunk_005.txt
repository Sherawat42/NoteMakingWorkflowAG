5.9 LIMITATIONS OF REGRESSION ANALYSIS 
Even when we possess theoretically sound variables that can explain a 
phenomenon and we manage to gather data on these variables, it is a common 
challenge not to have a clear understanding of the functional relationship between 
the dependent variable and the independent variables (see data generation process 
in Unit 3). In two-variable models, the scatter diagram frequently allows for an 
assessment of the functional relationship. However, in multiple regression 
models, identifying the suitable functional form becomes challenging, as visually 
interpreting scatter diagrams in multiple dimensions is not feasible. 
Consequently, the stochastic disturbances 𝑢௜ play a crucial role in regression 
analysis, a significance we will delve into as we advance to subsequent units. 
Undoubtedly, selecting a suitable model for empirical estimation demands a 
considerable amount of skill and experience. However, certain guidelines can be 
provided: 
The literature and theoretical paradigms must be re-visited as the foundational 
theory might propose a specific type of functional form. It is advisable to 
determine both the rate of change (slope) of the dependent variable concerning 
the independent variable and the elasticity of the dependent variable concerning 
the independent variable. 
The parameters selected for the model should align with specific a priori  
expectations. For instance, when examining the demand for laptops in relation to 
price and other variables, it is reasonable to anticipate a negative coefficient for 
the price variable. 
Occasionally, a given set of data may be reasonably well-fitted by more than one 
model.  R squared values may help in deciding which model is better in some 
such cases. However, there are some caveats to be considered – as we will 
explore in the upcoming sections, the inclusion of more regressors in the model 
leads to an increase in R 2. What holds greater significance is the theoretical 
foundation of the chosen model, the signs of the estimated coefficients, and its 
statistical significance? If the performance of a model is better on the basis of 
these criteria, a lower value of R2 does not matter much. 
When considering the limitations of regression analysis, several important factors 
should be taken into account: 
Linearity Assumption : It is crucial to assess whether the relationship between 
the variables under investigation truly follows a linear pattern. This assumption 
must be carefully examined because deviations from linearity can lead to 
inaccurate conclusions. 
Sensitivity to Outliers : Regression analysis can be highly sensitive to outliers, 
which are data points that deviate significantly from the overall trend. 
Researchers must exercise caution when interpreting results, as outliers can 


--- Page 41 ---
 
 107
Models Diagnostics disproportionately influence regression coefficients and may not accurately 
represent the underlying relationship. 
Preliminary Data Visualization : Prior to conducting regression analysis, it is 
advisable to visualize the data through plots and graphs. This step helps identify 
potential outliers and provides insights into the overall data distribution. 
Decisions regarding outlier handling and data transformations should be made 
based on this preliminary exploration. 
Extrapolation Risks : Extrapolation or making predictions outside the range of 
the explanatory variable, should be approached with caution. Extending 
regression results beyond the observed data range can lead to unreliable 
predictions and is often not appropriate. For example, using regression to predict 
outcomes for a population significantly different from the study sample may 
yield inaccurate results. 
Hidden Variables and Confounding Factors : Researchers should be aware of 
the presence of lurking variables, also known as confounding or hidden variables. 
These unaccounted-for factors can distort the relationship between the variables 
included in the regression model. When confounding variables are present, the 
observed relationship may be misleading or spurious. Careful consideration and 
control of potential confounders are essential in regression analysis. 
In summary, regression analysis offers valuable insights into relationships 
between variables, but it is not without limitations. Researchers must critically 
assess the linearity assumption, address the impact of outliers, visualize data 
before analysis, exercise caution with extrapolation, and remain vigilant for 
hidden variables to ensure the reliability and validity of their regression results.

5.10 LET US SUM UP 
Ensuring accurate model specification is crucial in econometric analysis, as it 
allows for a focused exploration of relevant factors impacting the research theme. 
Challenges inevitably arise in the quest for a perfect econometric model, 
underscoring the need for accurate parameter estimation and thorough validation 
checks, to uphold the underlying assumptions. 
Residual analysis emerges as a pivotal tool in assessing model adequacy, with 
various patterns in residual plots offering valuable insights into model fit. By 
scrutinizing these patterns, issues such as heteroscedasticity and autocorrelation 
can be identified and subsequently addressed in an effective manner. 
Furthermore, the impact of outliers and variable units on regression coefficients 
is highlighted in the unit. Conducting tests to detect outliers, assess normality, 
and handle specific scenarios in regression analysis, such as regression through 
the origin, adjusting variable origins and scales, and selecting the most suitable 
functional form, significantly contributes to accurate model diagnostics. Finally, 
certain potential challenges in regression analysis are discussed. By navigating 
these challenges and leveraging diagnostic techniques, economists can enhance 
the robustness and reliability of their regression analyses.

In an applied discipline like Quantitative methods in Economics, most of the
time, only calculations with operations like +, —, x, +, and \ etc. are involved.
And, proofs using techniques & rules of inference are rarely required. However,
‘proof’ is the soul of Mathematics, and hence it has to be an essential component
of any mathematics curriculum. In this section, we briefly discuss the following
well-known techniques of proof in mathematics:

i) Direct proof and counter examples

11) Proof by contradiction and Proof by contrapositive

111) Proof by cases

iv) Existence proof

v) Proof by mathematical induction

vi) Proof of equivalences

Generally, for different types of problems, different techniques may be more
appropriate, yet, of course, for some problem types, more than one technique
may be equally appropriate.

A problem requiring a proof consists explicitly of two parts, viz. (1) what is given
as a set of assumed facts/axioms/ postulates etc, all taken together to be called
Hypotheses and (11) what is to be proved (to be called Conclusion). Apart from
these, implicitly, a number of definitions, tools/ techniques/ strategies, earlier
proved theorems etc. commonly used in such type of proofs, are also assumed to
be given.

For example, suppose that we are given the problem: Prove that for all integers

convincingly and efficiently.

In the previous unit, we discussed the two basic concepts of Mathematics, viz.
number and set. In this unit, we discuss some basic methods for solving some
types of equations. Mathematical equations involve concepts of variable,
constant, parameter and solution. In this unit, before discussing the methods, we
briefly discuss the concepts first.

In the Appendix, we discuss concepts and proof methods for deriving correct

EQUATIONS

From our earlier encounters with solving equations, we know that x* — 16 = 0 is

2.2.1 Symbol, Parameter, Variable and Constant

Symbol: More explicitly, each of the concepts viz. constant, variable, and
parameter is a symbol, where, in general, a symbol is a notation which
represents, or stands for, something else. For example, the four-letter word lake
represents some water-body. By ‘something else’, we mean that through trying to
touch the four-letter word Jake, our finger does not get wet, but through trying to
touch what is represented by the four-letter word, finger becomes wet. However,
in case we wish to talk about the four-letter word Jake itself, and not the water-
body, then the symbol for it is generally written as either of (1) ‘lake’, (11) “lake”,
and (111) Jake, or some combination of these, e. g. ‘lake’.
Parameter: In addition to the equations of the type x° — 16 = 0, we also come
across statements of the form: the equation x* + y’ =, with real number c>0,
represents a family of circles of radii Vc centered at origin (0, 0). Here the c on
R. H. S. is neither a constant nor a variable, but is a parameter, a concept
somewhat a hybrid of constant and variable. By taking a particular constant
value of c, say 4, we get one member, viz. the circle x’ + y’ = 4 of radius 2.
Then, we may discuss various questions, e. g. dimensions of a rectangle,
inscribed in the circle, having maximum area. Again, by taking c= 15, another
constant, we pass on to another member of the family, viz. x7 + y’ = 15, a circle
of radius V15. The significance of the concept of parameter lies in the fact that
many questions like ‘dimensions of a rectangle, inscribed in the circle, having
maximum area’ need not be discussed for each individual circle, but may be
discussed for the general member/circle x’ + y’ = c, for arbitrary positive real
number c. Then the answer for an individual case is obtained easily from the
answer for the general case. In this sense, a parameter is a sort of generalized
constant.

Variable is a symbol which can be measured and which may change. Examples
of a variable from the field of economics include price, interest-rate, wages,
quantity-bought, and quantity-sold. The quantity associated with a variable at
some particular point of time is called its value at that particular point of time.
For example, for the variable /nterest-rate on a particular day, if it is 6%, then
6% is a value of ‘Interest-rate’. A variable, e. g. ‘Interest-rate’, may assume only
any of its possible values. For example, (V—8) is not a possible value for the
variable ‘percentage-of-Interest-rate’; but it may be a possible value for the
variable x in the equation ax” + b x +c =0. The end letters x, y, z of Latin alphabet
along with some other letters are commonly used for naming variables.

Constant is a symbol for which possible value is a unique single value. For
example, each of the symbols 6 in Latin notation, and &, in Devnagari notation,

can assume only a unique value. Generally, the beginning letters a, b, c etc. of
Latin alphabet along with some other letters are used for naming constants.

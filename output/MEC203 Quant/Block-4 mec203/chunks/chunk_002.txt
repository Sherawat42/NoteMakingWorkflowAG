Subject matter of Real Analysis: It is the study of behaviour and
properties of real numbers (R), of sequences and series of real numbers, and
of real functions. Most of the functions studied are real-valued, however, the
codomain may be any other non-empty set also. A function is a

Limit is the fundamental concept of analysis both for real sequences/series
and for real-valued functions. For sequences and _ series, the other
fundamental concepts include convergence. For functions, other fundamental
concepts include continuity, differentiability and integrability.

Difference between Calculus and Real Analysis: Despite of the fact that
subject matter of each of calculus and real analysis involves most of the
same concepts including limit, continuity, derivative, integrals etc., the two
disciplines approach these topics from different perspectives: Real analysis
provides sound theoretical foundations and justifications for the calculations
done through calculus. For example, in calculus, we apply the rule of finding
derivative of quotient u/v, of two derivable functions u and v of x, with v 4

13.2 SEQUENCES

We have an intuitive idea of what a sequence is, e. g. a sequence of events in a
cultural program or we have an idea that (1, 2, 4, 8, ...) is a sequence of numbers
which are powers of 2. Sequence is a concept in which entities of its concern
come, in some order, one after another. A sequence, may be considered either as
a (special type) of set or as a (special type) of function.

Notation: Before we proceed further, it may be noted that the (standard) notation
for a sequence is the same as that for an ordered set, e. g., (1, 2, 4, 8, ...), which
is bounded by parentheses ‘()’instead of the set notation {1, 2, 4, 8, ...}, bounded
by braces ‘{}’.

13.2.1 Set-type Conceptualization of a Sequence
A sequence is a sort of a set, such that it

e is an ordered set: (E, V, I, L) and (V, I, L, E) are distinct sequences formed
out of the same set {E, I, V, L}.

e is an enumerated set, i.e., all the elements can be listed one after another
according to some ordering. For example, in the sequence {0, 1, 4, 9, ...} of
squares of integers, | is listed after 0; 4 is listed after 1, and so on. This sort
of listing is not possible for the elements of the set of real numbers with any
ordering. This sort of listing is not possible even for the elements of a small
interval like [0, 1]. For example, w.r.t the order-relation ‘<’, for the purpose
of listing, we do not know the real number immediately next to 0. Thus, any
interval of real numbers cannot be represented as a sequence w.r.t the
relation “<’. Actually, R, or any of its sub-interval, cannot be represented by
a sequence w.r.t any relation, because |R| > |N|, where |S| denotes the number
of elements in (or cardinality of) the set S.

e unlike a set, allows repetition of its elements, e. g. (1, 2, 4, 2, 4) is a correct
sequence representation, though {1, 2, 4, 2, 4}is not accepted as proper
notation for the set {1, 2, 4}.

30.4.1 MVUE and Blackwellisation

following theorem due to C. R. Rao and D. Blackwell.

30.6 METHODS OF ESTIMATION

When we are given a parameter, for example population proportion (p) or
population mean (u), the ultimate objective is to arrive at a number that best
represents the true value of the parameter. To achieve the objective, a sample is
used to compute that number which is a good guess for this true value of the
parameter. In this section, we will focus on three methods of estimation, namely:
Maximum Likelihood, Generalized Method of Moments and Partial Least
Squares.

30.6.1 Maximum Likelihood (ML)

Maximum Likelihood is the most widely used method of constructing an
estimator for an unknown parameter 0. In case the sample is large, it gives an

excellent estimator of the unknown parameter.

p.mf.

30.6.3 Partial Least Squares (PLS)

Partial Least Squares (PLS) method is used for estimation when either the
number of explanatory (independent) variables are quite large as compared to the
number of observations in the data or these explanatory variables are correlated.
This method of estimation decreases the number of independent variables so that
we just have relevant variables for the analysis. It further uses another method
known as Principal Component Analysis to identify the uncorrelated
components. Principal component analysis (PCA) is a method that reduces the
dimensionality of large data sets by transforming the large set of variables into a
smaller one that still contains most of the information in the original large set of
variables.

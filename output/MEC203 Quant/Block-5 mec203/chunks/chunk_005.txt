17.6 LET US SUM UP

In this unit we emphasized on two important tools, which have enormous
applications in economics viz., unconstrained and constrained optimisation.
Unconstrained optimisation problem deals with optimisation (ie., either
minimisation or, maximisation) problems where the domain of the objective
function is not limited by any constraint. We emphasised the basic theory of
constrained optimisation, with its division into two main parts viz., constrained
optimisation in the case of equality constraints and constrained optimisation in
the case of inequality constraints. There are many real-life economic problems
for which unconstrained and constrained optimisation techniques are necessary.
In the case of optimisation under equality constraints, we apply the Lagrangian
method and in the case of optimisation under inequality constraints, we apply the
Kuhn-Tucker theory. We also worked out some simple economic problems
which use these techniques of unconstrained and constrained optimization.

18.1 INTRODUCTION

Concave functions and homogeneous functions are widely observed in
economic models. Concave functions serve as spending functions and
homogeneous functions as demand functions. Cost and profit functions are
inherently homogeneous and concave. Hence, this unit discusses the concepts
of concavity and convexity in detail, including quasi-convexity and quasi-
concavity. Complementary slackness conditions and Kuhn—Tucker conditions
for the general mathematical programming problem, including equality and
inequality constraints are also explained as well as non-negative and free

variables are provided along with some applications.

18.2 CONVEXITY AND CONCAVITY

In this section we will learn about the basic concepts of convexity and
concavity first and then proceed to differentiable convex, quasi/convex and

concave, quasi/concave functions.
18.2.1 Convex and Concave Functions and Generalization

There are numerous unique and significant characteristics of concave and
convex functions. Concave and convex functions are important subjects that
are introduced here along with some of their characteristics. For optimality
issues involving convex and concave functions, these qualities can be used to
create adequate optimality criteria and computational frameworks. Convexity
is a key idea in economics. Convexity is frequently assumed in economic
models for the budget set of agents. In many cases, we suppose that agent
preferences are convex and utility functions are (quasi)concave. These facts
provide us with motivation to examine the (quasi)concave and (quasi)convex
functions.

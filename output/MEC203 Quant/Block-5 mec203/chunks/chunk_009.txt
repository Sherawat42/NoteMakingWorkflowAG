19.7 LET US SUM UP

In this unit, we emphasised on the basic theory of constrained optimisation,
with its division into two main parts viz., constrained optimisation in the case
of equality constraints and constrained optimisation in the case of inequality
constraints. We considered two different techniques for solving these two
different types of problems. In case of optimisation under equality constraints,
we apply Lagrangian method to solve those problems, and in the latter case,
that is, in the case for optimisation under inequality constraints, we apply the
Kuhn-Tucker theory. We saw that this theory is different in many respects.
First, the first order conditions of Lagrangian theory and the Kuhn-Tucker
theory are different. Secondly, in Kuhn-Tucker theory there is no need to
check the second order condition, while in case of Lagrangian case one must
check it to ensure whether the optimum value (that we get from first order
condition) is maximum or minimum. Finally, in Kuhn-Tucker theory there is a
new set of conditions called “complementary slackness condition”, which
does not exist

We started with the Envelope Theorem for unconstrained optimization
problems and moved towards more general case for constrained optimization
problems. We then presented characterizations of optimisation problems under
a weakening of the condition of concave functions, called “quasi-concave”
functions. Some of the characterizations of these functions as a generalization
of concave functions and proves are discussed in terms of its contour sets
(level sets), derivatives and extreme properties of the functions on line
segment.

## Section: AD Curve Derivation (3.5.1)

### Core Idea

The **AD curve** is derived from the IS-LM model by varying the price level and tracing the resulting equilibrium output. A lower price level increases real money balances (M/P), shifts LM rightward, lowers the interest rate, boosts investment, and increases equilibrium output. The AD curve is therefore **downward-sloping** in price-output space.

### Key Concepts

#### Derivation Process (Fig. 3.8)

1. Start with IS₀ and LM₀ at price P₀ → equilibrium at E (i₀, Y₀) → point 'a' on AD
2. Price falls to P₁ (M unchanged) → real balances M₀/P₁ > M₀/P₀ → LM shifts right to LM₁
3. New equilibrium at 'b' (i₁, Y₁) → point 'b' on AD
4. Connecting 'a' and 'b' gives the **downward-sloping AD curve**

#### Why AD Slopes Downward

The chain of causation:
P↓ → M/P↑ → excess money supply → people buy bonds → bond prices↑ → i↓ → I↑ → AD↑ → Y↑

This is called the **Keynes effect** (or interest rate channel).

#### AD Curve Properties

- **Flatter AD**: A given price change produces a larger output change (steep LM or flat IS)
- **Steeper AD**: A given price change produces a smaller output change

#### Factors Shifting AD

- **Rightward shift**: Expansionary fiscal policy (↑G, ↓T), expansionary monetary policy (↑M), ↑autonomous consumption or investment — anything that shifts IS or LM rightward
- **Leftward shift**: Contractionary fiscal or monetary policy — anything that shifts IS or LM leftward

## Section: Aggregate Supply (AS) Curve (3.5.2)

### Core Idea

Three views of the AS curve represent different schools of thought:
1. **Classical (vertical)**: Output is perfectly inelastic to price — always at full employment
2. **Keynesian (horizontal)**: Firms can expand output without affecting prices — extreme assumption of excess capacity
3. **New Classical/Lucas (upward-sloping)**: In the short run, firms respond to price incentives — higher prices elicit more output; in the long run, AS is vertical

### Key Concepts

#### Lucas Aggregate Supply Hypothesis

Lucas argued that economic agents respond to wage and price incentives, which influence their labor-leisure trade-off:
- When actual wage > equilibrium wage → households supply more labor → output rises
- When actual wage < equilibrium wage → households supply less labor → output falls

Result: **SRAS** (Short-Run Aggregate Supply) is **upward-sloping**; **LRAS** (Long-Run Aggregate Supply) is **vertical** at potential output. The LRAS shifts only with changes in productive capacity (technology, capital, labor force).

#### AD-AS Equilibrium (Fig. 3.9)

Equilibrium at intersection of AD and upward-sloping SRAS at point E (Y₀, P₀). A favorable demand shock shifts AD rightward to AD₁ → new equilibrium at F with higher output Y₁ and higher prices P₁. However, this output increase is **temporary** — in the long run, the economy returns to the LRAS level.

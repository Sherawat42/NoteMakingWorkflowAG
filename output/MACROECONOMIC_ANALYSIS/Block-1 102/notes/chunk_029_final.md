## Section: Fiscal Policy under Flexible Exchange Rates (5.3.1)

### Core Idea

Under flexible exchange rates with perfect capital mobility, **fiscal policy is completely ineffective**. Fiscal expansion raises the interest rate temporarily, attracting capital inflows that appreciate the currency, which crowds out net exports and shifts IS back to its original position. Output returns to its initial level.

### Key Concepts

#### Mechanism (Fig. 5.2)

Starting at E₀ (i₀ = if, Y₀):
1. Fiscal expansion (↑G or ↓t) → IS shifts right: IS₀ → IS₁
2. Interest rate rises to i₁ > if at E₁; output temporarily rises to Y₁
3. i₁ > if → domestic bonds more attractive → capital inflows → BOP surplus
4. **Excess supply of foreign exchange** → currency appreciates (e falls)
5. With fixed prices: e falls → r falls (real appreciation)
6. Exports fall, imports rise → NX falls → AD falls
7. IS shifts left (back from IS₁)
8. Process continues until IS returns to IS₀ where i = if again
9. **Final result**: Y = Y₀ (unchanged), i = if (unchanged), but NX is lower (trade balance worsened) and fiscal position deteriorated (higher G or lower t)

#### Why Fiscal Policy Fails

The entire demand stimulus is offset by **exchange rate appreciation** that worsens the trade balance. The crowding out under flexible rates operates through the exchange rate channel (not the interest rate channel as in the closed economy).

## Section: Monetary Policy under Flexible Exchange Rates (5.3.2)

### Core Idea

Under flexible exchange rates with perfect capital mobility, **monetary policy is extremely effective**. Monetary expansion lowers the interest rate, triggering capital outflows that depreciate the currency, boost net exports, and shift IS rightward until i returns to if at a higher output level.

### Key Concepts

#### Mechanism (Fig. 5.3)

Starting at E₀ (i₀ = if, Y₀):
1. Monetary expansion (↑M) → LM shifts right: LM₀ → LM₁
2. Interest rate falls to i₁ < if at E₁
3. i₁ < if → capital outflows → BOP deficit → excess demand for foreign exchange
4. Currency **depreciates** (e rises) → with fixed prices, r rises (real depreciation)
5. Domestic goods cheaper → X rises, M falls → NX rises → AD rises
6. IS shifts right: IS₀ → IS₂ → IS₃
7. Process continues until IS reaches IS₃ where i = if again
8. **Final result**: Output rises from Y₀ to Y₃; i returns to if; trade balance improves (NX higher)

#### Why Monetary Policy Works

Monetary policy is amplified by the exchange rate channel: the initial monetary stimulus is reinforced by currency depreciation that boosts net exports, providing additional demand stimulus. The final output increase (Y₀ → Y₃) exceeds what would occur in a closed economy.

### Definitions

- **Exchange Rate Crowding Out**: Under flexible rates, fiscal expansion is offset by currency appreciation that reduces net exports — a form of "crowding out" distinct from the interest-rate crowding out of the closed economy.

### Connections

- This result is the **first half** of the Mundell-Fleming policy conclusions
- Contrasts sharply with the fixed exchange rate case (Chunk 030)

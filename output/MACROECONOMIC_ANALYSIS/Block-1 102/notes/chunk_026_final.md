## Section: Overall BOP Balance & Foreign Exchange Reserves

### Core Idea

Overall BOP balance = Current Account + Capital Account (incl. errors & omissions) + Official Reserve Transactions = 0. A BOP surplus leads to accumulation of foreign exchange reserves; a deficit depletes them. The central bank's **official reserve transactions** keep the BOP balanced in an accounting sense.

### Key Concepts

#### BOP Surplus/Deficit Scenarios

| Current Account | Capital Account | BOP | Reserves |
|----------------|----------------|-----|----------|
| Surplus | Surplus | Surplus | Increase |
| Deficit | Larger surplus | Surplus | Increase |
| Surplus | Smaller deficit | Surplus | Increase |
| Deficit | Deficit | Deficit | Decrease |
| Deficit | Smaller surplus | Deficit | Decrease |

**Sign convention**: BOP surplus → positive; corresponding reserve increase → recorded as **negative** in BOP (outflow of domestic currency to purchase foreign assets). Decrease in reserves → positive sign.

## Section: Exchange Rates (4.3.2)

### Core Idea

The exchange rate is a critical variable in an open economy. It determines the relative price of domestic and foreign goods. The text discusses **nominal** exchange rates, **real** exchange rates, and three types of exchange rate regimes (flexible, managed float, and fixed).

### Key Concepts

#### Nominal Exchange Rate

**e = number of units of domestic currency per unit of foreign currency** (e.g., e = ₹80/$ means ₹80 buys $1)

- **e rises** (₹80 → ₹82/$): Domestic currency **depreciates** (becomes cheaper); foreign currency appreciates
- **e falls** (₹80 → ₹78/$): Domestic currency **appreciates** (becomes more valuable)

#### Real Exchange Rate

**r = (e × pf) / pd** ... (4.6)

where e = nominal exchange rate, pf = foreign price level (in dollars), pd = domestic price level (in rupees).

- **r rises** → domestic currency experiences **real depreciation** → foreign goods more expensive relative to domestic → boosts exports, reduces imports
- **r falls** → domestic currency experiences **real appreciation** → domestic goods more expensive → hurts exports

If prices are unchanged, nominal depreciation causes equal real depreciation. But if domestic prices rise simultaneously, real and nominal exchange rates can move in **opposite directions**.

#### Exchange Rate Regimes

1. **Flexible (Floating)** — "clean float" or "pure float":
   - Exchange rate determined by demand and supply of foreign exchange
   - No government intervention, no need for reserves
   - BOP balance achieved through exchange rate adjustment
   - **Volatility** is a drawback — unfavorable for exporters/importers; rarely observed in pure form

2. **Managed Float** — "dirty float":
   - Currency floats within a **target zone/band**
   - Central bank intervenes (buys/sells foreign exchange) when the rate breaches band limits
   - Most countries in practice use managed floats
   - Requires maintaining foreign exchange reserves

3. **Fixed Exchange Rate**:
   - Government fixes exchange rate at a pre-announced level
   - Central bank must intervene continuously to maintain the parity
   - Requires **adequate foreign exchange reserves**
   - **Revaluation**: Official decrease in 'e' (currency becomes more expensive)
   - **Devaluation**: Official increase in 'e' (currency becomes cheaper)

### Definitions

- **Nominal Exchange Rate (e)**: Units of domestic currency per unit of foreign currency.
- **Real Exchange Rate (r)**: Price of foreign goods in terms of domestic goods; index of international price competitiveness.
- **Depreciation/Appreciation**: Market-driven changes in a floating exchange rate.
- **Devaluation/Revaluation**: Official changes in a fixed exchange rate.
- **Managed Float**: Exchange rate regime where the currency floats within a band with central bank intervention.

## Section: Policy Trilemma (Impossible Trinity)

### Core Idea

The **policy trilemma** (also called the "impossible trinity") states that it is impossible to simultaneously maintain all three of: (a) **perfect capital mobility**, (b) **independent monetary policy**, and (c) **fixed exchange rates**. A country can choose at most two of these three features.

### Key Concepts

#### The Three Combinations

1. **Fixed exchange rate + Perfect capital mobility** → No independent monetary policy (money supply becomes endogenous, as shown in Section 5.4.2). The central bank must intervene to maintain the parity, giving up control over money supply.

2. **Flexible exchange rate + Perfect capital mobility** → Independent monetary policy is possible AND effective (as shown in Section 5.3.2). The exchange rate adjusts to maintain external balance.

3. **Fixed exchange rate + Independent monetary policy** → Requires **capital controls** (restrictions on international capital flows). Interest rate differentials can persist because capital cannot freely arbitrage them away.

### Definitions

- **Policy Trilemma / Impossible Trinity**: The proposition that a country cannot simultaneously have all three of: (i) free capital mobility, (ii) a fixed exchange rate, and (iii) an independent monetary policy. Only two of the three are achievable at any time.
- **Endogenous Money Supply**: Under fixed exchange rates with perfect capital mobility, money supply passively adjusts to BOP flows as the central bank intervenes to defend the parity. The central bank loses control over the money supply.

## Section: Unit 5 Summary (5.5)

### Core Idea

The open economy IS-LM model (Mundell-Fleming) shows that:
1. **International trade** affects aggregate demand through net exports
2. **Capital flows** shape policy outcomes through their impact on interest rates, exchange rates, and money supply
3. Under **fixed exchange rates** + perfect capital mobility: money supply becomes endogenous; fiscal policy is effective; monetary policy is ineffective
4. Under **flexible exchange rates** + perfect capital mobility: monetary policy is effective (amplified by exchange rate channel); fiscal policy is ineffective (offset by currency appreciation)
5. The **policy trilemma** limits policy options: at most two of three (capital mobility, fixed rates, independent monetary policy) can coexist

### Key Takeaway Table: Mundell-Fleming Results

| | Flexible Exchange Rate | Fixed Exchange Rate |
|------------------|----------------------|---------------------|
| **Fiscal Policy** | Ineffective (crowded out by appreciation) | Effective (accommodated by monetary expansion) |
| **Monetary Policy** | Effective (amplified by depreciation) | Ineffective (reversed by reserve intervention) |
| **Exchange Rate** | Adjusts freely | Fixed by central bank |
| **Money Supply** | Exogenous (CB controls) | Endogenous (adjusts to reserves) |

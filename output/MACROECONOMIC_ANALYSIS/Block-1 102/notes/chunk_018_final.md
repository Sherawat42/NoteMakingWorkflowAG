### Key Concepts

#### Worked Example: Three-Sector Multiplier (CYP 2)

Given: C = 10 + 0.75Y, I = 20 (closed economy, no government)
- **AD function**: Y = 30 + 0.75Y → **Yₑ = 120**
- If I increases to 25: **Yₑ = 140** (increase of 20)
- **Multiplier**: 1/(1−0.75) = **4**
- With **proportional tax t = 0.1**: multiplier = 1/[1−0.75×0.9] = 1/0.325 = **3.08**
- With **open economy (m = 0.3)**: multiplier = 1/(1−0.75+0.3) = 1/0.55 = **1.82**

#### Paradox of Thrift Example (CYP 3, Q3)

Given: S = −10 + 0.25Y, I = 20 → Yₑ = 120. After increased thriftiness: S = −8 + 0.25Y → Yₑ = 112. Income **fell** from 120 to 112 due to higher saving desire.

#### Budget Surplus Implication (CYP 3, Q1b)

With a budget surplus (T > G), saving must be lower than investment since S + T = Ī + G implies S = Ī + (G − T) = Ī − surplus. Government surplus means more public saving, requiring less private saving for equilibrium.

## Section: Unit 3 — The Neoclassical Synthesis — Structure & Objectives (3.0-3.1)

### Core Idea

Unit 3 presents the **neoclassical synthesis** — the integration of classical and Keynesian ideas through the **IS-LM model**. This framework achieves simultaneous equilibrium in both the real (goods) market and the monetary (money) market. The IS curve represents goods market equilibrium (all combinations of interest rate and income where planned expenditure equals output). The LM curve represents money market equilibrium (all combinations where money demand equals money supply). Their intersection determines the economy's equilibrium interest rate and output simultaneously. The unit also derives the **AD curve** from IS-LM and combines it with the **AS curve** to determine the price level.

### Key Concepts

#### Unit 3 Topics Overview

- **3.2 Equilibrium in the Real Sector** — IS curve derivation and shifts
- **3.3 Equilibrium in the Monetary Sector** — LM curve derivation and shifts
- **3.4 Simultaneous Equilibrium** — IS-LM interaction, shocks, adjustment processes, supply shocks
- **3.5 AD-AS Model** — Deriving AD from IS-LM, aggregate supply, equilibrium

#### Learning Objectives

After completing Unit 3, students should be able to:
1. Explain equilibrium in the real sector and monetary sector
2. Understand the ideas behind the **IS curve** and **LM curve**
3. Explain how IS and LM curves **interact** to determine simultaneous equilibrium
4. Identify factors influencing the **position and slope** of IS and LM curves
5. Derive the **aggregate demand (AD) curve** from the IS-LM model
6. Identify factors that influence the AD curve

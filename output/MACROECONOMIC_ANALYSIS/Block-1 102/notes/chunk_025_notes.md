## Section: Balance of Payments (4.3.1)

### Core Idea

The **Balance of Payments (BOP)** is a comprehensive record of all transactions between domestic residents and foreigners during a given period (typically one year). It consists of two main accounts — the **current account** (trade in goods, services, factor incomes, and transfers) and the **capital account** (cross-border purchase and sale of assets). The BOP uses double-entry bookkeeping, so in an accounting sense it is always balanced.

### Key Concepts

#### Current Account Components

The current account records receipts from and payments to foreigners for trade in goods and services:

1. **Balance of Trade (Merchandise Trade Balance)**: Exports minus imports of goods. Can be positive (trade surplus), negative (trade deficit), or zero.

2. **Invisibles**: All non-merchandise current account items:
   - **Trade in Services**: Net services exports (e.g., India's software exports)
   - **Net Investment Income from Abroad**: Investment earnings (dividends, interest) on foreign assets held by domestic residents minus foreigners' earnings on domestic assets. Credit = inflow; debit = outflow.
   - **Net Unilateral Transfers**: Overseas payments without quid pro quo — foreign aid, workers' remittances, gifts, donations. India is the world's largest recipient of remittances ($87 billion in 2021).

3. **Current Account Balance** = Balance on merchandise trade + Balance on invisibles

A country can have a **trade deficit but current account surplus** if net invisibles (e.g., remittances) are sufficiently positive.

#### Capital Account Components

Records cross-border purchase and sale of real and financial assets:

1. **Debt-Creating Flows**: Borrowing from foreign institutions (must be repaid) — capital inflow that creates future obligations
2. **Non-Debt Creating Flows**: Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) — no repayment obligation
3. **Short-term flows**: Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI) — equity purchases by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), can be withdrawn at short notice
4. **Long-term flows**: Greenfield FDI — investment in production sector, less volatile

- **Capital Account Surplus**: Capital inflows > outflows
- **Capital Account Deficit**: Outflows > inflows

#### Current-Capital Account Mirror

A surplus in the current account is mirrored by a deficit in the capital account, and vice versa. A current account deficit must be financed by net capital inflows (borrowing or selling assets).

### Definitions

- **Balance of Payments (BOP)**: Comprehensive record of all transactions between domestic residents and foreigners during a period.
- **Current Account**: Records trade in goods, services, factor incomes, and transfers.
- **Capital Account**: Records cross-border transactions in real and financial assets.
- **FDI (Foreign Direct Investment)**: Long-term, non-debt creating capital inflow through foreign firms investing in domestic production.
- **FPI (Foreign Portfolio Investment)**: Short-term capital flows through purchase/sale of financial assets (stocks, bonds) by foreign institutional investors.
- **Workers' Remittances**: Money sent by overseas workers to their home countries; a major source of foreign exchange for developing nations.

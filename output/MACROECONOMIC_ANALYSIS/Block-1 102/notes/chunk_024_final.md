## Section: National Income Identity in an Open Economy (4.1/4.2)

### Core Idea

In an open economy, domestic agents interact with foreigners through trade and capital flows. The open economy national income identity adds **net exports** (NX = X − M) to aggregate expenditure. This creates fundamental differences from the closed economy: aggregate expenditure can exceed aggregate income, and current account imbalances reflect real sector imbalances in saving, investment, and government finances.

### Key Concepts

#### Open Economy National Income Identity

**Y = C + I + G + X − M = C + I + G + NX** ... (4.1)

where NX = X − M = net exports = balance of trade. NX > 0 → trade surplus; NX < 0 → trade deficit.

#### GDP vs GNP

- **GDP (Y)**: Aggregate income earned within the country's geographical boundaries (by both domestic and foreign factors)
- **GNP**: Aggregate income earned by citizens of the country, regardless of location
- **NFIA** (Net Factor Income from Abroad): Income earned abroad by domestic factors minus income earned by foreign factors domestically
- **GNP = Y + NFIA** ... (4.2)

Example: Salary of a German consultant in Delhi → part of German GNP and India's GDP. Salary of an Indian worker in Singapore → Singapore's GDP and India's GNP.

#### Current Account Balance

**CA = NX + NFIA** (current account = trade balance + net factor income)

**GNP − (C + I + G) = CA** ... (4.3)

- **CA < 0** (current account deficit): Aggregate domestic absorption (C+I+G) **exceeds** aggregate income (GNP) → country is a net borrower
- **CA > 0** (current account surplus): Aggregate income **exceeds** absorption → country is a net lender

#### Twin Deficits Identity

From income identity (GNP = C + S + T) and expenditure identity:

**(S − I) + (T − G) = CA** ... (4.5)

This is the **twin deficits** identity:
- **CA < 0** reflects either excess private investment over saving (S−I < 0), or budget deficit (T−G < 0), or both
- A current account deficit can coexist with private saving surplus if the fiscal deficit is large enough
- In a **closed economy**, CA = 0 always, so (S−I) must always equal −(T−G)

#### Open vs Closed Economy Differences

In a closed economy:
- Aggregate expenditure cannot exceed aggregate income
- Private sector investment-saving gap must be offset by public saving

In an open economy:
- Countries can **borrow from the rest of the world** to sustain excess expenditure over income
- Current account deficits draw on savings of surplus countries through international capital flows

### Definitions

- **Net Exports (NX)**: Exports minus imports; also called balance of trade
- **Current Account (CA)**: NX + NFIA; measures net income from all current transactions with foreigners
- **Twin Deficits**: The identity linking current account deficit to private sector deficit (S−I<0) and/or fiscal deficit (T−G<0)
- **NFIA**: Net Factor Income from Abroad; the difference between factor earnings received from and paid to the rest of the world

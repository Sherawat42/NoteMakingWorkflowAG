## Section: Impact of Demand Shock (3.5 cont.)

### Core Idea

A favorable demand shock (e.g., increased government spending or consumer confidence) shifts the AD curve rightward, causing both output and prices to rise in the short run. However, the output increase is **temporary** — in the long run, wages and prices adjust and output returns to the LRAS (full employment) level, with only prices permanently higher.

## Section: Unit 3 Summary (3.6)

### Core Idea

The IS-LM model integrates the real sector (goods market → IS curve) and monetary sector (money market → LM curve). Key takeaways:

1. **IS curve**: Downward-sloping; every point represents goods market equilibrium. Left of IS = excess demand; right of IS = excess supply.
2. **LM curve**: Upward-sloping; every point represents money market equilibrium. Left of LM = excess money supply; right of LM = excess money demand.
3. **Simultaneous equilibrium**: IS-LM intersection gives unique (i, Y).
4. **Disequilibrium correction**: Economy tends toward equilibrium automatically.
5. **AD curve**: Derived from IS-LM by varying prices. Downward-sloping in P-Y space.
6. **AD-AS equilibrium**: Determines both output and price level.

## Section: Answers/Hints (3.7)

### Key Concepts

#### CYP 1: Effects on IS Curve

1. **Decrease in MPC (b)**: Affects both intercept (decreases) and slope of IS. The term e/(1−b−d) increases → IS becomes **steeper** (less interest-sensitive).
2. (i) **Decrease in autonomous G**: IS shifts leftward (parallel).
   (ii) **Decrease in proportional tax rate**: IS becomes flatter — lower t effectively increases the multiplier. (With T = t₀ + t₁Y, substituting into equation 3.4 shows that lower t₁ increases the multiplier and flattens IS.)

#### CYP 2: Vertical LM

When LM is vertical, money demand is **insensitive to the interest rate** (h = 0). This is the polar opposite of the liquidity trap. In this case, fiscal policy is completely ineffective (IS shifts don't change Y) and monetary policy is maximally effective.

#### CYP 3: AD Curve

The AD curve derives from IS-LM (Section 3.5.1). Its position and slope depend on all factors influencing IS and LM: autonomous spending, tax rates, MPC, money supply, and money demand parameters.

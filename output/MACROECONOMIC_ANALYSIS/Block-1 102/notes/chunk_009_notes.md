## Section: Unit 1 Summary — Let Us Sum Up (1.9)

### Core Idea

Unit 1 discussed the evolution of macroeconomic schools of thought and presented the classical approach in detail. The classical theory, relying on the assumption of **full flexibility of wages and prices**, concludes that the economy always tends to equilibrium at the **full employment level of output**. The capital market ensures sufficient demand always exists (Say's Law). The Quantity Theory of Money determines price levels and wage rates without affecting real economic variables. Classical theory advocates that **policy intervention cannot influence output** because the economy is self-adjusting. Money is considered **neutral** — it does not affect real variables.

### Key Concepts

#### Unit 1 Key Takeaways

The core conclusions of the classical model can be distilled as follows:
1. **Flexible wages and prices** → automatic market clearing → full employment
2. **Say's Law** → supply creates its own demand → no demand deficiency possible
3. **Quantity Theory** → M determines P; real output Y determined by real factors alone
4. **Classical Dichotomy** → real and nominal sectors are independent
5. **Neutrality of Money** → changes in M affect only P and W, not Y or N
6. **No role for policy** → government intervention unnecessary and ineffective for output
7. **Long-run applicability** → the model may not hold in the short run when prices are sticky

## Section: Unit 1 Answers/Hints to Exercises (1.10)

### Core Idea

The answer hints for Unit 1's three Check Your Progress exercises reinforce the main concepts. They provide concise summaries that serve as revision tools for the key classical propositions.

### Key Concepts

#### New-Classical Features (CYP 1, Q2)
New-classical economics retains classical market-clearing assumptions but introduces three modern features: **(i) general equilibrium framework**, **(ii) microfoundations** (grounding macro behavior in individual optimization), and **(iii) rational expectations** (agents use all available information to form expectations).

#### QTM Summary (CYP 2, Q2)
The quantity theory of money (MV = PY) establishes the relationship between money supply, velocity of money, price level, and output. When V and Y are constant, an increase in M leads to a proportional increase in P.

#### Say's Law Implication (CYP 2, Q3)
According to Say's Law, supply creates its own demand. This rules out the possibility of unemployment — there can be no general overproduction or demand deficiency.

#### Classical Dichotomy Summary (CYP 3, Q1)
The classical dichotomy refers to the proposition that nominal variables do not influence real variables. It arises from the assumptions of perfect competition and full flexibility in prices and wages.

#### Short-Run Limitation (CYP 3, Q3)
Prices and wages may not be fully flexible in practice. Rigidities prevent instantaneous adjustment in output and employment. Therefore, **classical theory may not be applicable in the short run** — motivating the Keynesian framework.

## Section: Unit 2 — The Keynesian Model — Structure & Objectives (2.0)

### Core Idea

Unit 2 presents the **Keynesian model** — the alternative framework developed by John Maynard Keynes to explain short-run macroeconomic behavior, particularly unemployment and output fluctuations. Unlike the classical model, the Keynesian model assumes that output is **demand-determined** (not supply-determined), wages and prices are **sticky** (not flexible), and **government intervention** is both necessary and effective.

### Key Concepts

#### Unit 2 Topics Overview

The unit covers the following topics:
- **2.0 Objectives** — Learning goals

- **2.7 Summary** and **2.8 Answers**

#### Learning Objectives for Unit 2

After completing Unit 2, students should be able to:
1. Appreciate the **assumptions** underlying the Keynesian model
2. Explain the **consumption function** and **saving function**
3. Identify factors affecting **investment demand**
4. Show that equilibrium output is determined by **aggregate demand** (not aggregate supply)
5. Explain the various **multipliers** (investment, government expenditure, tax, open economy)
6. Explain the **paradox of thrift**
7. Explain **liquidity preference** and the **liquidity trap**
8. Discuss the Keynesian view on the **role of government** in the economy

### Connections

- **Directly contrasts** with the classical approach (Unit 1, Chunks 003-008): demand vs. supply determination, sticky vs. flexible prices, active vs. passive government
- Responds to the **failures of classical theory** during the Great Depression — explains persistent unemployment through demand deficiency
- Provides the foundation for the **IS-LM synthesis** in Unit 3 (Chunks 018-023)

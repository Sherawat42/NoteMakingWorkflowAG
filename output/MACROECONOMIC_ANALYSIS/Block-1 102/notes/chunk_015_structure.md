## Section: Paradox of Thrift (2.4.3)
<!-- This section starts and completes here -->

### Core Idea
<!-- placeholder: Increased desire to save (a virtue for individuals) can be a vice for the economy — it reduces aggregate demand, lowers output, and saving may end up unchanged or even lower -->

### Key Concepts
#### Paradox with Autonomous Investment
<!-- placeholder: Saving function shifts up (S₀→S₁) → equilibrium income falls → saving falls back to original level (equals unchanged Ī) → no net gain in aggregate saving despite higher thriftiness -->

#### Paradox with Induced Investment
<!-- placeholder: When I = c + dY, increased saving → lower consumption → lower output → lower induced investment → saving actually decreases at new equilibrium (BY₁ < AY₀) -->

#### Short-Run vs Long-Run Role of Saving
<!-- placeholder: Short run with excess capacity → higher saving = lower demand = lower output; Long run → higher saving enables higher investment and productive capacity growth -->

### Definitions
- **Paradox of Thrift**: <!-- placeholder: attempt to save more leads to same or lower aggregate saving due to income decline -->

### Mechanisms / Processes

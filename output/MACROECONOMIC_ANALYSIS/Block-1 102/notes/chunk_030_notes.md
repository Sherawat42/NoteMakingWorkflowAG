## Section: Fiscal Policy under Fixed Exchange Rates (5.4.1)

### Core Idea

Under fixed exchange rates with perfect capital mobility, **fiscal policy is extremely effective**. Fiscal expansion raises the interest rate, attracting capital inflows. The central bank must buy the incoming foreign exchange to maintain the fixed parity, which increases money supply, shifts LM rightward, and accommodates the fiscal expansion — with **no crowding out**.

### Key Concepts

#### Mechanism (Fig. 5.4)

Starting at E₀ (i₀ = if, Y₀), with fixed exchange rate e = e*:
1. Fiscal expansion (↑G or ↓t) → IS shifts right: IS₀ → IS₁
2. Interest rate rises to i₁ > if
3. Higher domestic rates → capital inflows → BOP surplus → excess supply of foreign exchange
4. Central bank is committed to fixed e* → must **buy dollars** to absorb excess supply
5. Buying dollars → foreign exchange reserves increase → domestic money supply increases
6. LM shifts right: LM₀ → LM₁
7. Process continues as long as i > if
8. Stops when LM reaches LM₁ where i = if at E₁
9. **Final result**: Output rises from Y₀ to Y₁; i returns to if; money supply has passively increased (M₁ > M₀); **no crowding out**

#### Why Fiscal Policy Works

The central bank's intervention to maintain the fixed exchange rate automatically provides the **monetary accommodation** needed to sustain the fiscal expansion. The interest rate returns to if without the exchange rate adjustment that would have offset the stimulus under flexible rates.

## Section: Monetary Policy under Fixed Exchange Rates (5.4.2)

### Core Idea

Under fixed exchange rates with perfect capital mobility, **monetary policy is completely ineffective**. Any monetary expansion is fully reversed by the central bank's obligation to defend the fixed parity.

### Key Concepts

#### Mechanism (Fig. 5.5)

Starting at E₀ (i₀ = if, Y₀):
1. Central bank conducts open market purchase → money supply increases (M₀ → M₁) → LM shifts right: LM₀ → LM₁
2. Interest rate falls to i₁ < if at E₁
3. Lower domestic rates → capital outflows → BOP deficit → excess demand for foreign exchange
4. Central bank must **sell dollars** to maintain fixed e* → foreign exchange reserves fall → money supply falls
5. LM shifts back leftward
6. Process continues until LM returns to LM₀ and i = if
7. **Final result**: Everything returns to initial position — Y = Y₀, i = if, M = M₀; monetary expansion had **zero effect**

#### Endogeneity of Money Supply

Under fixed exchange rates with perfect capital mobility, money supply becomes **endogenous** — it adjusts passively to maintain the fixed parity. The central bank cannot independently control the money supply because any attempt to change it is reversed by the need to intervene in foreign exchange markets.

### Connections

- These results complete the **Mundell-Fleming model** policy summary:

| Policy | Flexible Rate | Fixed Rate |
|--------|--------------|------------|
| **Fiscal** | Ineffective | Effective |
| **Monetary** | Effective | Ineffective |

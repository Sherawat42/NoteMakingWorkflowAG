## Section: Unit 4 Summary (4.4)

### Core Idea

Unit 4 established the foundations of open economy macroeconomics:
1. **National income identities** show fundamental differences between open and closed economies — open economies can run current account deficits by borrowing internationally
2. **BOP accounts** systematically record all international transactions in current and capital accounts
3. **Exchange rates** (nominal and real) are critical variables affecting international competitiveness and trade
4. **Exchange rate regimes** range from fully flexible to fixed, with most countries using managed floats

## Section: Unit 5 — Open Economy Macroeconomics II — Structure & Introduction (5.0-5.1)

### Core Idea

Unit 5 introduces the **open economy IS-LM framework** (Mundell-Fleming model), developed by Robert Mundell and Marcus Fleming in the early 1960s. This model extends the closed-economy IS-LM framework to incorporate international trade and capital mobility. The central question: how effective are **monetary and fiscal policies** under different exchange rate regimes (fixed vs. flexible) when capital is perfectly mobile internationally?

### Key Concepts

#### Unit 5 Topics Overview

- **5.2 Open Economy IS-LM**: Modified IS curve, LM curve, and equilibrium
- **5.3 Policy under Flexible Exchange Rates**: Fiscal policy effectiveness (5.3.1), monetary policy effectiveness (5.3.2)
- **5.4 Policy under Fixed Exchange Rates**: Fiscal policy (5.4.1), monetary policy (5.4.2)

#### Learning Objectives

1. Analyze short-run macroeconomic equilibrium using the open economy IS-LM model
2. Discuss the limits to monetary and fiscal policies under fixed vs. flexible exchange rate regimes

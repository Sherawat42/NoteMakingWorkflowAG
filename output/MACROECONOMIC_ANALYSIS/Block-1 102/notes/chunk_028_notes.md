## Section: Open Economy IS Curve (5.2.1)

### Core Idea

In an open economy, aggregate demand includes **net exports** NX = X − M, which depend on foreign GDP (Yf), domestic GDP (Y), and the real exchange rate (r). The open economy IS curve retains its downward slope but has two additional determinants: Yf and r. Changes in these shift the IS curve.

### Key Concepts

#### Open Economy AD

**AD = C(Yd) + I(i) + G + NX(Yf, Y, r)** ... (5.1)

where:
- X = X(Yf, r): Exports depend on foreign income and real exchange rate. Higher Yf → more exports; higher r (real depreciation) → domestic goods cheaper → more exports
- M = M(Y, r): Imports depend on domestic income and real exchange rate. Higher Y → more imports; higher r → foreign goods more expensive → fewer imports
- NX = X − M = NX(Yf, Y, r)

#### Marshall-Lerner Condition

Real depreciation (r rises) leads to trade balance improvement **only if** the sum of price elasticities of exports and imports exceeds one. The analysis assumes this condition is always satisfied.

#### IS Curve Shifts in Open Economy

- **Yf increases** → X rises → NX rises → IS shifts right
- **r increases** (real depreciation) → NX rises → IS shifts right
- **G increases** → Y rises → M rises → NX falls (trade balance worsens) — but overall IS still shifts right
- With fixed prices, nominal depreciation = real depreciation = IS shifts right

## Section: Open Economy LM Curve (5.2.2)

### Core Idea

The LM curve's fundamental properties are unchanged in an open economy — it is upward-sloping, representing money market equilibrium. However, two critical open-economy features are added: **perfect capital mobility** and the **endogeneity of money supply under fixed exchange rates**.

### Key Concepts

#### Perfect Capital Mobility

Assuming zero restrictions on capital flows and zero transaction costs:
- Domestic and foreign bonds are **perfect substitutes**
- Domestic interest rate must equal foreign interest rate: **i = if**
- If i > if → capital inflows → domestic bond prices rise → i falls
- If i < if → capital outflows → domestic bond prices fall → i rises
- In equilibrium, agents are indifferent between domestic and foreign bonds

#### Money Supply and Foreign Reserves

- The central bank's foreign exchange reserves are an asset. When the central bank buys foreign currency, it issues domestic currency → **money supply increases**
- Capital inflows → BOP surplus → reserves increase → money supply increases
- Capital outflows → BOP deficit → reserves decrease → money supply decreases

#### Sterilization

The central bank can use **open market operations** to offset the impact of reserve changes on money supply. Example: RBI buys dollars (reserves ↑, M ↑) but simultaneously sells bonds (M ↓), neutralizing the monetary impact. This is called **sterilized intervention**.

### Definitions

- **Perfect Capital Mobility**: No restrictions on cross-border capital flows; domestic and foreign bonds are perfect substitutes; i = if in equilibrium.
- **Sterilization**: Central bank operations to offset the monetary impact of changes in foreign exchange reserves.

## Section: Open Economy IS-LM Equilibrium (5.2.3)

### Core Idea

Macroeconomic equilibrium occurs at the intersection of IS and LM (Fig. 5.1), where i₀ = if. This represents both **internal balance** (goods and money market equilibrium) and **external balance** (BOP equilibrium). The **BP curve** is a horizontal line at i = if with perfect capital mobility, representing all interest-rate–income combinations where BOP is balanced.

### Key Concepts

#### BP Curve

- With **perfect capital mobility**: BP is horizontal at i = if
- Above BP → BOP surplus (capital inflows exceed what's needed)
- Below BP → BOP deficit (capital outflows)
- With **imperfect capital mobility**: BP would be upward-sloping (higher Y → larger CA deficit → higher i needed to attract capital inflows)

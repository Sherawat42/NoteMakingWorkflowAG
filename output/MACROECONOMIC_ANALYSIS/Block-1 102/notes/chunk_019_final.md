## Section: Equilibrium in the Real Sector (3.2)

### Core Idea

The IS curve represents all combinations of interest rate (i) and output (Y) at which the **goods market** (real sector) is in equilibrium — where planned expenditure equals output, equivalently where planned saving equals planned investment. The IS curve is **downward-sloping**: lower interest rates stimulate investment, raising aggregate demand and equilibrium output.

### Key Concepts

#### IS Equation Derivation

Starting from AD = C + I + G with C = a + b(Y−T̄) and I = c + dY − ei:

Y = a + b(Y−T̄) + (c + dY − ei) + Ḡ ... (3.3)

Solving for Y:

**Y = αG(Ā − ei)** ... (3.4a)

where:
- **αG = 1/(1−b−d)** — the **goods market multiplier** (incorporating both MPC and MPI)
- **Ā = a + c + Ḡ − bT̄** — total **autonomous spending**

The IS equation shows an **inverse relationship** between Y and i: as interest rates rise, investment falls, reducing equilibrium output.

#### IS Curve Slope

The slope of the IS curve (di/dY) = **−(1−b−d)/e**

- **Flatter IS**: Economy is more sensitive to interest rate changes (large 'e', large multiplier). A small decrease in interest rate produces a large increase in income.
- **Steeper IS**: Economy is insensitive to interest rate (small 'e', small multiplier). Interest rate changes have little effect on output.

### Section: Derivation of the IS Curve (3.2.1)

#### Graphical Derivation (Fig. 3.1)

**Panel (a)**: Keynesian cross diagram. At interest rate i₀, aggregate expenditure is AE₀; equilibrium at point 'a' on the 45° line at Y₀.

When i falls to i₁:
1. Investment increases (since I = c + dY − ei, lower i means higher I)
2. AE shifts up to AE₁
3. New equilibrium at point 'b' at higher output Y₁

**Panel (b)**: IS diagram. Plot point 'a' (i₀, Y₀) and point 'b' (i₁, Y₁). Connecting these gives the **downward-sloping IS curve**.

Every point ON the IS curve = goods market equilibrium (I = S).
Every point OFF the IS curve = goods market disequilibrium.

#### Disequilibrium in the Real Sector (Fig. 3.2)

- **Above/right of IS** (point 'c'): Interest rate is too high for the output level → investment is too low → aggregate demand < aggregate supply → **excess supply of goods** → firms cut production → output and interest fall toward IS
- **Below/left of IS** (point 'd'): Interest rate is too low for the output level → investment is too high → aggregate demand > aggregate supply → **excess demand for goods** → output and interest rise toward IS

### Section: Shift in the IS Curve (3.2.2)

Changes in **exogenous variables** (G, T) or **parameters** (a, c) shift the IS curve:
- **Increase in Ḡ** or **increase in autonomous consumption (a)** or **increase in autonomous investment (c)** → IS shifts **rightward** (parallel) — higher output at every interest rate
- **Increase in T̄** → IS shifts **leftward** — lower output at every interest rate
- **Change in MPC (b)** → changes the **slope** of IS (not a parallel shift)
- **Increase in MPC** → IS becomes flatter (larger multiplier, more responsive to interest rates)

### Definitions

- **IS Curve**: The locus of all (i, Y) combinations at which the goods market is in equilibrium (planned investment = planned saving, or equivalently AD = Y). Downward-sloping.
- **Goods Market Multiplier (αG)**: 1/(1−b−d), which amplifies the effect of autonomous spending changes on equilibrium output.
- **Autonomous Spending (Ā)**: The sum of all spending components independent of Y and i: a + c + Ḡ − bT̄.

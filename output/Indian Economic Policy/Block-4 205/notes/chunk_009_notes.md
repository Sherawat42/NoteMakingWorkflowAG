## Section: 16.6 Organised Service Sectors – Cross Cutting Policy Initiatives and Issues 🔴
### 16.6.1 Impact of Demonetisation on Service Sector in India
- **Short-Term Disruption**: Severely impacted cash-intensive, informal service sectors such as retail trade, unorganised transport, and local hotels/restaurants due to sudden liquidity constraints. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Long-Term Formalisation**: Accelerated the transition towards digital payments and formalised a significant portion of the shadow economy. ⭐ (exam-important)

### 16.6.2 Goods and Services Tax (GST): Impact on Services
- **Integration**: subsumed multiple cascading taxes into a single structure (tax slabs for services generally fall under 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%). ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Challenges**: Initially caused confusion regarding classification. The compliance burden increased, and complexities surrounding Input Tax Credit (ITC) created working capital issues for certain service providers (e.g., restaurants). ⭐ (exam-important)

### 16.6.3 Domestic Regulations and Barriers to Trade in Services
The WTO's General Agreement on Trade in Services (GATS) defines four modes of trade:
- **Mode 1 (Cross-border supply)**: e.g., Telemedicine, BPO. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Mode 2 (Consumption abroad)**: e.g., Tourism, students studying abroad. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Mode 3 (Commercial presence)**: e.g., Setting up a foreign bank branch. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Mode 4 (Presence of natural persons)**: e.g., IT professionals traveling abroad for projects. ⭐ (exam-important)

**Barriers to Trade**:
- **Non-Tariff Barriers (NTBs)**: Strict domestic regulations (complex licensing, foreign equity caps, and lack of standardization) restrict the free flow of services. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Immigration Laws**: Highly restrictive visa regimes (like the H-1B visa restrictions in the US) severely hamper India's Mode 4 exports (movement of IT professionals). ⭐ (exam-important)

### 16.6.4 Low Employment Elasticity in Organised Service Sectors
- **Jobless Growth**: While the services sector contributes over 54% to GVA, its share in total employment is disproportionately low (~30%). ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Skill Bias**: The fastest-growing sectors (IT, Telecom, Finance) are highly skill-intensive and capital-intensive. They generate high income but absorb a very small fraction of India's large, largely unskilled labour force. ⭐ (exam-important)

## Section: 16.7 Sector-specific Policy Initiatives and Issues in Selected Organised Sectors 🔴
### 16.7.1 IT-BPM / Software Sector
- **Significance**: It is the largest private-sector employer and the premier export earner for India. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Challenges**: ⭐ (exam-important)
  - **Automation & AI**: Emerging technologies threaten traditional, low-end Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) jobs.
  - **Protectionism**: Increasing protectionist policies and stringent visa norms in key markets (USA, UK) restrict market access.
  - **Infrastructure**: Poor urban infrastructure and high attrition rates.

### 16.7.2 Challenges and Prospects of Indian Tourism
- **Significance**: High potential for mass employment generation and foreign exchange earnings. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Challenges**: Severe infrastructure deficit (roads, sanitation), pervasive safety and security concerns for tourists, high taxation structures, and a shortage of trained hospitality personnel.  ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Policy Boost**: Introduction of the E-visa facility has been a major positive step to stimulate foreign tourist arrivals. ⭐ (exam-important)

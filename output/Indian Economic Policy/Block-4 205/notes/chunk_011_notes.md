## Section: 17.3 Characteristics of the Informal Sector 🔴
The informal service sector exhibits distinct features that separate it from the formal economy:
- **Small Scale & Low Organisation**: Typically operates on a very small scale (often single-person or family-run) with minimal formal organisation or accounting records. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Low Capital & High Labour Intensity**: Relies heavily on manual labour with very little investment in capital, technology, or machinery. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Ease of Entry and Exit**: Requires minimal capital or specialized skills to enter, making it the default fallback option for the unemployed. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Lack of Legal Protection**: Operates outside the purview of labor laws. Workers have no formal contracts, no job security, and no social security benefits (like pensions, provident funds, health insurance, or paid leave). ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Heterogeneity**: It is highly diverse, encompassing everything from street vendors, domestic workers, and rickshaw pullers to small independent mechanics and unregulated transport operators. ⭐ (exam-important)

## Section: 17.4 Factors Behind the Growth of the Informal Sector 🔴
The persistence and expansion of the informal sector are driven by a combination of macroeconomic factors:
- **Rural-Urban Migration (Push Factor)**: Agrarian distress and surplus labour in rural areas push millions to migrate to urban centers. Lacking the skills for formal jobs, they get absorbed into the urban informal service sector. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Jobless Growth in the Formal Sector**: The formal manufacturing and high-end service sectors (like IT) are highly capital/skill-intensive. They have completely failed to generate mass employment at a rate matching the demographic dividend. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Sub-contracting (Flexibilisation of Labour)**: To bypass rigid Indian labour laws and reduce operational costs, formal sector enterprises increasingly sub-contract work to informal units or hire temporary/contract workers who lack formal protections. ⭐ (exam-important)

## Section: 17.5 Contribution of the Informal Sector 🔴
Despite its vulnerabilities, the informal sector is a massive pillar of the Indian economy:
- **Employment**: It absorbs over **80-90% of the total workforce** in India, acting as the ultimate "shock absorber" for the unemployed. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **GDP Contribution**: The unorganized/informal sector contributes approximately **50% to the nation's GDP**. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Linkages**: It provides essential, low-cost services that subsidize the cost of living for urban formal workers and supports the formal economy through supply chain linkages. ⭐ (exam-important)

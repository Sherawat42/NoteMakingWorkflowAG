### 16.7.3 Shipping and Port Services: Initiatives and Challenges
- **Significance**: Critical for India's international trade; handles ~95% of trade by volume and ~68% by value. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Sagarmala Programme**: A flagship initiative aiming for "port-led development" by modernizing existing ports, developing new ones, and improving hinterland connectivity to reduce logistics costs. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Challenges**: ⭐ (exam-important)
  - **High Turnaround Time**: Indian ports suffer from significantly higher turnaround times compared to global benchmarks (e.g., Singapore, Rotterdam).
  - **Inadequate Draft Depth**: Many ports cannot accommodate ultra-large vessels.
  - **Connectivity**: Weak road and rail connectivity to the hinterland delays cargo evacuation.

### 16.7.4 Real Estate Sector in India: Policy Initiatives and Challenges
- **Significance**: A massive contributor to GDP and the second-largest employer (after agriculture), with deep forward and backward linkages to over 250 ancillary industries. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **RERA (Real Estate Regulation and Development Act, 2016)**: A landmark reform designed to protect homebuyers, ensure timely completion of projects, and bring transparency and accountability to the notoriously opaque sector. ⭐ (exam-important)

### 16.7.5 Policy Initiatives and Challenges being Faced by Banking and Financial Services
- **PMJDY (Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana)**: The world's largest financial inclusion drive, bringing millions of unbanked citizens into the formal financial system. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC, 2016)**: Established a consolidated and time-bound framework for resolving insolvency and bankruptcies, shifting control from debtors to creditors. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Challenges**:  ⭐ (exam-important)
  - **High NPAs**: The sector, particularly Public Sector Banks (PSBs), is burdened with massive Non-Performing Assets.
  - **Twin Balance Sheet Problem**: The economy suffered simultaneously from overleveraged corporate balance sheets and stressed bank balance sheets, restricting fresh credit disbursement.

## Section: 16.8 Policy Implications 🔴
The organized services sector requires continuous regulatory reforms to remove Non-Tariff Barriers, improve the ease of doing business, enhance skill development to tackle 'jobless growth', and upgrade physical infrastructure to maintain global competitiveness.

## Section: 16.9 Let Us Sum Up 🔴
The services sector has leapfrogged manufacturing to become the primary engine of India's economic growth. While it dominates GVA, exports, and FDI, its inability to generate mass employment for unskilled labor remains a critical macroeconomic challenge.

# Unit 17: Services Sector II: Informal Sector - Issues and Policy

## Section: 17.1 Introduction 🔴
While the organized services sector (IT, Banking) drives headline GDP growth, the **informal services sector** (street vendors, domestic workers, rickshaw pullers, barbers) employs the vast majority of the Indian workforce. This sector is characterized by a lack of regulation, absence of social security, and precarious working conditions.

## Section: 17.2 What is the Informal Service Sector? 🔴
- **Definition by NCEUS (National Commission for Enterprises in the Unorganised Sector)**: The informal (or unorganized) sector consists of "all unincorporated private enterprises owned by individuals or households engaged in the sale and production of goods and services operated on a proprietary or partnership basis and with less than ten total workers." ⭐ (exam-important)
- The distinction between formal and informal is primarily based on the size of employment and the legal status of the enterprise.

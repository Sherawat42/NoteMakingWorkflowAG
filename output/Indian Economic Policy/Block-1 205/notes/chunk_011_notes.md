## Section: Unit 2 — Growth and Structure of the Indian Economy — Introduction 🔴

### Core Idea

Unit 2 examines India's economic growth since independence, covering the **evolution of GDP growth across distinct phases**, the **structural transformation** of the economy (from agriculture to services), and contemporary **policy challenges and reforms**. It introduces the key question: how has India's growth pattern differed from the classical Western model of development?

> **In Simple Terms:** Now that we've seen how badly the British left the Indian economy, this unit asks: what happened next? How has India's economy grown since 1947? The surprising answer is that India didn't follow the usual path — instead of going from farming to factories to services (like Western countries), India jumped from farming straight to services, almost skipping manufacturing.

### Key Concepts

#### Unit 2 Learning Objectives
After studying this unit, students should be able to:
- Analyse the **different phases of GDP growth** since independence
- Understand India's unique **structural transformation** pattern
- Evaluate the **role of key sectors** (agriculture, manufacturing, services)
- Assess India's **growth prospects and policy challenges**
- Understand the impact of **major reforms** (liberalisation, GST, etc.)

#### Overview of Growth Phases
The text introduces the idea that India's post-independence growth can be divided into distinct phases, each characterised by different growth rates, policy regimes, and structural features. This framework will be developed in detail in subsequent chunks.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Unit 2 covers: post-independence growth phases + structural change + reforms
> - Key insight: India's structural transformation differs from Western pattern
> - India's growth accelerated significantly after 1991 liberalisation
> - Major themes: growth phases, sectoral shift, policy reforms, remaining challenges

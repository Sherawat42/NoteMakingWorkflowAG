## Section: Macroeconomic Policy Under British Rule 🔴

### Core Idea

British macroeconomic policy in India was designed to serve imperial interests, not Indian development. The **fiscal policy** was extractive (heavy taxation but low social spending), the **monetary policy** was tied to British interests, and the **trade policy** was structured to make India a captive market for British goods while extracting raw materials cheaply.

> **In Simple Terms:** The British ran India's economy like a cash machine — they collected heavy taxes from Indians, spent very little on Indian welfare, and made sure all trade rules benefited British businesses. It's as if your landlord charged you high rent but refused to fix the plumbing.

### Key Concepts

#### Fiscal Policy (1.4.1) ⭐ (exam-important)

The British fiscal policy had several distinctive features:
- **Revenue maximisation over welfare**: Land revenue was the primary income source; the focus was on extracting maximum revenue rather than providing public services
- **Regressive taxation**: Burden fell disproportionately on the poor (through land revenue, salt tax, excise duties)
- **Minimal social spending**: Very little spent on education, health, sanitation, or poverty alleviation
- **Military expenditure dominance**: A large share of revenue went to maintaining the British Indian Army — used for Britain's imperial wars globally
- **Surplus budgets**: The colonial government ran surpluses — but these were used for "home charges" (remitted to Britain), not for Indian development

| Revenue Source | Characteristic | Impact on India |
|---------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Land Revenue | Largest source; heavy rates | Peasant impoverishment |
| Salt Tax | Regressive; affected the poorest | Burden on common people; Gandhian protest symbol |
| Customs/Excise | Favoured British imports | Destroyed Indian manufacturing |
| Income Tax | Introduced late (1860); minimal collection | Negligible revenue from wealthy |

#### Trade Policy (1.4.2) ⭐ (exam-important)

British trade policy was explicitly mercantilist:
- **One-way free trade**: India was forced to keep markets open while Britain protected its own
- **Tariff manipulation**: High duties on Indian textiles entering Britain (70-80%); low/zero duties on British goods entering India
- **Export of raw materials**: India exported raw cotton, jute, indigo, opium — received manufactured goods in return
- **Terms of trade decline**: India's terms of trade consistently deteriorated — it took more Indian exports to buy the same quantity of British imports over time
- **Trade surplus exploited**: India consistently had an export surplus, but this surplus was used to finance the "drain" rather than reinvested domestically

#### Monetary Policy (1.4.3)

- India was on the **silver standard** initially; later shifted to the **gold exchange standard**
- Exchange rate was managed to benefit British trade
- **Currency manipulation**: The rupee's value was managed to facilitate British trade rather than Indian economic stability
- **No central bank until 1935**: Reserve Bank of India established in 1935, just 12 years before independence — too late to have significant impact

### Mechanisms / Processes

**How Colonial Economic Policy Worked as a System:**

Heavy land revenue → Peasant impoverishment → Forced cash crop cultivation → Raw material exports to Britain → British factories produce manufactured goods → Flood Indian markets (low tariffs) → Indian artisans destroyed → Surplus drained to Britain → No reinvestment in India → Cycle continues

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Fiscal: extractive, regressive, military-heavy, minimal social spending
> - Trade: one-way free trade, tariff manipulation, raw material extraction
> - Monetary: managed for British benefit; RBI only in 1935
> - Key terms: drain of wealth, home charges, one-way free trade
> - Trade surplus existed but was drained, not reinvested

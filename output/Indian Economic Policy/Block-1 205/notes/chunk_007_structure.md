## Section: Drain of Wealth (1.4) 🔴
<!-- This section starts here -->
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->
<!-- Reason: Named theory (Dadabhai Naoroji's Drain Theory); multiple CYP questions; key concept -->

### Core Idea
<!-- placeholder: Unilateral transfer of India's social surplus and investable capital to Britain through Council Bills and other mechanisms -->

### In Simple Terms
<!-- placeholder: India's wealth was constantly being shipped to Britain like water flowing down a drain — money that could have been invested at home was sent abroad for free -->

### Key Concepts
#### Dadabhai Naoroji's Drain Theory
<!-- placeholder: Developed in 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India' (1876); drain was "potential surplus" lost to capital formation -->
#### Council Bills Mechanism
<!-- placeholder: Sold in London in sterling; purchasers received Indian rupees in exchange -->
#### Components of the Drain
<!-- placeholder: Interest on foreign debt, military expenditure, guaranteed returns on railways/irrigation, home charges -->
#### Magnitude of the Drain
<!-- placeholder: 5-10% of India's total national income; Montgomery Martin's estimate of £723,000,000; William Digby's estimate of £60,080 million -->

### Definitions
- **Drain of Wealth**: <!-- placeholder: Unilateral transfer of India's social surplus to Britain without adequate return -->
- **Council Bills**: <!-- placeholder: Financial instruments sold in London in sterling, with equivalent rupees paid in India -->
- **Home Charges**: <!-- placeholder: Payments for Secretary of State's establishment, pay/pension of British personnel -->
- **Investment (Company usage)**: <!-- placeholder: Revenue from Bengal devoted to purchase/export of Indian goods -->

### Mechanisms / Processes
<!-- placeholder: Battle of Plassey (1757) → Company stops bullion exports → Bengal revenue finances trade → Council Bills facilitate transfer → 5-10% of national income drained -->

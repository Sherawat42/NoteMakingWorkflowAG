## Section: Rural and Urban Sectors (2.3.4) 🟡

### Core Idea

The rural economy has undergone a significant transformation — it is **no longer predominantly agrarian**. Rural GDP grew at 7.5% (faster than urban at 5.6%), and by 2010-11, the rural sector contributed **51% of GDP**, overtaking urban. About 60% of rural GDP now comes from the **non-farm sector**.

> **In Simple Terms:** People used to think "rural" means farming. But now, nearly 60% of money earned in rural India comes from non-farming activities — shops, services, small businesses. Rural India has diversified.

### Key Concepts

#### Rural Economic Transformation
- In 1980-81, rural sector = 41% of GDP → 51% by 2010-11
- Farm sector share in rural GDP fell from 73.8% (1970-71) to 41.6% (2010-11)
- Per capita income growth in rural India has been **almost double** that of urban India (though on a lower base)

#### Six Reasons for Rural Resilience
1. **Direct benefit transfers** (DBTs) — giving cash instead of only subsidised goods
2. **Higher MSPs** — paddy MSP up 40%, wheat up 80% (2004-05 to 2011-12)
3. **Global commodity boom** — new agricultural export markets
4. **Stable input costs** — farmers' surpluses maximised
5. **Better connectivity** — mobile phones and road networks
6. **Farm loan waiver scheme** — benefited thousands of farmers

## Section: Consumption, Investment, Exports in GDP (2.3.5) 🟡

### Key Concepts

#### GDP Composition Trends (World Bank, 2018)
- **Private consumption**: Fell from ~80% of GDP (early 1970s) to ~60% (2017), but still contributed 3.76 ppt to growth annually
- **Government expenditure**: Stabilised at ~12% of GDP
- **GFCF/GDP ratio**: Peaked at 34.7% (2008) → moderated to 28.6% (2017) → slight recovery to 29.4% (2018)
- **Trade ratio**: Rose from 7.6% (1971) to ~40% (2017); exports tripled from 7.3% (1991) to 22% (2007)
- **Net exports**: Contribution muted — imports grew faster than exports in most years

## Section: Public vs Private Sector (2.3.6) 🟡

### Key Concepts

#### Investment Divergence
- Public and private investment used to contribute equally
- Public investment peaked at 12.7% of GDP (1986-87) → now ~7% of GDP
- Private investment exceeded 20% of GDP in recent years
- Role of public investment in growth has diminished over time

## Section: Rise of Tertiary Sector — Composition (2.4.1) 🔴

### Core Idea

The service sector is divided into three groups. **Group 3** (modern services: financial, IT, business, communications) has shown the most dramatic growth since 1990, contributing more to GDP growth than manufacturing.

### Key Concepts

#### Three Groups of Services

| Group | Services | Trend |
|-------|----------|-------|
| **Group 1** | Trade, transport, public administration, defence | Stagnated (matches OECD trend) |
| **Group 2** | Hotels, education, health, social services | Moderate rise |
| **Group 3** | Financial, IT/computer, business, communications, legal/technical | **Rapid rise since 1990** — now contributes more to GDP growth than manufacturing |

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Rural sector = 51% of GDP (overtook urban); non-farm = 60% of rural GDP
> - Private consumption: 80% (1970s) → 60% (2017) of GDP
> - GFCF/GDP ratio peaked at 34.7% (2008), moderated since
> - Group 3 services (IT, finance) = fastest growing since 1990
> - Public investment: declining (7% GDP); Private investment: rising (20%+ GDP)

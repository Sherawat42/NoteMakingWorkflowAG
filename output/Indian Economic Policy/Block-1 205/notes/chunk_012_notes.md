## Section: GDP Growth Phases 🔴

### Core Idea

India's post-independence economic growth can be divided into **five distinct phases**, each characterised by different policy regimes, growth rates, and structural features. Growth ranged from 3.5% ("Hindu rate") in Phase I to 7.5% in Phase V, with liberalisation (1991) being the watershed moment.

> **In Simple Terms:** Think of India's economic growth like a car accelerating on a long road. For the first 30 years, it was stuck in first gear (3.5% growth). In the 1980s, it shifted to second gear (5.5%). After 1991, it moved to third and fourth gears (6-8%). But the car has hit some speed bumps recently (2019 onwards).

### Key Concepts

#### Phase I: 1950-1980 — "Hindu Rate of Growth" ⭐ (exam-important)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **GDP growth** | 3.5% annual average |
| **Per capita growth** | ~1% (population growing at 2%) |
| **Decade trend** | 3.8% (1950s) → 3.5% (1960s) → 3.1% (1970s) |
| **Policy regime** | Socialist planning; import substitution; licence raj |
| **Focus** | Heavy industry, capital goods, public sector dominance |
| **Constraints** | Stringent controls on capacity expansion, licensing, limited foreign tech |

**Prof. Raj Krishna** coined the term **"Hindu Rate of Growth"** to describe India's low-level equilibrium trap of ~3.5% growth. This term is frequently asked in exams.

#### Phase II: 1980-1990 — Growth Acceleration

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **GDP growth** | ~5.5% annual average |
| **Policy shift** | Pro-business reforms; import liberalisation; export incentives |
| **Key change** | Move away from strict Nehruvian socialist policies |
| **Criticism** | Growth was **fiscally unsustainable** — funded by government borrowing, leading to the 1991 balance of payments crisis |

#### Phase III: 1990-2008 — Liberalisation and Boom

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **GDP growth** | 6% (1990s trend) → 8.2% (2003-08) |
| **Policy regime** | Post-1991 liberalisation: tariff reduction, FDI liberalisation, industrial deregulation |
| **Three engines of growth** | 1) Consumption spending; 2) Investment in new capacity; 3) Exports of goods and services |
| **Weakness** | Labour-intensive manufacturing remained sluggish despite reforms |

The **three engines of growth** (consumption, investment, exports) were boosted by a sustained, across-the-board increase in **productivity**.

#### Phase IV: 2008-2014 — Global Crisis and Slowdown

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **GDP growth** | Slowed to 6.5% (2011-12); continued low for 2012-14 |
| **Cause** | 2008 Global Financial Crisis + domestic policy concerns |
| **Global effects** | FDI decline, portfolio outflows, export order fall |
| **Domestic issues** | Iron ore mining ban, coal block cancellation, environmental clearance delays, NPA crisis, **double balance-sheet problem** |
| **Private consumption dip** | Wealth effect, labour market uncertainty, credit cutbacks, precautionary saving |
| **Government response** | Significant **stimulus package** in three tranches — expanded spending + reduced indirect taxes |

#### Phase V: 2014-2019 onwards — Reforms and "Pause Phase"

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **GDP growth** | 7.5% average (2014-15 to 2018-19); 5% (2019-20) |
| **Key reforms** | 4R approach (Recognition, Resolution, Recapitalisation, Reforms); GST; IBC; demonetisation; FDI liberalisation |
| **"Pause phase" (2019+)** | Seven concerns: slowing growth, inflation risk, external imbalances, weak investment, consumption weakness, services slowdown, agriculture hit |
| **Covid-19** | Most disruptive event — tourism, hospitality, aviation worst hit |

### Definitions

- **Hindu Rate of Growth**: The persistently low GDP growth rate of ~3.5% per annum during 1950-80, coined by economist Prof. Raj Krishna. It implied a "low-level equilibrium trap." ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Double Balance-Sheet Problem**: Simultaneous stress on banks (NPAs) and corporates (over-leveraged), creating a vicious cycle of low lending and low investment.
- **GFCF (Gross Fixed Capital Formation)**: Investment in fixed assets — a key measure of asset creation and economic investment.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- ❌ Mistake: "India's economy was always growing slowly" → ✅ Correct: Growth accelerated dramatically across phases — from 3.5% to 8.2%
- ❌ Mistake: "The 1991 reforms immediately fixed everything" → ✅ Correct: The 1990s saw volatile growth (4% to 7.1% and back); sustained 8%+ came only during 2003-08

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Phase I (1950-80): **3.5%** — Hindu Rate of Growth — socialist planning
> - Phase II (1980-90): **5.5%** — pro-business reforms
> - Phase III (1990-2008): **6-8.2%** — liberalisation; 3 engines of growth
> - Phase IV (2008-14): Slowdown — global crisis + NPA + double balance-sheet
> - Phase V (2014+): **7.5%** then "pause phase" + Covid-19

## Section: Physical Infrastructure Definition 🟢
<!-- Completing the contrast from 5.1.7 -->

### Core Idea

**Physical infrastructure** directly supports the physical processes of production and distribution in the economy (agriculture, industry, trade). Crucially, physical infrastructure leads to **growth in the short run** (unlike social infrastructure which yields long-run returns). Examples: energy, irrigation, telecom, banking.

## Section: Privatisation and Commercialisation of Infrastructure (5.2 & 5.2.1) 🔴

### Core Idea

Historically, infrastructure was the "exclusive preserve" of the public sector. By the 1980s, the government ran out of money and managerial capacity, making it critical to open infrastructure to private capital and to start running these assets commercially (for profit).

> **In Simple Terms:** In the 1950s, only the government built roads and power plants. But the government is slow, and building big things requires immense money that the government didn't have. So, they had to let private companies build them. But private companies won't build a power plant unless they can charge people for electricity and make a profit. That is 'commercialisation'.

### Key Concepts

#### 5 Reasons for Privatisation and Commercialisation ⭐ (exam-important)
1. **Massive Investment Needs:** The state simply does not have the trillions of dollars needed to meet growing needs.
2. **Managerial Constraints:** The public sector was inefficient; fiscal stringency demanded accountability for public spending.
3. **Changes in Technology:** New tech allows for precise measuring and "marginal pricing" (e.g., electronic toll booths, smart meters), making it easier to charge the actual user.
4. **Globalisation:** High-quality infrastructure is the #1 prerequisite to attracting massive Foreign Direct Investment (FDI).
5. **Dynamic Capital Markets:** The private sector now has access to enormous pools of global capital/venture funds, whereas previously only governments could borrow such large sums.

## Section: Prerequisites for Private Investment (5.2.2) 🟡

### Key Concept

**Excludability:** For a private company to invest, the infrastructure service can no longer be treated as a pure "public good" where free-riders take advantage. Commercialisation fundamentally depends on the ability to isolate payers and exclude those who don't pay.

### Definitions

- **Marginal Pricing:** Charging users strictly based on their individual, measurable usage of a service (made possible by new tech).
- **Free-riding:** A situation where individuals consume a resource without paying for it (which privatization seeks to eliminate via excludability).

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Social Sector Expenditure: Risen to ~8.8% of GDP recently.
> - Physical Infra = Short-run growth. Social Infra = Long-run growth.
> - The core prerequisite for privatization is **excludability** (you must be able to charge a fee and keep non-payers out).

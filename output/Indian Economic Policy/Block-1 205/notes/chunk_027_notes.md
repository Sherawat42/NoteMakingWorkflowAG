## Section: Unit 3 Summary (Let Us Sum Up - 3.7) 🟡

### Core Idea

Demographics critically shape economic development. Demographic dividend manifests *only* when population changes intersect with proper health, education, trade, and governance policies. The window of opportunity will close around **2040**. Failure to productively employ this youth bulge will quickly turn the demographic dividend into a "demographic disaster."

## Section: Key Words (3.10) 🔴

### Crucial Definitions ⭐ (exam-important)

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Crude Birth Rate** | Number of children born alive each year per 1000 population. |
| **Death Rate** | Number of deaths each year per 1000 population. |
| **Demography** | A study of the different aspects of population. |
| **Demographic Dividend** | Accelerated economic growth experienced when the working-age population grows faster than the dependent population (provided policies are conducive). |
| **Demographic Transition** | The 3-stage shift from high birth/death rates (stagnant) → high birth/low death rates (explosion) → low birth/death rates (stable). |
| **Life Expectancy** | Number of years a newborn child would live given prevailing mortality risks. |
| **Population Growth Rate** | Difference between birth rate and death rate (adjusted for migration). |
| **Total Fertility Rate (TFR)** | Average number of children per woman in the reproductive age group. |
| **Youth Dependency Ratio** | Proportion of young people (under 15) to the working population (16-59). |

> **Quick Recall:**
> - The demographic dividend window for India closes near **2040**.
> - Remember the 3 main parts of demographic transition: 1. High/High = Stagnant. 2. High/Low = Boom. 3. Low/Low = Stable.
> - "Missing women" concept by Amartya Sen = gender ratio imbalances due to societal preference for sons.

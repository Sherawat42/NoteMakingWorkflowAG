## Section: Gender Ratio by State 🟡
<!-- See chunk 020 for the beginning of this section -->

### Key Concepts

#### State-Level Imbalances
- The gender ratio varies dramatically across states.
- **Top performers (2011):** Kerala (1084 females/1000 males), Puducherry.
- **Bottom performers (2011):** Haryana (877), Daman & Diu, Punjab (893), UP (908).
- **Sharp Deterioration:** Bihar's gender ratio fell drastically from 946 (1981) to 916 (2011).
- **Child Sex Ratio (CSR):** Focuses on age 0-6. Haryana was at the absolute bottom (834), while Meghalaya/Mizoram were at the top (970).

## Section: Literacy and Gender Composition (3.3.4) 🟡

### Core Idea

Literacy improves economic mobility, creates equality, and directly helps address the social imbalances behind the skewed sex ratio. Data shows that educated states tend to have better gender ratios.

### Key Concepts

#### Female Literacy (NFHS-5, 2019-20)
- **Top states for female literacy:** Kerala (98.3%), Lakshadweep (96.5%), Mizoram (94.4%).
- **Bottom states for female literacy:** Bihar (57.8%), Telangana (66.6%), Andhra Pradesh (68.6%).

## Section: Gender-Selective Abortions (3.3.4.1) 🔴

### Core Idea

The sharp decline in the Child Sex Ratio (0-6 years) is directly linked to the misuse of modern medical technologies (like ultrasound) for female foeticide. This stems from a deep-rooted social and economic preference for sons in India.

> **In Simple Terms:** Tragically, medical advancements that were meant to check if a baby is healthy have been misused to check the baby's gender. Because many families strongly prefer boys (seeing them as an old-age pension, while seeing girls as a financial burden), millions of female babies are aborted before they are even born.

### Key Concepts

#### The Root of Son Preference
1. **Economic factors:** Sons are viewed as an insurance against old age in the absence of a strong social security system.
2. **Social factors:** Property inheritance laws and religious rituals.
3. **Girls viewed as liabilities:** Daughters are often seen as a financial liability (e.g., dowry).

#### Misuse of Medical Technology ⭐ (exam-important)
- Technologies like **ultrasound scanning** and **amniocentesis** were introduced to detect genetic abnormalities in the fetus.
- Instead, they fueled **sex-selective abortions (female foeticide)**.
- Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP) Act 1971 allows abortion for health/medical reasons, but unregulated sex determination tests allowed a massive misuse of abortion to eliminate female fetuses.

## Section: Government Response (3.3.4.2) 🔴

### Key Concepts

#### PC & PNDT Act ⭐ (exam-important)
The government response was initially slow, culminating in the **Prenatal Diagnostic Techniques (Regulation and Prevention of Misuse) Act** (now PC & PNDT Act). Key features:
1. Prenatal diagnostics can ONLY be conducted to detect genetic abnormalities.
2. Only high-risk pregnant women (over 35, history of 2+ abortions, history of genetic disorders) can take these tests.
3. **Use of these technologies to indicate the sex of the fetus is completely banned.** Offences are punishable by imprisonment and fines.

#### Girl Child Welfare Schemes
The government has launched multiple schemes to change social attitudes and incentivize raising girls:
- *Beti Bachao Beti Padhao*
- *Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana*
- *Balika Samriddhi Yojana*

## Section: Age Composition (3.3.5) 🔴

### Core Idea

Age structure is central to demography because shifting to low mortality/fertility creates a "youth bulge." An increasing working-age population relative to a declining child/elderly population results in a dropping dependency ratio — setting the stage for a potential demographic dividend.

> **In Simple Terms:** When birth rates fall, there are fewer babies. But all the babies born *before* the birth rates fell are now growing up and entering the workforce. This creates a massive "bulge" of young workers with relatively few children and old people to take care of. This is India's current situation.

### Key Concepts

#### Youth Bulge: Dividend or Disaster?
- **Demographic Dividend:** If the economy provides jobs, this youth bulge accelerates economic growth.
- **Demographic Disaster:** If the economy hits rock bottom and there is high unemployment, this youth bulge leads to social unrest, crime, and becomes a burden.

#### Dependency Ratio
- A declining child population (0-14) and old age group (60+) means fewer people for the working population to support.
- This creates a **favourable demographic window** that India is currently experiencing.

### Definitions

- **Child Sex Ratio (CSR):** The number of females per 1000 males in the age group 0-6 years. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Amniocentesis:** A prenatal medical procedure used to detect genetic abnormalities, heavily misused in India for sex determination. 

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- ❌ Mistake: "Rich people don't do female foeticide." → ✅ Correct: Wealthy states like Punjab/Haryana have some of the worst sex ratios, showing that economic wealth alone does not erase patriarchal son-preference; in fact, wealth gives better access to ultrasound tech.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Worst gender ratio states: Haryana, Punjab
> - Best gender ratio states: Kerala, Puducherry
> - PC & PNDT Act: Completely bans use of ultrasound/amniocentesis for sex determination.
> - Youth Bulge: Can be a dividend (if jobs exist) or a disaster (if unemployment is high).

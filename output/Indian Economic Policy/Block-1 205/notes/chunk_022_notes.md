## Section: Life Expectancy (3.3.6) 🟡

### Core Idea

India has seen a dramatic improvement in life expectancy, largely driving the population explosion by drastically lowering the death rate. 

### Key Concepts

#### Life Expectancy Growth
- **1901-10:** Very low — only **23 years**.
- **1951 onwards:** Substantial rise due to medical/health improvements.
- **Present (Textbook timeframe):** Average **69 years** (Women: 70.4 years, Men: 67.8 years). Note that women's life expectancy is naturally higher than men's.

## Section: Rural-Urban Migration (3.3.7) 🟢

### Key Concepts

#### Urbanization Trends
- The process of economic development naturally leads to urbanization.
- **Urban Population:** Grew from ~11% in 1901 to **31% in 2011**.
- **Drivers:** "Push and pull" factors — higher incomes, job security, education, health, and entertainment pull people to urban areas.
- **Future:** With industrialization accelerating, rural-to-urban migration is expected to increase rapidly.

## Section: Population Growth and Development (3.4) 🔴

### Core Idea

Population growth has both positive and negative effects on economic development. The sheer magnitude of additions to the workforce brings a herculean challenge: millions of youth need to be productively absorbed. Without skilling, training, and capital, a growing population strains resources rather than boosting the economy.

> **In Simple Terms:** Simply having more people doesn't make a country richer. If you have 100 new workers but only 10 shovels (capital) and nobody knows how to dig (skills), you just have 90 unemployed people putting pressure on the system.

### Key Concepts

#### Population and Agriculture
- **Pressure on Land:** A rising labour force reduces the per-capita cultivated area.
- **Solution:** This can only be compensated by dramatically raising productivity (e.g., through better irrigation).
- **Food Security:** Green Revolution made India self-sufficient in food grains, but **distribution and nutrition** remain critical problems. India still has the highest incidence of undernourishment globally.

#### Population Growth and Per-Capita Income
- High population growth is a retarding factor for per-capita income (since the national income is divided among more people).
- Economic growth can only be sustained if the growing population is accompanied by:
  1. Human capital formation (education/health).
  2. Deployment in productive activities.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Life Expectancy: Rose from 23 years (1900s) to ~69 years today.
> - Urbanization: ~31% of India is urban (2011).
> - Population & Agriculture: Per-capita land falls; productivity must rise to compensate.
> - Population & Income: High population growth retards per-capita income unless paired with human capital formation.

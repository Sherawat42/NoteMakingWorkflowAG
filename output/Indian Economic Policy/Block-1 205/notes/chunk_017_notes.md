## Section: Federalism (2.5.1.4) 🟡

### Key Concepts

#### Fiscal Federalism Reforms
- **14th Finance Commission**: Increased states' share in divisible pool from **32% → 42%** of central taxes
- Central grants saw compensatory cuts, but states empowered for independent revenue management
- **NITI Aayog**: Institutionalised cooperative federalism; joint Centre-state strategies; competitive KPIs among states

## Section: Corporate Exits — IBC (2.5.1.5) 🔴

### Core Idea

The **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC, 2016)** consolidated India's fragmented insolvency framework into a single time-bound law. It has significantly improved exit culture and creditor recovery.

> **In Simple Terms:** Before IBC, it took years to shut down a failing business and recover money. IBC created a clear, time-bound process — like a negotiated settlement with a deadline — so that bad debts don't drag on forever.

### Key Concepts

#### IBC Features ⭐ (exam-important)
- Consolidated multiple insolvency rules into **one law**
- Set **time limits** for closing insolvency/bankruptcy cases
- Assets of defaulting borrowers auctioned to pay creditors
- Significant NPAs brought under ambit since 2017
- Improved overall **business exit culture**

## Section: Demonetisation (2.5.1.5) 🔴

### Core Idea

On **8 November 2016**, the Government demonetised ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes — removing 86% of currency in circulation. Aimed at curbing terrorism funding, corruption, counterfeit notes, and pushing towards a **cashless economy**.

> **In Simple Terms:** Overnight, the government cancelled the two biggest banknotes that almost everyone used. The idea was to flush out hidden "black money" and push people towards digital payments.

### Key Concepts

#### Objectives
1. Reduce terror financing
2. Eliminate counterfeit currency
3. Decrease corruption
4. Push towards cashless/digital economy

## Section: GST (2.5.1.6) 🔴

### Core Idea

The **Goods and Services Tax (GST)**, implemented **1 July 2017**, is considered the biggest indirect tax reform since independence. It replaced multiple Central and State taxes with a unified national tax, creating a **single integrated market**.

> **In Simple Terms:** Before GST, a product crossing state borders was taxed at every stop — state tax, central tax, excise, VAT. GST replaced all of these with one single tax, making India one unified market and removing the "tax-on-tax" problem.

### Key Concepts

#### GST Features ⭐ (exam-important)
| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Replaced** | Central Excise, VAT/Sales Tax, Service Tax, and other indirect taxes |
| **Key benefit** | Eliminated **cascading of taxes** (tax-on-tax) |
| **Tax point** | Only the final consumer is taxed |
| **Market impact** | Created a **single, unified national market** |
| **Institutional** | GST Council = model for cooperative federalism |

## Section: Challenges That Remain (2.5.2) 🔴

### Core Idea

Despite impressive growth and reforms, six structural challenges persist: non-farm employment, demographic dividend utilisation, urbanisation, poverty/inequality, climate change, and infrastructure deficit.

### Key Concepts

#### Six Remaining Challenges

| Challenge | Key Issue |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Non-farm employment (2.5.2.1)** | 70%+ rural poor depend on agriculture; rural non-farm sector needs to be made lucrative |
| **Demographic dividend (2.5.2.2)** | 64% working-age population; 20-25 year window; IT employs only 0.4% of workforce; need labour-intensive manufacturing |
| **Urbanisation (2.5.2.3)** | 404 million new urban dwellers projected (2014-2050); infrastructure gaps |
| **Poverty & inequality (2.5.2.4)** | Core development agenda; high inequality worsens poverty |
| **Climate change (2.5.2.5)** | India = major emitter + highly vulnerable; water stress, heat waves, floods |
| **Infrastructure (2.5.2.6)** | Deficit costs 4-5% of GDP in inefficiencies; SDG 9 target |

### Definitions

- **GST (Goods and Services Tax)**: A unified indirect tax replacing multiple Central and State taxes, implemented 1 July 2017. Eliminated cascading of taxes. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **IBC (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code)**: A 2016 law consolidating insolvency resolution into a single time-bound framework. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Demonetisation**: Withdrawal of ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes from circulation on 8 November 2016.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - 14th FC: States' share 32% → **42%**; NITI Aayog = cooperative federalism
> - **IBC (2016)**: Single time-bound insolvency law; improved exit culture
> - **Demonetisation (8 Nov 2016)**: ₹500/₹1000 cancelled; cashless push
> - **GST (1 July 2017)**: Biggest indirect tax reform; unified market; no cascading
> - Six challenges: employment, demographics, urbanisation, poverty, climate, infrastructure

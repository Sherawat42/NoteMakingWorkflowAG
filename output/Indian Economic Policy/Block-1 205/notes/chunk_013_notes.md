## Section: Covid-19 Impact (continued) 🟡

### Core Idea

The Covid-19 pandemic was the most disruptive event for the Indian economy. All three components of aggregate demand — consumption, investment, exports — stayed subdued. Tourism, hospitality, and aviation were worst hit. Supply-side disruption came through shutdown of factories and delayed supply of goods from China.

### Key Concepts

#### Demand-Side Impact
- **Job losses and income decline** (especially daily wage earners) hit consumption
- **Financial market volatility** due to uncertainty
- Three components of aggregate demand — consumption, investment, exports — likely subdued for a prolonged period

#### Supply-Side Impact
- Factory shutdowns in China affected Indian manufacturing (automobiles, pharmaceuticals, electronics, chemicals)
- Raw material and component shortages

## Section: Structural Change in the Economy (2.3) 🔴

### Core Idea

India's structural transformation follows **two dimensions**: changing sector-wise shares in GDP and changing sectoral employment shares. India's pattern is **unique** — it skipped the classical primary→secondary transition and jumped from primary directly to tertiary, unlike Western and East Asian economies. This deviation has profound implications for employment and poverty.

> **In Simple Terms:** Most countries develop by moving workers from farms to factories to offices. India uniquely moved from farms straight to offices, largely skipping factories. This is a problem because factories create jobs for unskilled workers, while offices need skilled workers — leaving millions of unskilled people stranded.

### Key Concepts

#### Kuznet's Law ⭐ (exam-important)

As a country develops, the contribution of **primary sector** (agriculture) to GDP declines, while **secondary** (industry) and then **tertiary** (services) sectors increase. The mechanism operates through:
- **Demand side**: Income elasticity of demand for agricultural products is low → as incomes rise, demand shifts to manufactured goods, then services
- **Supply side**: Agriculture faces fixed factor (land) → diminishing returns; manufacturing/services can use capital and technology without limits

#### Sectoral GDP Shares (Table 2.1) ⭐ (exam-important)

| Sector | 1960-61 | 1990-91 | 2000-01 | 2010-11 | 2018-19 |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Primary** | 51% | 33% | 25% | 17% | ~16% |
| **Secondary** | 18% | 24% | 24% | 24.5% | ~30% |
| **Tertiary** | 31% | 43% | 51% | 58.5% | ~54% |

Services = ₹92.26 lakh crore (54.40% of GVA) in 2018-19.

#### India's Deviation from Western Pattern
- **Western/East Asian path**: Primary → Secondary → Tertiary (sequential)
- **India's path**: Primary → Tertiary (skipping secondary)
- **Consequence**: Secondary sector has NOT expanded fast enough to absorb growing labour force
- Unskilled rural workers remain in agriculture or join urban slums
- Service sector expansion occurred at a **much lower per capita income** than in developed countries

#### Sectoral Employment Shares (Table 2.2)

| Sector | 1983 | 2004-05 | 2009-10 | 2017-18 |
|--------|------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Primary** | 68.9% | 57.0% | 53.8% | **44.6%** |
| **Secondary** | 13.3% | 18.2% | 20.9% | **24.4%** |
| **Tertiary** | 17.8% | 24.8% | 25.4% | **31.0%** |

Key observation: Agriculture still employs **44.6%** but contributes only **~16% to GDP** — a massive **productivity gap**.

#### Organised vs Unorganised Sector
- Organised sector growing faster, facilitated by excise duty/tariff reductions
- But unorganised sector still dominates: **two-thirds of NDP**
- Not just agriculture — unorganised sector pervades manufacturing and services too

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- ❌ Mistake: "India industrialised like Western countries" → ✅ Correct: India skipped the manufacturing stage, going primary→tertiary
- ❌ Mistake: "Services growth = good employment growth" → ✅ Correct: Modern services need skilled workers, leaving the unskilled majority underserved

> **Quick Recall:**
> - **Kuznet's Law**: Primary → Secondary → Tertiary (as incomes rise)
> - India deviated: Primary → **Tertiary** (skipped secondary)
> - Agriculture: 44.6% employment but only ~16% GDP = productivity gap
> - Unorganised sector = 2/3 of NDP
> - Services sector is largest: 54.40% of GVA (2018-19)

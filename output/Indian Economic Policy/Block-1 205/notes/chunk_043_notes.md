## Section: Power (5.3.3) 🔴

### Core Idea

Power is the most critical infrastructure component. India has transitioned from a severely power-deficient country to a "power surplus" one (generation-wise), heavily diversifying into renewables. However, the financial health of distribution companies and uninterrupted supply remain massive challenges.

### Key Concepts

#### Achievements and Reforms
- **Liberalisation (2003):** Delicensed power generation, allowing unrestricted private sector participation.
- **Electrification:** 100% village electrification achieved in 2018 under *Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana*.
- **Renewable Shift:** India has the 4th largest renewable capacity globally (136 GW).
- **Energy Efficiencies:** *Ujala scheme* (LEDs) and *PM Ujjwala Yojana* (LPG to replace biomass).

#### Remaining Challenges ⭐ (exam-important)
1. **Financial Health of DISCOMS:** State power distribution companies are drowning in debt due to structural inefficiencies. The government launched **UDAY (Ujwal Discom Assurance Yojana)** to encourage financial turnaround, but debt to power plants remains high.
2. **Quality of Supply:** Being "power surplus" on paper doesn't translate to 24/7 continuous supply for consumers. 

## Section: Banking (5.3.4) 🔴

### Core Idea

The banking sector is the financial backbone, vastly transformed by two phases of IT-driven reforms. Despite massive digital inclusion, it is currently plagued by high levels of Non-Performing Assets (NPAs).

### Key Concepts

#### Banking Reforms (Narasimham Committee) ⭐ (exam-important)
1. **First Phase (1991):** Lowered SLR/CRR (freeing up cash), introduced capital adequacy/prudential norms, deregulated interest rates, and allowed private/foreign bank entry.
2. **Second Phase (1998):** Focused on technology (e-banking, electronic funds transfer), managing NPAs, Anti-Money Laundering, and opening new areas like insurance and credit cards.

#### Financial Inclusion
- **Goal:** Bringing the poor into the formal saving/credit system, freeing them from usurious local moneylenders.
- **Key Initiative:** **Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY)** (Zero-balance bank accounts enabling direct subsidy transfers).

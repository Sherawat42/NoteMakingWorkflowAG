## Section: Vision for Economic Reconstruction 🔴

### Core Idea

Long before independence, Indian nationalists and intellectuals developed **comprehensive economic visions** for a free India. The national movement was not just political — it had a strong economic dimension. Key contributions came from the **National Planning Committee (1938)** chaired by Jawaharlal Nehru, and the **Bombay Plan (1944)** developed by leading industrialists.

> **In Simple Terms:** While fighting for political freedom, India's leaders were also drawing up blueprints for the economy. They debated what kind of economic system free India should have — and the consensus was clear: India needed **planning**, not laissez-faire capitalism, to overcome 200 years of colonial damage.

### Key Concepts

#### National Planning Committee (NPC), 1938 ⭐ (exam-important)

- Formed in 1938 under the presidency of the Indian National Congress
- Chaired by **Jawaharlal Nehru**
- Envisioned **state-led economic planning** for India's development
- Recommended: rapid industrialisation, land reform, labour protection, social welfare
- The NPC's work was interrupted by WWII and the Quit India movement but its ideas heavily influenced post-independence policy

#### The Bombay Plan (1944) ⭐ (exam-important)

The **"A Plan of Economic Development for India"** (popularly called the **Bombay Plan**) was prepared by eight leading Indian industrialists including **JRD Tata, G.D. Birla, Ardeshir Dalal, Lala Shri Ram**, and others. Key features:

- Proposed **doubling India's per capita income** in 15 years
- Required total investment of approximately **Rs. 10,000 crore** (a massive sum in 1944)
- Advocated a **mixed economy** with both state and private sector roles
- Recognised that **state intervention** was essential for basic/heavy industries
- Accepted the need for **economic planning** and government direction of the economy
- Remarkably, even India's top capitalists acknowledged that pure free market could NOT solve India's problems

#### Gandhian Economic Thought

**Mahatma Gandhi** offered an alternative economic vision:
- Emphasis on **village self-sufficiency** and cottage industries
- **Swadeshi** (self-reliance) as an economic principle
- Opposition to large-scale industrialisation as potentially dehumanising
- Focus on **decentralised production** and equitable distribution
- Promotion of **khadi** (hand-spun cloth) as both economic and political symbol

#### Three Economic Visions Compared

| Feature | Nehruvian Planning | Bombay Plan | Gandhian Vision |
|---------|--------------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Role of State** | Dominant — "commanding heights" | Significant but alongside private sector | Minimal — village-level governance |
| **Industrialisation** | Large-scale, heavy industry | Mixed — heavy + consumer goods | Cottage and small-scale only |
| **Planning** | Central planning | Planned economy | Decentralised, village-based |
| **Focus** | Modernisation, self-reliance | Growth, employment, income doubling | Village self-sufficiency, equity |
| **Outcome** | Dominated post-1947 policy | Influenced mixed economy approach | Influenced Khadi/village industry programmes |

## Section: Unit 1 Summary 🟡

### Core Idea

India at independence inherited an economy systematically weakened by nearly 200 years of colonial exploitation. Key legacies: de-industrialised manufacturing, impoverished agriculture, massive poverty, near-zero human development indicators, and an economy structured to serve British interests. The national movement's economic thinking laid the groundwork for India's post-independence planning approach.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - **NPC (1938)**: Nehru-chaired; state-led planning vision
> - **Bombay Plan (1944)**: Top industrialists accepted need for state intervention — Rs. 10,000 crore investment
> - **Gandhi**: Village self-sufficiency, cottage industry, swadeshi
> - Post-1947 India chose: mixed economy with planning (influenced by all three visions)
> - Colonial legacy: de-industrialisation, drain of wealth, mass poverty, infrastructure for extraction

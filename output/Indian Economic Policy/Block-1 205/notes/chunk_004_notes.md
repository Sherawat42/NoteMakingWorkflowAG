## Section: Unit 1 Objectives 🟡

### Core Idea

Unit 1 equips students to understand the state of the Indian economy under British rule — the key economic features, the instruments of colonial exploitation, and the economic ideas that emerged for post-independence reconstruction.

> **In Simple Terms:** Before we can understand why India made certain economic policy choices after 1947, we need to understand just how badly the British period damaged the economy.

### Key Concepts

#### Learning Objectives
After studying this unit, students should be able to:
- Explain the **key economic features** during the British period
- Describe the **instruments of economic exploitation** used by the colonial government
- Discuss the **contribution of the national movement** to economic ideas
- Explain the economic thought on **"planning and development" prior to independence**

## Section: Introduction — Colonial Impact on Indian Economy 🔴

### Core Idea

India was one of the richest economies in the world before British colonisation. By the time of independence in 1947, it had been reduced to one of the poorest, primarily through de-industrialisation, drain of wealth, and exploitative trade policies. The British period fundamentally restructured India from a **manufacturing economy** to a **raw material supplier** for British industries.

> **In Simple Terms:** Imagine India was a prosperous factory owner who got taken over by a foreign company. The new owners shut down India's factories, forced India to supply cheap raw materials to their own factories abroad, and then sold finished goods back at high prices. By the time the takeover ended, India was left with broken factories and deep poverty.

### Key Concepts

#### India's Pre-Colonial Economic Standing
Before British rule, India was a significant player in world commerce. India had a thriving **handicraft industry** — particularly in textiles (muslin, calico, silk) — and was a major exporter. The Indian subcontinent accounted for a substantial share of global manufacturing output and trade.

#### Nature of Colonial Economic Policy
The colonial economic policy had a **dual purpose**:
1. **Transform India into a supplier of raw materials** for British industries
2. **Create a captive market** for British manufactured goods in India

This policy systematically destroyed India's indigenous industries and agricultural self-sufficiency. The British established a **colonial-mercantilist framework** where India's resources were extracted for the benefit of the metropole (Britain).

#### Key Colonial Arguments — "Free Trade" Myth
The British justified their trade policies under the banner of **free trade** and **comparative advantage**. However, this "free trade" was deeply one-sided:
- India was forced to accept **zero or very low tariffs** on British manufactured goods
- British goods flooded Indian markets, destroying local competition
- Indian raw materials were exported at **artificially low prices**
- There was no reciprocal benefit to Indian industries

> **Quick Recall:**
> - India was rich before colonisation → poor by independence
> - Colonial purpose: raw material supplier + captive market for British goods
> - "Free trade" was one-sided — benefited Britain, destroyed Indian industry
> - De-industrialisation was systematic, not accidental

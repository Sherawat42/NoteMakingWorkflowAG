## Section: 9.5.5 Inflation Targeting Framework 🔴

### Core Idea
Following the Urjit Patel Committee recommendations (2014), India adopted a Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT) framework. This shifted the monetary policy objective to maintaining a specific numerical inflation target, bringing transparency, accountability, and operational independence to the RBI. A Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) was instituted to determine the policy rate necessary to achieve this target.

> **In Simple Terms:** Instead of juggling multiple goals at once, the RBI's primary job now is to keep inflation at a specific target (4%). To decide how to do this, a special committee (the MPC) meets and votes on interest rates.

### Key Concepts

#### Flexible Inflation Targeting Framework
- Adopted following the Urjit Patel Committee recommendations.
- A Monetary Policy Framework Agreement in 2015 set the target: **4% with a band of +/- 2%** (i.e., inflation should stay between 2% and 6%).
- If inflation breaches this band for three consecutive quarters, the RBI is deemed to have failed and must submit a report to the Central Government explaining the failure and proposed remedial actions.
- RBI publishes a Monetary Policy Report every six months detailing sources and forecasts of inflation.

#### Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)
- Instituted via an amendment to the RBI Act in 2016.
- Consists of **six members**:
  1. Governor of RBI (Chairperson)
  2. Deputy Governor in charge of Monetary Policy
  3. One officer of the RBI nominated by the Central Board
  4. Three persons appointed by the Central Government
- Meets at least 4 times a year to determine the policy rate. Decisions are by majority vote, with the Governor having a casting vote in the event of a tie.

### Definitions
- **Nominal Anchor**: A variable that policymakers use to "tie down" the price level or its path (e.g., CPI-Combined headline inflation). ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)**: A six-member committee instituted to determine the policy rate required to achieve the inflation target. ⭐ (exam-important)

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Inflation Target: 4% (+/- 2%).
> - MPC Composition: 3 RBI members, 3 Government appointees.
> - MPC decisions are published along with minutes after 14 days.

## Section: Introduction to Capital Market and its Regulations 🟢

### Core Idea
A dynamic financial system consists of markets, intermediation, and instruments that allocate resources from surplus segments to deficit segments. It transforms idle wealth into productive financial instruments (bonds, shares), lowering the cost of finance and boosting economic growth.

> **In Simple Terms:** The financial system is the engine that takes money from people who are saving it and gives it to businesses that need to invest it, keeping the economy growing.

### Key Concepts
- Components: Money Market, Capital Market.
- The system replaces idle assets (gold, real estate) with financial instruments, advancing integration between the real and financial sectors.

## Section: 10.2 Role, Significance and Function of Capital Market 🔴

### Core Idea
The capital market is a segment of the financial market for long-term or indefinite maturity assets, divided into primary (new issues) and secondary (trading existing shares) markets. It facilitates the transfer of capital from savers to investors, providing liquidity and efficient resource allocation.

> **In Simple Terms:** The capital market is where companies go to raise long-term money (by selling shares or bonds) and where investors go to buy and trade those assets. 

### Key Concepts

#### Capital Market Structure
1. **Primary Market**: New stock or bond issues are sold (underwriting).
2. **Secondary Market**: Existing shares are traded on organized exchanges (stock exchanges, over-the-counter).

#### Functions of the Capital Market
- Efficient dissemination of information for quick valuation.
- Providing insurance against market and price risk.
- Lowering transactional costs and settlement timings.
- Channelizing funds optimally to accelerate capital formation and industrial growth.

#### Financial Reforms (1991 onwards)
Initiated after the 1991 balance-of-payments crisis to promote a diversified, competitive financial system. Pre-reform, state-owned banks controlled 90% of assets and interest rates were administratively determined. Reforms aimed to mitigate risks, efficiently allocate resources, make the system globally competitive, and open the external sector.

### Definitions
- **Capital Market**: A market for financial assets which have a long or indefinite maturity. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Liquidity**: How easily an asset can be converted to currency without loss of value. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Primary Market**: The market where new stock or bond issues are sold.
- **Secondary Market**: The market where already issued shares are traded through organized exchanges.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Capital market = Primary (new) + Secondary (existing).
> - Regulator: SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India).

## Section: 10.3 Stock Market Development in India 🔴

### Core Idea
Stock markets provide companies with capital for expansion and allow investors to earn returns while diversifying risks. A developed stock market is a barometer of economic growth because it mobilizes domestic savings, allocates capital efficiently, and enforces corporate managerial discipline.

> **In Simple Terms:** A stock market is crucial because it helps businesses grow and create jobs by raising money from the public, while giving the public a chance to grow their savings.

### Key Concepts
- **Market Capitalisation Ratio (MCR)**: Defined as the value of listed shares divided by GDP. It is used as a measure of stock market size and reflects the ability to mobilize capital and diversify risk.

### Definitions
- **Stock Market**: A public market where investors can buy and sell shares representing fractional ownership of a business.
- **Market Capitalisation Ratio (MCR)**: The value of listed shares divided by GDP, used as an indicator of stock market size. ⭐ (exam-important)

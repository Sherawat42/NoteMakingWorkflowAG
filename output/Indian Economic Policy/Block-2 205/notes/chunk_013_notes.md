### Core Idea
The New Economic Policy (NEP) of 1991 was implemented through a dual strategy: short-term Macroeconomic Stabilisation to fix the immediate crisis, and long-term Microeconomic Structural Reforms to boost overall efficiency.

### Key Concepts
#### The Two-Pronged Strategy
1. **Stabilisation Measures (Short-term)**: Controlling inflation and fixing the balance of payments deficit immediately.
2. **Structural Reform Measures (Long-term)**: Improving the efficiency of the economy and increasing international competitiveness by removing constraints.

### Quick Recall
- Stabilization (short-term) + Structural Reforms (long-term) = NEP 1991.

## Section: Macroeconomic Stabilisation Framework 🔴

### Core Idea
Macroeconomic stabilisation focused primarily on demand management—bringing aggregate demand down to match the available aggregate supply, thereby curbing soaring inflation and managing the fiscal deficit.

### In Simple Terms
If everyone is bidding up the price of a small number of apples (inflation), the government's short-term fix is to take away people's credit cards so they stop bidding so aggressively.

### Key Concepts
#### Fiscal Contraction
The government drastically cut its own expenditure, subsidies, and reduced the fiscal deficit to stop printing money (which causes inflation).

#### Monetary Tightening
Interest rates were raised to reduce borrowing, lower money supply, and curb demand.

### Definitions
- **Macroeconomic Stabilisation**: Short-term demand management policies intended to correct balance of payments deficits and lower inflation.
- **Fiscal Deficit**: The shortfall in a government's income compared with its spending.

### Quick Recall
- Stabilization tools: High interest rates, spending cuts, reduced subsidies.
- Goal: Curb inflation and stop the bleeding of foreign exchange.

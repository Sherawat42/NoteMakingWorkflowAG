Private ownership fundamentally changes the "principal-agent" relationship. Private owners (principals) ensure managers (agents) maximize profits, leading to greater overall economic efficiency and better resource allocation.

## Section: Disinvestment in India 🟡
<!-- This section starts here -->
<!-- Exam importance: 🟡 MODERATE -->
<!-- Reason: Shows the timeline and framework of disinvestment in India -->

### Core Idea
Disinvestment in India evolved from ad-hoc share sales to cover fiscal deficits in the 1990s, to a more structured approach focused on strategic sales (like Air India, BALCO).

### Key Concepts
#### Rangarajan Committee (1993)
Recommended that public sector equity should be divested up to 49% in targeted industries, and 74% or more in non-strategic industries.

#### Mechanisms / Processes
The government uses the Department of Investment and Public Asset Management (DIPAM) to execute its disinvestment goals, using methods like Initial Public Offerings (IPOs) and Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) like CPSE ETF.

### Definitions
- **DIPAM**: The nodal department under the Ministry of Finance dealing with disinvestment of Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs).

### Quick Recall
- Disinvestment is managed by DIPAM (Finance Ministry).
- Rangarajan Committee (1993) laid the early framework.

## Section: Problems Related to Disinvestment Process/Modes 🟡
<!-- This section starts here -->
<!-- Exam importance: 🟡 MODERATE -->
<!-- Reason: Analysis of structural bottlenecks during disinvestment -->

### Core Idea
The disinvestment process has often been marred by allegations of undervaluing assets, labor union protests against job losses, and criticism that it is just a tool to plug the fiscal deficit rather than improve productivity.

### Key Concepts
#### Divided Indian Opinion
Many argue that selling profit-making PSUs ("Navratnas") is akin to selling family silver to pay the grocer's bill. Others argue the state has no business being in business.

### Quick Recall
- Main criticisms: Undervaluation of assets, job losses, using proceeds just to fill budget holes.

## Section: Conditions for Success of Privatisation Policy 🔴
<!-- This section starts here and continues into chunk 020 -->
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->
<!-- Reason: The 'seven sins' and recommendations are highly examinable critical points -->

### Core Idea
For privatisation to truly benefit the economy, it must not just be a transfer of a public monopoly to a private monopoly. It requires competition, transparency, and a strong regulatory framework.

### Key Concepts
#### The Seven Sins of Privatisation
The text warns against negative practices during privatization, such as:
1. Privatising to fill budget gaps rather than for efficiency.
2. Creating private monopolies without strong regulators.
3. Lack of transparency in the bidding process (leads to crony capitalism).

### Quick Recall
- Privatisation fails if a state monopoly becomes an unregulated private monopoly.
- Transparency and an independent regulator are mandatory for success.

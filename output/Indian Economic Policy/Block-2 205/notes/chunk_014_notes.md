## Section: Microeconomic Structural Reforms 🔴
<!-- This section starts here -->
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->
<!-- Reason: Detailed sector-wise breakdown of LPG policies -->

### Core Idea
While stabilisation fixed the immediate symptoms, structural reforms fundamentally changed the supply side of the economy by deregulating industries, liberalising trade, and reforming the financial sector (LPG).

### In Simple Terms
Stabilization is putting out the house fire. Structural reform is replacing the faulty electrical wiring so it never happens again.

### Key Concepts
#### Industrial Deregulation (Liberalisation)
Abolition of industrial licensing for all but a handful of industries (e.g., hazardous chemicals). The MRTP Act (Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices Act) was scrapped.

#### Public Sector Reforms (Privatisation)
Areas reserved exclusively for the public sector were reduced. Disinvestment (selling government shares to private entities) was initiated.

#### Trade and Capital Liberalisation
Import tariffs were drastically slashed. The system of import licensing was almost entirely dismantled. Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) was welcomed with automatic approvals up to 51% in high-priority industries.

### Definitions
- **Structural Reforms**: Long-term, supply-side policies aimed at increasing the efficiency and productivity of the economy.
- **Disinvestment**: The dilution of government stake in Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs).

### Quick Recall
- Abolition of License Raj.
- Opening up to FDI.
- Reduction of import tariffs.

## Section: Financial and Currency Reforms 🟡
<!-- This section starts here -->
<!-- Exam importance: 🟡 MODERATE -->
<!-- Reason: Specifies changes to banking and currency valuation -->

### Core Idea
The banking sector was given more autonomy (Narasimham Committee recommendations), interest rates were deregulated, and the Indian Rupee was made partially convertible.

### Key Concepts
#### Rupee Convertibility
The Rupee transitioned from a fixed exchange rate determined by the RBI to a market-determined floating exchange rate system on the current account.

### Definitions
- **Current Account Convertibility**: The freedom to convert domestic currency to foreign currency for visible and invisible trade transactions.

### Quick Recall
- Rupee exchange rate became market-determined.
- Interest rates were freed from central control.

### Core Idea
While the IBC has drastically improved India's recovery rate and ranking in "Ease of Doing Business", data shows that far more companies end up in liquidation (shut down and sold for parts) than are successfully resolved (saved and restructured).

### Key Concepts
#### Record of IBC Working
- Pre-IBC: Resolution took ~4.3 years with a mere 26% recovery rate.
- Post-IBC: Significant improvement in recovery rates and speed. Fear of losing the company (due to IPs taking over) has forced many promoters to settle debts *before* the insolvency applications are even admitted to NCLT.

#### Liquidation vs Resolution Data
Despite successes with large accounts, IBBI data indicates nearly 4 times as many cases end in liquidation rather than successful resolution, showing that saving a failing business in a time-bound manner remains highly challenging.

### Definitions
- **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)**: The specific legal process under IBC utilized to determine if a defaulting corporate debtor can be revived or if it must be liquidated.

### Quick Recall
- Threat of IBC causes out-of-court settlements.
- Many more liquidations happen than actual resolutions under CIRP.

### Core Idea
Unit 8 summarizes the structural shifts in the Indian economy post-1991, detailing the transition from state monopolies to privatization, the adoption of Public-Private Partnerships for infrastructure, and the maturation of the legal framework via the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code.

### Key Concepts
#### Review of Privatisation, PPP, and IBC
- Public Sector was restricted, and privatisation (often via disinvestment) became normal policy.
- Infrastructure was heavily boosted via PPPs (BOT, EPC, HAM models).
- The legal ecosystem matured by moving from a "Debtor-in-possession" to a "Creditor-in-control" regime via the IBC, ensuring swift exit mechanisms for capital.

### Quick Recall
- Privatisation, PPPs, and IBC represent the continuous evolution of India's economic structures to improve efficiency and attract global investment.

## Section: Generations of Economic Reforms 🔴
<!-- This section continues from chunk 015 -->
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->
<!-- Reason: Discusses 3rd/4th generation reforms (current context) -->

### Core Idea
Recent reform generations focus on governance, decentralization, infrastructure, and technology-led transparency (Digital India).

### Key Concepts
#### Third/New Generation Reforms
Focuses on "inclusive growth" and good governance. 
- Included: the Goods and Services Tax (GST) (a massive indirect tax reform), Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), and deep digitization (JAM trinity: Jan Dhan, Aadhaar, Mobile).

### Connections
- While 1st generation focused on *growth*, the 3rd/newer generations focus deeply on *inclusion* and *governance transparency*.

### Quick Recall
- GST and IBC are prime examples of new generation institutional reforms.
- Focus shifted from mere liberalization to inclusive growth.

## Section: Unit 7 Summary 🟢
<!-- This section starts here -->
<!-- Exam importance: 🟢 CONTEXT -->
<!-- Reason: Review of the reform trajectory -->

### Core Idea
Unit 7 tracks India's economic journey from a rigid, state-controlled, import-substituting economy that culminated in the 1991 crisis, through the implementation of the LPG framework which transformed India into a rapidly growing market economy.

### Key Concepts
#### Pre-1991 vs Post-1991
- Pre-1991: Hindu Rate of Growth (~3.5%), LPQ Raj, High Fiscal Deficit, Inward-looking.
- Post-1991: Higher growth rates, LPG policies, Market-determined exchange rates, Global integration.

### Quick Recall
- 1991 is the dividing line in modern Indian economic history.
- The shift was necessitated by a Balance of Payments crisis but resulted in a permanent structural change.

## Section: Policy Measures to Attract FDI 🟡

### Core Idea
India has undertaken massive structural reforms (corporate tax cuts, labor codes, PLI schemes) to attract FDI, moving up in Ease of Doing Business rankings. However, the 2020 Consolidated FDI Policy mandates government approval for investments from countries sharing a land border with India to prevent opportunistic takeovers.



### Key Concepts
#### Structural Reforms
- Corporate tax rate cut to 15% for new manufacturing.
- Production Linked Incentives (PLI).
- Consolidation of labor laws.
- Expanded MSME definitions.

#### Consolidated FDI Policy 2020
Restricts automatic route investments from entities based in countries sharing a land border with India (e.g., China). Such investments now require explicit government approval.

### Quick Recall
> **Quick Recall:**
> - PLI: Production Linked Incentive.
> - FDI from bordering countries requires explicit government approval to prevent opportunistic takeovers.

### Edge Cases & Caveats
- Investments from neighboring countries (land borders) cannot use the automatic route.



## Section: Capital Outflow - Overseas Foreign Direct Investment (OFDI) 🔴
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->
<!-- Reason: Shift from India as merely a host country to India as a global investor -->

### Core Idea
Indian companies increasingly invest abroad (OFDI) to tap new markets, acquire strategic assets, and overcome domestic saturation. Policy has liberalized significantly since 2000, allowing automatic approvals up to high multiples of a company's net worth.

### In Simple Terms
> **In Simple Terms:** OFDI is when an Indian company goes to another country to open a branch, buy a foreign company, or set up a joint venture. They do this to find new customers or get new technology.

### Key Concepts
#### Drivers of OFDI
Market-seeking (expanding to new markets), acquiring technical know-how (strategic assets), low factor costs abroad, bypassing domestic saturation, and supporting exports.

#### Evolution of OFDI Policy
- **Phase I (1992-94)**: "Automatic Route" introduced with low limits ($2 million).
- **Phase II (1995-99)**: "Fast Track Route" under RBI, limits linked to export earnings, allowed acquisitions.
- **Phase III (2000 onwards)**: Under FEMA, limits drastically raised (up to 400% of net worth in 2007, currently 100%).

#### JV vs. WOS
- **Joint Venture (JV)**: Foreign entity formed where there are other foreign promoters holding a stake along with the Indian party.
- **Wholly Owned Subsidiary (WOS)**: Entire capital is held by the Indian party.

#### Trends and Destinations
OFDI is mainly financed through equity and loans. It predominantly flows into financial/business services and manufacturing. Top destinations reflect a shift toward developed nations (Singapore, Mauritius, USA, UK, Netherlands).

### Definitions
- **OFDI**: Investments by way of contribution to the capital of a foreign entity, signifying a long-term interest. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **JV**: Joint Venture.
- **WOS**: Wholly Owned Subsidiary.



### Examples
**Example: OFDI**
OYO acquiring Copenhagen-based data science firm Danamica to expand its business in Europe is an example of OFDI.



### Quick Recall
> **Quick Recall:**
> - Top OFDI destinations: Singapore, Mauritius, USA.
> - Funding sources include AD bank withdrawals, share swaps, and ECB proceeds.

## Section: Trade and Economic Development 🔴
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->
<!-- Reason: Fundamental concepts of how trade impacts an economy and the tools used for analysis -->

### Core Idea
Foreign trade acts as an engine of growth by providing technology flow, generating competitive pressure, allowing economies of scale, and reducing poverty. Various tools like Degree of Openness and Terms of Trade are used to analyze it.

### In Simple Terms
> **In Simple Terms:** Trading with other countries helps an economy grow by bringing in new technology, forcing local businesses to be competitive, and giving them a bigger market to sell in.

### Key Concepts
#### Contribution of Foreign Trade
Increases total factor productivity via technology, creates dynamic change through competition, helps exploit economies of scale, and generally increases worker welfare (higher wages, cheaper imports).

#### Trade Policy Analysis Tools
Metrics include Volume of Trade, Share of exports/imports in GDP (average propensity to export/import), Share in world trade, Degree of Openness, Composition, Sectoral/Geographical Orientation, and Terms of Trade.

### Definitions
- **Degree of Openness**: Measured as (Exports + Imports) / GDP.
- **Terms of Trade (TT)**: The terms at which exports exchange for imports; relative price of exports compared to imports on world markets. ⭐ (exam-important)

### Mechanisms / Processes
- **Pressure for Dynamic Change**: Foreign trade creates competitive pressure from imports and in export markets, leading to better resource allocation.
- **Welfare Improvement**: Larger exports translate to higher wages, cheaper imports benefit consumers, and technology transfers favor skilled labor.



### Quick Recall
> **Quick Recall:**
> - High Degree of Openness = more integrated into world trade.
> - Favorable TT means a country can buy more imports for a given amount of exports.



## Section: India's Foreign Trade: Volume and Composition 🔴
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->
<!-- Reason: Key statistics, trends, and composition changes in India's trade -->

### Core Idea
India's share in global trade is increasing but still small (~1.7% exports, 2.5% imports). India has a large trade deficit in merchandise but a growing surplus in services trade. The composition of exports has shifted from primary goods to manufactured/engineering goods.

### In Simple Terms
> **In Simple Terms:** Over the years, India's trade has grown massively. Instead of just exporting raw materials like we used to, we now export engineered goods and IT services. Our imports are mostly dominated by crude oil, which heavily influences our trade deficit.

### Key Concepts
#### Merchandise Trade Volume
Total merchandise trade grew significantly from 1980 to 2018-19, but exports have seen sluggish growth or contraction recently, resulting in a large trade deficit.

#### Trade in Services
India is a major player in global service trade (especially IT and BPO), maintaining a net surplus that helps offset the merchandise trade deficit.

#### Composition of Merchandise Trade
- **Exports**: Transformed from primary goods to non-primary. Top exports are engineering goods, petroleum products, and gems & jewellery.
- **Imports**: Dominated by crude oil (POL), electronic goods, machinery, and gold. POL imports are highly dependent on global prices.

#### Composition of Services Trade
Dominated by telecommunications, computer, and information services (41-46%), followed by other business services.

### Definitions
- **POL**: Petroleum, Oil, and Lubricants.

### Mechanisms / Processes
- **Contraction Factors**: Exports contract due to slowdowns in world output, global trade tensions, protectionism, and a lack of diversification in India's export basket.

### Quick Recall
> **Quick Recall:**
> - Top Exports: Engineering goods, petroleum products, gems & jewellery.
> - Top Imports: Crude oil (POL), electronic goods, machinery, gold.
> - Trade Deficit: Persistent in merchandise, but partially offset by a surplus in services (IT/BPO).

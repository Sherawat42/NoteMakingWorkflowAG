## Section: External Debt (Highlights) 🟢
<!-- Continues from chunk 006 -->
<!-- Exam importance: 🟢 CONTEXT -->
<!-- Reason: Statistical highlights of external debt -->

### Core Idea
India's external debt is predominantly long-term and US dollar-denominated. Its debt service ratio has improved, and external debt relative to GDP remains sustainable compared to other developing nations.

### In Simple Terms
> **In Simple Terms:** Most of the money India owes to foreign countries is long-term, and our ability to pay it back (debt service ratio) has improved. This means our foreign debt is safe and manageable right now.

### Key Concepts
#### Debt Composition
At end-March 2020, commercial borrowings were the largest component (39.4%), followed by NRI deposits. Most debt is US dollar-denominated (53.7%). The debt service ratio is relatively low (6.5% in 2020).

### Definitions
- **Debt Service Ratio**: Indicates the claim that servicing of external debt makes on current receipts.

### Quick Recall
> **Quick Recall:**
> - Largest component: Commercial borrowings.
> - Most debt is US dollar-denominated.
> - Debt is mostly long-term, reducing vulnerability.

## Section: Foreign Capital: Types and Trends 🔴
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->
<!-- Reason: Core unit introduction, definitions of FDI and FPI, and overall trends -->

### Core Idea
Foreign capital bridges savings, technology, and foreign exchange gaps in developing economies. It primarily flows into India as Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), which is long-term, and Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI), which is often short-term "hot money."

### In Simple Terms
> **In Simple Terms:** Foreign capital is money coming from outside to help businesses grow. FDI is like building a factory (long-term), while FPI is like buying stocks on the market (short-term and easy to sell).

### Key Concepts
#### Types of Foreign Capital
1. Foreign Investment (FDI and FPI)
2. External Commercial Borrowings (ECB)
3. NRI Deposits
4. External Assistance (Loans, Grants)

#### FDI vs. FPI
- **FDI**: Investment with a lasting interest (10%+ equity) in an unlisted or listed company. Focuses on control and long-term operations.
- **FPI**: Investment (<10% equity) in tradable instruments. Easily liquidated, often termed "hot money," making it highly volatile during global crises.

#### Sectoral and Geographic Distribution
- **Sectors**: FDI primarily flows into the services sector (communication, financial, retail) followed by manufacturing.
- **States**: Highly concentrated in Maharashtra, Karnataka, Delhi, and Gujarat.
- **Source Countries**: Top sources are Singapore and Mauritius.

### Definitions
- **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)**: Investment through capital instruments by a person resident outside India resulting in 10% or more equity ownership. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI)**: Investment of less than 10% of equity, more easily traded and not representing a controlling stake. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **External Commercial Borrowings (ECB)**: Commercial loans raised by eligible resident entities from recognized non-resident entities.

### Mechanisms / Processes
- **FDI**: Multinational corporations setting up branches, buying factories, or acquiring ≥10% equity in an Indian company.
- **FPI**: Foreign individuals or mutual funds buying shares on the Indian stock market (<10% equity).

### Examples
- **FDI**: A foreign car company building a manufacturing plant in Maharashtra.
- **FPI**: A foreign mutual fund buying shares of an Indian IT company.

### Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Treating FDI and FPI as the same. → ✅ Correct: FDI involves lasting interest/control (≥10%), while FPI involves passive/financial interest (<10%).

### Edge Cases & Caveats
- External Assistance tied to specific conditions ("tied aid") is generally not accepted by India.

### Quick Recall
> **Quick Recall:**
> - Top FDI sources: Singapore, Mauritius.
> - Top FDI sectors: Services, Manufacturing.
> - FPI is volatile "hot money."



## Section: Evolution of Inward Foreign Capital Policy 🟡
<!-- Continues into: Chunk 008 -->
<!-- Exam importance: 🟡 MODERATE -->
<!-- Reason: Historical timeline of investment policy -->

### Core Idea
India's FDI policy evolved from highly restrictive (pre-1991 under FERA) to progressively liberalized, moving to an automatic route for most sectors under FEMA (post-1991).

### In Simple Terms
> **In Simple Terms:** Before 1991, India was very strict about foreign companies setting up shop, only allowing it if they brought new technology. After 1991, India opened its doors and made it much easier for foreign money to flow in, allowing 100% ownership in many areas.

### Key Concepts
#### Pre-1991
Restrictive, driven by FERA, equity capped at 40%, cautious welcome requiring technology transfer.

#### Post-1991
Liberalization, FERA replaced by FEMA, introduction of the automatic route, lifting of equity caps in most sectors.

### Definitions
- **FERA**: Foreign Exchange Regulation Act (restrictive).
- **FEMA**: Foreign Exchange Management Act (liberalized).

### Mechanisms / Processes
- **Automatic Route**: FDI is permitted without prior government approval.
- **Government Route**: FDI requires prior approval from the government.

### Quick Recall
> **Quick Recall:**
> - Pre-1991: Restrictive (FERA), 40% equity limit.
> - Post-1991: Liberal (FEMA), Automatic route introduced, 100% equity permitted in most sectors.

### Core Idea
India's Current Account Deficit is driven by a merchandise trade deficit but buffered by a surplus in invisibles (remittances and services). The Capital Account, driven by FDI and ECB inflows, usually provides the financing needed to cover the CAD and build forex reserves.



### Key Concepts
#### Role of Invisibles
Net private transfers (remittances from Indians overseas) and services exports (IT/BPO) provide a massive surplus, offsetting much of the merchandise trade deficit.

#### Capital Account Developments
FDI and External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) have surged. Portfolio investments remain volatile. Official external assistance has lost significance relative to private capital flows.



## Section: External Debt 🟡

<!-- Exam importance: 🟡 MODERATE -->
<!-- Reason: Important macroeconomic indicators of financial stability -->

### Core Idea
External debt is money owed to foreign creditors. India's external debt policy emphasizes long-term debt over short-term debt to minimize vulnerability, keeping its debt-to-GDP and debt-service ratios at manageable levels.

### In Simple Terms
> **In Simple Terms:** External debt is the money a country borrows from outsiders (like the World Bank or foreign banks). It's safe if it's long-term and used for things that help the economy grow, but dangerous if it's short-term and the country can't pay it back.

### Key Concepts
#### Classification of External Debt
- **Long-term**: ECBs, loans from multilateral agencies (IMF, World Bank), NRI deposits.
- **Short-term**: FII investments in T-bills, short-term trade credit.

#### External Debt Indicators
Metrics to assess sustainability include Debt-to-GDP ratio, Debt Service Ratio, Short-term debt to total debt, and Forex reserves to total debt.

#### Policy Approach Post-1991
Restrictions on size and end-use of ECBs, caps on non-resident deposit interest rates, encouraging non-debt flows (FDI/FPI), and limiting short-term debt.

### Definitions
- **External Debt**: The component of total debt held by creditors of foreign nations.



### Quick Recall
> **Quick Recall:**
> - High short-term debt makes a country vulnerable to BoP crises.
> - India's debt is mostly long-term commercial borrowings and non-resident deposits.

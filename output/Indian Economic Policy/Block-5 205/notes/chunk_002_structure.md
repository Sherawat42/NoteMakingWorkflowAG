## Section: International Trade Policy and Instruments 🔴
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->
<!-- Reason: Core concepts, definitions of trade instruments, and formulas -->

### Core Idea
International trade policies are government actions toward international trade. They use various instruments like tariffs, quotas, and subsidies to regulate trade flows and protect domestic industries.

### In Simple Terms
> **In Simple Terms:** Trade policies are the rules a country sets for buying from and selling to other countries. Instruments like tariffs are just taxes on imported goods to make them more expensive than locally made ones.

### Key Concepts
#### Nominal and Effective Rate of Protection
Nominal rate of protection (NRP) measures the proportional difference between domestic and world prices. Effective rate of protection considers the net effect on producers, accounting for tariffs on both outputs and imported inputs.

### Definitions
- **Specific Tariffs**: Taxes levied as a fixed charge for each unit of goods imported. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Ad-valorem Tariffs**: Taxes levied as a fraction of the value of the imported goods. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Export Subsidies**: A payment by the government to a firm or individual that ships a good abroad.
- **Import Quotas**: A direct restriction on the quantity of a good that is imported.
- **Voluntary Export Restraints (VER)**: An export quota administered by the exporting country.
- **Local Content Requirements**: A regulation that requires some specified fraction of a final good be produced domestically.



### Quick Recall
> **Quick Recall:**
> - NRP = (Pd – Pw) / Pw × 100
> - Effective Protection = [VT – VW] / VW
> - VERs are always more costly to the importing country than an equivalent tariff.



## Section: International Trade Agreements: A Brief History 🟡
<!-- Exam importance: 🟡 MODERATE -->
<!-- Reason: Historical context of GATT and WTO rounds -->

### Core Idea
The GATT was established post-WWII to reduce tariffs through multilateral rounds. It was succeeded by the WTO in 1995 to formalize trade rules and dispute settlements.



### Key Concepts
#### GATT Trade Rounds
Major rounds include the Kennedy Round (across-the-board 50% tariff reductions), Tokyo Round, Uruguay Round (reduced average tariffs by 40% and liberalized agriculture/clothing), and the Doha Round (aimed at helping developing countries but stalled).

### Definitions
- **GATT**: General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade.
- **WTO**: World Trade Organisation.



## Section: Trade Policy of India (Pre and Post 1991) 🔴
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->
<!-- Reason: Crucial historical shift in Indian economic policy, core theories like Export Pessimism -->

### Core Idea
Before 1991, India followed a protectionist, import-substituting policy driven by export pessimism. Post-1991, facing a balance of payments crisis, India liberalized its trade, reduced tariffs, and opened up to FDI.

### In Simple Terms
> **In Simple Terms:** Before 1991, India didn't want to rely on other countries, so it put high taxes on imports. But it ran out of money, forcing it to open its markets to the world in 1991.

### Key Concepts
#### Export Pessimism
The belief that export earnings of underdeveloped countries are constrained because they export primary goods (price-takers) to monopsony buyers in developed countries, leading to deteriorating terms of trade (Prebish-Singer hypothesis).

#### 1991 Trade Policy Reforms
Initiated due to an external debt crisis and IMF loan conditions. Involved a major reduction in average tariffs, simplification of quotas, and a shift toward outward-oriented policies.

### Definitions
- **Import Substitution**: Promoting domestic production by making imported goods more expensive via high tariffs. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Monopsony**: A market with only one or a few buyers.

### Mechanisms / Processes
1. Balance of payment crisis → 2. Approach IMF for loan → 3. Trade policy reform (tariff reduction, quota removal) → 4. Outward oriented regime.



### Quick Recall
> **Quick Recall:**
> - Pre-1991 objectives: stabilize BOP, encourage industrialization, self-reliance.
> - Prebish-Singer hypothesis explains why primary goods exporters face deteriorating terms of trade.



## Section: FDI Policy in India 🟡

<!-- Exam importance: 🟡 MODERATE -->
<!-- Reason: Historical progression of FDI regulation -->

### Core Idea
FDI policy evolved from strict control under FERA to liberalization starting in the 1980s and especially with the New Industrial Policy of 1991.



### Key Concepts
#### Evolution of FDI Policy
Strict regulation in the 1970s via FERA (Foreign Exchange Regulation Act) limiting foreign equity to 40%. The 1991 policy allowed up to 51% automatic approval in specific sectors, shifting toward export-oriented FDI.

### Definitions
- **FERA**: Foreign Exchange Regulation Act.

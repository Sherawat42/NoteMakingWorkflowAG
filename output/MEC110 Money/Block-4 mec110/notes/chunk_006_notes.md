## Section: Credit Rating — Concept, Use, and Indian Agencies 🔴

### Core Idea
Credit rating is an evaluation of a borrower's **creditworthiness** — its ability to repay debt on time and in full. Agencies use letter scales (e.g., AAA highest → D lowest) to signal default risk. Globally, **Moody's** and **Standard & Poor's (S&P)** dominate. India has **seven** SEBI-registered agencies; **CRISIL, ICRA, CARE** are the largest, all using comparable AAA-to-D scales.

> **In Simple Terms:** A credit rating is a school report card for a borrower. AAA students rarely default and get the cheapest loans. D students are basically failing — if they get loans at all, they pay punitive rates. Investors look at the report card before lending.

### Key Concepts

#### Why credit rating matters
- Helps lenders/investors assess **default risk**.
- High rating → lower interest rates, better loan terms.
- Low rating → harder to obtain credit, higher rates, less favourable terms.
- Based on borrower's financial history: income, assets, liabilities, repayment record.

#### Indian credit rating agencies (seven SEBI-registered)
1. **Credit Rating Information Services of India Limited (CRISIL)**
2. **Investment Information and Credit Rating Agency of India (ICRA) Limited**
3. **Credit Analysis and Research (CARE) Limited**
4. **Acuite Ratings & Research**
5. **Brickwork Ratings India Private Limited**
6. **India Ratings and Research Pvt. Ltd.**
7. **Infometrics Valuation and Rating Pvt. Ltd.**

CRISIL is the **oldest**, followed by ICRA and CARE.

#### CRISIL rating scale (Table 12.1 from source)

| Rating | Explanation | Credit Risk | Degree of Safety |
|--------|-------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **CRISIL AAA** | Highest degree of safety regarding timely servicing | Lowest risk | Highest |
| **CRISIL AA** | High degree of safety | Very low risk | High |
| **CRISIL A** | Adequate degree of safety | Low risk | Adequate |
| **CRISIL BBB** | Moderate degree of safety | Moderate risk | Moderate |
| **CRISIL BB** | Moderate **risk of default** | Moderate risk of default | — |
| **CRISIL B** | High risk of default | High risk of default | — |
| **CRISIL C** | Very high risk of default | Very high risk of default | — |
| **CRISIL D** | In default or expected to be soon | Worst risk | — |

(Source: crisilratings.com/en/home/our-business/ratings/credit-ratings-scale.html)

#### Establishment dates and specialisations
| Agency | Established | Specialisation |
|--------|--------------|------------------|
| **CRISIL** | 1987 | Operates in UK, USA, Poland, China, Hong Kong, Argentina + India; rates commercial entities on strengths, market reputation, market share, board |
| **ICRA** | 1991 | Corporate governance, mutual funds, structured finance, performance ratings; AAA → D, same explanations as CRISIL |
| **CARE** | 1993 | Bank loan ratings, short-term and long-term debt instruments; AAA → D, same explanations as CRISIL |

### Definitions
- **Credit rating** ⭐: Evaluation of the creditworthiness of a borrower; measure of ability to repay debt on time and in full.
- **Credit rating agency** ⭐: Agency performing credit ratings; e.g., CRISIL, ICRA, CARE.
- **AAA**: Highest credit rating — lowest credit risk.
- **D**: Lowest credit rating — in default.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: AAA borrowers cannot default. → ✅ Correct: AAA = lowest *risk* of default, not zero risk.
- ❌ Mistake: All Indian rating agencies have different rating scales. → ✅ Correct: ICRA and CARE use **the same explanations** as CRISIL (AAA → D).

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Indian agencies: 7 registered. **CRISIL (1987) → ICRA (1991) → CARE (1993)**.
> - Scale: AAA → D, low → high risk.
> - Global majors: **Moody's, S&P**.

## Section: Bond Market in India — Composition, Regulators, Growth, Instruments 🔴

### Core Idea
India's bond market is part of the broader **debt market** (fixed-income market). It is jointly regulated by **RBI** (G-secs and money-market regulator) and **SEBI** (corporate and municipal bond regulator). The market is dominated by government securities, currently valued at **US$2.59 trillion**, with corporate bonds at US$567 billion. Recent inclusion in JP Morgan and Bloomberg global indices and an S&P outlook upgrade reflect rising international relevance.

### Key Concepts

#### Why debt markets matter (5 roles)
1. Efficiently mobilise and allocate resources.
2. Fund government developmental activities.
3. Transmit monetary-policy signals.
4. Manage liquidity (short and long term).
5. Provide a credible alternative to banks.

#### Regulators

| Body | Scope |
|------|-------|
| **RBI** (money-market regulator) | Regulates and facilitates **government bonds and other government securities** on the government's behalf |
| **SEBI** (securities-market regulator) | Regulates **corporate and municipal bonds** issued by listed (or to-be-listed) entities |

- **CP listing**: If issuer wants to list a Commercial Paper, **disclosures must comply with SEBI** requirements (even though CP is otherwise an RBI-side money-market instrument).

#### Indian bond-market scale and growth
- **Total bond market**: US$2.59 trillion.
- **Annual growth**: **12.3%** (INR terms, year-on-year).
- **Corporate bond segment**: US$567 billion; YoY growth **12.6%**.
- Government securities outstanding: **₹1,68,85,262 cr (US$2.02 tr)**.
- Corporate bonds outstanding: **₹47,28,935 cr (US$567 bn)**.
- 10-year (2013–2023) growth rate: **12.6% p.a.** in INR.
- **Indian bonds added to JP Morgan and Bloomberg global indices**.
- **S&P Global Ratings upgraded India's outlook from "stable" to "positive"** — citing policy consistency, reforms, infrastructure investment.
- Western corporate bond markets reach **50–65% of GDP**; India's is far lower → big growth runway.
- India's GDP target: **US$5 trillion**, much of it to be financed via corporate bonds.

#### Classification of Indian debt instruments (by issuer)

| Market segment | Issuer | Instruments |
|----------------|--------|-------------|
| **Government bonds / G-secs** | Central, State Governments | Zero-coupon bonds, coupon bonds, **Treasury Bills**, **STRIPS**, State Development Loans, Gold Bonds, **Sovereign Green Bonds** |
| **Corporate — public sector** | Government Agencies / Statutory Bodies | Government Guaranteed Bonds, Debentures |
| **Corporate — public sector** | Public Sector Units (PSU) | Bonds, Commercial Papers |
| **Corporate — public sector** | — | Municipal Bonds |
| **Corporate — private sector** | Corporates | Bonds, Commercial Papers |
| **Corporate — private sector** | Banks | Certificate of Deposits, Bonds, Structured instruments, Perpetual Bonds |
| **Corporate — private sector** | Financial Institutions | Certificate of Deposits, Bonds, Structured Instruments, Commercial Paper |

### Mechanisms / Processes

#### Brief on key debt instruments

**Government Securities (G-secs):**
- Issued by RBI on behalf of GoI.
- **Dated G-secs**: 1 to 30 years, fixed interest, **semi-annual payments**, principal as scheduled.
- **T-bills (short term)**: 91 / 182 / 364 days, discounted papers (zero-coupon).
- Risk-free (no credit risk).

**STRIPS** (Separate Trading of Registered Interest and Principal Securities):
- "Stripping" = separating a coupon-bearing bond into individual coupon and principal components.
- Example: a 5-year bond with semi-annual coupons → **10 coupon zero-coupon bonds + 1 principal zero-coupon bond**.

**Sovereign Green Bonds:**
- Approved by GFWC; issued by RBI on government's behalf to **finance green initiatives**.
- Ministry of Finance maintains a **green register** for monitoring.
- Annual report: bond issuances, approved projects, expenditures, current status, carbon-intensity impact.

**Certificate of Deposit (CD):** (cross-reference Chunk 002)
- Issued in **demat form** or as Usance Promissory Notes.
- **Banks**: 7 days–1 year maturity.
- **Financial Institutions**: 1 year–3 years maturity.
- Higher return than bank term deposit.
- Rated by CARE, ICRA, CRISIL, FITCH → enhances secondary-market tradability.
- Denomination: ₹1 lakh and multiples thereof.

**Commercial Papers (CPs):** (cross-reference Chunk 002)
- 7 days to 365 days; corporate (non-bank) issuer at discount.
- Minimum investment: **₹5 lakh face value** and multiples.
- Rated by CARE, ICRA, CRISIL, FITCH.
- Higher return than fixed deposits and CDs.
- Investment-grade CPs traded in secondary market depending on demand.
- Carries credit risk.

### Definitions
- **Debt market / fixed-income market** ⭐: Market for instruments offering fixed returns; bond market is its core.
- **G-secs**: Government securities issued by central/state governments.
- **STRIPS** ⭐: Separate Trading of Registered Interest and Principal Securities — splitting a coupon bond into zero-coupon components.
- **Sovereign Green Bond** ⭐: Government bond issued via RBI to finance green projects, monitored via Ministry of Finance's green register.
- **Treasury Bill (T-bill)**: Zero-coupon short-term G-sec (91/182/364 days).

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Indian bond market = US$2.59 tr; growing 12.3% YoY in INR.
> - Regulators: **RBI for G-secs/money market**, **SEBI for corporate/muni bonds + listed CPs**.
> - STRIPS: 5-yr semi-annual coupon bond → 10 + 1 = 11 zero-coupon instruments.
> - CDs: bank 7d–1y, FI 1–3y; min denomination ₹1 lakh.
> - CPs: 7–365 days; min investment ₹5 lakh face value.

## Section: Unit 12 Summary & Key Words 🟢

### Core Idea
Unit 12 covered bonds as a long-term loan-raising instrument, valuation as PV of cash flows, three yield measures (YTM, YTC, current yield), bond classification, credit rating, and the structure/regulators of the Indian bond market.

### Key Concepts

#### Key words (Unit 12)
- **Bond**: Long-term debt instrument.
- **Coupon**: Interest amount on a bond.
- **Bond market**: Place where bonds are traded.
- **Value of bond** ⭐: Intrinsic value = PV of all coupons + PV of redemption value.
- **Price of bond**: Market price determined by demand and supply.
- **YTM**: Rate of return if bond held till maturity.
- **YTC**: Rate of return if bond held until call.
- **Current yield**: Rate of return per ₹1 of investment (annual interest / current price).
- **Credit rating**: Evaluation of borrower's creditworthiness.
- **Credit rating agency**: Performs credit rating; e.g., CRISIL, ICRA, CARE.

> - Unit 12 take-aways: bond = long-term IOU; value = PV of cash flows; 3 yields (YTM/YTC/CY); 7 Indian rating agencies.

## Section: Unit 13 — Derivatives: Introduction 🔴

### Core Idea
**Derivatives** are financial instruments whose value is **derived** from an underlying asset (stock, bond, currency, commodity, index). The most common forms are options, futures, forwards, and swaps. Derivatives let investors and firms hedge risk or speculate on price movements; they can be highly **leveraged**, amplifying both gains and losses.

> **In Simple Terms:** A derivative is a contract whose price piggybacks on something else. If a bet pays out based on tomorrow's stock price of Reliance, the bet itself is a derivative — its value rides on Reliance's value.

### Key Concepts

#### What makes a derivative a derivative
- **Derivative (A)** derives value from an **underlying asset (B)**.
- Underlying assets: shares, bonds, currencies, commodities, indices, debentures, etc.
- Common derivative types: **options, futures, forwards, swaps**.

#### Legal definition (India)
- **Securities Laws (Second Amendment) Act, 1999**: officially included derivatives in the "Securities" definition.
- Per **Securities Contracts (Regulations) Act**: a derivative comprises securities derived from debt instruments, shares, loans, risk instruments, or contracts for differences. Includes contracts deriving value from prices or index of underlying securities.

### Definitions
- **Derivative** ⭐: Financial instrument that derives its value from an underlying asset such as a stock, bond, currency, commodity, or index.
- **Underlying asset** ⭐: The asset whose price drives a derivative's value.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Derivative ↔ underlying: A's value depends on B's.
> - 4 common types: **Options, Futures, Forwards, Swaps**.
> - Legal basis in India: **Securities Laws (Second Amendment) Act, 1999**.

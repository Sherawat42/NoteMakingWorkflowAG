## Section: Meaning and Characteristics of Bonds 🔴

### Core Idea
A **bond** is a long-term debt instrument in which the issuer commits to pay a fixed or floating interest rate (coupon) and repay the principal after a fixed period. Bonds carry six defining characteristics: long-term tenor, par value, coupon, maturity, issue price (par/premium/discount), and redemption value (par or premium).

> **In Simple Terms:** A bond is a long-term IOU. You lend money now, you collect interest annually, and at maturity you get your principal back. The "rules" of the IOU — face value, interest rate, period — are fixed when the bond is issued.

### Key Concepts

#### Real-life issuance example
- **14 March 2023**: Tata Capital Financial Services Limited (TCFSL) issued ₹2,000 crore bonds, **8.3% p.a. coupon**, **2 years 11 months** maturity.
- TCFSL receives ₹2,000 crore upfront, pays ₹16,600 crore interest annually (note: text says "₹16,600" — this is annual interest in ₹, i.e., ₹16,600 per ₹2 lakh face value; equivalently 8.3% on ₹2,000 cr = ₹166 cr annual; preserving source figure).
- Principal repaid at end of 2 years 11 months.

#### Six characteristics of bonds
1. **Long-term instruments** — long-term debt.
2. **Par (face) value** — stated face value on certificate; **does not change**.
3. **Coupon** — fixed or floating interest, except in zero-coupon bonds; usually annual but can be semi-annual or quarterly; **calculated on face/par value**.
4. **Maturity period** — generally definite, sometimes indefinite (perpetual bonds).
5. **Issue price** — at par / premium (price > face) / discount (price < face).
6. **Redemption** — at par or premium; theoretically possible at discount but not feasible in practice (investor wouldn't accept less than invested).

### Definitions
- **Bond** ⭐: Long-term debt instrument with issuer obliged to pay fixed/floating interest and repay principal after a fixed period.
- **Par value (face value)** ⭐: Stated face value mentioned on the bond certificate; doesn't change over the bond's life.
- **Coupon (rate)** ⭐: Periodic interest, computed on par value.
- **Redemption at premium / par / discount** — based on whether redemption value > / = / < face value.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Issue price relations: **Premium > Par > Discount**.
> - Coupon is calculated on **face value**, not market price.
> - Zero-coupon bonds are the exception — they pay no periodic interest.

## Section: Classification of Bonds 🔴

### Core Idea
Bonds are classified along five axes: **issuer, coupon, maturity, option, convertibility**. Each axis produces standard subtypes investors and issuers use to match risk-return profiles.

### Mechanisms / Processes

#### 1) By issuer

| Subtype | Description | Indian examples |
|---------|-------------|------------------|
| **Government bonds (G-secs)** | Issued by central or state governments; majority of Indian bond market | Fixed-rate bonds, Floating-rate bonds, **Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs)**, Inflation-Indexed Bonds, **7.75% GOI Savings Bonds (2018)** — interest rate **7% to 10%** |
| **Corporate bonds** | Issued by companies; riskier than G-secs; rate depends on creditworthiness | LIC Housing Finance (7.95%, mat. 29 Jan 2028, ₹1,477 cr); Hindustan Petroleum (7.74%, mat. Mar 2028); IRFC (8.1%, issued 23 Feb 2012, mat. 23 Feb 2027, FV ₹1,000) |
| **Municipal bonds (munis)** | Issued by local authorities | **Indore Municipal Corporation** (allotted **20 Feb 2023**: 8.25%, mat. 20 Feb 2026, ₹61 cr) |
| **Foreign bonds** | Issued by foreign government or foreign corporation | — |

**Government bond subtypes:**
- **Fixed-rate bonds (FRBs)**: fixed interest payments throughout life.
- **Floating-rate bonds**: variable interest payments.
- **Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs)**: invest in digital gold at average gold price; **SGB Scheme 2022-23**: 2.5% p.a. on nominal value; redeemable at end of **8 years**.
- **Inflation-Indexed Bonds**: prices and interest adjusted to CPI or WPI.
- **7.75% GOI Savings Bond** (2018): replaced 8% savings bond; not for NRIs, minors, or HUFs.

#### 2) By coupon
- **Zero coupon (pure discount) bonds**: no interest; issued at discount, repaid at par. Investor earns capital return only.
  - Example: FV ₹100, ₹10 discount → issuer receives ₹90; investor pays ₹90, gets ₹100 at maturity.
- **Coupon bonds (regular)**: carry interest.
  - **Fixed coupon bonds** — fixed interest.
  - **Floating coupon bonds** — variable interest.
  - Investor earns capital + revenue returns.

#### 3) By maturity
- **Perpetual bonds** — indefinite maturity.
- **Non-perpetual bonds** — definite maturity.

#### 4) By option (embedded)
- **Puttable (retractable) bond** — bondholder can sell back to issuer at predetermined put price (usually higher than face value) before maturity.
- **Callable bond** — issuer can buy back from bondholder at specified call price before maturity.

#### 5) By convertibility
- **Convertible bonds** — convertible into issuer's shares per terms.
- **Non-convertible bonds** — cannot convert; usually carry a **higher coupon** than convertibles.

### Definitions
- **Government bonds (G-secs)** ⭐: Issued by central or state governments; risk-free; rates 7–10%.
- **Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB)** ⭐: Government scheme for digital gold investment at average gold price.
- **Inflation-Indexed Bond**: Coupon and principal adjusted to CPI/WPI.
- **Corporate bond** ⭐: Issued by a company; risk depends on credit rating.
- **Municipal bond ("muni")**: Issued by local authority.
- **Zero-coupon bond** ⭐: No coupons; deep discount issue, par redemption.
- **Perpetual bond** ⭐: Indefinite maturity.
- **Puttable bond** ⭐: Investor's right to sell back early.
- **Callable bond** ⭐: Issuer's right to redeem early.
- **Convertible bond** ⭐: Convertible into equity per terms.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Convertible bonds always carry higher coupons. → ✅ Correct: **Non-convertible** bonds usually carry **higher** coupons.
- ❌ Mistake: Puttable = callable. → ✅ Correct: Put = investor's option to sell back; Call = issuer's option to buy back.
- ❌ Mistake: Zero-coupon = no return. → ✅ Correct: Zero-coupon delivers return as **capital appreciation** (issued at discount, redeemed at par).

> **Quick Recall:**
> - 5 axes: **Issuer / Coupon / Maturity / Option / Convertibility**.
> - SGB 2022-23: 2.5% p.a., 8-year tenor.
> - 7.75% GOI Savings Bond not for NRIs, minors, HUFs.

## Section: Valuation of Bonds 🔴

### Core Idea
The **value** (intrinsic value) of a bond is the **present value of all coupons + redemption value** discounted at the required rate. The buy/sell decision compares this intrinsic value to the market price: undervalued ⇒ buy; overvalued ⇒ sell.

### Mechanisms / Processes

#### General bond valuation formula
$$V = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{C_k}{(1+i)^k} + \frac{RV}{(1+i)^n}$$

- $V$ = intrinsic value of bond.
- $C_k$ = coupon (interest) in year k.
- $RV$ = redemption value.
- $i$ = discount rate (required return).
- $n$ = years to maturity.
- $1/(1+i)^k$ = **Present Value Factor (PVF)** for year k. PVFs can be obtained from PV tables, log technique, calculator, MS-Excel, or online calculators.

#### Perpetual bond valuation
- For a perpetual bond with constant coupon $C$ and discount rate $i$:
  - The cash-flow series $\sum C/(1+i)^k$ is an **infinite geometric series** with first term $C/(1+i)$ and common ratio $1/(1+i)$.
  - Sum of infinite GP: $S_\infty = a/(1-r)$.

$$V_{\text{perpetual}} = \frac{C/(1+i)}{1 - 1/(1+i)} = \frac{C}{i}$$

#### Buy/sell decisions
| Situation | Decision |
|-----------|----------|
| Market price > value | **Overvalued** — don't buy; if held, sell |
| Market price < value | **Undervalued** — buy; if held, keep |

### Examples

**Example 12.1 — Coupon bond redeemed at premium:**
- FV = ₹1,000; Coupon rate = 8% → C = ₹80; n = 3 yrs; i = 12%; redemption at 10% premium → RV = ₹1,100.
- $V = 80(0.8929) + 80(0.7972) + 80(0.7118) + 1,100(0.7118)$
- $V = 71.43 + 63.76 + 56.94 + 782.98 = ₹975.13$.
- Decisions:
  - Market price ₹1,050 > ₹975.13 ⇒ **overvalued**, don't buy.
  - Market price ₹800 < ₹975.13 ⇒ **undervalued**, buy.

**Example 12.2 — Perpetual bond:**
- C = 8% × ₹1,000 = ₹80; i = 10%.
- $V = C/i = 80/0.10 = ₹800$.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Bond value = PV of all coupons + PV of redemption value.
> - Perpetual bond: $V = C/i$.
> - Decision rule: market price < value ⇒ buy; > ⇒ sell/avoid.

## Section: Bond Yields — YTM, YTC, Current Yield 🔴

### Core Idea
Three yield measures describe the return on a bond. **YTM** is the discount rate that equates PV of cash flows to current price (held to maturity); **YTC** is the equivalent but to a call date; **Current Yield** is just annual interest ÷ price (and ignores capital gain/loss and time value of money).

### Mechanisms / Processes

#### Yield to Maturity (YTM)
- Definition: the discount rate $i$ that satisfies
  $$P = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{C_k}{(1+\text{YTM})^k} + \frac{RV}{(1+\text{YTM})^n}$$
- Solution: trial-and-error (slow) or use approximation.
- **Approximation formula** (for constant coupon C, face value F, price P, n years):

$$\text{Approximate YTM} = \frac{C + (F-P)/n}{(F+P)/2}$$

#### Yield to Call (YTC)
- Same as YTM, but uses **call price** instead of RV and **call period** s instead of n:

$$P = \sum_{k=1}^{s} \frac{C_k}{(1+\text{YTC})^k} + \frac{\text{Call price}}{(1+\text{YTC})^s}$$

#### Current Yield
$$\text{Current Yield} = \frac{\text{Annual interest (coupon)}}{\text{Current price of bond}}$$
- Ignores redemption value and time value of money → "not an appropriate measure of yield" for long-term decisions.

### Examples

**Example 12.3 — Approximate YTM:**
- FV ₹1,000; coupon rate 10% → C = ₹100; n = 5 yrs; P = ₹800.
- Approx YTM = $\frac{100 + (1{,}000 - 800)/5}{(1{,}000 + 800)/2} = \frac{100 + 40}{900} = 140/900 = 0.1555$ ≈ **15.55%**.
- Interpretation: Investing ₹800 in this bond and holding 5 years yields ~15.55%.

**Worked example (Current Yield):**
- 10% coupon bond (FV ₹1,000) trading at ₹980. Annual interest = ₹100.
- Current yield = ₹100 / ₹980 = **10.20%**.
- Interpretation: Investing ₹1 today earns ₹0.102 interest at year-end.

**Worked example (Approx YTM, second case from check-your-progress):**
- Coupon rate 7%, FV ₹500 → C = ₹35; P = ₹350; n = 10.
- Approx YTM = $\frac{35 + (500 - 350)/10}{(500 + 350)/2} = \frac{35 + 15}{425} = 50/425 = 0.1176$ ≈ **11.76%**.
- Note: True trial-and-error YTM = **12.396%**.

### Definitions
- **YTM (Yield to Maturity)** ⭐: Return on a bond if held to maturity; the discount rate equating PV of cash flows to current price.
- **YTC (Yield to Call)** ⭐: YTM equivalent computed to the call date and call price (for callable bonds).
- **Current Yield** ⭐: Annual coupon ÷ current price; ignores capital gain/loss and time value of money.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Current yield = YTM. → ✅ Correct: Current yield ignores redemption value and time value.
- ❌ Mistake: Approximate YTM gives the exact yield. → ✅ Correct: It's an approximation; trial-and-error gives the exact YTM (e.g., 11.76% approx vs 12.396% exact).
- ❌ Mistake: YTC uses face value at maturity. → ✅ Correct: YTC uses **call price** at the **call date**.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - YTM equation: $P = \sum C/(1+\text{YTM})^k + RV/(1+\text{YTM})^n$.
> - Approx YTM = $\dfrac{C + (F-P)/n}{(F+P)/2}$.
> - Current yield = Coupon / Price (ignores time value).

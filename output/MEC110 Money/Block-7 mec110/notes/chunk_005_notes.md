## Section: Unit 27 Introduction — What are NPAs? 🔴

### Core Idea
A **non-performing asset (NPA)** is a loan or advance in default or arrears — when the principal is due, or interest payments are late or missed. Banks' main role is lending; NPAs reflect **asset quality, credit risk, and resource allocation efficiency**. The level of NPAs creates serious concern for banking-system stability. NPAs can be manageable in the short term, but accumulating NPAs threaten resilience and growth. India's NPA accumulation cannot be attributed to a single event or timeline — it spans both small and major banks.

> **In Simple Terms:** A loan a bank has effectively lost or fears losing. Too many of these and the bank's profits, reputation, and ability to lend all suffer.

### Definitions
- **Non-Performing Asset (NPA)** ⭐: Loan or advance that is in default or in arrears. A loan is in arrears when the principal is due or interest payments are late or missed. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Performing Asset / Standard Asset** ⭐: Asset generating regular income with the expected usual commercial risk; standard assets do not require supplementary provisions.
- **Loan in Default**: When the lender considers the loan agreement broken and the debtor unable to meet obligations.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - NPA = loan in default / arrears
> - Reflects asset quality, credit risk, allocation efficiency
> - 90 days non-repayment is the standard NPA classification trigger

## Section: Profile of NPAs in India — Gross vs Net NPAs 🔴

### Core Idea
For measurement, NPAs are classified as **Gross NPAs** (absolute total of NPAs) and **Net NPAs** (Gross NPAs minus specific provisions). Gross NPAs show the total irrecoverable amount; Net NPAs show the **actual burden** on the bank after provisioning.

### Definitions
- **Gross NPAs** ⭐: Indicates the absolute amount of the total value of loans that are due within 90 days in a particular quarter or financial year.
- **Net NPAs** ⭐: Net value of NPAs after the bank makes specific provisions for them. Arrived at by subtracting doubtful and unpaid assets (provisions) from gross NPAs.
- **Gross NPA Ratio** ⭐: Gross NPAs / Gross Advances.

### Key Formula
$$\text{Net NPAs} = \text{Gross NPAs} - \text{Gross Advances} - \text{Provisions}$$
*(as stated in source — note the unusual subtraction of Gross Advances; standard textbook formula is Gross NPAs − Provisions)*

$$\text{Gross NPA Ratio} = \frac{\text{Gross NPAs}}{\text{Gross Advances}}$$

### Direct vs Net NPAs
| Metric | Meaning | What it shows |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| Gross NPAs | Absolute amount considered irrecoverable | Total NPA value for a particular financial year |
| Net NPAs | After provisioning | Actual burden on the bank |

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Gross NPAs = absolute irrecoverable amount
> - Net NPAs = Gross − provisions (true burden)
> - Gross NPA Ratio = Gross NPAs / Gross Advances

## Section: Nature of NPAs — Three Categories by Period of Default 🔴

### Core Idea
RBI categorises NPAs into three classes based on **period of default**: sub-standard, doubtful, and loss assets. Banks must classify each NPA into one category. Beyond term loans, NPAs also include over-due overdraft / cash-credit accounts, agricultural loans by crop-season rules, and any account with payments overdue 90+ days.

### Three Categories

| Category | Period of default | Description |
|----------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Sub-Standard Asset** ⭐ | Outstanding ≤ 12 months as NPA | First-stage NPA |
| **Doubtful Asset** ⭐ | Outstanding > 12 months as NPA, already classified sub-standard | Higher-risk NPA |
| **Loss Asset** ⭐ | Uncollectible / little to no recovery; bank may consider some recovery value left as it has not been written off | Bank must record loss; entire loan written off |

### Other Forms of NPAs (beyond term loans)
1. **Overdraft / Cash-Credit (OD/CC) accounts** out of order for more than **90 days**
2. **Agricultural loans**:
   - Short-period crops: NPA if instalment of principal/interest unpaid for **two crop seasons**
   - Long-period crops: NPA if instalment of principal/interest unpaid for **one crop season**
3. **Any other account** with payment overdue more than 90 days

### Mechanisms / Processes
**NPA classification trigger:**
1. Loan becomes NPA after **90 days of non-repayment**
2. Bank asks borrower to liquidate assets / mortgage as per loan agreement
3. If no assets pledged → bank may write off as bad debt and **sell to a "bad bank"** (e.g., **NARCL — National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited**)
4. NARCL is a public-sector asset-reconstruction company in India established to acquire and aggregate NPAs

### Definitions
- **Sub-Standard Asset** ⭐: Loan/advance that has remained NPA for ≤ 12 months.
- **Doubtful Asset** ⭐: Loan/advance that has been NPA for > 12 months and was previously classified sub-standard.
- **Loss Asset** ⭐: Loan/advance that is uncollectible or has little/no recovery value, but has not yet been wholly written off — must record loss; entire amount must be written off completely.
- **NARCL (National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited)** ⭐: Public-sector asset-reconstruction company in India established to acquire and aggregate NPAs ("bad bank"). ⭐
- **Bad Bank**: Specialised institution that takes bad loans off bank balance sheets to ease the burden.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Sub-standard ≤ 12 months
> - Doubtful > 12 months (was sub-standard)
> - Loss = uncollectible
> - 90-day rule for general NPAs; 1–2 crop seasons for agriculture
> - **NARCL** = India's "bad bank"

## Section: Magnitude and Trend of NPAs in India 🔴

### Core Idea
NPAs grew sharply in the mid-2000s during the 2006–2008 boom (over-optimism, infrastructure pipeline). The Gross NPA ratio of scheduled commercial banks peaked above **11% in March 2018** (over **14% for public-sector banks**), then declined steadily: 8.2% (Mar 2020), 7.3% (Mar 2021), down to **2.8% in March 2024** (per RBI's FSR). Public-sector banks dominate NPAs (75.75% of total net NPAs in March 2022). The decline partly reflects use of write-offs (which erode the capital base) and pandemic-era moratoria.

### Key Numbers

| Indicator | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Gross NPA ratio, March 2018 | > 11% (PSBs > 14%) |
| Gross NPA ratio, March 2020 | 8.2% |
| Gross NPA ratio, March 2021 | 7.3% |
| Gross NPA ratio, March 2024 | 2.8% |
| Total net NPAs, March 31, 2022 | ₹2,04,225.90 crore |
| Fraud as proportion of PSB gross advances, FY 2013-14 (peak) | 1.32% |
| Fraud as proportion of PSB gross advances, FY 2021-22 | 0.05% |

### Net NPAs in Indian Banks (March 31, 2022) — Table 27.1

| Bank type | Net NPAs (₹ crore) | Share (%) |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------|
| Public Sector Banks | 1,54,745.39 | 75.75 |
| Private Sector Banks | 43,733.24 | 21.44 |
| Foreign Banks | 3,022.72 | 1.48 |
| Small Finance Banks | 2,724.55 | 1.33 |
| **Total** | **2,04,225.90** | **100.00** |

### Trend Drivers
- Mid-2000s boom + over-optimism (2006–2008)
- Easy credit during high-growth period
- 2017–18: huge spike in NPAs, then decline
- "Dirty dozen" of top industrialists contribute majority NPAs in India
- NPAs in agriculture / priority sector are **comparatively lower** than corporate houses
- PSB NPAs > Private-sector-bank NPAs

### Definitions
- **Twin Balance Sheet (TBS) Problem** ⭐: Situation in which the **borrower (corporate sector)** is under stress due to high debt and the **lender (banks)** is under stress due to non-repayment of loans (NPAs). Both balance sheets — borrower's and lender's — have problems. ⭐
- **Early Warning System (EWS)** ⭐: Software-based monitoring system used by banks to track customer portfolios regularly and identify signs of distress in borrower asset quality (signs not catchable manually). Procedure varies bank to bank. ⭐
- **"Dirty Dozen"**: Slang for India's top industrialists who account for a major portion of NPAs.

### Causes of the Decline (Chandrasekhar, 2022)
- **Write-offs** (erode banks' capital base)
- **Time-bound moratorium on debt repayments** during the pandemic for small units / selected borrowers
- Government-guaranteed lending to MSMEs

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Mar 2018: Gross NPA ratio > 11% (PSBs > 14%) → Mar 2024: 2.8%
> - PSBs hold ~76% of net NPAs (Mar 2022)
> - Twin Balance Sheet (TBS) = stressed borrower + stressed lender
> - EWS = software-based early-distress detection
> - Decline driven partly by write-offs + pandemic moratorium

## Section: Major Causes of NPAs — Bank-Specific & Macro 🔴

### Core Idea
Causes of NPAs split into **bank-specific factors** (six) and **macroeconomic factors** (three). Beyond these, ten reasons explain the emergence and persistence of NPAs in India — ranging from the 2000–2008 boom-era over-lending and the 2008–09 corporate-profit collapse, to mining bans, environmental-permit delays, lax lending norms, and weak institutional safeguards.

### Causes of NPAs (Das & Uppal, 2021)

| Type | Factors |
|------|---------|
| **Bank-specific** | (a) non-performing advances, (b) deposits, (c) non-interest income, (d) interest income, (e) operational efficiency, (f) capital adequacy |
| **Macroeconomic** | (a) GDP growth, (b) rate of inflation, (c) interest rate |

### Reasons for NPAs in India (Naveen J., 2022)
1. 2000–2008 boom: Indian economy in expansion → banks (especially PSBs) lent extensively to companies
2. 2008–09 financial crisis: corporate profits decreased; mining-projects ban
3. Substantial delay in environmental permits — affected infrastructure (power, iron, steel)
4. Rising raw-material prices + supply shortage
5. Relaxed lending norms; foregone analysis of financials and credit ratings (especially for big corporate houses)
6. Other contributing absences:
   - (a) credit-information bureau
   - (b) poor lending strategies / low scrutiny by banks
   - (c) wilful defaults
   - (d) economic slowdown and government policies
   - (e) inadequate risk and supervision
   - (f) lack of adequate capital
   - (g) low level of technology
   - (h) dishonest and poor credit documentation
   - (i) concentration in few customers / groups
   - (j) corruption, political and government interventions

### Other Causal Drivers
- Large frauds — top management forced by politicians/bureaucrats to lend to unscrupulous borrowers
- Bad loans advanced compromising due diligence
- Death of potential earning members in family
- Lack of entrepreneurial information / sense on borrower's part
- Negligence / ineffectiveness of recovery tribunals

### Definitions
- **Wilful Default**: Borrower has the capacity to repay but deliberately does not.
- **Credit-Information Bureau**: Institution that tracks borrowing histories — its absence allows under-screened lending.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: "NPAs are mainly an agriculture problem in India" → ✅ Correct: NPAs in **agriculture and priority sector** are comparatively **lower** than for corporate houses; "dirty dozen" of top industrialists drive the bulk.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Bank-specific factors: NPAs, deposits, interest/non-interest income, operational efficiency, capital adequacy
> - Macro factors: GDP growth, inflation, interest rate
> - 2000–2008 boom + 2008 GFC + lax lending = origin of current NPA stock
> - Corporate NPAs > priority-sector / agricultural NPAs

## Section: RBI's Approach to NPAs 🔴

### Core Idea
RBI has progressively tightened NPA-resolution rules. Key milestones: **Strategic Debt Restructuring Scheme (2015, with SEBI)**, **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (2016)** with NCLT, and the **2019 fresh guidelines** mandating 30-day default recognition, board-approved resolution policies, weekly default reporting for borrowings over ₹50 million, and inter-creditor agreements.

### Key Initiatives Timeline

| Year | Initiative | Purpose |
|------|------------|---------|
| **2015** | **Strategic Debt Restructuring (SDR) Scheme** (with SEBI) | Help FIs recover loans by **taking over distressed companies** — gave banks substantial power in management of defaulting borrowers |
| **2016** | **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)** | Cases filed with **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT)** — recovery of creditor assets |
| **2019** | **Fresh RBI guidelines** | (i) 30-day default recognition, (ii) board-approved policies for stressed-asset resolution, (iii) credit reporting on borrowings > ₹50 million, (iv) weekly default reporting, (v) inter-creditor agreements |

### Recent RBI Stance
- Prudential asset-liability management framework for domestic banks
- Curbs on total leverage
- Plugging regulatory arbitrage between banks and other financial entities
- RBI Governor warned (March 17, 2023) against asset-liability mismatches; cited the 2023 **US banking crisis** as an example of mismatch-driven crisis
- RBI flagged **risks of private cryptocurrencies** to financial stability
- Long-term goal: full implementation of international standards in regulation, supervision, governance, resolution, and deposit insurance

### Definitions
- **Strategic Debt Restructuring (SDR) Scheme** ⭐ (2015): RBI–SEBI scheme to help FIs recover loans by taking over distressed companies; gave banks management power over defaulting firms.
- **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016** ⭐: Plays an important role in recovery of creditor assets; cases filed with the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT).
- **NCLT (National Company Law Tribunal)**: Adjudicating authority for IBC cases.
- **Inter-Creditor Agreement**: Agreement among multiple lenders on resolution strategy for a stressed asset.

## Section: Impact of NPAs 🔴
<!-- Continues in chunk 006 — final impact discussion + measures + recent policy -->

### Core Idea
NPAs trigger a **vicious cycle** affecting bank profitability, credit creation, investor/depositor confidence, and macroeconomic growth. They increase operating costs (recovery), hurt interest earnings, raise lending rates (which feeds back to less credit), erode reputation, and amplify risk aversion. NPA-driven write-offs erode banks' capital base, partly offset by government recapitalisation of PSBs. The Twin Balance Sheet crisis significantly contributed to India's late-2010s growth deceleration.

### Channels of Impact

| Channel | Effect |
|---------|--------|
| **Profitability** | NPAs negatively affect ability to generate income; ↓ Return on Assets, ↓ Return on Equity |
| **Income vs Profit** | Positive relation between gross NPAs and total income; **negative** relation between gross NPAs and total profit |
| **Lending rates** | Banks raise lending rates to maintain margins → ↓ borrowing for infrastructure / industrial projects → ↓ credit creation → ↓ revenue |
| **Confidence** | Investors, lenders, depositors lose confidence; reputation harm |
| **Risk-aversion** | Banks become risk-averse → hesitate to extend credit |
| **Capital base** | Write-offs erode capital base — PSB recapitalisation only partially neutralises |
| **Macro growth** | Twin Balance Sheet crisis significantly contributed to late-2010s growth deceleration |

### Mechanisms / Processes
**NPA Vicious Cycle:**
↑ NPAs → ↓ interest earnings + ↑ recovery costs → ↓ profitability → ↑ lending rates → ↓ credit demand and creation → ↓ economic activity → ↑ corporate stress → ↑ defaults → ↑ NPAs

### Definitions
- **Twin Balance Sheet Crisis Effect**: NPAs combined with non-financial-firm balance-sheet deterioration significantly contributed to the deceleration of growth in the late 2010s.

### Connections
- Builds on: Twin Balance Sheet (this chunk), Vicious-cycle pattern (Chunk 001)
- Continues into: Measures, Effectiveness, Recent Policy (Chunk 006)

> **Quick Recall:**
> - NPAs → ↓ profit + ↑ lending rates + ↓ credit + ↓ confidence
> - Vicious cycle similar to crisis vicious cycle (Unit 24)
> - Late 2010s growth slowdown partly driven by Twin Balance Sheet crisis
> - Positive relation: NPAs ↔ income; **Negative**: NPAs ↔ profit

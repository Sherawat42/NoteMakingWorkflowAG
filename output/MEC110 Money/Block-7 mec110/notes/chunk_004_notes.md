## Section: Issues in Financial Stability 🔴

### Core Idea
Five major **issues** can arise when policymakers try to maintain financial stability — different from "factors" in that they are systemic problems that *threaten* stability rather than just driving it. The five are: systemic risk, shadow banking, cyber-security, international spillovers, and too-big-to-fail.

> **In Simple Terms:** Five things that keep regulators up at night: cascade failures (systemic), unregulated lenders (shadow banking), hackers (cyber), foreign contagion (spillovers), and "rescue-me-no-matter-what" mega-banks (too-big-to-fail).

### Five Issues

| # | Issue | What it is | How it threatens stability |
|---|-------|------------|----------------------------|
| **a** | **Systemic risk** | Risk imposing a barrier in the efficient financial system that can severely impact the entire economy | Contagion from one institution's failure → cascade through markets → collapse, loss of confidence |
| **b** | **Shadow banking** | Credit and financial services provided by **unregulated** entities (unlike banks) | Risky lending, no accountability, no review mechanism → builds systemic risk |
| **c** | **Cyber-security** | Cyber attacks on financial institutions, payment systems | Disrupts fund flow, exposes sensitive info, enables money laundering / unauthorised transactions → loss of public confidence |
| **d** | **International spill-ins and spill-outs** | Cross-border financial-shock spread via market interconnections | Loss of confidence, currency depreciation, asset-price volatility, increased systemic-risk threat |
| **e** | **Too-big-to-fail** | Some institutions are so large their failure would catastrophically hit the economy | Government/RBI guarantee → moral hazard (excessive risk-taking by these institutions); erodes public confidence (taxes used to bail out big banks) |

### Definitions
- **Systemic Risk** ⭐: Risk that imposes a barrier in the efficient financial system, with severe impact on the entire economy. Sources: interconnections and interdependence between institutions/markets, and contagion effects from the failure of one institution. Refers to risk inherent to the entire market or market segment due to certain events (natural disasters, inflation, war). ⭐
- **Shadow Banking** ⭐: System where credit and related financial services are provided by entities that are **not regulated**, unlike banks. ⭐
- **Cyber-Security Threat**: Cyber attacks (illegal access, fraudulent transactions, money laundering) on banks, NBFCs, investment firms, payment systems.
- **International Spill-ins / Spill-outs**: Cross-border financial-shock transmission via interconnected markets — disruption in one country quickly affects others.
- **Too-Big-To-Fail (TBTF)** ⭐: Some institutions are so large that their failure would catastrophically harm the economy; their implicit guarantee creates moral hazard. ⭐

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: "Shadow banking is illegal banking" → ✅ Correct: Shadow banking is **unregulated** (not necessarily illegal) — entities like some NBFCs that provide bank-like services without bank-level regulation.
- ❌ Mistake: "Too-big-to-fail makes banks safer" → ✅ Correct: TBTF creates **moral hazard** — these banks take **excessive risks** because they expect to be bailed out.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - 5 issues: systemic risk, shadow banking, cyber-security, international spillovers, too-big-to-fail
> - TBTF → moral hazard
> - Shadow banking = unregulated → systemic risk

## Section: Challenges in Financial Stability 🔴

### Core Idea
Eight challenges make financial-stability planning difficult in India. Some are structural (complexity, organisational deficiencies, informal sector), some are macroeconomic (rising prices, fiscal deficit, NPAs), some are external (outside influences), and some are informational/behavioural (asymmetric information, behavioural biases).

### Eight Challenges

| # | Challenge | Description |
|---|-----------|-------------|
| **a** | **Highly complex** | Indian financial system has wide range of instruments and institutions, exposing it to liquidity, credit, systemic risk |
| **b** | **Rising prices (inflation)** | Inflation beyond control reduces purchasing power, savings value; raises borrowing cost; discourages investment and production |
| **c** | **Fiscal deficit** | Government spending > earnings → public debt → tax pressure / spending cuts → negative impact |
| **d** | **Growing NPAs** | Bad loans (loans not repaid) reduce profitability of FIs → liquidity crisis → instability |
| **e** | **Organisational deficiencies** | Large informal sector, weak infrastructure, lack of robust regulation make it hard for RBI / government to regulate |
| **f** | **Outside influences** | Global commodity-price changes, FX fluctuations, shifts in global economic conditions affect trade and investment |
| **g** | **Unequal access to information** | Asymmetric information → adverse selection + moral hazard → distrust → instability |
| **h** | **Behavioural biases** | Herd behaviour, overconfidence → excessive-risk decisions → instability; deeply rooted in psychology, hard to predict/rationalise |

- Sets up: Unit 27 NPAs (Chunk 005), Unit 29 behavioural finance (later chunks)

## Section: Risks Triggering Financial Instability — Eight Risk Types 🔴

### Core Idea
Eight risks can trigger financial instability. They overlap with — but extend — the five "risks in financial markets" introduced in Unit 24, adding **systemic, cyber-security, market, and political** risks. Each risk has standard control tools.

### Eight Risks and Their Control Tools

| # | Risk | What it is | Control Tools |
|---|------|------------|---------------|
| **a** | **Cyber-security threats** | Cyber attacks disrupting fund flow, exposing info, enabling fraud/money laundering | Robust cyber-laws |
| **b** | **Systemic risk** | Cascade barrier with severe economy-wide impact | Macro-prudential policies |
| **c** | **Credit risk** | Borrower fails to meet financial obligations on time / defaults | Credibility-score check, collateral |
| **d** | **Market risk** | Loss arising from market-price changes (e.g., asset-price bubbles), market volatility | Setting interest rates, regulating FIs, macro-prudential policy |
| **e** | **Operational risk** | Lack of strong internal system → fraud, errors, technical failures | Risk-management framework, internal control system, regulatory supervision |
| **f** | **Liquidity risk** | Shortage of liquid funds to meet obligations as they arise | Adequate capital and liquidity reserves; liquidity-risk-management methods |
| **g** | **Interest rate risk** | Changes in interest rates affecting financial-instrument value and FI profitability | Stress testing, interest-rate risk-management framework |
| **h** | **Political risk** | Political events (policy changes, geopolitical tensions) damaging investor confidence | Diversified geographic exposure; risk-management framework |

### Definitions
- **Credit Risk** (Unit 26 keyword): Probability of a financial loss resulting from a borrower's failure to repay a loan.
- **Liquidity Risk** (Unit 26 keyword): Risk that a company or individual will not have enough cash to meet financial obligations on time.
- **Foreign Exchange Risk** (Unit 26 keyword): Risk of financial impact due to exchange-rate fluctuations.
- **Market Risk** (Unit 26 keyword) ⭐: Possibility that an individual or entity will experience losses due to factors affecting overall investment performance in financial markets.
- **Operational Risk** (Unit 26 keyword): Uncertainties and hazards a company faces in day-to-day business activities.
- **Political Risk** ⭐: Risk arising from political events or actions like changes in government policy or geopolitical tensions; damages investor confidence.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - 8 risks: cyber, systemic, credit, market, operational, liquidity, interest-rate, political
> - Each risk has a corresponding control toolkit
> - Macro-prudential policy is the systemic-risk antidote

## Section: Stability Measures for Ensuring Financial Stability 🔴

### Core Idea
Five measures help achieve financial stability. They span regulation (robust framework), prevention (macro-prudential), supervision (monitoring), response (crisis-management framework), and external coordination (global cooperation).

### Five Measures

| # | Measure | What it does |
|---|---------|--------------|
| **a** | **Robust regulatory framework** | Promotes fairness, transparency, accountability, risk management → sound financial system |
| **b** | **Macro-prudential policies** | Prevent systemic risk via counter-cyclical capital buffers, reserve requirements, loan-to-value ratios |
| **c** | **Supervision and monitoring** | Regular checks on FI compliance and financial health |
| **d** | **Crisis management framework** | Set of policies/procedures to manage and respond to a crisis |
| **e** | **Global cooperation** | Coordination among global regulators, central banks, organisations — share information, coordinate policy |

### Definitions
- **Macro-prudential Policy** ⭐: Policies that prevent systemic risk; includes counter-cyclical capital buffers, reserve requirements, loan-to-value ratios. ⭐
- **Micro-prudential Norms**: Regulation focused on stability of **individual** banking institutions and protection of their customers, rather than the economy as a whole.
- **Crisis Management Framework**: Set of policies and procedures for managing and responding to a crisis.
- **Counter-Cyclical Capital Buffer**: Macro-prudential tool — additional capital requirement in good times, released in bad times.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: "Macro-prudential and micro-prudential policies are the same" → ✅ Correct: **Macro-prudential** focuses on system-wide stability; **micro-prudential** focuses on individual institutions and their customers.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - 5 measures: regulation, macro-prudential, supervision, crisis-management, global cooperation
> - Macro-prudential targets system-wide systemic risk
> - Micro-prudential targets individual institutions

## Section: Financial Stability and Development Council (FSDC) 🔴

### Core Idea
The FSDC is India's **apex forum** for financial stability, established by the Government in **December 2010**. It strengthens mechanisms for financial stability, inter-regulatory coordination, and financial-sector development. Chaired by the Union Finance Minister, it brings together top regulators (RBI Governor, SEBI, IRDA, PFRDA, etc.). Its sub-committee, chaired by the RBI Governor, meets more often. The Financial Stability Unit (FSU) of the RBI serves as the Sub-committee's Secretariat.

> **In Simple Terms:** When India's regulators (RBI, SEBI, IRDA, PFRDA) need to coordinate on financial stability, they sit at the FSDC table — chaired by the Finance Minister.

### Composition

**FSDC (chaired by Union Finance Minister) — members:**
1. Governor of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
2. Finance Secretary and/or Secretary of the Department of Economic Affairs
3. Secretary of the Department of Financial Services
4. Chief Economic Adviser, Ministry of Finance
5. Chairperson, Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
6. Chairperson, Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority (IRDA)
7. Chairperson, Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA)

**FSDC Sub-committee (chaired by RBI Governor) — additional members beyond all FSDC members:**
- Four Deputy Governors of the RBI
- Additional Secretary of the DEA responsible for FSDC affairs
- **Member Secretary**: Executive Director of RBI overseeing financial stability
- **Secretariat**: Financial Stability Unit (FSU) of the RBI

### FSDC Purview
- Macro-prudential supervision of the economy
- Oversight of large financial conglomerates
- Resolution of inter-regulatory coordination issues
- Financial-sector development matters
- Initiatives on financial literacy and inclusion

### Definitions
- **FSDC** ⭐: Apex forum to strengthen mechanisms for upholding financial stability, reinforcing inter-regulatory coordination, and fostering development of the financial sector. Established December 2010, chaired by the Union Finance Minister. ⭐

### Connections
- Builds on: Concept of Financial Stability (Chunk 003)

> - Chair: Union Finance Minister

> - Sub-committee chaired by RBI Governor; Secretariat = FSU of RBI

## Section: Financial Stability Report (FSR) 🟡

### Core Idea
The FSR is a **biannual report** issued by the RBI presenting the consolidated assessment of the FSDC Sub-committee on financial-stability risks and the financial system's robustness. The text covers three issues: **June 2023, December 2023, June 2024 (29th issue)**. Each highlights global headwinds versus Indian resilience, then reports CRAR, CET1, GNPA, NNPA ratios for SCBs and NBFCs, plus stress-test projections.

### FSR Snapshots

| Indicator | June 2023 (Mar 2023 data) | December 2023 (Sep 2023 data) | June 2024 (Mar 2024 data) |
|-----------|---------------------------|--------------------------------|---------------------------|
| **SCB CRAR** | 17.1% (historical high) | 16.8% | 16.8% |
| **SCB CET1** | 13.9% | 13.7% | 13.9% |
| **SCB GNPA** | 3.9% (10-year low) | 3.2% (multi-year low) | 2.8% (multi-year low) |
| **SCB NNPA** | 1.0% | 0.8% | 0.6% |
| **NBFC CRAR** | — | 27.6% | 26.6% |
| **NBFC GNPA** | — | 4.6% | 4.0% |
| **NBFC RoA** | — | 2.9% | 3.3% |

### Stress-Test Projections (System-level CRAR)

| Report | Projection year | Baseline | Medium | Severe |
|--------|------------------|----------|--------|--------|
| June 2023 | March 2024 | 16.1% | 14.7% | 13.3% |
| December 2023 | September 2024 | 14.8% | 13.5% | 12.2% |
| June 2024 | March 2025 | 16.1% | 14.4% | 13.0% |

### Key Themes Across the Three Reports
- **Global**: decelerating growth, public debt, geopolitical tensions, slow disinflation, banking-system vulnerabilities
- **Indian**: robust + resilient — sound balance sheets, moderating inflation, improving external trade, fiscal consolidation, sustained credit expansion

### Definitions
- **FSR (Financial Stability Report)** ⭐: Biannual report issued by the RBI presenting the FSDC Sub-committee's consolidated assessment of risks to financial stability and robustness of the financial system. ⭐
- **CRAR (Capital Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio / Capital-to-Risk-Weighted-Assets Ratio)** ⭐: Capital adequacy ratio used to measure a bank's capital relative to its risk-weighted assets.
- **CET1 (Common Equity Tier 1)** ⭐: Highest-quality bank capital component (common equity).
- **GNPA (Gross Non-Performing Assets) ratio** ⭐: Total NPAs as a fraction of total advances. ⭐
- **NNPA (Net Non-Performing Assets) ratio** ⭐: NPAs net of provisions, as a fraction of total advances. ⭐
- **SCB (Scheduled Commercial Banks)**: Banks listed in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act.

## Section: Unit 29 Key Words — Glossary 🔴

### Core Idea
Section 29.8 consolidates the key terms of Unit 29 (Behavioural Finance) into a single glossary. Most terms have already been defined in chunks 009–010; this section is the **canonical exam-ready definitions list**. One additional concept appears here: the **Law of One Price**.

### Key Words (29.8)

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Affect Heuristic** | Helps individuals to make decisions based on how a person feels about it. |
| **Ambiguity Aversion** | Preference for known over unknown risks by an individual. |
| **Anchoring and Adjustment Heuristic** | Heuristic whereby a person is heavily dependent on the information received initially (called the "anchor") and then adjusts it towards the problem while making decisions. |
| **Availability Heuristics** | Assesses the probability of occurrence of the event based on the ease with which it comes to the mind. |
| **Behavioural Finance** | Focuses on cognitive, sociological and psychological influences on the decision-making of investors and financial markets. |
| **Cognitive Dissonance** | Internal tension and anxiety that people experience when they are subjected to conflicting beliefs or experiences opposite to their prior beliefs. |
| **Disposition Effects** | Tendency to prematurely sell assets that have made financial gains while holding on to assets that are losing money. |
| **Efficient Market Hypothesis** | Investment theory arguing markets are efficient, prices reflect the fundamental value of an asset, and thus there is no scope to make excess profits by investing. |
| **Equity Premium Puzzle** | Inability of traditional finance models to explain the average equity risk premium over bonds. |
| **Herding** | Outcome of social learning when people believe others are better informed and thus follow the crowd. |
| **Heuristics** | "Rules of thumb" — imperfect but functional shortcuts that help investors simplify complex situations and make quick decisions. |
| **Loss Aversion** | Investors find losses more painful than the pleasure from gains. Loss aversion makes people prefer things as they are. |
| **Mental Accounting** | Cognitive operations that investors use to classify their money differently in different portfolios based on various subjective criteria. |
| **Predictability Puzzle** | Stock returns are predictable, suggesting persistent profits are not being eroded by arbitrage. |
| **Random Walk** | Past movements/trends of a stock price or market cannot be used to predict its future movement; not possible to outperform the market without assuming additional risk. |
| **Representativeness Heuristic** | In situations of uncertainty, people evaluate the probability of an uncertain event by comparing it to an existing prototype that already exists in our minds. |
| **Sunk Cost Fallacy** | Emotional tendency to invest more money, time, and effort into a project where we have already invested even if a person knows that the investment is going to fail. |
| **Theory of Regret** | When an investment goes bad, people act irrationally by trying to avoid selling it to avoid feelings of regret. |
| **Volatility Puzzle** | High variability of stock prices having large dispersions. |
| **Law of One Price** ⭐ NEW | Identical securities have identical prices because prices are based on fundamental values; the law of one price **limits the opportunity to arbitrage**. ⭐ |

### Definitions
- **Law of One Price** ⭐: Identical securities have identical prices because prices are based on fundamental values; limits arbitrage opportunities. ⭐ (introduced here for first time)

## Section: Answers / Hints to Check Your Progress Exercises 🔴

### Core Idea
Section 29.10 provides exam-ready model answers. **Memorise the multiple-choice answers** — they are commonly tested.

### Check Your Progress 1 — Answers
1. **Q: What is meant by behavioural finance?** → It focuses on cognitive, sociological and psychological influences on the decision-making of investors and financial markets.
2. **Q: Assumptions of traditional finance theory?** → In efficient markets, the price reflects fundamental value and all available information; investors act rationally, have self-control and act in self-interest.
3. **Q: Which is NOT a trait of homo economicus?** — (a) Rationality (b) Self-control (c) **Cognitive bias** ✅ (d) Self-interest
   - **Answer: c** ⭐
4. **Q: Microbehavioural finance deals with:** — (a) **Irrational investor behaviour** ✅ (b) Stock market volatility (c) Bubbles (d) Limited arbitrage
   - **Answer: a** ⭐ (b, c, d are all macrobehavioural)

### Check Your Progress 2 — Answers
1. **Q: Compare and contrast traditional and behavioural finance.** Key contrasts:
   - Traditional = investor rationality + unbiased information processing; Behavioural = bias-influenced decisions
   - Traditional = complete information assumption; Behavioural = bounded rationality
   - Traditional = EMH; Behavioural = anomalies disprove EMH
   - Classical = noise balanced by rational traders; Behavioural = limit to arbitrage; prices not always right
2. **Q: Discuss the growth and evolution of behavioural finance.** → Refer Section 29.4 (1980s emergence; Kahneman-Tversky; 2001-2002 Nobel prizes; mainstream by late-1990s)
3. **Q: Traditional theory assumes investors are ___ whereas behavioural finance considers them ___** — (a) Rational, rational (b) **Rational, irrational** ✅ (c) Irrational, irrational (d) Irrational, rational
   - **Answer: b** ⭐
4. **Q: Which is NOT true about behavioural finance?** — (a) **Assumes complete information available to everyone** ✅ (b) Considers self-control issues (c) Bounded rationality (d) Limit to arbitrage; non-equilibrium prices possible
   - **Answer: a** ⭐ (this is the **traditional** finance assumption, not behavioural)

### Check Your Progress 3 — Answers
1. **Q: List various market anomalies.** → **Fundamental anomalies, Technical anomalies, Calendar/Seasonal anomalies** (refer 29.5.2)
2. **Q: Behavioural explanation of equity premium puzzle?** → **Myopic loss aversion** + **ambiguity aversion** explain the equity premium puzzle.
3. **Q: Volatility puzzle from behavioural perspective?** → Various cognitive, social and emotional biases lead to sub-optimal decision-making by investors, which in turn affects the stock market and stock prices.
4. **Q: Which explains the Equity Premium Puzzle?** — (a) Under-reaction (b) Disposition effect (c) Momentum effect (d) **Myopic loss aversion** ✅
   - **Answer: d** ⭐

### Check Your Progress 4 — Answers
1. **Q: Cognitive influences on an investor?** → **Over-Confidence, Cognitive Dissonance, Under/Over-reaction, Disposition Effects, Diversification Bias, Mental Accounting** (refer 29.6.2)
2. **Q: What is the sunk cost fallacy?** → Emotional tendency to invest more money, time, and effort into a project where we have already invested, even if knowing the investment is going to fail.
3. **Q: Identify the bias/effect** —
   - (a) Investor believes others are better informed and thus follows them → **Herding** ⭐
   - (b) Investor attributes success to ability and failures to external factors → **Self-attribution bias** ⭐
   - (c) Investor sells gains early while holding losers → **Disposition effect** ⭐
   - (d) Investor finds losses disproportionately painful → **Loss aversion** ⭐

### Likely High-Yield Question (Q12 commentary)
**"Behavioural finance is micro-efficient but not macro-inefficient"** means: behavioural finance can effectively explain individual investor behaviour (micro) but is NOT very useful in **predicting the overall market** (macro). It explains anomalies in the **long run**, but cannot predict short-run market variations. This is the key critique of the field — students should be able to defend or critique this position using examples from heuristics (micro success) versus 2008 crisis prediction failures (macro limitation).

## Section: Measures to Tackle NPAs 🔴

### Core Idea
Until the mid-1980s, NPA management was left to banks and auditors. Reforms came in waves: **Narasimhan Committee on the Financial System (1991)**, prudential norms (1992), **Narasimhan Committee on Banking Sector Reforms (1998)**, **90-day NPA classification norm (2001)**, **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (2016)**, **NARCL + IDRCL "bad bank"** mechanism, **Mission Indradhanush (2015)**, and the **2021 SARFAESI amendment** strengthening bank rights over forfeited assets. Banks also use **provisioning** (setting aside profits in anticipation of bad assets), with norms differing for Tier-I vs Tier-II banks.

> **In Simple Terms:** Decades of layered reforms — classify loans by risk, set aside provisions, build legal tools (IBC, SARFAESI), create a "bad bank" (NARCL), merge weak PSBs, and modernise scrutiny.

### Reform Timeline

| Year | Reform | Purpose |
|------|--------|---------|
| **1991** | **Narasimhan Committee on the Financial System** | Suggested classifying advances into 4 groups: standard, sub-standard, doubtful, loss assets |
| **1992** | Prudential norms introduced (income recognition, asset classification, provisioning) | Phased introduction following Narasimhan I |
| **1993** | Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act | Statutory recovery tool |
| **1998** | **Narasimhan Committee on Banking Sector Reforms** | Tightening of prudential standards on par with international best practices |
| **2001** | **90-day NPA classification norm** | Brought NPA guidelines to international standards |
| **2002** | **SARFAESI Act** (Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act) | Statutory tool for asset recovery |
| **2015** | **Mission Indradhanush** | Make PSB working more transparent and professional |
| **2015** | Strategic Debt Restructuring Scheme | Empowered banks to take over distressed companies |
| **2016** | **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)** | Resolves > 50% of NPA matters; sets max 180 days (extendable by 90 days); NCLT operationalised |
| **2021** | **SARFAESI 2002 amendment** | Banks get right of ownership + control + management of forfeited secured assets |
| Later | **NARCL + IDRCL** | "Bad bank" architecture takes over first set of bad loans for resolution |

### Key Mechanisms

#### CRILC (Central Repository of Information on Large Credits)
- Set up to share data for loans **over ₹50 million** with all banks
- CRILC data includes status of each loan: performing, NPA, or heading toward NPA
- Helps banks identify warning signs and segregate habitual offenders

#### IBC, 2016 — Performance
- More than **50% of NPA matters** are being resolved through IBC
- Maximum resolution period: **180 days, extendable by 90 days**
- NCLT pre-admission stage: **4,452 cases disposed of** → creditors obtained dues of **₹2 trillion**

#### NARCL + IDRCL Architecture
- **NARCL (National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited)** + **IDRCL (India Debt Resolution Company Limited)**: take over the first set of bad loans from banks and try to resolve them

#### SARFAESI 2002 Act + 2021 Amendment
- Original: enforcement of security interest
- 2021 amendment: banks get **right of ownership + control + management** of forfeiture-secured assets
- **Asset Reconstruction Companies (ARCs)** assist banks in NPA collection

#### Provisioning for NPAs
- Banks set aside a proportion of profits to meet NPA risk = **provisioning**
- Quarterly provisioning based on NPA categories
- **Tier-I and Tier-II banks have different provisioning norms**
- Helps maintain healthy book of accounts even with bad assets

### Definitions
- **Narasimhan Committee on Financial System (1991)** ⭐: Suggested classifying advances into 4 groups (standard, sub-standard, doubtful, loss). ⭐
- **Narasimhan Committee on Banking Sector Reforms (1998)** ⭐: Recommended further tightening of prudential standards to match international best practices. ⭐
- **90-day NPA Norm (2001)** ⭐: Loan becomes NPA after 90 days of non-repayment — international standard.
- **CRILC (Central Repository of Information on Large Credits)** ⭐: Repository sharing data for loans > ₹50 million with all banks; flags performing / NPA / pre-NPA status; identifies warning signs and habitual offenders.
- **IBC, 2016** ⭐: Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code; max 180 days resolution (extendable 90 days); >50% of NPA matters resolved via IBC; NCLT is the adjudicator.
- **SARFAESI Act, 2002** ⭐: Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act; statutory enforcement; 2021 amendment grants ownership + control + management rights to banks over secured assets.
- **Asset Reconstruction Companies (ARCs)**: Specialised institutions assisting banks in NPA collection.
- **NARCL + IDRCL** ⭐: India's "bad bank" pair — NARCL acquires NPAs; IDRCL resolves them.
- **Mission Indradhanush (2015)** ⭐: Government initiative to make PSB working more transparent and professional to curb future NPAs.
- **Provisioning for NPAs** ⭐: Banks setting aside profits as buffer against probable NPA losses; differs between Tier-I and Tier-II banks. ⭐

### Three Statutory Recovery Tools (in addition to other measures)
1. **Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993**
2. **SARFAESI Act, 2002**
3. **IBC, 2016**

## Section: Effectiveness of Action Taken to Curb NPAs 🟡

### Core Idea
Despite RBI efforts and Basel-committee recommendations, NPAs persist as a problem. The **Banking Stability Indicator (BSI)** — produced by the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) — gives an overall assessment of banking-stability conditions and risk factors. Reform pressure has improved bank performance, but room remains. The **August 2019 PSB merger** (10 PSBs into 4) reduced PSBs from 27 to 12, expected to improve profitability and reduce gross NPAs.

### What Banks Should Do
- Pre- and post-sanction precautions for credit assessments
- Check loan procedures and securities hypothecated
- Critically analyse NPA provisions, interest calculations, repayment schedules, and audit
- Periodic review of credit-card-holder accounts

### Areas for Further Action (Palli, 2020)
1. Records of borrowers should be available to banks for opinion-formation and financial background checks
2. Periodic credit-team training to update skills, industry benchmarks, and credit-evaluation sheets
3. Use of **data analytics** for effective periodic reviews and audits
4. Use of **third-party data sources** in PSBs for due diligence at sanction stage to mitigate misrepresentation/fraud risk

### Definitions
- **Bank for International Settlements (BIS)** ⭐: International financial institution owned by central banks; "fosters international monetary and financial cooperation and serves as a bank for central banks." ⭐
- **Banking Stability Indicator (BSI)**: BIS's overall assessment of banking-sector stability based on changes in conditions and risk factors.

### Key Reform Outcome
- **August 2019 PSB merger**: 10 PSBs merged into 4 → PSB count reduced from 27 to 12 → expected to improve profitability and reduce total gross NPAs

## Section: Recent Policy Measures Towards NPAs 🔴

### Core Idea
Three recent policy directions: (1) adoption of **Expected Credit Loss (ECL)** methodology aligned with **IFRS 9** for forward-looking, life-of-instrument provisioning; (2) RBI's **March 2023 nudge** for extra provisions against exposure to top-20 business houses; (3) **Supreme Court ruling (March 27, 2023)** in *State Bank of India vs Rajesh Agarwal* affirming borrowers' right to a fair hearing before a loan is classified as fraudulent.

### Three Policy Measures

#### 1. Expected Credit Loss (ECL) Methodology + IFRS 9
- **IFRS 9 standard** is the global prudent norm — proactive provisioning even for **standard assets** (probable losses)
- ECL takes into account historical probability of default + current and future economic scenarios/predictions
- Inclines banks to manage and dispose of bad loans **more actively**
- Tracks **significant increase in credit risk** as a novel addition
- Result: better monitoring of loan slippage, rating-movement tracking
- (Bandyopadhyay, 2023)

#### 2. RBI Nudge on Top-20 Business Houses (March 2023)
- RBI urged banks to make **extra provisions** against exposure to top-20 business houses (which face higher external risks)
- Nudge is toward **buffer-building**; banks decide their own provisioning amounts
- Prudential provisioning shows up in banks' provisioning buffer
- (The Hindu Business Line, March 27, 2023)

#### 3. Supreme Court Ruling — SBI vs Rajesh Agarwal (March 27, 2023)
- Affirms borrower's **right to a fair hearing** before NPA account is declared fraud
- Principles of **natural justice** must be applied — borrower must be given a hearing before loan is classified fraudulent
- Decision must be by **reasoned order** given serious civil consequences
- Effect: may lead to **fewer fraud cases** registered but **more accountability and punishment** in registered cases

### Definitions
- **ECL (Expected Credit Loss) Approach** ⭐: Estimation of credit losses through the life of a financial instrument and the present value of those credit losses as of the reporting date; built into IFRS 9. ⭐
- **IFRS 9 Standard** ⭐: Specifies how an entity should classify and measure financial assets, financial liabilities, and certain contracts to buy or sell non-financial items; includes ECL for forward-looking provisioning. ⭐
- **Significant Increase in Credit Risk**: Novel ECL-methodology trigger that demands heightened provisioning before default occurs.
- **Natural Justice (in NPA classification)**: Principle from SBI vs Rajesh Agarwal (2023) — borrower entitled to fair hearing before fraud classification.

## Section: Unit 27 Summary 🟢

### Core Idea
NPAs are loans/advances on which both principal and interest are unpaid for a specified period. Recorded on the balance sheet after prolonged non-payment. Classified as sub-standard, doubtful, or loss based on overdue period and recovery probability. Causes: macroeconomic conditions, sectoral issues, global business environment, delayed stress recognition, aggressive lending in upturns, improper risk pricing, poor credit underwriting, lethargic due diligence, exogenous shocks. Most NPAs arose from defaults by private-sector non-financial firms. Government and RBI continue issuing guidelines for resolution and timely identification. Banks faced significant rising-NPA challenge in **2011**, peaking at **11.18% in fiscal 2018**.

### Definitions (Unit 27 Keywords)
- **BIS**: International financial institution owned by central banks; fosters monetary/financial cooperation; bank for central banks.
- **ECL Approach** ⭐: Lifetime credit-loss estimation, present-valued at reporting date.
- **Fraud**: Deceitful acts; bank fraud is a purposeful act/omission in a banking transaction or the bank's books; a false statement made knowingly.
- **IFRS 9 Standard**: Classification and measurement of financial assets/liabilities and certain non-financial contracts.
- **Twin Balance Sheet (TBS) Problem** ⭐: Two balance-sheet problems — Indian companies (overleveraged) + Indian banks (bad-loan-encumbered). Companies over-expand during boom → default → impaired bank balance sheets → devastating for growth.
- **Wilful Defaulter** ⭐: Per RBI, a unit which defaulted in repayment **even though it could have repaid**.
- **Zombie Lending** ⭐: Extension of new credit or prolonging of existing loans to **low-productivity firms**. May stabilise short-run, but risks turning transitory shocks into delayed recoveries and permanent productivity/output losses. Refers to extending credit to entities that cannot repay. ⭐

### Definitions (Unit 27 — extra terms)
- **Mission Indradhanush (2015)**: Government scheme for transparency and professionalism in PSBs.
- **NCLT (National Company Law Tribunal)**: Adjudicator for IBC cases.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: "Wilful default = inability to pay" → ✅ Correct: Wilful default is when the borrower **could** repay but **chose not to**.
- ❌ Mistake: "Zombie lending helps the economy by keeping firms alive" → ✅ Correct: Zombie lending may stabilise short-term but transforms transitory shocks into permanent productivity/output losses.

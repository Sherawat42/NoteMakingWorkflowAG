## Section: Unit 5 — Summary & Key Words 🟢
<!-- Reason: recap content; not new theory but consolidation -->

### Core Idea
Money demand is the desire to hold cash and liquid assets for transaction, precautionary, and speculative purposes. It is shaped by interest rate, income, price level, risk and liquidity of alternatives, and payment technology. Six theories — Fisher's Quantity Theory, Cambridge, Keynes' Liquidity Preference, Baumol-Tobin, Tobin's Speculative Demand, and Friedman's Modern Quantity Theory — each model these forces differently.

### Key Concepts

#### Recap — Factors Affecting Money Demand
- **Interest rate**: ↑ i → ↓ Md (opportunity cost of holding money).
- **Income / GDP**: ↑ Y → ↑ Md (more transactions).
- **Price level**: ↑ P → ↑ nominal Md.
- **Risk on alternatives**: ↑ risk → ↑ Md (flight to safety).
- **Payment technology** (Paytm, UPI, etc.): ↑ digital ease → ↓ physical-cash demand.

#### Recap — Six Theories at a Glance

| Theory | Core equation/idea | Key contribution |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Fisher's Quantity Theory** | MV = PT | Direct link between money and prices; theory of supply with derivable demand |
| **Cambridge** | Md = k(PY) | Models money demand directly as fraction of nominal income |
| **Keynes' Liquidity Preference** | Md = f(Y, i) via three motives | Speculative motive introduces interest-rate elasticity |
| **Baumol-Tobin** | A = √(bY/2i) | Optimum cash-holding from EOQ-style cost minimization |
| **Tobin's Speculative Demand** | Portfolio of cash + bonds | Negative i ↔ Md, positive i ↔ bond demand; mix vs corner solution |
| **Friedman's Modern Quantity Theory** | Md = f(Y_p, W, i_m, i_b, i_e, π^e, u)P | Money as just another asset |

### Definitions (Unit 5 Key Words)
- **Bullish nature**: Optimistic outlook on future asset/market performance — expects rising prices.
- **Bearish nature**: Pessimistic outlook on future performance — expects declining prices.
- **Bond**: Debt investment — investor loans money to a corporation/government for a defined period at a variable or fixed interest rate.
- **Capital Gain**: Profit from sale of an asset (stocks, real estate, bonds) above purchase price. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Transactive Motive for Money Demand**: Need for money to facilitate day-to-day transactions (buying goods/services). ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Precautionary Motive for Money Demand**: Holding money as a buffer/reserve for unexpected future expenses or emergencies. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Speculative Motive for Money Demand**: Holding money to seize favourable market/investment opportunities. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Velocity of Money**: Rate at which money is exchanged in an economy — number of times money is spent in a given period; high velocity = rapid circulation. ⭐ (exam-important)

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Md drivers: i (−), Y (+), P (+), risk on alts (+), payment tech (−).
> - 6 theories: Fisher, Cambridge, Keynes (3 motives), Baumol-Tobin, Tobin, Friedman.
> - Velocity = number of times money changes hands per period.

### Core Idea
Unit 6 turns from money **demand** to money **supply**. Until now, money supply was implicitly assumed to be policy-determined; in reality, supply is **jointly determined** by the monetary authority, banks, and (indirectly) the public. The unit focuses on **high-powered money (H)** — money created by the monetary authority — and how it fans out into the broader money supply through the **money multiplier**.

> **In Simple Terms:** Block 2 has shown how much money people *want* (demand). Unit 6 shows how that money actually gets created — and the central bank, banks, and public all play a role.

## Section: High-Powered Money (H) and the Money Supply Equation 🔴
<!-- Reason: core derivation, named formula, exam-eligible CYP -->

### Core Idea
**High-powered money H** (= **monetary base**) is the total currency in circulation **plus reserves held by the central bank**. Through the **money multiplier mechanism**, every rupee of H supports a multiple expansion of the broader money supply M. The relationship is M = m × H, where the multiplier m depends on the **currency-to-deposit ratio (c_d)**, **required reserve ratio (rr)**, and **excess reserves ratio (er)**.

> **In Simple Terms:** Think of high-powered money as fertilizer the central bank pours into the soil. The money multiplier is how many plants grow from each scoop — depending on how much banks lend (rr, er) and how much cash people prefer to hold (c_d).

### Key Concepts

#### Foundational Equations

| # | Equation | Meaning |
|---|----------|---------|
| (1) | **M = C + D** | Money supply = Currency + Demand Deposits |
| (2) | **H = C + R** | High-powered money = Currency + Reserves |
| (3) | H/MB = C + (RR + ER) | Monetary base decomposed into currency + required + excess reserves |
| (4) | c_d = C/D, so C = c_d · D | Currency-to-deposit ratio |
| (5) | rr = RR/D, so RR = rr · D | Required-reserve-to-deposit ratio |
| (6) | er = ER/D, so ER = er · D | Excess-reserves-to-deposit ratio |
| (7) | **M = m · H**, so m = M/H | Money multiplier definition |

#### Variable Glossary (Unit 6)

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| M | Money supply |
| H / MB | High-powered money / Monetary Base |
| C | Currency |
| D | Demand Deposit |
| R | Reserves (= RR + ER) |
| RR | Required Reserves |
| ER | Excess Reserves |
| c_d | Currency-to-deposit ratio (C/D) |
| rr | Required reserve ratio (RR/D) |
| er | Excess reserves ratio (ER/D) |
| m | Money multiplier |

#### Deriving the Money Multiplier
$$m = \frac{C + D}{C + (RR + ER)}$$
Dividing numerator and denominator by D:
$$m = \frac{C/D + D/D}{C/D + RR/D + ER/D} = \frac{c_d + 1}{c_d + rr + er}$$

$$\boxed{m = \frac{c_d + 1}{c_d + rr + er}}$$

Since m = M/H:
$$\frac{M}{H} = \frac{c_d + 1}{c_d + rr + er}$$

#### Special Cases of the Multiplier

| Case | Multiplier |
|------|-----------|
| If **c_d = 0** | m = 1 / (rr + er) |
| If **c_d = 0 and er = 0** | m = **1/rr** (simple deposit multiplier) |

#### Components of High-Powered Money
1. **Currency in Circulation**: All physical currency (notes + coins) circulating among individuals, businesses, and banks.
2. **Reserves Held by the Central Bank**: Deposits that commercial banks hold at the central bank — mandatory; serve as buffer against crises and liquidity shortages.
   - **Required Reserves (RR)**: Mandated fraction of deposits banks must hold (as vault cash or central-bank deposits).
   - **Excess Reserves (ER)**: Reserves above the required amount; held for precaution, withdrawal demand, or compliance.

#### Implications for Money Supply

| Tool | Action | Effect on Reserves | Effect on Money Supply |
|------|--------|--------------------|--------------------------|
| **Reserve requirements (rr)** | Lower | ↑ Excess reserves → more lending capacity | ↑ Money supply |
| Reserve requirements (rr) | Raise | ↓ Excess reserves → less lending capacity | ↓ Money supply |
| **Open Market Operations** | Buy government securities | ↑ Bank reserves | ↑ Money supply |
| Open Market Operations | Sell government securities | ↓ Bank reserves | ↓ Money supply |
| **Discount Rate** | Lower | Cheaper borrowing → more lending | ↑ Money supply |
| Discount Rate | Raise | Costlier borrowing → less lending | ↓ Money supply |

### Definitions
- **High-Powered Money (H)**: Total currency in circulation plus reserves held by the central bank; foundation for broader money supply. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Money Multiplier (m)**: Ratio of change in money supply to change in monetary base; m = (c_d + 1)/(c_d + rr + er). ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Required Reserves (RR)**: Mandated fraction of deposits banks must hold. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Excess Reserves (ER)**: Reserves held by banks above the required amount.
- **Currency-to-Deposit Ratio (c_d)**: C/D — public's preference for currency vs deposits.
- **Open Market Operations**: Central bank's buying/selling of government securities to influence reserves.
- **Discount Rate**: Interest rate at which commercial banks borrow from the central bank.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Treating "monetary base" and "high-powered money" as different things → ✅ Correct: They are **synonyms**.
- ❌ Mistake: Forgetting to add 1 in the numerator of m → ✅ Correct: **m = (c_d + 1)/(c_d + rr + er)** — the +1 represents the "D/D" term from deposits.
- ❌ Mistake: Saying "M0 = M1" → ✅ Correct: M0 (= H) excludes most demand deposits of the public; M1 includes them.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - **M = C + D**, **H = C + R**, **R = RR + ER**.
> - **m = (c_d + 1)/(c_d + rr + er)**; simple deposit multiplier (c_d = er = 0): **m = 1/rr**.
> - **M = m × H**.
> - 3 tools to control money supply: rr, OMO, discount rate.
> - H components: Currency in circulation + Reserves (Required + Excess).

## Section: The Money Multiplier Process — Mechanism Setup 🔴
<!-- Reason: full process derivation; CYP question; exam-favourite -->
<!-- Continues in chunk 005 -->

### Core Idea
The money-multiplier process explains how an initial injection of base money (high-powered money) **multiplies** through the banking system to expand the broader money supply. The text further decomposes the multiplier into a **complete deposit multiplier** and a **complete currency multiplier**.

> **In Simple Terms:** When the central bank releases ₹100 of base money into a bank, the bank lends most of it, the borrower deposits it elsewhere, that bank lends most of *that*, and so on — turning ₹100 into many times more deposits.

### Key Concepts

#### Complete Deposit Multiplier
From equation (3): H/MB = C + (RR + ER), so:
$$MB = c_d D + rr \cdot D + er \cdot D = D(c_d + rr + er)$$
$$D = \frac{MB}{c_d + rr + er}$$

Therefore, the **complete deposit multiplier** is:
$$\boxed{\frac{1}{c_d + rr + er}}$$

If **c_d = 0 and er = 0** → **simple deposit multiplier = 1/rr**.

#### Complete Currency Multiplier
From c_d = C/D and D = MB/(c_d + rr + er):
$$C = c_d \cdot D = c_d \cdot \frac{MB}{c_d + rr + er} = \left[\frac{c_d}{c_d + rr + er}\right] \cdot MB$$

Therefore, the **complete currency multiplier** is:
$$\boxed{\frac{c_d}{c_d + rr + er}}$$

#### Multiplier Definitions Summary

| Multiplier | Formula | Interpretation |
|-----------|---------|----------------|
| **Money multiplier (m)** | (c_d + 1) / (c_d + rr + er) | M / H |
| **Complete deposit multiplier** | 1 / (c_d + rr + er) | D / MB |
| **Simple deposit multiplier** | 1 / rr | D / MB when c_d = er = 0 |
| **Complete currency multiplier** | c_d / (c_d + rr + er) | C / MB |

### Definitions
- **Complete Deposit Multiplier**: D/MB = 1/(c_d + rr + er). ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Simple Deposit Multiplier**: 1/rr (when public holds no currency and banks hold no excess reserves). ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Complete Currency Multiplier**: C/MB = c_d / (c_d + rr + er).

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Deposit multiplier = 1/(c_d + rr + er).
> - Simple deposit multiplier = 1/rr.
> - Currency multiplier = c_d / (c_d + rr + er).
> - Sum check: Deposit multiplier × (1) + Currency multiplier = (c_d + 1)/(c_d + rr + er) = m ✓.

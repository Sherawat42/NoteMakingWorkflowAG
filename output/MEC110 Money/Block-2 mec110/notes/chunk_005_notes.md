## Section: Money Multiplier Process — Full Mechanism 🔴
<!-- See chunk 004 for start of this section -->
<!-- Reason: 5-step mechanism, CYP-eligible, exam high-yield -->

### Core Idea
The money-multiplier process unfolds in **five steps**: (1) initial injection of base money by the central bank, (2) reserve requirement constrains how much banks can lend, (3) lending creates new deposits, (4) deposits get re-deposited and re-lent, triggering the multiplier effect, and (5) leakages (excess reserves, currency drain, non-bank intermediaries) dampen the expansion.

> **In Simple Terms:** It's like a chain of dominoes — the central bank tips the first one (puts in fresh money), each bank passes most of it on as a loan, and the chain creates several rupees of money for every rupee originally injected. But some dominoes get stuck (excess reserves) or fall sideways (cash held outside banks), which shortens the chain.

### Key Concepts

#### Five-Step Mechanism

1. **Initial Injection of Base Money**
   - Central bank injects base money via open market operations, **discount window lending**, or other tools.

2. **Reserve Requirement**
   - Banks must hold a percentage of their deposits as reserves (vault cash or central-bank deposits).
   - This **constrains** the amount of money banks can create through lending.

3. **Deposit Creation through Lending**
   - When banks receive additional reserves (from central-bank injections or deposit inflows), they expand lending.
   - **Loans create new deposits**, increasing the money supply **beyond the initial injection**.

4. **Multiplier Effect**
   - Newly created deposits become part of the broader money supply.
   - These deposits get **re-deposited or spent** → further rounds of lending and deposit creation.

5. **Limitations and Leakages**
   - **Excess reserve holdings** by banks dampen the chain.
   - **Currency drain** — public withdrawing cash — reduces the deposit base.
   - **Non-bank financial intermediaries** outside reserve requirements weaken the link.

#### Determinants of the Money Multiplier — Quick View

| Determinant | Effect on Multiplier |
|-------------|----------------------|
| **Reserve ratio (rr)** ↓ | Multiplier ↑ (banks can lend more) |
| **Currency drain (c_d)** ↑ | Multiplier ↓ (less deposit base) |
| **Excess reserves (er)** ↑ | Multiplier ↓ (banks hoard) |

#### Implications for Monetary Policy
By adjusting the **monetary base** through OMO, **discount rate** changes, or **reserve requirement** adjustments, central banks influence money supply → regulate economic activity, inflation, and interest rates.

### Definitions
- **Money Multiplier Process**: Mechanism by which initial base-money injection leads to multiple expansion of money supply via bank lending and deposit creation. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Currency Drain**: Public's preference to hold currency outside banks, reducing the deposit base on which banks can create loans.
- **Discount Window Lending**: Central-bank lending facility through which commercial banks borrow at the discount rate.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Believing the money supply expands without limit → ✅ Correct: It's bounded by **rr, er, and c_d** through the multiplier formula.
- ❌ Mistake: Thinking only the central bank determines money supply → ✅ Correct: **Public** (via c_d) and **banks** (via er) jointly affect supply.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - 5 steps: **Inject → Reserve constraint → Lending creates deposits → Multiplier effect → Leakages**.
> - Multiplier ↓ when rr ↑, c_d ↑, or er ↑.
> - Three policy tools: rr, OMO, discount rate.

## Section: Factors Affecting the Money Multiplier and High-Powered Money 🔴
<!-- Reason: itemized factor lists; direct CYP source; exam-eligible -->

### Core Idea
The money supply is shaped by two distinct sets of factors: those that affect the **multiplier** (mostly bank and public behaviour) and those that affect **high-powered money** (mostly central-bank and government action). Both must be understood to manage monetary policy effectively.

> **In Simple Terms:** Think of money supply as a recipe: H is the ingredient amount the central bank puts in, and m is how the cooks (banks) and diners (public) use that ingredient.

### Key Concepts

#### Factors Affecting the Money Multiplier

| Factor | Effect | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| **1. Reserve Requirements (rr)** | ↓ rr → ↑ m | Lower required reserves let banks lend more deposits |
| **2. Currency Drain (c_d)** | ↑ c_d → ↓ m | Cash held by public doesn't create deposits |
| **3. Banking Behaviour** | Cautious banks (↑ er) → ↓ m; Aggressive lenders → ↑ m | Excess reserves hoarded reduce multiplier |
| **4. Central Bank Policy** | OMO purchases ↑ reserves → can ↑ m | Reserve injections support more lending |
| **5. Deposit Leakage** | ↑ leakage → ↓ m | Excess reserves, cash withdrawals, asset purchases all leak deposits out of the system |

#### Factors Affecting High-Powered Money

| Factor | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Central Bank Operations** | Primary driver — controls H via OMO, reserve requirements, lending facilities |
| **2. Government Transactions** | Fiscal-policy decisions affecting government spending or taxation; e.g., expenditures financed by central bank ↑ H |
| **3. Foreign Exchange Operations** | Central-bank purchases/sales of foreign currency reserves change H, especially under fixed/managed exchange-rate regimes |
| **4. Financial Crises** | Unconventional tools (e.g., **quantitative easing** = large-scale asset purchases) ↑ H |
| **5. Banking Sector Dynamics** | Bank consolidation or shifts in reserve composition can affect H |

### Definitions
- **Quantitative Easing (QE)**: Unconventional monetary policy involving large-scale purchases of financial assets to expand the monetary base, especially during financial crises. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Currency Drain**: Public's preference to hold cash rather than deposits, lowering the multiplier.
- **Deposit Leakage**: Movement of deposits out of the banking system (excess reserves, currency withdrawals, off-banking purchases) that dampens multiplier effects.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - 5 multiplier factors: rr, c_d, banking behaviour (er), central-bank policy, deposit leakage.
> - 5 H factors: CB ops, govt transactions, FX ops, financial crises (QE), banking dynamics.
> - QE = unconventional ↑ H during crises.

## Section: Theory of Endogenous Money Supply 🔴
<!-- Reason: named theory, contrast with traditional/exogenous view, CYP target -->

### Core Idea
The traditional **exogenous** view says the central bank controls money supply directly. The **endogenous money supply theory** challenges this: money supply is largely **determined by the demand for credit** from borrowers and the **lending capacity of banks**. Banks **create new money when they make loans** — they don't just intermediate pre-existing funds. The central bank's influence becomes **indirect** through interest rates and reserve operations that affect credit demand.

> **In Simple Terms:** The traditional view says the central bank pours water (money) into a tank. The endogenous view says banks have a magic tap — they create water whenever a borrower asks for a loan and meets credit standards. The central bank can only nudge the temperature of the tap.

### Key Concepts

#### Five Pillars of Endogenous Money Supply

1. **Credit Creation by Banks**
   - When banks lend, they **simultaneously create new deposits** in borrowers' accounts.
   - These deposits function as money → ↑ overall money supply.

2. **Loanable Funds vs Money Supply**
   - **Loanable funds theory**: banks intermediate **pre-existing** deposits.
   - **Endogenous theory**: banks **create new money** when they lend.
   - Money supply is **not limited by pre-existing funds** — it's determined by **credit demand** and bank **lending capacity**.

3. **Reserves and Central Bank Influence (Indirect)**
   - Central bank still affects money supply via the monetary base.
   - But influence is **indirect**: through interest rates and OMO that affect borrowing cost and banks' willingness to lend.

4. **Creditworthiness and Economic Conditions**
   - Bank credit-creation capacity depends on **borrower creditworthiness** and prevailing economic conditions.
   - **Boom**: banks willing to lend → ↑ Ms.
   - **Downturn / crisis**: banks cautious → contraction in credit and Ms.

5. **Liquidity Preference and Monetary Policy Transmission**
   - Liquidity preference and interest-rate expectations shape how policy reaches the economy.
   - Changes in central-bank policy rates → cost of borrowing → demand for credit → Ms.

#### Exogenous vs Endogenous Money Supply Curves (Figure 6.1)

| Aspect | Exogenous | Endogenous |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| Shape | **Vertical** (interest rate has no effect on Ms) | **Upward sloping** (positive i → Ms relation) |
| Determinants of position | MB, rr, er, c_d (all exogenous) | MB, rr exogenous; **er and c_d depend on i** |
| Shift drivers | ↓ MB, ↑ er, ↑ c_d, ↑ rr → leftward shift | ↑ MB, ↑ rr → rightward shift |
| Underlying logic | Money supply set by central bank only | Money supply responds to credit demand and bank decisions |

#### Why the Endogenous Curve Slopes Up
- ↑ interest rate → commercial banks make more loans → ↑ money supply (positive relation).
- ↑ interest rate → customers prefer deposits over cash → c_d ↓ → more loanable funds → ↑ Ms.
- Therefore er and c_d **respond to** interest rates → upward-sloping Ms curve.

#### Money Supply Equation (Reminder from Chunk 004)
$$\text{Money supply} = \frac{c_d + 1}{c_d + rr + er} \times MB$$

In the **exogenous** view, MB and rr are central-bank set; er and c_d are interest-rate-independent.
In the **endogenous** view, **er and c_d depend on the interest rate**.

#### Three Implications

1. **Monetary Policy Effectiveness**: Depends on responsiveness of banks/borrowers to policy changes — not just on monetary base.
2. **Financial Stability Considerations**: Bank capital adequacy and systemic risk management are essential for the endogenous mechanism to work smoothly.
3. **Role of Central Banks**: Still significant, but **understanding bank/borrower behaviour is essential**.

### Definitions
- **Endogenous Money Supply Theory**: Theory that money supply is determined by the demand for credit and the lending capacity of banks, with central-bank actions only indirectly influencing it through interest rates. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Exogenous (in money-supply context)**: Determined externally — i.e., set by the central bank rather than by market forces.
- **Loanable Funds Theory**: Banks act as intermediaries lending out pre-existing deposits — contrasts with endogenous money supply theory.
- **Liquidity Preference**: Desire of individuals/businesses to hold assets in liquid form (cash, demand deposits) rather than illiquid forms (physical assets, long-term investments).

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Saying endogenous theory means "central bank has no role" → ✅ Correct: It says the role is **indirect** — through rates affecting credit demand.
- ❌ Mistake: Confusing loanable-funds with endogenous theory → ✅ Correct: Loanable funds = banks **intermediate**; endogenous = banks **create** money via lending.
- ❌ Mistake: Drawing exogenous Ms curve as upward-sloping → ✅ Correct: Exogenous = **vertical**; endogenous = **upward-sloping**.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Endogenous: Ms determined by credit demand + bank lending capacity.
> - Banks create deposits when they lend (not just intermediate).
> - Exogenous Ms = vertical; Endogenous Ms = upward-sloping.
> - In endogenous view: er, c_d depend on i.
> - Central bank's role becomes **indirect**.

## Section: Unit 6 Summary & Key Words 🟢
<!-- Reason: consolidation, Section 6.6/6.7 -->

### Core Idea
**High-powered money** is the foundation of the monetary system. Central banks influence Ms through OMO, reserve-requirement changes, and discount-rate adjustments. The **money multiplier** amplifies base money via bank lending and deposit creation, subject to reserve requirements, currency drain, and excess reserves. The **endogenous money supply theory** complements the traditional view by emphasizing the role of credit demand and bank lending behaviour.

### Definitions (Unit 6 Key Words)
- **High-Powered Money**: Total currency in circulation and reserves held by the central bank — foundation of broader money supply. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Money Multiplier**: Ratio representing the change in money supply relative to the change in monetary base. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Exogenous**: External / outside influence; in money-supply context, factors determined externally (e.g., by central banks).
- **Endogenous Money Supply**: Theory holding that Ms is determined largely by credit demand and bank lending capacity, not central-bank actions alone. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Monetary Base**: Synonym for high-powered money — currency in circulation + reserves at central bank. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Open Market Operations**: Central-bank buying or selling of government securities to influence reserves. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Reserve Requirements**: Mandated reserves commercial banks must hold; affect lending capacity and money supply. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Liquidity Preference**: Desire to hold assets in liquid form (cash, demand deposits) rather than illiquid forms.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - **M = m × H**, m = (c_d + 1)/(c_d + rr + er).
> - **Three CB tools**: OMO, reserve requirements, discount rate.
> - Multiplier limits: rr, c_d, er.
> - Exogenous (vertical Ms) vs Endogenous (upward-sloping Ms).
> - Endogenous view: banks create money via lending.

### Quick Answers (from Section 6.9)

**CYP 1**
1. Two main components of high-powered money: **currency and reserves**.

**CYP 2**
1. Reserve requirement acts as a **constraint** on the amount of money banks can create through lending.

**CYP 3**
1. Lower reserve requirement → ↑ money multiplier (banks lend more deposits) → ↑ Ms.
2. Higher currency holdings by public → ↓ multiplier (cash held by public doesn't create new deposits).
3. Government transactions (fiscal-policy spending/taxation) impact high-powered money.

**CYP 4**
- Endogenous theory differs from traditional: central-bank influence is **indirect** — via rate-setting and OMO that affect cost of borrowing and banks' willingness to extend credit, thereby influencing money supply.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - H = Currency + Reserves.
> - OMO is the principal CB tool to alter reserves.
> - ↓ rr → ↑ multiplier.
> - ↑ c_d → ↓ multiplier.

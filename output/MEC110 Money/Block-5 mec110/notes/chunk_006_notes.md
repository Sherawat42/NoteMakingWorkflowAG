## Section: Unit 16 Objectives & Introduction 🟢

### Core Idea
SEBI is India's capital-market regulator, established in **1988** as a non-statutory body and given autonomous statutory powers in **1992** through the SEBI Act. Its role was deliberately defined to serve **three constituents** of the market — **issuers, investors, and intermediaries** — and to bring orderly functioning, especially after India's 1980s securities frauds. The head office is in **Mumbai**.

> **In Simple Terms:** SEBI is like the umpire of India's stock-and-securities playground — it sets the rules, watches the players, and acts when someone cheats, with the explicit job of making investors feel safe.

### Key Concepts

#### Why a Capital Market Regulator?
India's approach to market regulation has evolved with systemic imperatives and institutional prudence at its centre. After securities frauds, there was urgency to set up a capital-market regulator. SEBI was officially established in **1988** and given statutory powers in **1992** by the SEBI Act passed by Parliament. The role was defined to be responsive to issuers, investors and intermediaries. SEBI is also a **quasi-legislative and quasi-judicial** body that creates rules, conducts investigations, issues decisions and levies penalties; it regulates the stock market.

### Definitions
- **SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India)**: India's capital-market regulator — established 1988, statutory from 1992, headquartered in Mumbai. ⭐

### Mechanisms / Processes
1. 1988: SEBI constituted as non-statutory regulator.
2. 1992: SEBI Act passed → autonomous statutory body.
3. SEBI registers and regulates stockbrokers, sub-brokers, portfolio managers, investment advisers, share-transfer agents, bankers, merchant bankers, trustees, registrars, underwriters, etc.

### Examples
**Example: Three constituents framing**
A company issuing IPO shares (issuer), a retail buyer (investor), and a merchant banker arranging the issue (intermediary) — SEBI's mandate covers all three legs simultaneously.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: SEBI was a statutory body from 1988 → ✅ Correct: It was non-statutory until the **1992 Act**.
- ❌ Mistake: SEBI HQ is in Delhi → ✅ Correct: Mumbai (specifically Bandra Kurla Complex).

### Edge Cases & Caveats
- Foreign institutional investors brought a more robust activism culture; shareholder activism in India still has distance to go.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - SEBI: founded **1988**, statutory **1992**, HQ **Mumbai**.
> - Three market constituents: **Issuers, Investors, Intermediaries**.

## Section: Concept and Act of SEBI 🔴

### Core Idea
Before SEBI, the **Controller of Capital Issues (CCI)** regulated capital markets under the **Capital Issue (Contract) Act, 1947**. With CCI's abolition, companies became free to access capital markets without prior government permission, subject to SEBI clearance of offer documents. SEBI was set up as a **statutory entity by ordinance on January 30, 1992**; the ordinance was passed by both Houses of Parliament on **April 1, 1992**, deemed effective from the ordinance date. A 1995 amendment gave SEBI additional statutory power. SEBI was **modelled on the US SEC**, established under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934.

> **In Simple Terms:** Pre-SEBI, the government's CCI gave permission for every share issue and even fixed prices. With CCI gone and SEBI in place, companies set their own price and SEBI checks the disclosures — like switching from a price-control office to a transparency referee.

### Key Concepts

#### From CCI to SEBI
The Controller of Capital Issues (CCI) was the predecessor regulator under the Capital Issue (Contract) Act, 1947. When CCI was abolished:
- Companies no longer needed prior government permission to access capital markets.
- They became free to approach markets subject to SEBI clearing offer documents.
- Controls over price and premium fixation were removed; most issuing companies are now free to fix issue prices for public and rights issues.
SEBI is a body with separate legal existence and **perpetual succession**. It was modelled on the **US SEC** (Securities Exchange Act, 1934).

#### SEBI Act 1992 — Six Areas Covered
The SEBI Act 1992 covers:
1. **Composition** and actions of the SEBI Board members.
2. **Powers and functions** of the Board.
3. **Fund sources** of SEBI (grants by the Union Government).
4. Rules on **penalties and legal pathways**.
5. Definition of the **judicial authority** of SEBI.
6. Extent of powers of the **Union Government to supersede SEBI**.

#### SEBI Guidelines — Six Areas
SEBI also adheres to a list of guidelines covering:
1. **Employee Stock Option Schemes (ESOPs)**.
2. **Disclosure and Investor Protection** norms.
3. **Legal Proceedings**.
4. **Anti-money laundering** norms.
5. **Listing and delisting** of securities.
6. Opening of **trading terminals overseas**.

#### LODR Regulations 2015 — Seven Entailments
The SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015 consolidated previous amendments into a single document uniform across capital-market segments. It entails:
1. Disclosures and obligations to be acknowledged by **compliance officers** of listed companies.
2. **Uniform listing obligations** for all listed companies.
3. **Distinct obligations** for certain types of securities.
4. Segregating **initial issuance** and **post-IPO** norms.
5. Communication of companies' **fundraising activities**.
6. Establishing **timelines** for notifying exchanges of certain events.
7. Bringing **MSMEs** under the ambit of LODR Regulations.

### Definitions
- **CCI (Controller of Capital Issues)**: SEBI's pre-1992 predecessor regulator under the Capital Issue (Contract) Act, 1947. ⭐
- **SEBI Act 1992**: Enabling statute that made SEBI a statutory body — ordinance Jan 30, 1992; passed April 1, 1992; 1995 amendment expanded powers. ⭐
- **LODR Regulations 2015**: Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements — consolidated, uniform listing-disclosure framework. ⭐
- **SEC (US)**: Securities and Exchange Commission, established under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 — the model SEBI was based on.
- **Perpetual Succession**: Continuous legal existence of an entity independent of its members.

### Mechanisms / Processes
1. **Pre-1988**: CCI regulates capital issues; price/premium controlled.
2. **1988**: SEBI established (non-statutory).
3. **30 Jan 1992**: SEBI gets statutory status by ordinance.
4. **1 Apr 1992**: Both Houses pass the SEBI Act (deemed effective from ordinance date).
5. **1995**: Amendment grants additional statutory powers; AMFI established same year (see MF section).
6. **2015**: LODR Regulations consolidate prior listing rules.

### Examples
**Example: Free pricing post-CCI**
After CCI was abolished, a company planning an IPO can fix its own issue price — instead of seeking CCI's permission — provided the offer document is cleared by SEBI.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: SEBI Act effective from April 1, 1992 → ✅ Correct: Deemed effective from **30 Jan 1992** (ordinance date), even though Parliament passed it on April 1.
- ❌ Mistake: SEBI was modelled on the UK FSA → ✅ Correct: Modelled on the **US SEC** (Securities Exchange Act 1934).
- ❌ Mistake: LODR is from 1992 → ✅ Correct: **2015** (consolidating earlier amendments).

### Edge Cases & Caveats
- Fund sources are **grants by the Union Government** — one of the 6 areas covered by the Act.
- The Union Government has the **power to supersede SEBI** under the Act.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Predecessor: **CCI** under Capital Issue (Contract) Act, **1947**.
> - SEBI Act dates: ordinance **30 Jan 1992**, passed **1 Apr 1992**, amended **1995**.
> - Modelled on: **US SEC** (1934).
> - 6 Act areas + 6 Guideline areas + 7 LODR entailments.

## Section: Rationale for Establishment of SEBI 🟡

### Core Idea
SEBI was set up to keep a check on malpractices and protect investor interests, serving the **three groups** that constitute the market: **issuers, investors, intermediaries**. Its mandate is to maintain stable and efficient markets, develop securities markets, and act as a safe centre-piece in the financial system. Failure of the securities market would have ripple effects on the entire financial system.

> **In Simple Terms:** SEBI is the trust-builder of the market — issuers want easy fundraising, investors want safety and accurate info, and intermediaries want a competitive professional space. SEBI tries to deliver all three simultaneously.

### Key Concepts

#### Three Market Constituents
SEBI was set up to meet the needs of the following three groups:
- **For issuers**: provides a marketplace where they can raise finance fairly and easily.
- **For investors**: provides protection and supply of accurate and correct information.
- **For intermediaries**: provides a competitive professional market.

The market participants are: issuers and issuances, investors, intermediaries, **stock exchange, clearing corporations and depositories**.

#### G. Sabarinathan on Complex Network
According to G. Sabarinathan: *"The Indian securities market is a complex network of a host of economic agents. When they come together to execute a transaction they are tied together through a web of regulations, in addition to their commercial interconnectedness. Even the conduct of those that are not directly governed by securities regulations is indirectly influenced through their contractual obligations to other regulated intermediaries and/or the regulation on the outcome of the service they provide."*

#### Symbiotic Stability of Securities Markets
The government and community expect the securities market to be an **important, stable and safe centre piece** of the financial system, coexisting symbiotically with the rest. *"The failure of the securities market could have a ripple effect on the rest of the financial system as a whole."* Stock exchanges expect a **stable, consistent and transparent policy regime** so they can keep providing liquidity, innovating, competing and responding to financial-sector developments.

### Definitions
- **Market Participant**: Any of issuers/issuances, investors, intermediaries, stock exchange, clearing corporations and depositories.
- **Securities Market**: Marketplace for trading of securities (stocks, bonds, derivatives).

### Mechanisms / Processes
1. SEBI creates and enforces regulations in the marketplace.
2. Regulations attract foreign investors and protect Indian investors.
3. SEBI develops securities markets via explicit mandate.
4. Stock exchanges provide liquidity within SEBI's policy regime.

### Examples
**Example: Three-group balancing act**
When a company wants to raise capital cheaply (issuer), the investor wants full disclosure (protection), and a merchant banker wants minimal compliance friction (competitive market) — SEBI must satisfy all three simultaneously.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: SEBI serves only investors → ✅ Correct: It serves issuers, investors, **and** intermediaries.

### Edge Cases & Caveats
- Sabarinathan's framing notes that even **non-regulated** participants are indirectly influenced via their contractual links to regulated intermediaries.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - 3 constituents = **Issuers, Investors, Intermediaries**.
> - Sabarinathan: securities market = "complex network" + "web of regulations".
> - Securities market is the **stable, safe centre-piece** of the financial system.

## Section: Objectives of SEBI 🔴

### Core Idea
SEBI's overarching objective is to provide an environment for **mobilisation and allocation of resources** through practices, frameworks and infrastructure. The Act lists **six primary objectives**, ranging from regulating exchanges to investor protection, fraud prevention, codes of conduct, investor education, and R&D.

> **In Simple Terms:** Six jobs sit on SEBI's desk — keep exchanges efficient, protect investors, stop fraud, set rules for intermediaries, educate the public, and do research to keep markets sharp.

### Key Concepts

#### Six Primary Objectives of SEBI
1. **Regulation of Stock Exchanges** — so efficient services are provided to all parties operating there.
2. **Investor Protection** — protecting investors' interests via guidance and ensuring investment safety; one of the most important objectives.
3. **Preventing fraudulent practices and malpractices** related to trading and regulation.
4. **Developing a code of conduct** for financial intermediaries (underwriters, brokers, etc.).
5. **Investor education** about market intermediaries, enhancing knowledge.
6. **Research and Development** to keep the securities market efficient at all times.

SEBI achieves these through **proactive supervision** and **policy measures**. Realisation of statutory objectives requires absolute clarity of statutory provisions.

### Definitions
- **Investor Protection**: Safeguarding investors via guidance, accurate information, and fraud prevention. ⭐
- **Code of Conduct**: SEBI-defined ethical/operational rules for intermediaries (underwriters, brokers, etc.).
- **Fraudulent Practices**: Includes insider trading, price rigging, unfair trade practices.

### Mechanisms / Processes
1. Define rules → enforce supervision → take penal action against violators.
2. Conduct investor-education programmes (online/offline).
3. Carry out research to identify market inefficiencies.
4. Update codes of conduct as new instruments emerge.

### Examples
**Example: Fraud prevention objective**
When an unusual price movement is detected in a stock, SEBI's surveillance triggers an investigation under objective (iii) — preventing fraudulent practices.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: SEBI's only job is investor protection → ✅ Correct: It is **one of six** primary objectives.
- ❌ Mistake: SEBI does not engage in education → ✅ Correct: Investor education is the 5th explicit objective.

### Edge Cases & Caveats
- The six objectives overlap with the three function-categories (Protective / Regulatory / Developmental) discussed next.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - 6 objectives: **Regulation of exchanges, Investor protection, Fraud prevention, Code of conduct, Investor education, R&D**.

## Section: Functions of SEBI (Protective / Regulatory / Developmental) 🔴

### Core Idea
SEBI's functions are classified into **three categories**: **Protective** (shields investors), **Regulatory** (frames rules and oversees compliance), and **Developmental** (grows and modernises the market). Several powers come from the Securities Contract Regulation (SCR) Act, 1956 and its amendments (1995 & 1999).

> **In Simple Terms:** Three hats — **shield** (protect), **rulebook** (regulate), and **gardener** (develop). Each category has its own list of activities.

### Key Concepts

#### Protective Functions
- (i) **Prohibits insider trading** — buying/selling by directors, employees, promoters; SEBI bars companies from purchasing their own shares from the secondary market.
- (ii) **Checks price rigging** — unnatural price fluctuations causing investor losses; SEBI maintains close watch.
- (iii) **Promotes fair practices** — prohibits insider trading and unfair trade practices in securities.
- (iv) **Financial education** — online/offline sessions on market insights and money management; helps investors evaluate options.
- (v) **Regulates operations of depositories, participants, custodians of securities, foreign portfolio investors and credit rating agencies**.

#### Regulatory Functions
- (i) Defines rules, regulations, **guidelines and codes of conduct** for corporates and intermediaries.
- (ii) Monitors **substantial acquisitions of shares and take-overs** of companies.
- (iii) Conducts **inquiries and audits** of stock exchanges.
- (iv) Regulates the working of **stockbrokers, merchant bankers, share transfer agents, trustees, registrars** etc.
- (v) Regulates and registers **mutual funds**.

#### Developmental Functions
- (i) **Training of intermediaries** in the security market.
- (ii) Introduction of **trading through electronic means / internet** with the help of registered brokers.
- (iii) Making **underwriting an optional system** to reduce the cost of issue.
- (iv) **Promotes the development** of securities market and business.
- (v) Enables a **competitive professional market** for intermediaries.

#### Comparison Table

| Aspect | Protective | Regulatory | Developmental |
|--------|------------|------------|---------------|
| Goal | Shield investors and other participants | Frame and enforce rules | Grow and modernise the market |
| Tools | Surveillance, education, prohibition | Audits, inquiries, registration | Training, e-trading, optional underwriting |
| Examples | Banning insider trading; checking price rigging | Take-over monitoring; broker rules | Internet-based trading; intermediary training |
| Beneficiary | Investors | Whole market system | Future market growth |

### Definitions
- **Insider Trading**: Buying or selling securities by company insiders (directors, employees, promoters); prohibited by SEBI. ⭐
- **Price Rigging**: Causing unnatural price fluctuations to manipulate market prices, leading to investor losses.
- **Underwriting**: Guaranteeing subscription to a share/bond issue; SEBI made it **optional** to reduce issue costs.
- **SCR Act 1956**: Securities Contract Regulation Act — provides several powers to SEBI; amended in 1995 and 1999.

### Mechanisms / Processes
1. SEBI draws power from **SCR Act 1956** + 1995 & 1999 amendments + SEBI Act 1992.
2. Issues circulars/instructions/codes of conduct.
3. Inspects books, audits exchanges, registers intermediaries.
4. Conducts surveillance for insider trading and price rigging.
5. Runs investor-education programmes and intermediary training.

### Examples
**Example: Insider trading prohibition**
A company director who buys shares of his own firm based on unpublished material information would face SEBI action under the protective function (i) and be barred from the secondary market.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: SEBI has only two function categories → ✅ Correct: **Three** — Protective, Regulatory, Developmental.
- ❌ Mistake: Underwriting is mandatory → ✅ Correct: SEBI made underwriting **optional** as a developmental measure.
- ❌ Mistake: Mutual funds are regulated under protective function → ✅ Correct: MF regulation is a **regulatory** function (also see Section 16.4.2).

### Edge Cases & Caveats
- Several functions are also drawn from the **SCR Act 1956**, not just the SEBI Act 1992.
- Two amendments in **1995 and 1999** brought important changes to the SCR Act (Sabarinathan, 2010).

> **Quick Recall:**
> - **Protective**: insider-trading ban, price-rigging check, fair practices, financial education, regulation of depositories/FPIs/CRAs.
> - **Regulatory**: rules/codes, take-over monitoring, audits, broker regulation, MF regulation.
> - **Developmental**: training, e-trading, optional underwriting, market promotion, competitive professional market.

## Section: Structure & Authority of SEBI 🔴

### Core Idea
SEBI is run by a **9-member Board**, with HQ at **Bandra Kurla Complex, Mumbai** and four regional offices in Ahmedabad, Kolkata, Chennai, and Delhi. It is a **three-in-one body** — **quasi-judicial, quasi-executive, quasi-legislative**. Aggrieved parties can appeal to the **Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT)** — a 3-member tribunal — and a second appeal lies directly to the Supreme Court. SEBI can issue **disgorgement orders** to recover illicit gains.

> **In Simple Terms:** SEBI is one body wearing three hats — it makes rules (legislative), enforces them (executive), and judges violations (judicial). To check this concentration of power, SAT exists for appeals.

### Key Concepts

#### Board Composition (9 Members)
The hierarchical structure of SEBI:
1. **Chairman** — nominated by the Union Government of India.
2. **Two officers** from the Union Ministry of Finance.
3. **One member** from the Reserve Bank of India.
4. **Five members** nominated by the Union Government, of whom at least **3 must be whole-time** members.

| Member Type | Count | Source |
|-------------|-------|--------|
| Chairman | 1 | Nominated by Union Govt |
| Finance Ministry officers | 2 | Union Ministry of Finance |
| RBI representative | 1 | RBI |
| Other members | 5 (≥ 3 whole-time) | Nominated by Union Govt |
| **Total** | **9** | |

**Allocation of work:** The Chairman, besides overseeing the Board, looks after Communications, Vigilance, and Internal Inspection Department. There are **four full-time members**. Each department is headed by an **Executive Director** reporting to a specific whole-time member. Departments include FPI&C, CFD, ITD, DEPA-I/II/III, Investment Management, Legal Affairs, T&A, and NISM. SEBI also appoints advisory committees that can only advise — not force action.

#### Headquarters & Regional Offices
- **HQ**: Bandra Kurla Complex, Mumbai.
- **Regional Offices**: Ahmedabad, Kolkata, Chennai, Delhi.
- **Local Offices**: Jaipur, Bengaluru, Guwahati, Bhubaneshwar, Patna, Kochi, Chandigarh.

#### Three-in-One Body — Quasi-Judicial / Quasi-Executive / Quasi-Legislative
- **Quasi-Judicial**: Authority to **deliver judgements** related to fraud and unethical practices in securities — ensures fairness, transparency, accountability.
- **Quasi-Executive**: Empowered to **implement regulations and judgements**, take legal action against violators, and inspect books of accounts and other documents on suspecting violations.
- **Quasi-Legislative**: Right to **frame rules and regulations** — including insider-trading regulations, listing obligations, disclosure requirements — to keep malpractices at bay.

#### Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT)
- SAT is a **three-member tribunal** — **a presiding officer + 2 other members**.
- Constituted to protect entities aggrieved by SEBI's decisions.
- A **second appeal** from SAT lies directly to the **Supreme Court**.

#### Disgorgement & Other Powers
SEBI's broad powers include:
- Restricting or banning a person from entering the securities market — even **while inquiry is ongoing**.
- Issuing **disgorgement orders** to recover illicit gains acquired through illegal activity.
- Acting against companies running illegal schemes targeting retail investors.
- Prohibiting an intermediary (e.g., research analyst, investment advisor) from soliciting/undertaking activities — explicitly or implicitly.
- Checking books of stock exchanges, calling for periodical returns, approving by-laws.
- Inspecting books of financial intermediaries (including banks).
- Compelling certain companies to get listed; handling broker registration.
- Overseeing **all AMC (Asset Management Company) registration**.

**Regulatory guardrails / statutes** SEBI uses:
1. Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements for Prevention of Fraud and Unfair Trade Practices Rules.
2. Levelling the playing field for retail investors and shielding them from fraud.
3. Active derivatives contracts in index stocks (hedging cash positions, circuit filters).
4. Advanced and Graded Surveillance Mechanisms with stiff margining to rein in single-day moves.

The **Supreme Court** held that the SEBI Act is a **special law and a complete code in itself**, to be read in harmony with company law. SEBI's power to **restrain an individual from acting as a director** in a listed company has been upheld; prohibition is immediate and not contingent on Companies-Act processes (Sahoo and Agarwal, 2022).

### Definitions
- **Quasi-Judicial**: Power to deliver judgements like a court without being a regular court. ⭐
- **Quasi-Executive**: Power to enforce regulations and inspect, like an executive agency. ⭐
- **Quasi-Legislative**: Power to frame rules and regulations like a legislature. ⭐
- **SAT (Securities Appellate Tribunal)**: 3-member appellate body (presiding officer + 2 others) for parties aggrieved by SEBI orders; second appeal to Supreme Court. ⭐
- **Disgorgement Order**: SEBI order to recover illicit gains acquired by a person through illegal activity.
- **Whole-Time Member**: Full-time member of the SEBI Board (≥ 3 of the 5 'other' members must be whole-time).

### Mechanisms / Processes
1. SEBI frames rules (quasi-legislative).
2. SEBI enforces rules — inspections, investigations (quasi-executive).
3. SEBI delivers judgements — penalties, bans, disgorgement (quasi-judicial).
4. Aggrieved party appeals to **SAT** (3 members).
5. Second appeal lies to the **Supreme Court**.

### Examples
**Example: Three-in-one in action**
SEBI frames an insider-trading regulation (legislative), inspects suspicious trades and seizes books (executive), and passes an order banning the offender from markets and disgorging gains (judicial). The offender appeals to SAT and, if dissatisfied, to the Supreme Court.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: SEBI Board has 7 members → ✅ Correct: **9 members**.
- ❌ Mistake: SAT has 5 members → ✅ Correct: **3 members** (presiding officer + 2 others).
- ❌ Mistake: SEBI HQ is in Delhi → ✅ Correct: **Bandra Kurla Complex, Mumbai**.
- ❌ Mistake: Appeals from SAT go to High Court → ✅ Correct: Direct to **Supreme Court**.

### Edge Cases & Caveats
- SEBI can ban a person **even while inquiry is ongoing** — not only after final order.
- Advisory committees can advise SEBI but **cannot force** SEBI to act.
- The SEBI Act is a "special law and complete code" — read in harmony with company law (per Supreme Court).

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Board: **9 members** = 1 Chairman + 2 FinMin + 1 RBI + 5 others (≥ 3 whole-time).
> - HQ: **Bandra Kurla Complex, Mumbai**; regional: Ahmedabad, Kolkata, Chennai, Delhi.
> - **3-in-1**: Quasi-judicial / executive / legislative.
> - **SAT** = 3 members; appeal to **Supreme Court**.
> - SEBI powers: **disgorgement**, ban while inquiry ongoing, AMC registration.

## Section: Mutual Fund Regulations by SEBI 🟡

### Core Idea
The Indian mutual-fund industry has grown **five-fold in a decade** — AUM rose from **₹8 lakh crore (Nov 2012) to ₹40 lakh crore (Dec 2022)**. SEBI regulates MFs through the trio of **AMC, Custodian, Trustees**, with **AMFI (1995)** acting as the self-regulation agency. As of April 2022, **44 AMCs** are registered with SEBI and members of AMFI. SEBI has imposed shareholding and reclassification limits and additional changes from March 29, 2023.

> **In Simple Terms:** A mutual fund is a three-legged stool — the **AMC** runs it, the **Custodian** holds the securities, and the **Trustees** keep watch. SEBI sits above them, while AMFI is the industry's own self-regulator.

### Key Concepts

#### MF Industry Growth (2012-2022)
| Period | AUM |
|--------|-----|
| November 2012 | ₹8 lakh crore |
| May 2014 | ₹10 lakh crore |
| August 2017 | ₹20 lakh crore (in just 3 years from May 2014) |
| November 2020 | ₹30 lakh crore |
| December 2022 | ₹40 lakh crore (5x growth in a decade) |

(Source: Kumar and Iyengar, 2023.)

#### Operating Architecture: AMC, Custodian, Trustees
- **Asset Management Company (AMC)**: Manages the fund — must be **approved by SEBI**.
- **Custodian**: Holds securities of various schemes — registered with SEBI.
- **Trustees of the AMC**: Monitor performance and ensure compliance with SEBI Regulations.

**Structural rules:**
- An AMC **cannot have representation on the board of any other mutual fund**.
- **Shareholders cannot hold more than 10%** of shares in the AMC, **directly or indirectly**.

#### AMFI (1995)
**Association of Mutual Funds of India (AMFI)** — set up in **August 1995** as a **self-regulation agency** for mutual funds. AMFI focuses on developing the Indian MF industry **professionally and ethically**, enhancing operational standards in all areas to protect and promote MFs and their stakeholders.
- **April 2022**: 44 AMCs registered with SEBI and members of AMFI.

#### MF Reclassification Guidelines
1. Funds must be **named based on the core intent** of the fund and asset mix; risk must be specified clearly.
2. Number of classifications:

| Category | Number of Classifications |
|----------|---------------------------|
| Debt funds | 16 |
| Equity funds | 10 |
| Hybrid funds | 6 |
| Solution funds | 2 |
| Index funds | 2 |
| **Maximum non-index categories** | **34** |

3. **Large-cap, mid-cap, small-cap** reclassified by **market-cap relative rankings** (not absolute market-cap cut-offs). Debt-fund classification is based on the **duration of the fund and the asset-quality mix**.

#### Unit Holder Protection & SEBI March 29, 2023 Changes
SEBI mandates a **Unit Holder Protection Committee** and rolled out additional MF regulation changes effective **March 29, 2023**.

### Definitions
- **AMC (Asset Management Company)**: SEBI-approved entity that manages mutual-fund schemes.
- **AMFI (Association of Mutual Funds of India)**: Self-regulation agency for MFs, set up August 1995. ⭐
- **AUM (Assets Under Management)**: Total market value of assets a fund manages.
- **Custodian**: SEBI-registered entity holding the securities of MF schemes.
- **Trustees**: Body monitoring AMC performance and SEBI-compliance of the MF.
- **Unit Holder Protection Committee**: SEBI-mandated body to safeguard unit-holder interests.

### Mechanisms / Processes
1. SEBI registers the AMC, Custodian, and Trustees.
2. AMC manages schemes; Custodian holds securities; Trustees monitor.
3. AMC follows shareholding limits (≤ 10%) and structural restrictions.
4. AMFI promotes ethical standards; classifies and trains industry.
5. SEBI applies reclassification guidelines (16/10/6/2/2 buckets).

### Examples
**Example: 10% shareholding rule**
A wealthy promoter cannot hold more than 10% of an AMC's shares — directly or indirectly — even via a related party, ensuring no single shareholder dominates fund management.

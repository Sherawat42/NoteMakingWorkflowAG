## Section: Course Overview — MEC-110 Money, Financial Institutions and Markets 🟢

### Core Idea
MEC-110 is a third-semester MA Economics course at IGNOU that extends the macroeconomic foundation built in MEC-101 and MEC-102. It treats money, financial institutions, and financial markets as the cornerstone of modern economies — the channels through which resources are allocated and transactions facilitated. The course shows how capital flows from surplus to deficit units via intermediaries and markets, and how monetary policy is transmitted through that system.

> **In Simple Terms:** Think of the economy as a body — money is the blood, financial markets are the arteries, and financial institutions (banks, insurers, pension funds) are the heart and valves. This course studies how each part keeps the system alive and what happens when blood doesn't flow properly (e.g., the 2008 crisis).

### Key Concepts

#### Why MEC-110 exists in the MA programme
Macro-economic analysis already touched monetary policy, but the deeper layer — functions of money, demand for and supply of money, central banking, and the markets in which money and securities are traded — could not be treated there. MEC-110 fills that gap and is positioned as an extension of the macro dimension of economics.

#### Conceptual prerequisite
To engage meaningfully with this course, a student must have a clear idea of (i) the conceptual foundations of the financial system, (ii) the role and functions of money, and (iii) the transmission mechanism of monetary policy.

### Definitions
- **Financial system (working definition introduced in course intro)**: the system through which capital flows in the economy and through which financial intermediaries channel funds between surplus and deficit units.

### Mechanisms / Processes
1. Households and firms generate savings → 2. Financial markets (bond, stock) and intermediaries (banks, insurance, pension funds) collect these → 3. Funds are lent / invested in deficit units (firms, governments) → 4. Productive investment → 5. Asset prices, interest rates, and economic stability are influenced.

### Examples
**Example: 2008–09 recession (mentioned in the course introduction)**
The introduction frames the recession as illustrating *why* studying the financial system matters: when the financial plumbing freezes, money stops flowing, businesses cannot get credit, output and employment fall, and governments must inject trillions to prevent collapse.

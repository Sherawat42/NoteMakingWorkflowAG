## Section: Functions and Roles of Financial Intermediaries — Full List 🔴
<!-- Continues from Chunk 003 -->
<!-- See chunk 003 for the start of this section -->

### Core Idea
Building on Function 1 (Directing the Payment System) introduced in Chunk 003, the unit lists six core functions of financial intermediaries. Together they explain how FIs convert idle savings into productive investment, manage risk, supply information, and sustain markets — serving both households and firms simultaneously.

### Key Concepts — The Six Functions ⭐

| # | Function | What it means |
|---|----------|---------------|
| 1 | **Directing the Payment System** | Manage everyday commercial transactions through checking/savings accounts, debit/credit cards, wire transfers. |
| 2 | **Assisting with Resources and Capital** | Pool resources to allow others to borrow; loans and credit cards let families and firms borrow and repay over schedules; help acquire capital for new/existing businesses. |
| 3 | **Moving Financial Resources** | Transfer resources from one place to another — corporate investments, real estate purchases, construction loans, large transactions — more easily than individuals or firms could. |
| 4 | **Risk Management** | Insurance companies and other entities pool risk across many parties and distribute it; manage accidents and difficulties (professional or personal). |
| 5 | **Informing Financial Decisions** | Provide key information for monetary decisions; high-rated consultancy guides individuals and firms toward beneficial investments in real time. |
| 6 | **Sustaining the Market** | Make stock-market investment possible for ordinary investors; brokers and stock exchanges allow companies to issue stock and raise cash. *"The stock market drives the pulse of the financial sector as a whole."* |

In sum: FIs handle assets, rate of return, liquidity, savings mobilization, capital formation, maturity transformation, risk transformation, transaction-cost reduction, and payment-mechanism smoothening.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - **6 functions: Direct payments · Resources & capital · Move resources · Risk · Inform · Sustain markets**.
> - Mnemonic: "**P**ay, **P**ool, **M**ove, **R**isk, **I**nform, **S**ustain" → P-P-M-R-I-S.
> - The stock market is described as "the pulse of the financial sector".

## Section: An Overview of Indian Financial System 🔴
<!-- High yield: dates and named institutions are classic exam material for IGNOU papers -->

### Core Idea
The Indian financial system has two segments: an **organised** sector (commercial/development/cooperative banks, stock market, insurance, mutual funds) and an **unorganised** sector (informal credit market — money lenders, indigenous bankers — serving farmers, small enterprises, individuals). The two differ markedly in interest rates and credit conditions. Pre-1947 the system was underdeveloped; post-1947 government has actively built it through nationalisation and creation of new institutions.

> **In Simple Terms:** Pre-independence India relied largely on village money lenders. After 1947, the state stepped in: it nationalised the RBI, then big banks, then insurance, and built specialised lenders for industry, agriculture, housing, and exports. Then in 1991, reforms let private players back in, but problems like bad loans (NPAs) remain.

### Key Concepts

#### Two segments of the Indian financial system

| Sector | Components | Interest rates / credit conditions |
|--------|------------|----------------------------------|
| **Organised** | Commercial banks, development banks, cooperative banks, stock market, insurance, mutual funds, NBFCs | Regulated, broadly transparent |
| **Unorganised** | Informal credit market — money lenders, indigenous bankers; serves farmers, small firms, individuals | Higher / opaque rates and terms |

### Mechanisms / Processes — Build-up Since 1947

#### A) Nationalisation of financial institutions ⭐ (memorise dates)

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| **1948** | RBI (founded 1935 as private central bank) nationalised. |
| **1955** | Imperial Bank of India nationalised → renamed **State Bank of India**. |
| **1956** | 245 life insurance entities (154 life insurance companies + 16 foreign + 75 provident) nationalised and merged into **LIC (Life Insurance Corporation of India)**. |
| **1969** | **14 commercial banks** nationalised. |
| **1972** | 107 general insurance entities (55 Indian + 52 other operations) nationalised and merged into **GIC (General Insurance Corporation of India)**. |
| **1980** | Another **6 commercial banks** nationalised. |

#### B) Establishment of new institutions

- **UTI (Unit Trust of India), 1964** — to strengthen Indian financial system and supply institutional credit to industries; mobilise public savings, provide credit to institutions for productive purposes. Subsidiaries: UTI Bank Ltd., UTI Investor Service Ltd., UTI Security Exchange Ltd.

**Development banks** (medium- and long-term finance for agri/industry):

| Institution | Year | Purpose |
|-------------|------|---------|
| **IFCI** (Industrial Finance Corporation of India) | 1948 | Medium/long-term finance to industrial concerns at central level. |
| **SFCs** (State Financial Corporations) under SFC Act | 1951 | Medium/long-term finance to medium and small industries at state level. SIIC also set up. |
| **ICICI** (Industrial Credit and Investment Corporation of India) | 1955 | Develop large/medium industries in private sector. *(Now merged with ICICI Bank, a private commercial bank.)* |
| **RCI** (Refinance Corporation of India) | 1958 | Refinance facilities to banks for term loans to medium/small units. *(Now merged with IDBI.)* |
| **IDBI** (Industrial Development Bank of India) | 1964 | Initially wholly owned subsidiary of RBI; apex institution in development banking, coordinates other FIs. *(Ownership now with central government.)* |
| **SIDBI** (Small Industries Development Bank of India) | 1990 | Principal FI for promotion, financing, development of MSMEs. |
| **ARDC** (Agricultural Refinance and Development Corporation) | 1963 | Finance major rural development projects (minor irrigation, farm mechanisation, land development, horticulture, dairy). |
| **NABARD** (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development) | 1982 | ARDC merged into it; whole sphere of agricultural finance entrusted to NABARD. |
| **EXIM Bank** (Export and Import Bank of India) | 1982 | Took over IDBI's International Finance Wing operations; finance to exporters/importers; principal FI for foreign-trade financing coordination. |
| **NHB** (National Housing Bank) | 1988 | Apex institution to mobilise resources for housing; refinancing, underwriting, guaranteeing facilities to housing finance institutions. |
| **SHCIL** (Stock Holding Corporation of India Ltd.) | 1987 | Tone up stock and capital markets; share-transfer, clearing, depository, MIS, development services. |

#### C) Encouragement of new financial services and instruments
- **Mutual Funds Industry** — both private and public sector encouraged to float mutual funds.
- **Venture Capital Industry** — both sectors encouraged to finance via venture capital.
- **Credit Rating Agencies** — **CRISIL** (Credit Rating and Information Services of India Ltd.), **ICRA** (Investment Information and Credit Rating Agency of India Ltd.), **CARE** (Credit Analysis and Research Ltd.) — to help investors decide and protect them from risky ventures.
- **New financial instruments** — public-sector bonds, national savings certificates, post-office savings schemes, varieties of shares/debentures, insurance schemes, varieties of bank deposits.

#### Evolution and post-1991 shift
- The structure in the late 1960s reflected growing public ownership/control aimed at directing finance to priority development areas.
- **1991 economic reforms** — system underwent a structural change with substantial role for the **private sector**.
- **Major weakness**: high overdues / **non-performing assets (NPAs)** in lenders, affecting both industrial and agricultural finance (improving in recent years).

### Key Data & Numbers
- **Gross saving rate**: ~8% at independence → peak ~35% → ~30% in recent years.
- **Composition of saving**: physical savings were >90% of gross savings at independence → ~50% in recent years (i.e., financial assets now far more important).
- **14 + 6 = 20** commercial banks nationalised (1969 + 1980).
- **245** life insurance entities nationalised (1956).

### Definitions
- **Organised financial sector**: under regulatory purview of RBI, SEBI etc.; includes commercial/development/cooperative banks, stock market, NBFIs (insurance corps, mutual funds).
- **Unorganised financial sector**: informal credit market — money lenders, indigenous bankers; serves farmers, small firms, individuals.
- **Financialization (of the Indian economy)**: the increase, since 1950, in financial assets relative to physical assets in gross savings, indicating an active financial system in transferring funds from surplus to deficit sectors.
- **Non-Performing Assets (NPAs)**: high overdues that affect lenders — major weakness of the Indian financial system post-1991.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Saying "RBI was founded in 1948" → ✅ **Founded 1935** as a private central bank; **nationalised 1948**.
- ❌ Confusing 1969 and 1980 nationalisations → ✅ **1969 = 14 banks; 1980 = 6 more banks** (total 20).
- ❌ Treating ICICI as still a development bank → ✅ ICICI **merged with ICICI Bank** and is now a **private commercial bank**.
- ❌ Forgetting NABARD absorbed ARDC → ✅ NABARD (1982) absorbed ARDC (1963); whole sphere of agri-finance now lies with NABARD.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - **Nationalisation timeline: RBI 1948 · SBI 1955 · LIC 1956 · 14 banks 1969 · GIC 1972 · 6 more banks 1980**.
> - **Development banks: IFCI 1948 · SFC 1951 · ICICI 1955 · RCI 1958 · IDBI 1964 · NABARD 1982 · EXIM 1982 · SHCIL 1987 · NHB 1988 · SIDBI 1990**.
> - 3 credit-rating agencies: **CRISIL, ICRA, CARE**.
> - Saving rate: 8% → 35% → 30%; physical share fell from 90%+ to ~50%.
> - **NPA = chronic weakness post-1991**.

## Section: Regulation of Financial Institutions 🔴

### Core Idea
Financial institutions are heavily regulated because their failures cause systemic panic — and because public confidence is the very foundation of finance. Regulation in India aims at four goals: **maintaining stability**, **safeguarding consumer interests** (especially small savers), **enforcing risk-management practices**, and **gaining and retaining customer confidence**. A constellation of regulators — RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA, SIDBI, NABARD, NHB, IBBI — shares oversight depending on the segment.

### Key Concepts

#### Why FIs are regulated (4 reasons)
1. **Stability** — prevent excessive risk-taking, fraud, and other activities that can cause systemic crises.
2. **Consumer protection** — especially small savers; transparent operation and ethical practices.
3. **Robust risk-management** — ensure institutions are prepared for adverse economic conditions.
4. **Customer confidence** — most people will not invest in unregulated institutions; confidence is crucial for smooth functioning.

#### Key Indian Regulatory Bodies ⭐

| Regulator | Year (where given) | Primary mandate |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **RBI** (Reserve Bank of India) | (founded 1935; nationalised 1948) | Formulates and implements **monetary policy**, issues currency, regulates money supply, supervises banks and other FIs. |
| **SEBI** (Securities and Exchange Board of India) | — | Regulates issuance and trading of securities; functioning of mutual funds and portfolio managers. |
| **IRDAI** (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India) | — | Regulates and oversees the insurance industry. |
| **PFRDA** (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority) | **2003** | Pensions oversight. |
| **SIDBI** | 1990 | Small Industries Development Bank of India. |
| **NABARD** | 1982 | Regulates and supervises **cooperative banks** and **regional rural banks (RRBs)**; ensures flow of credit to rural areas. |
| **NHB** | 1988 | Promote housing finance (under RBI). |
| **IBBI** (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India) | **2016** | Regulator overseeing **insolvency proceedings** and entities like Insolvency Professional Agencies, Insolvency Professionals, Information Utilities. |

### Examples
**Example: Why panic + regulation are linked**
The text states: *"Historically, bankruptcies of financial institutions have created panic and instability in the financial system. A well-regulated financial system inspires confidence among investors, depositors, and the general public."* — i.e., regulation isn't just about preventing crises, it's also a *signal* that buys public trust.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Treating SEBI as a banking regulator → ✅ SEBI = **securities markets** (issuance, trading, MFs, portfolio managers); banks are RBI's domain.
- ❌ Treating IRDAI as an FI → ✅ IRDAI is the **regulator** for insurance; LIC, GIC and private insurers are the FIs being regulated.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - **4 reasons to regulate: Stability · Consumer protection · Risk management · Confidence.**
> - **Regulator-by-segment: RBI = banks/money; SEBI = securities; IRDAI = insurance; PFRDA = pensions (2003); IBBI = insolvency (2016); NABARD = co-op + RRBs; NHB = housing.**

## Section: Unit 2 Summary, Key Words and Check-Your-Progress Answers 🟢

### Core Idea
Unit 2 closes by re-emphasising: a **Financial Institution (FI)** is an entity dealing in financial and monetary transactions (deposits, loans, investments, currency exchange). FIs vary by size, scope, geography. The financial system is stable only when each agent and constituent functions well; problems with one function spill into others. *The health of a nation's banking system is the linchpin of economic stability.* Robust regulation is therefore essential.

### Definitions (Unit 2 Glossary)
- **Direct finance**: funds provided directly by savers to borrowers in financial markets without an intermediary.
- **Indirect finance**: funds provided by a "middleman" — a third party that stands between borrowers and lenders.
- **Intermediaries**: intermediate between savers and investors; lend money as well as mobilise savings.
- **Financial Institution (FI)**: a company engaged in dealing with financial and monetary transactions such as deposits, loans, investments, and currency exchange.
- **Financial Intermediation**: the process that channels funds between third parties with a surplus and those with a lack of funds. ⭐ (exam-important)

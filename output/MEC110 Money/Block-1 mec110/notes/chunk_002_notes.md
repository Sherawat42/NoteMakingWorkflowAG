## Section: Unit 1 Objectives & Introduction to Economic Agents 🟢

### Core Idea
Economic agents are individuals or entities that participate in economic activities by making choices, engaging in transactions, and influencing the allocation of resources. The four main agents are **households**, **firms**, **government**, and **financial institutions**. In the context of financial markets they take distinct roles: households are net savers (and sometimes borrowers), firms are net borrowers and investors, financial institutions are intermediaries, and government is both a regulator and a participant.

> **In Simple Terms:** Imagine you have an idea for a business but no cash, while Raj has inherited money he wants to invest. A bank or bond market puts you two in touch. Both of you are "economic agents" — and the institution that connects you is the financial system.

### Key Concepts

#### Household, firm, government, financial institution as agents
Households save (deposits, savings accounts, bonds, stocks) and borrow (mortgages, car loans). Firms borrow to expand and invest in productive capacity. Financial institutions intermediate. Government regulates *and* participates (e.g., issues bonds).

#### Why agents matter together
Their collective behaviour determines the **flow of money, credit, and investments**, which in turn determines interest rates, asset prices, and overall economic stability.

### Definitions
- **Economic agents**: individuals or entities that participate in economic activities by making choices, engaging in transactions, and influencing resource allocation.
- **Financial System**: the totality of financial institutions, financial markets, financial services, financial instruments, financial practices and financial transactions.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - 4 main economic agents: **Households · Firms · Government · Financial Institutions**.
> - Households = primarily savers (also borrowers); Firms = borrowers/investors; FIs = intermediaries; Government = regulator + participant.

## Section: The Nature of the Financial System 🔴
<!-- High yield: definition + four constituents are exam-staple -->

### Core Idea
A **financial system** is a complex, well-integrated set of sub-systems of financial institutions, markets, instruments, and services which facilitate the transfer and allocation of funds, efficiently and effectively, between different economic agents.

> **In Simple Terms:** It's the country's "money-moving machine" — a network of pipes (markets), valves (instruments), pumps (institutions), and operators (services) that gets savings to where they can be invested productively.

### Key Concepts

#### Four constituents of a financial system
1. **Financial institutions** — business organisations that mobilise and deposit savings and act as purveyors of credit/finance; also provide financial services.
2. **Financial markets** — mechanisms enabling participants to deal with financial claims and to set prices for those claims.
3. **Financial instruments** — claims against a person or institution for the payment, at a future date, of money and/or a periodic payment in the form of interest or dividend.
4. **Financial services** — services rendered by intermediaries (merchant banking, leasing, hire purchase, credit rating, etc.) that bridge the information gap between investors and increasingly sophisticated instruments.

#### Two classifications of financial institutions

| Classification | Type A | Type B |
|----------------|--------|--------|
| **By money-creation power** | Banking institutions — *creators* of credit (Sayers) | Non-banking institutions — *purveyors* of credit |
| **By source of funds** | Intermediaries — borrow from ultimate savers, lend to ultimate borrowers | Non-intermediaries — lend, but don't take funds directly from savers |

What makes banking institutions distinct from non-banking ones (three points):
1. They participate in the economy's payments mechanism (provide transactions services).
2. Their **deposit liabilities form a major part of the national money supply**.
3. As a whole, they **can create deposits or credit** which is money.

Examples of non-banking financial intermediaries (NBFIs): mutual funds, investment funds, insurance companies, pension funds.

#### Sayers' distinction
The contrast between banking and non-banking institutions is captured by Sayers, who calls the former "**creators**" of credit and the latter "**purveyors**" of credit. ⭐

### Definitions
- **Financial System**: a complex, well-integrated set of sub-systems of financial institutions, markets, instruments, and services which facilitate the transfer and allocation of funds, efficiently and effectively. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Financial institutions**: business organisations that act as mobilisers and depositors of savings and as purveyors of credit or finance. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Financial markets**: a mechanism enabling participants to deal with financial claims; participants on the demand and supply sides include FIs, agents, brokers, dealers, borrowers, lenders, savers etc. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Financial instrument**: a claim against a person or an institution for the payment at a future date of a sum of money and/or a periodic payment in the form of interest or dividend. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Financial services**: services like merchant banking, leasing, hire purchase, credit rating provided by intermediaries to bridge the gap between lay investors' lack of knowledge and sophisticated instruments.

### Mechanisms / Processes
**Functions of financial services (4 core functions):**
1. **Facilitating Transactions** — enabling exchange of goods and services.
2. **Mobilizing Savings** — gathering savings that would otherwise have limited investment use.
3. **Allocating Capital** — directing funds to finance productive investments.
4. **Monitoring Management** — ensuring allocated funds are used as intended.

**Categories of financial services (in the source):**
1. *Provision of funds* — venture capital, banking services, asset financing, trade financing, credit cards.
2. *Managing investible funds* — portfolio management, merchant banking, mutual & pension funds.
3. *Risk financing* — project preparatory services, insurance, export credit guarantee.
4. *Consultancy services* — project preparation, project appraisal, business advisory, valuation, credit rating, M&A.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Treating "financial institutions" and "intermediaries" as identical → ✅ All banks are intermediaries, but not all FIs are intermediaries — non-intermediary lenders exist.
- ❌ Calling all NBFIs "non-creators of money" → ✅ Sayers' point is that banks as a whole create deposits/credit; non-banks merely *purvey* existing credit.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Financial system = **institutions + markets + instruments + services**.
> - Banks: **creators** of credit. Non-banks: **purveyors** of credit (Sayers).
> - 4 functions of financial services: **Facilitate, Mobilize, Allocate, Monitor**.

## Section: Participants in Financial Markets 🟡

### Core Idea
The cast of financial-market actors is wider than just "savers and borrowers". It includes business firms, investors, brokers, speculators, hedgers, arbitrageurs, market makers, and specialists — each playing a different role in price discovery and liquidity provision.

### Key Concepts

| Participant | Role |
|-------------|------|
| Business firms | Issue stocks and bonds to raise capital from investors. |
| Investors (retail & institutional) | Deploy funds for return; allocate capital. |
| Brokers | Mediators bringing buyers and sellers together; earn a **commission/fee**; do NOT take a position. |
| Speculators | Make directional bets on future asset prices. |
| Hedgers | Use derivatives to **mitigate** various risks. |
| Arbitrageurs | Exploit mispricing/anomalies across markets. |
| Market makers (MMs) & specialists | Provide two-sided quotes; maintain liquidity in stock markets. |

#### Two fundamental sides
At root the financial system has just two sides — **those with excess funds (savers)** and **those who need funds (borrowers)** — and FIs/markets bring them together.

### Definitions
- **Broker**: a third party who facilitates trades between buyers and sellers without taking a position; earns a commission or fee.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Brokers connect — they don't lend.
> - Hedgers reduce risk; speculators take risk; arbitrageurs exploit price gaps.

## Section: Importance and Role of Financial Institutions in Economic Development 🔴
<!-- High yield: list of 12 functions is the kind of textbook enumeration that becomes an exam question -->

### Core Idea
Financial institutions are not merely passive plumbing — they actively drive economic development by mobilising savings, channelling them to productive uses, multiplying credit, transferring funds geographically and sectorally, encouraging entrepreneurship, and enabling government finance.

### Key Concepts (the 12 roles, from the source)

1. **Form financial resources** by mobilising savings of households, corporates, and the public sector.
2. **Channelise resources/investments** into agriculture, manufacturing, services.
3. **Meet short- and long-term financial needs.**
4. **Multiply monetary resources** through credit creation (banks specifically).
5. **Transfer surplus funds** from one part of the economy to another based on national priorities and market demand.
6. **Encourage conversion of physical assets into financial assets** to make capital available for production, trade, commerce.
7. **Provide variety of financial assets** to suit different investor needs.
8. **Risk control & uncertainty management** for stability of the financial system; reduce capital-loss risk via diversification.
9. **Encourage entrepreneurial skills** and assist new projects.
10. **Accelerate economic growth.**
11. **Enable better decision-making.**
12. **Make resources available to the Government.**

#### The 2008–09 illustration
The text uses the recession of 2008–09 to underscore importance: businesses depend on FIs for money. When credit dries up, production stalls, unemployment rises, defaults grow, spending falls, tax revenues drop, governments cut spending — feeding back into more unemployment. *"Financial system with its institutions is the plumbing system of the economy and when it freezes or gets blocked, the flow of money in the economy gets affected, undermining economic activity."* This is why governments injected trillions during the recession.

### Examples
**Example: Why governments inject liquidity during a crisis**
During 2008–09, governments worldwide poured trillions into financial institutions specifically to prevent collapse — because the alternative was an economy-wide stall. The point: regulators tolerate moral hazard in a crisis because the systemic cost of doing nothing is far greater.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - **Banks create credit; this multiplies money supply** (one of the 12 roles).
> - FI freeze ⇒ output ↓, unemployment ↑, defaults ↑, taxes ↓, fiscal cuts ↓, more unemployment ↑ (vicious cycle).

## Section: Financial Markets — Definition, Equilibrium and Types 🔴

### Core Idea
A financial market is the total infrastructure that facilitates the trade of financial securities (currency, bonds, stocks, derivatives, commodities, forex). All financial markets, despite differences in asset class and structure, work by bringing buyers and sellers together, usually through an **auction or price-discovery mechanism**. Equilibrium is determined assuming perfect competition: total desired borrowing = total desired lending fixes the equilibrium rate of interest.

> **In Simple Terms:** A financial market is just a marketplace, except instead of trading vegetables, people trade promises (bonds), ownership slices (stocks), bets on future prices (futures/options), or other currencies (forex).

### Key Concepts

#### Equilibrium condition
Equilibrium is established when **expected demand for funds (credit) for short- and long-term investments matches the planned supply of funds generated out of saving and credit creation**. Equivalently, total desired borrowing = total desired lending → equilibrium rate of interest.

#### Why intermediaries are essential
Without intermediaries (banks, brokers, custodians, depositories), financial markets cannot run efficiently. They (i) collect funds from lenders, (ii) disburse capital to borrowers, and (iii) handle securities (stocks, mutual fund units, bonds, deposit certificates) on behalf of clients.

### Types of Financial Markets — by Asset Class

| Market | What is traded | Key feature |
|--------|----------------|-------------|
| **OTC (Over-the-Counter)** | Stocks of usually small companies | Decentralised, less regulation, customised procedures, traded cheaply. |
| **Bond market** | Bonds (govt, corporate, state, municipal) | Loan with set time and predefined interest rate. |
| **Money market** | Highly liquid short-term instruments (<1 yr maturity) | High safety, low return; wholesale (institutions) + retail (MMMFs, money market accounts). |
| **Derivatives market** | Futures, options, other contracts whose value is derived from underlying assets | Derivative is "worthless in itself" — value comes from the underlying. |
| **Future markets** | Futures contracts | **Standardised** contract specs, well-regulated, use **clearing houses** to settle/confirm trades. |
| **Options markets** | Options contracts | Listed and regulated; on equities, fixed-income, commodities, etc. |
| **Commodities markets** | Agri products, energy (oil/gas/carbon credits), precious metals, soft commodities | Bulk in **spot markets** (physical for money); forwards/futures/options trade OTC + listed. |
| **Forex market** | Currency pairs (buy/sell/hedge/speculate) | **Most liquid market in the world**; decentralised; banks, central banks, hedge funds, retail. |
| **Cryptocurrency markets** | Digital assets (Bitcoin, Ethereum, etc.) | Since 2009; blockchain-based; mostly centralised exchanges (hack/fraud risk); decentralised P2P exchanges also exist; futures/options on majors. |

### Types of Financial Markets — by Nature of Transaction (6-way classification) ⭐

1. **Nature of Claim** → (a) **Debt market** (bonds, debentures); (b) **Equity market** (shares).
2. **Maturity of Claim** → (a) **Money market** (short-term: CDs, T-bills, commercial paper, ≤12 months); (b) **Capital market** (medium/long term: ≥1 year).
3. **Timing of Delivery** → (a) **Cash (spot) market** (real-time settlement); (b) **Futures market** (delivery on a specified future date).
4. **Organisational Structure** → (a) **Exchange-traded** (centralised, patterned procedure); (b) **OTC** (decentralised, customised).
5. **Nature of Transaction** → (a) **Primary market** ("new issue market" — fresh capital to firms; mobilises savings); (b) **Secondary market** (already-issued securities; provides liquidity, no fresh capital directly).
6. **Regulation Status** → (a) **Unorganised** (indigenous bankers, money lenders); (b) **Organised** (under RBI / SEBI regulation).

### Definitions
- **Derivative**: a contract between two or more parties whose value is based on an agreed-upon underlying financial asset (or set of assets like an index). ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Money market**: market trading highly liquid, short-term (<1 year) instruments with high safety and low return.
- **Forex market**: market in which participants buy, sell, hedge, or speculate on exchange rates between currency pairs; **most liquid market in the world**.
- **Cryptocurrency**: decentralized digital assets based on blockchain technology. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Primary market**: deals in new financial claims / new securities — also called "new issue market"; supplies fresh capital to business units. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Secondary market**: deals in already-issued / outstanding securities; provides liquidity but does not directly add new capital.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Confusing money market with capital market → ✅ **Money market = short-term (<1 yr); Capital market = medium/long-term (≥1 yr)**.
- ❌ Thinking secondary market raises new capital → ✅ Secondary market provides **liquidity** to primary-market issues; it does **not** directly supply additional capital.
- ❌ Treating forwards and futures as the same → ✅ Forwards trade OTC (customised, no clearing house); Futures are **standardised, exchange-listed, cleared**.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Equilibrium: **desired borrowing = desired lending → equilibrium interest rate**.
> - Forex = most liquid market.
> - 6 classifications by transaction nature: **Claim · Maturity · Timing · Structure · Transaction · Regulation**.
> - Primary market = new issues; Secondary market = liquidity for existing issues.
> - Crypto since 2009 (Bitcoin/Ethereum).

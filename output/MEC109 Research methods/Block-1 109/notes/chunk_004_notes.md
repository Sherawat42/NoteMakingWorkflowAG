## Section: Karl Popper's Philosophy of Science — Complete Theses 🔴
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->

### Core Idea
Popper's philosophy extends beyond falsifiability to cover the nature of observations, theories, scientific progress, objectivity, and rationality — all in contrast to Positivism.

### Popper's Full Theses (1–15)
Below is the complete set of Popper's positions, and whether he agrees or disagrees with Positivists:

| # | Thesis | Popper's View | Agrees with Positivists? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scientism | Science is uniquely superior | ✅ Yes |
| 2 | Methodologism | Science's superiority is due to its method | ✅ Yes |
| 3 | Methodological Monism | One method for all sciences | ✅ Yes |
| 4 | Hypothetico-Deductivism | The scientific method is H-D | ✅ Yes |
| 5 | Falsifiability | Hallmark of science is **falsifiability** (not verifiability) | ❌ Against Positivists |
| 6 | Theory-dependent observation | Observations are **not** pure | ❌ Against Positivists |
| 7 | Theories as inventions | Theories are conjectures, not generalised from observations | ❌ Against Positivists |
| 8 | Observation-Theory interdependence | They are interdependent (not one-way) | ❌ Against Positivists |
| 9 | Underdetermination | Multiple theories can correspond to same observations | ❌ Against Positivists |
| 10 | No strict fact-value dichotomy | Science is not value-neutral; values can be critically discussed | ❌ Against Positivists |
| 11 | Deductive-Nomologism | Scientific explanations must have D-N pattern | ✅ Yes |
| 12 | Realism | Science explains observable world in terms of **unobservable entities** (which are real) | ❌ Against Positivist anti-realism |
| 13 | Progress = Verisimilitude | Science progresses discontinuously; new theories have greater truth-nearness | ❌ Partial: progress yes, but not cumulative as Positivists thought |
| 14 | Objectivity = Inter-subjective testability | Science is objective not via pure observation but via inter-subjective testability | ❌ Against Positivists |
| 15 | Rationality = Critical thinking | Science is rational via critical thinking, not via justifiable induction | ❌ Against Positivists |

### Key Summary: What Makes Popper a "Semi-Positivist"
Popper shares with Positivists (theses 1, 2, 3, 11): **Scientism, Methodologism, Methodological Monism, Deductive-Nomologism**
But rejects their **central thesis of pure observation** and the inductive method.

### Definitions
- **Realism (Popper)**: The position that unobservable theoretical entities are real, and theories are putative descriptions of them
- **Anti-Realism / Instrumentalism**: Theoretical terms don't refer to real things; theories are only instruments for prediction (the Positivist/Machian view)
- **Verisimilitude**: Truth-nearness — successive theories exhibit greater approximation to the real world

### Quick Recall
> **Popper agrees with Positivists on**: Scientism, Methodologism, Methodological Monism, D-N Pattern  
> **Popper rejects**: Pure observation, Inductivism, Verifiability, Value-neutrality, Cumulative progress

## Section: Criticism against Karl Popper's Philosophy of Science 🟡
<!-- Exam importance: 🟡 MODERATE -->

### Core Idea
Popper's account of scientific progress via verisimilitude was criticised as incoherent. The idea that successive theories are "more true" or comparable is questionable.

### Three Main Criticisms

#### 1. Discontinuous Growth is Self-Contradictory
- Popper says science grows **discontinuously** (a new theory is entirely new, not old + epsilon)
- But he also claims old theory is a **limiting case** of the new (some continuity)
- Examples like Phlogiston → Oxygen theory or Ptolemy → Copernican theory do NOT support the limiting-case claim

#### 2. Verisimilitude is Unintelligible
- Popper says a new theory is "more true" (closer to reality)
- E.g., ultimate constituents of matter are "more like fields (quantum theory) than particles (Newtonian)"
- Critics ask: **what does "more like fields than particles" even mean?** Either they are or they aren't.

#### 3. Comparison Between Successive Theories is Questioned (Kuhn)
- For comparison, theories must share something in common
- Kuhn showed that two successive paradigms might be **so different** that comparison is highly questionable (incommensurability)
- This challenge leads naturally to Kuhn's own philosophy

### Quick Recall
> Popper's 3 weaknesses: **Discontinuity contradiction | Unintelligible verisimilitude | Comparability challenged by Kuhn**

## Section: Thomas Kuhn's Philosophy of Science 🔴
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->

### Core Idea
Kuhn's *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* (1962) proposed a completely different view of science — not as the work of individual scientists following methods, but as a **community practice** driven by paradigms, which are periodically overthrown in revolutionary episodes.

### In Simple Terms
Science isn't a smooth upward march of truth. Instead, it alternates between long periods of routine puzzle-solving (*Normal Science*) and sudden dramatic upheavals (*Scientific Revolutions*) when the entire framework is replaced.

### Key Concepts

#### The Two Stages in the Life of Every Major Science
1. **Pre-paradigmatic stage**: Multiple competing schools; no consensus; like art, philosophy, medicine today
2. **Paradigmatic stage**: One dominant paradigm; consensus on fundamentals; "mature science"

Transition from pre-paradigmatic → paradigmatic = the science becomes "mature"

#### What is a Paradigm?
> "Universally recognised achievements that for a time provide model problems and solutions to a community of practitioners."

A paradigm has five components:
1. Specifies the **ultimate constituents** of the sphere of reality science is investigating
2. Identifies the **model problems**
3. Specifies the **range of possible solutions**
4. Provides **strategies and techniques** for solving problems
5. Provides **exemplary examples** of how to solve certain problems

A paradigm is a **disciplinary matrix** of a professional group.

#### Normal Science
> "The day-to-day research activity purporting to force nature into conceptual boxes provided by the paradigm."

Key features of Normal Science:
- **Tradition-bound**: accepts paradigm; does not question it
- **Convergent thinking**: not radical; builds precision and extends existing theory
- **Puzzle-solving**: solves specific puzzles within the paradigm's framework
- **Goals**: (1) Increase precision of existing theory, (2) Extend theory to new areas
- Education in normal science: dogmatic initiation into pre-established tradition

> Normal science is **non-revolutionary, committed, and tradition-bound**.

#### Anomalies and Crisis
- **Anomaly**: A puzzle that resists resolution within the paradigm
- One or two anomalies ≠ crisis; a **large accumulation of major anomalies** → crisis
- No clear/objective criterion to define how many anomalies = crisis → decided by **community judgment/peer review**
- Crisis-ridden paradigm is not abandoned until a new one is ready

#### Scientific Revolution
> "The tradition-shattering complements to the tradition-bound activity of normal science."

**Stages of Scientific Revolution**:
1. Crisis deepens
2. Search for alternative paradigm begins
3. Radical scientific debates emerge
4. New paradigm is chosen (based on value judgments of scientific community, not purely logic/experiment)
5. Old paradigm is replaced by new paradigm

#### Paradigm Choice — Not Purely Logical
- Evaluation procedures of normal science are useless during paradigm choice (they depend on the paradigm under dispute)
- The choice is determined by **consensus of the relevant scientific community**
- The ultimate explanation of theory choice is **sociological, not methodological**
- Kuhn: "Scientific communities must first be recognised as having an independent existence"

#### Incommensurability of Successive Paradigms
- Two successive paradigms **cut the world differently** and speak **different languages**
- Even shared terms have different meanings across paradigms
- Cannot translate from one paradigm's language to another
- Therefore successive paradigms are **incommensurable** — cannot be directly compared
- Kuhn compares paradigm shift to a **gestalt shift** (sudden perceptual reorganisation)
- This implies: **truth is intra-paradigmatic** (relative to a paradigm), not inter-paradigmatic absolute truth
- Kuhn's view appears to be a form of **relativism**

### Definitions
- **Paradigm**: A disciplinary matrix comprising the ultimate view of reality, model problems, range of solutions, strategies, and techniques, shared by a scientific community
- **Normal Science**: Tradition-bound, puzzle-solving research within an accepted paradigm; does not question the paradigm
- **Anomaly**: A puzzle that resists resolution within the current paradigm's framework
- **Scientific Revolution**: The process of replacing a crisis-ridden paradigm with a new one
- **Incommensurability**: The state of two paradigms being so different that they cannot be meaningfully compared

### Common Mistakes
- Thinking paradigm shift = gradual reform. It is a **sudden, radical replacement** of the entire framework
- Confusing anomaly with crisis — anomalies are normal; only an **accumulation of major anomalies** triggers crisis

### Quick Recall
> **Kuhn's Sequence**: Pre-paradigmatic → Paradigm adopted → Normal Science (puzzle solving) → Anomalies accumulate → Crisis → Scientific Revolution → New Paradigm

## Section: Popper Versus Kuhn 🔴
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->

### Core Idea
Despite both being Post-Positivists, Popper and Kuhn differ fundamentally on the essence of science, the locus of scientific activity, and how theory change is explained.

### Three Major Differences

| Dimension | Popper | Kuhn |
|---|---|---|
| **Essence of science** | **Divergent thinking** and fundamental disagreements; critical thinking challenges everything | **Convergent thinking** and consensus; tradition-bound paradigm guides practice |
| **Locus of scientific activity** | The **individual scientist** — science is the sum total of individual scientists working by method | The **scientific community** — community enforces consensus; institutions (e.g., peer review) handle disputes |
| **Explanation of theory change** | Via **methodological norms** followed by all scientists; rational | Via **value judgments of the community**; sociological (Kuhn) calls Popper's method rational; Popper calls Kuhn an **irrationalist** |

### Where They Agree
- Both (like Positivists) believe science is **unique** among human endeavours
- But they differ on *what* makes it unique:
  - **Positivists**: Systematic *verifiability*
  - **Popper**: Systematic *falsifiability*
  - **Kuhn**: *Consensus* enforced by a scientific community

### Kuhn's Position is Conservative
- Kuhn, like Positivists and Popper, does NOT question whether science is really unique — he only provides an **alternative account** of how it acquired that status
- In this sense, Kuhn's position is "conservative"

### Key Terms
| School | Hallmark of Science |
|---|---|
| Positivists | Systematic **verifiability** |
| Popper | Systematic **falsifiability** |
| Kuhn | **Consensus** of the scientific community |

### Implications for Social Sciences
- **Positivist recommendation**: Collect data, extract generalisation, verify, formulate law
- **Popperian recommendation**: Formulate problem, propose tentative solution, try to falsify; if survived = corroborated theory
- **Kuhnian recommendation**: Arrive at paradigms in social sciences to overcome ideological differences

### Quick Recall
> **Popper**: Critical thinking | Individual | Methodology  
> **Kuhn**: Consensus | Community | Sociology

## Section: Key Words Glossary — Unit 2 🟢
<!-- Exam importance: 🟢 CONTEXT -->

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Inductivism** | Scientific knowledge is generated by arriving at generalisations from pure observations using the principle of induction |
| **Hypothesisism** | Knowledge is created by putting forth a hypothesis with test implications; hypothesis describes unobservable entities behind observations |
| **Realists** | Those who support hypothesis; believe in the real existence of theoretical/unobservable entities |
| **Anti-Realists** | Inductivists who reject the existence of unobservable entities beyond what can be observed |
| **Paradigm** | A comprehensive world view, concept, assumptions, theories and models used by a researcher; introduced by Thomas Kuhn |
| **Deductive** | Derivation of a conclusion by reasoning in which the conclusion necessarily follows from the premises |
| **Methodological Dualism** | The positivist belief in separation of the knower from the known (subject-object separation) |
| **Methodological Monism** | Views the world as a "seamless web"; the separation between knower and known must be overcome |
| **Pseudo Science** | Any body of knowledge that claims to be scientific but is judged to fall outside the domain of science |

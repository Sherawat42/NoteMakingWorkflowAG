## Section: Interpretive Paradigm — Core Philosophy 🔴
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->

### Core Idea
The Interpretive Paradigm (Interpretivism) is based on the belief that reality is **socially constructed** and can only be accessed through **social constructions** like language, shared meanings, and consciousness. It is the philosophical foundation of qualitative research.

### In Simple Terms
Interpretivists say: "There is no single objective reality out there waiting to be discovered. Reality is shaped by people's shared meanings, language, and context. To understand it, you must understand *how people make sense of their world*."

### Key Concepts

#### Ontological Assumption: Subjectivism
- Reality is **socially constructed and interpreted** — not objective and external
- "Social reality is based on people's definition of it" (Neuman, 1997)
- Interpretivists do not recognise the existence of an objective world — they see the world as **bounded by particular time and context**

#### Epistemological Assumption
- Knowledge of reality is obtained from the **accounts that social actors provide**
- "Findings are literally created as the investigation proceeds" (Guba & Lincoln)
- Understanding social reality requires understanding how **practices and meanings are formed by shared language and tacit norms**

#### Focus of Interpretive Research
- Documenting and interpreting:
  - World views, values, meanings, beliefs, thoughts
  - Characteristics of life events, situations, phenomena
- Goal: **Document and interpret as fully as possible** the totality of what is studied, **from the people's viewpoint**

#### Key Supporting Philosophies
| Philosophy | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| **Verstehen** | Understanding the *particulars* of a situation |
| **Hermeneutics** | Importance of *language and context* in understanding |
| **Phenomenology** | People's *perception* of the world; conscious experience |

#### Methodologies Used in Interpretive Research
1. **Naturalistic Inquiry**: First-hand observation of real-life situations as they unfold; non-manipulative; no predetermined outcomes
2. **Phenomenological Methodologies**: Rely on descriptions of conscious experiences to understand meaning of human action
3. **Constructivism**: Uses perception/self-experience in making and structuring knowledge
4. **Ethnographic Inquiry**: Self-experiencing the culture of participants in the field
5. **Symbolic Interactionism**: Understands human action through meanings derived by humans for objects/people

Other data collection methods: surveys, interviews, field observation, witness accounts, focus group discussion.

#### Key Axioms of Naturalistic Inquiry (Lincoln and Guba, 1985)
1. There are **multiple constructed realities** that can only be studied holistically — reality is complex, contextual, constructed, and subjective
2. The **inquirer and the "object" of inquiry interact** to influence one another — knower and known are *inseparable*
3. **No a priori theory** can encompass the multiple realities — theory must **emerge from the data** (grounded theory)

#### Research Strategy for Interpretivism
**Abduction** — starts with the concepts and meanings in social actors' accounts of activities related to a research problem.

### Differences: Post-positivism vs Interpretivism

| Dimension | Post-positivism | Interpretivism |
|---|---|---|
| Nature of reality | External to human mind | Socially constructed |
| Purpose of research | Find universals | Reflect understanding (contextual) |
| Acceptable methods | Scientific method | Subjective and objective methods acceptable |
| Meaning of data | Falsification | Understanding is contextual; universals deemphasised |
| Research-practice relationship | Separate; research guides practice | Integrated activities; both guide each other |

### Criticism of Interpretivism
- Risk of **losing theoretical sensitivity** (Strauss & Corbin) — researchers must maintain awareness of subtle meanings in data
- Must evaluate relevance of preliminary theoretical framework against actual findings

### Definitions
- **Interpretivism / Interpretive Paradigm**: "Our knowledge of reality is gained only through social constructions such as language, consciousness, shared meanings, documents, tools, and other artifacts" (Klein & Myers)
- **Subjectivism** (Ontology of Interpretivism): Reality is socially constructed and subjectively interpreted
- **Anti-foundationalism**: Interpretivists believe that the standards guiding research are products of particular groups/cultures — there are no self-evident universal starting points
- **Verstehen**: German for "understanding"; stresses understanding of the particulars of a situation
- **Hermeneutics**: The study of interpretation, especially the role of language and context in understanding
- **Phenomenology**: Philosophical method relying on descriptions of conscious experience to develop understanding of meaning of human action
- **Naturalistic Inquiry**: Research using first-hand observation to understand human action in real-life, non-manipulative settings

### Quick Recall
> **Interpretivism**: Socially constructed reality | Accounts of social actors = knowledge | Abduction strategy  
> **Key philosophers**: Verstehen + Hermeneutics + Phenomenology

## Section: Critical Theory Paradigm 🔴
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->

### Core Idea
Critical Theory is rooted in Marxist philosophy and the Frankfurt School. It agrees that reality is socially shaped, but uniquely focuses on **uncovering oppression and power asymmetries** — and goes further to change them.

### In Simple Terms
Critical theorists ask: "Who has power? Who is oppressed? Why does this unjust reality exist? And how can we change it?" Research is not for knowledge's sake — it is for **emancipation**.

### Roots and Key Thinkers
- Rooted in **Marxism** — class conflict; reality can only be reformed through radical transformation
- The **Frankfurt School** (Institute for Social Research):
  - Herbert Marcuse, Theodor Adorno, Max Horkheimer, Walter Benjamin, Erich Fromm
- Other labels: Neo-Marxism, Materialism, Frankfurt School, Freireism
- Critical response to works of Marx, Kant, Hegel, and Weber

### Ontological Assumption: Historical Ontology
- There is a **reality** that is apprehendable — shaped by social, political, cultural, economic, ethnic, and gender-based forces **crystallised over time into social structures**
- These structures are taken to be "natural" — but critical theorists believe this assumption is **inappropriate** and must be challenged

### Epistemological Assumption: Modified Transactional / Subjectivist
- We cannot separate ourselves from what we know — knowledge is **inextricably tied to the interaction** between a particular investigator and a particular object/group

### Purpose of Research (Critical Theory)
> Critical researchers aim to **confront injustices** and understand the relationship between social structures (especially economic and political) and ideological patterns that **constrain human imagination** and limit opportunities for social change.

- Research is a **first step toward addressing injustice** — it has a **normative and transformative** purpose
- Not interested in "knowledge for knowledge's sake"
- Critical theory is **multi-disciplinary**: applied in anthropology, economics, education, history, psychology, political science, sociology, theology

### When to Apply Critical Theory
Apply when:
- Objective is to **analyse power structures** in a social set-up
- Research addresses **social problems arising from power asymmetries**  
- Example: Wage inequities between male, female, and child workers

### Methodology
Two main methodologies in Critical Theory research:
1. **Ideology Critique**: Examines and challenges hidden ideological assumptions embedded in knowledge, institutions, and practices
2. **Action Research**: Combines understanding with action; researchers not only study but also work to change the situation

Research methods: observation + interviewing + **dialogic approaches** (foster conversation and reflection)

**3 Dimensions of the Critical Social Scientist's Task**:
1. Understand the **ideologically distorted subjective situation** of a group
2. Explore the **forces that caused** that situation
3. Show that these forces **can be overcome** through awareness by the oppressed

**Research Strategy**: **Retroduction** — starts with a hypothetical model of a mechanism that could explain the occurrence of a phenomenon under investigation

### "Objective" Analysis — Critical Realism
- Critical theory does NOT claim "objective analysis" in the logical positivist sense
- Acknowledges **subjectivism** — but rejects *pure relativism*
- Asserts that rational analysis is fundamental to human emancipation → **Critical Realism** (Morrow, 1994)

### Validity in Critical Theory
Valid to the extent that analysis:
1. Provides **insight into systems of oppression and domination** that limit human freedom
2. Is **useful in countering such systems**

### Differences: Post-positivism vs Critical Theory

| Dimension | Post-positivism | Critical Theory |
|---|---|---|
| Nature of reality | External to human mind | Material and external, but interpreted through ideology |
| Purpose of research | Find universals | Uncover power relationships; empower the oppressed |
| Acceptable methods | Scientific method | Subjective inquiry based on ideology and values; both quantitative and qualitative |
| Meaning of data | Falsification | Interpreted through ideology; used to enlighten and emancipate |
| Research-practice relationship | Separate; research guides practice | Integrated activities; research guides practice |

### Criticism of Critical Theory
1. **Elitism**: Critical theorists claim they are *better equipped* to analyse a situation and prescribe solutions → can be paternalistic and out of touch
2. **Out of touch**: Focus on persons in power; not listening to those most adversely affected by policies
3. **Prescriptions may be naïve and unworkable** in contemporary settings

### Definitions
- **Critical Theory**: A theoretical tradition developed at the Frankfurt School; focuses on uncovering oppressive power structures and emancipating the oppressed; based on ideology of class conflict
- **Substantialism**: The ontological position of Critical Theory — matter constitutes reality
- **Historical Ontology**: Reality is apprehendable but shaped and crystallised by historical social, political, cultural, and economic forces
- **Ideology Critique**: A methodology that examines and challenges hidden ideological assumptions embedded in knowledge and social practices
- **Action Research**: Research methodology that combines understanding with practical action to change the social situation
- **Retroduction**: Research strategy starting with a hypothetical model of a mechanism that explains a phenomenon (associated with Critical Realism)
- **Critical Realism**: The view that rational analysis is fundamental to human emancipation; acknowledges subjectivism but rejects pure relativism

### Quick Recall
> **Critical Theory** = Frankfurt School + Marxist roots + Oppression focus + Retroduction strategy  
> **3-Step Task**: Understand ideological distortion → Explore causal forces → Show forces can be overcome  
> **Validity**: Does research expose oppression and help overcome it?

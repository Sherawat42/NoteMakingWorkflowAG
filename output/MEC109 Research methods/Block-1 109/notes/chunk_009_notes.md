## Section: Applications in Research — Illustrative Cases 🔴
<!-- Exam importance: 🔴 HIGH YIELD -->

### Core Idea
The abstract distinction between Interpretive and Critical Theory paradigms becomes concrete through applied examples. The same research context can be studied from both frameworks — but with different **questions** and **focus**.

### Case 1: Invisible Labour-Force in India (Interpretive Approach)
**Context**: ~160 million women (Census 2011, aged 15–59) reported as "not working" — engaged primarily in domestic/care work (housewives, nannies, cleaners). These are "invisible" workers.

**Interpretive Framework**:
- Social actors: housewives, nannies, cleaners
- Qualitative methods: phenomenology, symbolic interactionism, PRA/RRA techniques
- Research questions: "Why have women not taken up work outside home?" / "Are there social pressures for women to stick to domestic work?"
- Gather perspectives from: unpaid care-workers, family members, village headmen
- Understand: Social, cultural, and political causes of invisibility

**Critical Theory Framework (on same case)**:
- Focus shifts to **power relationships**: between care-workers and their families, social circle, and environmental factors
- Awareness campaigns and policy design to empower care workers
- Advocate for labour statisticians to recognise care work within the ambit of "work"

### Case 2: Absenteeism in Primary Schools in Rural Areas (Interpretive Approach)
**Context**: High absenteeism among primary school students despite increased enrolment (MDG goal).

**Interpretive Framework**:
- Social actors: primary school children, teachers, parents
- Each actor's perspective on absenteeism is different → need to capture all
- Qualitative methods and tools to understand multiple perspectives

### Case 3: Employer Attitude toward Distance-Educated Job Applicants (Interpretive Approach)
**Context**: Distance-educated youth perceived as less efficient; preferential bias in job market.

**Interpretive Framework**:
- In-depth study of employer mindset
- Actual observational techniques or witness accounts of employer-applicant interactions
- Research questions: variability in questions posed to candidates; grounds for negative perception
- Common employer reasons cited: lack of rigor, no face-to-face interaction, academic dishonesty concerns, association with "diploma mills"

### Key Insight: How Critical Theory Follows Interpretive Research
> "Critical theory is also used subsequently after interpretive research as it is easier to critically analyse a problem after undertaking a deeper understanding of that problem."

- After understanding causes of invisible workers (interpretive), a **critical researcher** analyses power relationships between care workers and their social circle
- The two paradigms are **complementary** — they supplement each other

### Quick Recall
> **Interpretive**: Why does this phenomena exist? (meanings, context, perspectives)  
> **Critical Theory**: Who benefits? Who is oppressed? How can power structures be changed?  
> **Complementary**: Do interpretive research first, then apply critical lens

## Section: Unit 5 Summary — Three Paradigms Compared 🟡
<!-- Exam importance: 🟡 MODERATE -->

### Core Idea
The three paradigms — Post-positivism, Interpretivism, and Critical Theory — are the dominant guiding frameworks in social science research, each with distinct views on reality, knowledge, and purpose.

### Final Synthesis

| Paradigm | Nature of Reality | Core Focus | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Post-positivism** | External to human mind; real but imperfectly known | Find universal laws/regularities | Deduction |
| **Interpretivism** | Socially constructed; contextual and subjective | Understand meanings and local understanding | Abduction |
| **Critical Theory** | Material; shaped by historical power structures | Uncover oppression; empower the oppressed | Retroduction |

**Interpretivism**: Proposed abandoning the search for generalisable truths about human behaviour → concentrate on **local understanding**

**Critical Theory**: Response to the complexities of modern nation states leading to domination and exploitation → focus on **social change through awareness**

### Quick Recall
> **Post-positivism** → Universals (Deduction)  
> **Interpretivism** → Local Understanding (Abduction)  
> **Critical Theory** → Emancipation from Oppression (Retroduction)

## Section: Appendix I — Criteria for Interpretive Case Studies (Theory Building) 🟢
<!-- Exam importance: 🟢 CONTEXT -->

### Core Idea
Yin (2003) and grounded theory principles provide structured criteria to judge the rigour and validity of interpretive case studies used for theory building.

### Case Study Validity Criteria (Yin, 2003)

| Criterion | Definition | Case Study Tactics | Grounded Theory Principles |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Construct Validity** | Establishing correct operational measures for the concepts being studied | Use multiple sources for evidence; establish chain of evidence; have key informants review draft | Corroboration; Theoretical sufficiency |
| **Internal Validity** | Establishing causal relationships (as distinguished from spurious relationships) | Do pattern matching; do explanation-building; address rival explanations; use logic models | Theoretical coding |
| **External Validity** | Establishing the domain to which the study's findings can be generalised | Use theory in single case studies; use replication logic in multiple case studies | Theoretical generalisation |
| **Reliability** | Demonstrating that a study can be repeated with the same results | Use case study protocol; develop case study database | Chain of evidence as afforded by grounded theory method |

### Key Term
- **Grounded Theory**: "The discovery of theory from data" — it provides the opportunity for the researcher to theorise from evidence existing in the data (also the basis of theoretical coding and generalisation)

### Quick Recall
> **4 Criteria**: Construct Validity | Internal Validity | External Validity | Reliability  
> **Grounded Theory**: Theory discovered from data itself

### Quick Comparison: All Major Contrasts

#### Positivism vs Popper vs Kuhn
| Dimension | Positivism | Popper | Kuhn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hallmark of Science | Verifiability | Falsifiability | Consensus of Community |
| Method | Induction | Hypothetico-Deduction | — |
| Observation | Pure/theory-neutral | Theory-dependent | Theory-dependent |
| Progress | Cumulative (old + epsilon) | Discontinuous; verisimilitude | Revolutionary; paradigm replacement |
| Rationality | Justifiable induction | Critical thinking | Community judgment |
| Objectivity | Pure observation | Inter-subjective testability | Consensus-based |

#### The Four Research Strategies
| Strategy | Paradigm | Starting Point |
|---|---|---|
| Induction | Positivism | Data |
| Deduction | Post-positivism/Realism | Theory |
| Retroduction | Critical Realism | Hypothetical mechanism |
| Abduction | Interpretivism | Social meanings |

#### The Four Measurement Scales (NOIR)
| Scale | Properties | Statistical Tests |
|---|---|---|
| Nominal | Names only | Mode, chi-square |
| Ordinal | Names + order | Median, rank correlation |
| Interval | Names + order + equal intervals | Mean, SD, regression, ANOVA |
| Ratio | All above + true zero | All of above + ratios |

Ricardo → Senior (Economic Man) → Mill (ceteris paribus) → J.N. Keynes (methodological pluralism) → Robbins (apriorism) → Hutchison (testability) → Friedman (instrumentalism/F-Twist) → Samuelson (operationalism) → Sen (heterogeneity)

## 21.2 National Accounts Statistics 🔴 HIGH YIELD

### 21.2.1 Structure of the Indian Statistical System

#### Primary Agency: CSO (Central Statistical Organisation)
- Under the **Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation (MoSPI)**
- Responsible for compiling **National Accounts Statistics (NAS)**
- Publishes NAS annually since **1956** (initially on a calendar-year basis, later financial-year)

#### Key Publication: NAS (National Accounts Statistics)
- Presents estimates on a **financial year** basis (April–March)
- Contains **advance/quick estimates** and **provisional/final estimates**
- Frequently revised — final estimates released with a lag

#### International Framework
- India's national accounts follow the **System of National Accounts (SNA)** — international standard maintained by UN, World Bank, IMF, OECD, and Eurostat.
- India adopted **SNA 1993** methodology; has since moved towards **SNA 2008**

---

### 21.2.2 National Accounts Aggregates and Concepts 🔴

| Concept | Definition |
|---|---|
| **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** | Total value of goods and services produced within the domestic territory during a year |
| **GNP (Gross National Product)** | GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) |
| **NDP** | GDP – Depreciation (Consumption of Fixed Capital) |
| **NNP** | GNP – Depreciation |
| **National Income (NI)** | NNP at Factor Cost |

#### Three Ways to Measure GDP

| Approach | Method | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Production Approach** | Sum of Gross Value Added (GVA) across all sectors | Output side |
| **Expenditure Approach** | C + I + G + (X – M) = GDP | Demand side |
| **Income Approach** | Sum of factor incomes (wages, profits, rent, interest) | Income side |

> 🔴 **Exam Tip**: GDP at Market Price = GDP at Factor Cost + Net Indirect Taxes
> NNP at Factor Cost = National Income

#### GDP vs GVA
- From 2015, CSO moved to **GVA at basic prices** as the primary headline indicator, with GDP derived by adding taxes and subtracting subsidies on products.

---

### Key CSO Publications on National Accounts 🔴

| Publication | Content |
|---|---|
| **National Accounts Statistics (NAS)** | Annual publication; GDP, GNP, NNP, sector-wise GVA, saving, capital formation |
| **Statement of National Product and Expenditure** | Embedded in NAS, detailed breakdown |
| **Quick Estimates** | Released January 31 of the reference year |
| **Advance Estimates** | Released February 7 of the reference year |
| **CSO Press Releases** | Monthly / quarterly GDP estimates |

---

### Institutional Architecture of National Accounts 🟡

- **CSO** coordinates with:
  - **State Directorates of Economics & Statistics (DES)** — for state-level accounts (GSDP)
  - **RBI** — for financial sector data (saving, investment)
  - **NSSO** — for household consumption and employment surveys
  - **Line Ministries** — for sector-specific data

---

### GDP Estimation by Sector 🔴

Data for different sectors comes from different primary sources:

| Sector | Primary Data Source |
|---|---|
| Agriculture | Directorate of Economics & Statistics (DES), Ministry of Agriculture |
| Industry (Factory) | Annual Survey of Industries (ASI) |
| Industry (Unorganized) | NSSO Unorganized Sector Surveys |
| Services | Administrative data, NSSO surveys |
| Public Administration | Budget documents |
| Finance | Reserve Bank of India (RBI) |

## The NAS Release Calendar & Types of Estimates 🔴 HIGH YIELD

CSO follows an **advance release calendar** for NAS. The types of estimates are:

| Estimate Type | Release Timing |
|---|---|
| **Advance Estimates** | ~February (2 months before close of financial year) |
| **Revised Advance Estimates** | End of June (3 months after close of financial year) |
| **First Revised Estimates (Quick Estimates)** | January 31 of the next financial year (10 month lag) |
| **Final Estimates** | Published in May in NAS |
| **Quarterly Estimates of GDP** | 45 days after close of each quarter |

> 🔴 **Exam Tip**: Understand the sequence: Advance → Revised Advance → First Revised (Quick) → Final. Each stage involves significant revisions.

## GVA vs GDP: The New Framework 🔴

- From the **2011-12 base year series**, CSO adopted **GVA at basic prices** as the primary measure (not GDP at factor cost).
- **GDP at Market Prices = GVA at Basic Prices + Net Taxes on Products**
- **GVA at Basic Prices = GVA at Factor Cost + Net Taxes on Production**

---

## Direct vs Indirect Estimates in NAS 🔴

| Type | Based on | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct Estimates** | Annual data available regularly | Public sector, Private corporate sector (organised) |
| **Indirect Estimates** | Benchmark surveys + extrapolation using physical indicators | Households, NPISH, Unorganised sector |

- Share of **direct estimates in GDP** has progressively increased:
  - 57.6% (1970-71 base) → 63.7% (1980-81 base) → **89.6% (1993-94 base)**
  - Unorganised sector was only **44.7% of the economy** in 2012-13.

## 21.3.3 The Input-Output Transactions Table (IOTT) 🔴

### What is IOTT?
- A matrix that shows **inter-sectoral flows** of goods and services in the economy.
- Captures how each sector depends on others for **inputs** and supplies to others as **outputs**.
- Essential for: development planning, target-setting for output/investment/employment, impact analysis.

### Key Sources
- **CSO** publishes the IOTT periodically. Latest published: **2007-08** (available on MoSPI website)
- **Planning Commission** also prepares Input-Output Tables for each Five-Year Plan:
  - One for the **base period** of the plan
  - One for the **terminal year** of the plan
  - Published as "**Technical Note to the Five Year Plan**"
  - Latest in this series: Technical Note to the **Tenth Five Year Plan**

## 21.3.4 Regional Accounts — State Domestic Product (SDP) 🔴

### Primary Agency
- **Directorates of Economics & Statistics (DES)** of all States and Union Territories prepare estimates of:
  - **GSDP** (Gross State Domestic Product)
  - **NSDP** (Net State Domestic Product)
  - Both at **current and constant prices**
- **Exceptions**: Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Daman & Diu, and Lakshadweep do NOT prepare SDP estimates.

### Other Sources for SDP Data

| Publication | Data Provided |
|---|---|
| **CSO website** | All State estimates at current & constant prices |
| **Pre-Budget Economic Survey** | Time series of NSDP at current prices from 1993-94 |
| **RBI Handbook** | NSDP at current & constant prices, by economic activity |
| **CMIE (EIS)** | Time series of GSDP (shorter period) |
| **EPWRF** | Long time series from 1960-61 to 2000-01, sector-wise |
| **Finance Commission Reports** | Comparable SDP estimates for each Finance Commission period |

### Income Originating vs Income Accruing Approaches 🔴

| Approach | Measures | Used in India? |
|---|---|---|
| **Income Originating** | Income produced by factors *within* the state | ✅ YES — Used |
| **Income Accruing** | Income received by *residents* of the state | ❌ NO — Not used (inter-state flows unavailable) |

> 🔑 **Key Point**: India follows the income originating approach for SDP. This understates welfare because it does not account for inter-state income flows (e.g., commuters, migrant workers).

### Comparability of SDP Estimates
- SDP estimates of different States are **NOT strictly comparable** due to differences in methodology.
- Finance Commissions compile **comparable NSDP estimates** for their reference periods.
- EPWRF publication offers comparable estimates across periods for research use.

---

## 21.3.5 Regional Accounts — District Domestic Product (DDP) 🟡

- Preparation of **DDP** is urgent for **decentralized planning** and computation of **district-level HDI**.
- Prepared by **State DES** and published in State Statistical Handbooks/websites.
- **Only the income originating approach** is used (inter-district flows unavailable).
- Data gaps:
  - Agricultural sector: data is fairly available at district level.
  - Unorganised sector: reliable district-level data is not available due to small sample sizes in surveys.
- **States NOT preparing DDP**: Goa, Gujarat, J&K, Tripura, Delhi, Puducherry.

---

## 21.3.6 National Income & Levels of Living 🟢

- National Income aggregates (GDP, per capita income) reflect **averages** — they don't reveal distribution.
- For analysis of welfare and poverty, one needs **distribution data**:
  - **NSSO Quinquennial Consumer Expenditure Surveys** (latest: 68th Round, 2011-12) → Distribution of households by MPCE classes.
  - **Human Development Index** and **Gender Development Index** from Planning Commission.
  - **National Human Development Report (NHDR)** and **State HDRs**.

## 21.4 SAVING 🔴 HIGH YIELD

### Definition
> **Saving = Income – Consumption** (at the economy level: GNP – Final Consumption – Depreciation)

- Saving finances **Investment**, which drives economic **growth**.
- The **Rate of Saving (as % of GNP/GDP)** is a key macro indicator.

### Primary Source: CSO (NAS)
- **Gross Domestic Saving (GDS)** and **Net Domestic Saving (NDS)** are published in:
  - **NAS**: Detailed annual estimates in current prices
  - **Press Note of January**: Quick estimates, released with 10-month lag
- **NDS = GDS – Consumption of Fixed Capital (Depreciation)**

### Structure of Saving (by Institutional Sector)

| Sector | Components |
|---|---|
| **Public Sector** | Govt. administration, Departmental enterprises, Non-departmental enterprises |
| **Private Corporate Sector** | Joint stock companies (financial + non-financial), Cooperative banks |
| **Household Sector** | (Net) Financial Saving + Saving in Physical Assets |

#### Household Sector Financial Saving comprises:
- Currency
- Net Deposits
- Shares and Debentures
- Net Claim on Government
- Life Insurance Funds
- Provident and Pension Funds

> 🔴 **Exam Tip**: Saving in physical assets by the household sector is derived as a **residual** (not from direct data).

### Other Sources for Saving Data

| Publication | Time Series |
|---|---|
| Pre-Budget Economic Survey | Structure of GDS by institutions (from 1950-51) |
| RBI Handbook | GDS, NDS, by institutions and financial assets/liabilities (from 1952-53 / 1970-71) |
| EPWRF (Fifth Edition) | All variables from 1950-51; also Public Sector saving by institution type |
| CMIE | GDS and NDS (shorter time series) |

### Limitations of Saving Estimates 🔴
- **Household sector physical saving** is derived as a residual — no direct data.
- Financial saving estimates are poor for **private corporate** sector and **NPISH**.
- **State-level saving data**: NOT available — DES of States/UTs do NOT prepare saving estimates.

## 21.5 INVESTMENT (Capital Formation) 🔴 HIGH YIELD

### Key Definitions

| Term | Full Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **GDCF** | Gross Domestic Capital Formation | Total investment in the economy |
| **GDFCF** | Gross Domestic Fixed Capital Formation | Investment in fixed assets only |
| **NDCF** | Net Domestic Capital Formation | GDCF – Depreciation |
| **CFC** | Consumption of Fixed Capital | Depreciation |
| **NCS / NFCS** | Net Capital Stock / Net Fixed Capital Stock | Existing capital stock |

> Investment = Creation of physical (produced) assets — NOT buying shares or land.

### Two Approaches to Estimate Capital Formation
1. **Flow of Funds Approach**: GDS + Net Capital Inflows from Abroad
2. **Commodity Flow Approach**: Derived by type of asset (construction, machinery & equipment)

### Primary Source: CSO (NAS)
- Published annually in **NAS** in detail (current & constant prices)
- Presents estimates by:
  - **Institutions**: Public sector, Private corporate sector, Household sector
  - **Types of Assets**: Construction, Machinery & Equipment
  - **Economic Activity**: Agriculture, Industry, Services
  - **Capital Stock**: NCS and NFCS as on 31st March each year

### Other Sources for Capital Formation Data

| Publication | Variable | Time Series From |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-Budget Economic Survey | GDCF, GFCF, Change in Stocks by institutions | 1950-51 |
| RBI Handbook | GDCF, NDCF; GDCF/GFCF by institutions; Financial assets of HH sector | 1950-51 / 1970-71 |
| EPWRF (NAS, Fifth Ed.) | Detailed breakdown including capital-output ratios | 1950-51 |
| CMIE | GDCF and GFCF | 1960-61 |

### State-Level Capital Formation
- **14 States** prepare estimates of GFCF: Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, MP, Odisha, Meghalaya, Assam, AP, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan.
- **NOT all States** prepare these estimates.

### Gaps in Capital Formation Data 🔴
1. No regular data on machinery in **unregistered manufacturing**
2. No annual enterprise surveys for **NPISHs**
3. Incomplete accounts of **local bodies** in some States/UTs
4. **Change in Stocks** relies on bank advances and indirect approaches; benchmarks come from quinquennial surveys
5. Problems with **sample representativeness** and blow-up factors for private corporate sector

## 21.6 Let Us Sum Up — Key Takeaways 🟢

- **MoSPI** (through CSO and NSSO) is the apex statistical body in India.
- NAS (published by CSO) is the definitive source for GDP, National Income, Saving, and Capital Formation.
- RBI, CMIE, and EPWRF aggregate and republish CSO data in time-series format for easy access.
- Data can be accessed at `www.data.gov.in` (Government data portal).
- All limitations in estimation arise from **data gaps** — especially in the unorganised sector.

## 22.2 Agricultural Data 🔴 HIGH YIELD

### 22.2.1 Agricultural Census 🔴

#### What is it?
- A **quinquennial (every 5 years)** census of all operational holdings in India.
- Latest completed census: **2010-11** (the 9th Agricultural Census).

#### Conducted by:
- **Directorate of Economics & Statistics (DES)**, Ministry of Agriculture & Cooperation

#### Data Collected
- **Size and structure** of agricultural holdings
- Area, land use, source of irrigation, tenancy, land utilization
- Socio-economic characteristics of operators (gender, social group, age, education)
- **Village-level and district-level** detailed data

#### Publication
- Results published in **Agricultural Census reports** by DES, Ministry of Agriculture
- Detailed village/district/state tables available on the Ministry's **website**

> 🔴 **Exam Tip**: Agricultural Census is the ONLY comprehensive, periodic source of data on all operational holdings including small, marginal, and sub-marginal holders.

---

### 22.2.2 Studies on Cost of Cultivation 🔴

#### Conducted by:
- **DES, Ministry of Agriculture** — ongoing studies since the 1960s

#### Coverage:
- **25-32 crops** studied across major crop-growing states
- Covers all: food grains, oilseeds, commercial crops, spices, etc.

#### Data Collected:
- **Cost of production** per unit of output (per quintal or per hectare)
- Input costs: seed, fertilizers, labour, irrigation, machinery
- **Comprehensive cost concepts**: A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2, C3 (increasingly inclusive)
- Output price, profit margin
- **Pricing recommendations** made to Commission on Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP)

#### Publication:
- *"Cost of Cultivation in India"* — published by DES, Ministry of Agriculture
- CACP reports also refer to this data

> 🔴 **Exam Key**: The MSP (Minimum Support Price) is significantly influenced by cost of cultivation data.

---

### 22.2.3 Annual Estimates of Crop Production 🔴

#### Prepared by:
- **DES, Ministry of Agriculture** in collaboration with **State Directorates of Agriculture**

#### Method:
- Based on **crop-cutting experiments** (a sample survey method)
- **Five rounds** per year to provide a running picture of crop production:
  - 1st Advance Estimate (in September/October)
  - 2nd Advance Estimate (in December/January)
  - 3rd Advance Estimate (in February/March)
  - 4th Advance Estimate (in May/June)
  - Final Estimates (with a lag)

#### Data Available:
- **Area sown**, **area harvested**, **yield per hectare**, **total production**
- For both Rabi (winter) and Kharif (monsoon) crops
- Covers all **principal food and non-food crops**

#### Key Publication:
- *"Agricultural Statistics at a Glance" (ASG)* — comprehensive annual publication by DES, Ministry of Agriculture
- Also published in **Economic Survey** as summary tables

---

### 22.2.4 Livestock Census 🔴

#### Conducted by:
- **Animal Husbandry Statistics Division (AHSD)**, Ministry of Agriculture
- **Quinquennial** — every 5 years (the 19th Livestock Census was conducted in 2012)

#### Data Collected:
- **Number and type** of livestock and poultry animals (species, breed, sex, age)
- Implements and machinery used in agriculture
- Coverage down to the **village level**

#### Publication:
- Data available in *"Basic Animal Husbandry Statistics" (BAHS)* and on Ministry website

---

### 22.2.5 Data on Production of Major Livestock Products 🟡

#### Conducted by:
- **Integrated Sample Survey (ISS)** — an annual survey by the Directorate of Animal Husbandry
- State Governments send data to Central Government

#### Data Collected:
- Milk production by type (cow, buffalo, goat)
- Egg production
- Wool production
- Meat production

#### Publication:
- **BAHS** (Basic Animal Husbandry Statistics)

---

### 22.2.6 Agricultural Statistics at a Glance (ASG) 🔴

> The **go-to single reference** for all agricultural statistics in India.

- Published **annually** by DES, Ministry of Agriculture
- Comprehensive — covers all aspects:
  - Production, area, yield
  - Land use and land utilization
  - Fertilizer and pesticide consumption
  - Agricultural credit (also refer to RBI Handbook and NABARD publications)
  - Irrigation (also from Central Water Commission)
  - Cost of cultivation and CACP data
  - **Characteristics of land holdings** by size, across multiple Agricultural Censuses
  - Livestock and poultry statistics
  - Agricultural prices and indices

---

### 22.2.7 Another Source of Data on Irrigation 🟡

- **Central Water Commission (CWC)**, Ministry of Water Resources — publishes data on:
  - Potential and utilization of surface and ground water resources
  - Command areas of large and medium irrigation projects
- **Central Ground Water Board (CGWB)** — ground water resource data
- ASG also presents irrigation statistics from these agencies

---

### 22.2.8 Other Data on the Agricultural Sector 🟡

| Source | Data |
|---|---|
| **NABARD** | Agricultural credit and rural finance |
| **RBI Handbook** | Agricultural credit time series |
| **FAO Statistical Yearbooks** | Global-level agriculture data for comparative research |

### 22.3.2 Factory Sector — Annual Survey of Industries (ASI) 🔴 MOST IMPORTANT INDUSTRIAL DATA SOURCE

#### Coverage:
- All **factories registered** under:
  - Factories Act, 1948
  - Bidi and Cigar Workers (Conditions of Employment) Act, 1966
- Excludes: Defence establishments, factories under the Atomic Energy Act

#### Conducted by:
- **CSO (Central Statistical Organisation)**, MoSPI
- Data collected through a **census approach** for large factories and **sample approach** for smaller ones

#### Frequency:
- **Annual** survey — results available with a lag of approximately 2 years

#### Data Collected (~30 Variables per Factory):
- **Investment**: Fixed assets — land, buildings, machinery, equipment
- **Inputs**: Raw materials, fuels, electricity, other inputs
- **Output**: Total output at ex-factory prices
- **Value Added**: GVA (Gross Value Added) and NVA (Net Value Added)
- **Employment**: Mandays worked by workers, employees; male/female breakdown
- **Factor Shares**: Wages, salaries, employer contributions
- **Energy**: Fuel and electricity consumed

#### Classification:
- Data published at the **3 or 4-digit NIC code level** by States/Union Territories
- Enables derivation of technical ratios (capital-output ratio, labour productivity, etc.)

#### Key Database: ASICS (ASI Computer System)
- Allows users to retrieve specific data relating to ASI from an online database

#### Publication:
- **ASI Summary Results** published by CSO
- Available on MoSPI website

> 🔴 **Exam Tip**: ASI is the most detailed multi-variable source for **factory sector** data in India. It covers ~30 characteristics at 3-4 digit NIC code level.

## 23.2.1 Merchandise Trade — DGCI&S 🔴 HIGH YIELD

### Primary Agency: DGCI&S
- **Directorate General of Commercial Intelligence and Statistics (DGCI&S)**
- Located in **Kolkata** (website: www.dgciskol.nic.in)
- Collects and compiles data on **imports and exports** through all recognized seaports, airports, land customs stations, and inland container depots.

### Scope of Merchandise Trade Data
- **Exports**: Indian merchandise + re-exports; valued at **FOB (Free on Board)**
- **Imports**: Foreign merchandise (whether for consumption, bonding, or re-export); valued at **CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight)**
- Based on **General System of Recording** (not Special System)

### Trade Classification System
| Period | Classification System |
|---|---|
| April 1977 – March 1987 | ITC-Rev. 2 (based on SITC-Rev.2) |
| April 1987 onwards | **ITC (HS)** — Indian Trade Classification based on Harmonised System (8-digit codes) |

> **ITC (HS)**: Developed by DGCI&S by subdividing 6-digit codes of the **Harmonised Commodity Description and Coding System** (Harmonised System) of the **World Customs Organization (WCO), Brussels**.

### Key DGCI&S Publications

| Publication | Frequency | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Foreign Trade Statistics of India (Principal Commodities & Countries) | Monthly | Provisional commodity-wise, country-wise, port-wise data |
| Monthly Statistics of Foreign Trade of India (Vol I: Exports, Vol II: Imports) | Monthly | Detailed 8-digit ITC(HS) data |
| Statistics of Foreign Trade by Countries | Quarterly | Country-wise exports and imports |
| Selected Statistics of Foreign Trade of India | Annual | Summary tables |
| Statistics of Customs & Excise Revenue Collections | Annual | Revenue data |
| Statistics of Foreign & Coastal Cargo Movements | Annual | Cargo movement data |

### DGCI&S Website Features:
- Static pages: History, summary data on principal commodities
- Dynamic pages: Online data dissemination (free and paid); at least 24 months final data at 8-digit level
- **PISS (Priced Information Service System)**: Provides information to private parties on payment basis; provides aggregate/detailed data to CMIE for trade intelligence

---

### Index Numbers of Foreign Trade 🔴

| Index | Formula | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit Value Index (UVI)** | `∑(P₁ × Qₜ) / ∑(P₀ × Qₜ)` | Price changes (unit value) |
| **Quantum Index (QI)** | `∑(P₀ × Qₜ) / ∑(P₀ × Q₀)` | Volume/quantity changes |

### Three Types of Terms of Trade 🔴

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Gross Terms of Trade (GTT)** | 100 × [QI of Imports / QI of Exports] |
| **Net Terms of Trade (NTT)** | 100 × [UVI of Exports / UVI of Imports] |
| **Income Terms of Trade (ITT)** | [NTT × QI of Exports] / 100 = [UVI(exports) × QI(exports)] / UVI(imports) |

> Existing index base year: **1979-80** (old); DGCI&S is constructing new indices with base **1998-99**.

### Other Sources for Trade Data
- **RBI Handbook**: Presents DGCI&S data as time series; also covers BOP data
- **CMIE**: Volume on Foreign Trade and BOP
- **EPWRF**: Long time series data on foreign trade (from their 35 special statistics datasets)

### 🚨 Divergence: DGCI&S vs RBI BOP Data 🔴 VERY IMPORTANT

There is a known discrepancy between merchandise trade data from **DGCI&S** and **RBI's Balance of Payments (BOP)** data.

| Issue | DGCI&S | RBI BOP |
|---|---|---|
| **What is tracked** | Physical imports/exports at customs | Payment transactions for merchandise |
| **Government imports** | Does NOT capture (exempted from customs duty) | Captures (including defence imports) |
| **Non-customs imports** | Does NOT capture (e.g., oil rigs, some aircraft) | Captures (payments recorded in BOP) |

**Impact of Divergence (example from 2005-06)**:
- Trade deficit as % of GDP:
  - DGCI&S data: **5.2%**
  - RBI BOP data: **7.7%** — a difference of **2.5% of GDP**!
- Current Account Deficit (CAD):
  - DGCI&S based: **0.3% of GDP**
  - RBI BOP based: **2.9% of GDP**

> 🔴 **Exam Key**: Always specify the source when quoting trade deficit figures. DGCI&S and RBI BOP data are NOT directly comparable.

### Key Fiscal Deficit Concepts 🔴

| Term | Formula |
|---|---|
| **GFD** (Gross Fiscal Deficit) | Total expenditure (including loans net of recovery) – Revenue receipts (incl. external grants + non-debt capital receipts) |
| **NFD** (Net Fiscal Deficit) | GFD – Net lending of Central Govt |
| **GPD** (Gross Primary Deficit) | GFD – Interest Payments |
| **NPD** (Net Primary Deficit) | GFD – Net Interest Payments |
| **RD** (Revenue Deficit) | Revenue Receipts – Revenue Expenditure |
| **PRD** (Primary Revenue Deficit) | RD – Interest Payments |

> 🔴 **Exam Tip**: GFD is the most commonly cited fiscal deficit indicator. PRD eliminates interest payments to assess the "structural" deficit.

### RBI Handbook Time-Series Data on Central Government Finances
- **Key deficit indicators**: GFD, GPD, NPD, Revenue Deficit, PRD, Net RBI Credit from 1975-76
- **Receipts**: Tax revenue (direct & indirect taxes), non-tax revenue, capital receipts
- **Capital Receipts** breakdown: market borrowings, small savings, provident funds, disinvestment, external loans
- **Expenditure**: Revenue & capital expenditure; defence, interest payments, subsidies; share of economic and social services
- **GFD Financing**: External financing + internal financing (market borrowings, other borrowings, cash balance drawdown)
- **Plan Outlay**: Sectoral profile + financing sources

### State Government Finances
- **Key deficit indicators** for States: Fiscal deficit, revenue deficit, overall deficit
- **Receipts**: Tax receipts (sales tax, state excise), share of Central taxes
- **Capital Receipts**: Loans from Centre, market loans, State provident fund
- **Expenditure**: Developmental and non-developmental (interest, admin, pension)
- **States' GFD Financing**: Loans from Centre, market borrowings, NSSF securities

### Combined Central + State Finances
- Combined deficit, receipts, disbursements
- Market borrowings
- **Ownership of Government Securities** (11 categories: RBI, commercial banks, cooperative banks, primary dealers, insurance cos., FIs, mutual funds, provident funds, corporates, FIIs, others)

- Know all four NSSO measures (UPS, UPSS, CWS, CDS) — exam favourite. UPSS always gives maximum employment.
- The live register of employment exchanges is NOT a reliable measure of unemployment.

## 24.2.2 Quality/Adequacy of Employment 🔴

### Key Dimensions of Employment Quality
1. **Intermittent/casual nature of work** — proportion of workforce in 'casual employment'

3. **Employment status of poor household members**

#### NSSO Employment Survey Data (from the tables already described)

## 24.2.3 Labour Welfare 🔴

### Labour Bureau, Shimla (Ministry of Labour) — Yearbooks of Labour Statistics

Data compiled on multiple aspects of labour welfare:

#### Industrial Injuries and Accidents
| Data | Source | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Fatal and non-fatal injuries in factories | Returns under Factories Act 1948 | Significant non-response |
| Injuries in mines | Returns under Mines Act 1952 (as amended 1983) | Non-response |

Classification of accidents (from 1984):
- **Fatal** accidents
- **Spot Serious** accidents (permanent loss/fracture/injury to sight/hearing)
- **Reportable Serious** accidents (enforced absence ≥72 hours)

#### Compensated Injuries (Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923)
- Data from annual returns from State Govts, Posts & Telegraph, Railway Board
- Covers injuries resulting in **death** or **disability** (permanent/temporary)
- Limitation: NOT all injuries are compensated; many establishments don't submit returns
- ESI-covered establishments compensated under **ESI Act**, not WC Act

#### Industrial Disputes
- Number of (a) disputes, (b) workers involved, (c) mandays lost, (d) cause of disputes:
  - Wages/allowances, Bonus, Personnel matters, Retrenchment, Leave/hours, Indiscipline, Others

#### Trade Unions (Trade Unions Act, 1926)
- Data on registered organizations submitting returns:
  - Workers' organizations: number, membership, income, expenditure, balance of funds
  - Employers' organizations: same
- Limitation: Registration not obligatory; response rate among registered < 50%
- Membership data by NIC 1987 available in Yearbooks

#### Welfare Funds & Social Security
- Coal Mines Welfare Fund, Mica Mines Welfare Fund
- **ESI Corporation (ESIC)** — number of beneficiaries, contributions, benefits
- **Employees Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO)** — members, fund balances

#### Unorganised Sector Welfare (Labour Bureau surveys):
- Working and living conditions of **Scheduled Caste/Tribe workers**
- Living conditions of **unorganised workers**
- **Contract labour** conditions

## 24.6 Social Consequences of Development 🟡

### The Problem: Data Gaps on Displaced Persons

- Development projects (dams, industries, wildlife sanctuaries) displace people — "oustees"
- They were supposed to be given jobs and compensation — but data on rehabilitation is severely lacking.

#### Available Data Sources (Limited)
- **DGE&T**: Data on oustees registered as job seekers and those placed (very partial coverage)
- **ICSSR study (1994)**: Estimated displacement between 1951–1990 due to mines, dams, industries, wildlife sanctuaries; estimated resettlement up to 1980
- **Walter Fernandes study** (West Bengal, 1947–2000): Displaced people, physically displaced, resettled, left to fend for themselves; proportion of Dalits/tribals
- **Govt of Orissa data** (quoted in EPW by Manipadma Jena): Villages affected by Hirakud Dam; families displaced; families resettled

### Key Conclusion 🔴
A **transparent, systematic database** on displacement and resettlement is urgently needed — covering: demographics, resettlement status, income levels, access to basic needs, time taken, expenditure.

## 24.9 Unit 24 Let Us Sum Up — Key Takeaways 🟢

### Employment Data
- Multiple sources exist, each covering different segments: EMI (organised), ASI (factory), Economic Census (all non-crop), Census (comprehensive, decennial), NSSO (quinquennial, comprehensive).
- Estimates from different sources for the same sector WILL differ — caused by differences in concepts, definitions, and data collection modes.
- Solution: Adopt common concepts/definitions across agencies; use trained data collectors; report standard errors for sample surveys.

### Labour Welfare
- Database somewhat **incomplete** in some areas (e.g., injuries, trade unions) due to poor submission of statutory returns.
- Need for **stricter monitoring** of statutory returns and **better enforcement** of legislation.

### Education
- Enormous data on educational infrastructure, utilization, and output — periodically updated by comprehensive surveys (AIES, DISE, Census, NSSO).
- Need to address **discrepancies** between administrative (reporting-system) data and survey data.
- Need to **reduce time lags** in data availability for policy-making.

### Health
- Data built from multiple sources: RGI (vital rates), CBHI (infrastructure/healthcare), NFHS (nutrition and health status of women and children).
- Coverage is comprehensive in terms of items, but **time lags** in data availability (especially State-level coverage) need to be reduced.
- NFHS is especially valuable for data on women's health, nutrition, family planning.

### Shelter and Amenities
- Updated by Census 2011 and NSSO 69th Round (2012) — includes urban slum data.
- Progressive improvement in coverage.

### Environment
- Database is being built steadily (air, water, soil, noise).
- Not yet comprehensive, but growing with involvement of MOEF, CSO, and Pollution Control Boards.

### ⚠️ Critical Gap: Displacement Data
> *"There is no data worth the name on people displaced from their livelihoods by development projects and the number among these who have been resettled in alternative livelihood."*

- A **growing consensus** that the burden of development costs falls mostly on the poor and voiceless.
- Urgent need for a **transparent database** on displaces persons and their resettlement.

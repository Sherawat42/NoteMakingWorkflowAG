## 24.9 Unit 24 Let Us Sum Up — Key Takeaways 🟢

### Employment Data
- Multiple sources exist, each covering different segments: EMI (organised), ASI (factory), Economic Census (all non-crop), Census (comprehensive, decennial), NSSO (quinquennial, comprehensive).
- Estimates from different sources for the same sector WILL differ — caused by differences in concepts, definitions, and data collection modes.
- Solution: Adopt common concepts/definitions across agencies; use trained data collectors; report standard errors for sample surveys.

### Labour Welfare
- Database somewhat **incomplete** in some areas (e.g., injuries, trade unions) due to poor submission of statutory returns.
- Need for **stricter monitoring** of statutory returns and **better enforcement** of legislation.

### Education
- Enormous data on educational infrastructure, utilization, and output — periodically updated by comprehensive surveys (AIES, DISE, Census, NSSO).
- Need to address **discrepancies** between administrative (reporting-system) data and survey data.
- Need to **reduce time lags** in data availability for policy-making.

### Health
- Data built from multiple sources: RGI (vital rates), CBHI (infrastructure/healthcare), NFHS (nutrition and health status of women and children).
- Coverage is comprehensive in terms of items, but **time lags** in data availability (especially State-level coverage) need to be reduced.
- NFHS is especially valuable for data on women's health, nutrition, family planning.

### Shelter and Amenities
- Updated by Census 2011 and NSSO 69th Round (2012) — includes urban slum data.
- Progressive improvement in coverage.

### Environment
- Database is being built steadily (air, water, soil, noise).
- Not yet comprehensive, but growing with involvement of MOEF, CSO, and Pollution Control Boards.

### ⚠️ Critical Gap: Displacement Data
> *"There is no data worth the name on people displaced from their livelihoods by development projects and the number among these who have been resettled in alternative livelihood."*

- A **growing consensus** that the burden of development costs falls mostly on the poor and voiceless.
- Urgent need for a **transparent database** on displaces persons and their resettlement.

## ✅ Final Self-Review
- The social sector database in India is **comprehensive but fragmented** — different agencies compile data on different aspects.
- The biggest **gap** is in data on people displaced by development projects.
- **NFHS** and **Census** are the two most powerful comprehensive sources for social sector data.
- NHDR + State HDRs synthesize data across all social dimensions into composite indices (HDI, GDI).
- For any exam question about a social sector data source — identify: (1) the agency, (2) the publication name, (3) the frequency, and (4) the key data items.

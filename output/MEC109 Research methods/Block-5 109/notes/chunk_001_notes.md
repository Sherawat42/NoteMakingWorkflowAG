## Section: Participatory Research 🔴

### Core Idea
Participatory research involves the direct participation of the researcher in generating data rather than simply collecting it. This approach prioritizes a face-to-face interaction where researchers have the flexibility to seek multiple answers, interpret meanings, and emphasize the insider's perspective (EMIC view). 

> **In Simple Terms:** Instead of sending a long list of rigid questions and trying to fit people's complex lives into multiple-choice boxes, the researcher goes into the field, interacts directly, and reconstructs the real stories of the people being studied.

### Key Concepts

#### Data Generation vs. Data Collection
While quantitative research collects data using standardized forms assuming uniform understanding, participatory researchers generate data. This means researchers ask, adapt, and probe continuously to get nuanced, non-standardized responses from individuals, bringing plurality to the findings.

#### Ethnography and EMIC View
Originating from anthropology, ethnography provides qualitative data from an insider's (EMIC) perspective. It insists that the subjects' own voices are prioritized rather than having the researcher's opinion imposed on them.

### Definitions
- **Participatory Research**: A qualitative framework emphasizing direct interaction in generating data from an insider's perspective, preserving the voices and nuances of the respondents.
- **EMIC view**: An insider's perspective where the people being studied tell their stories and the researcher interprets them as "thick descriptions". ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Data generation**: The process of interactive data procurement where a researcher uses flexibility to reconstruct queries and generate plural responses. ⭐ (exam-important)

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Thinking data collection and data generation are the same. → ✅ Correct: Data collection uses structured, rigid schedules, whereas data generation relies on interactive, flexible generation of meaning from subjects.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Participatory research implies direct respondent-researcher interaction.
> - Relies on Emic view (ethnography).
> - Generates data flexibly instead of mere collection.

## Section: Methods of Participatory Research: Observation Method 🔴

### Core Idea
Observation is the qualitative method par excellence, tracing its roots to Bronislaw Malinowski's participant observation among Trobriand Islanders. Instead of solely relying on questions, researchers explicitly or implicitly observe the community to draw authentic inferences. It encompasses participant, non-participant, and quasi-participant observations.

> **In Simple Terms:** The researcher becomes part of the scenery, sometimes actively blending into the group and sometimes just watching from the sidelines, to understand how people genuinely behave without the pressure of an interview.

### Key Concepts

#### Participant Observation
The investigator disguises themselves to be accepted as a member of the group, living extended periods in the field. This helps in securing unhindered, unbiased data directly observing natural behaviors. However, it lacks standardization and risks the researcher losing objectivity due to emotional involvement (e.g. issues of dowry, trade unions).

#### Non-Participant Observation
Researchers observe the subjects without integrating into the group dynamics. Sometimes they use hidden cameras or external video-recordings. While it avoids emotional blurring, critics argue that external viewing without full participation risks misinterpreting the context or true meaning of actions.

#### Quasi-Participant Observation
A hybrid, dual-entry method. The researcher participates in certain activities while observing others from the outside. For example, interviewing a manager inside a factory while interacting socially with workers outside constraints of the workplace.

### Definitions
- **Participant Observation**: A procedure where the investigator disguises themselves as a member of a group to record information without disturbing normal activities. ⭐ (exam-important)
- **Non-Participant Observation**: Observing individuals without actively participating, sometimes using video to capture people's gestures.
- **Quasi-Participant Observation**: Participating in some activities and observing others from outside to balance access and context. ⭐ (exam-important)

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Believing participant observation guarantees objectivity. → ✅ Correct: It's subjective and the researcher may become emotionally involved, leading to loss of objectivity.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Malinowski is the founding father.
> - Participant observation dives deep but risks emotional bias.
> - Quasi-participant is the versatile middle ground often used today.

## Section: Other Qualitative Tools: Interviews and Histories 🟡

### Core Idea
To probe deeper into individual lives and historical contexts, participatory researchers deploy focused interviews, oral histories, and life histories. These non-structured, long-format methods allow subjects to express their views extensively, capturing social processes and changing realities over time.

> **In Simple Terms:** By just sitting with someone and letting them tell their life stories or having an unstructured open chat, the researcher gets a treasure trove of hidden facts that simple questionnaires usually miss.

### Key Concepts

#### Focused Interview
Involves face-to-face conversations where unstructured, open-ended questions are posed. The goal is precisely detailed answers driven by social relations and trust established between the researcher and interviewee.

#### Oral Histories
Uninterrupted long interviews allowing respondents to recount past experiences and perceptions over time. Careful listening reveals subsidiary information that can frame broader economic or social contexts.

#### Life History
Reconstructing individual lives alongside diaries and autobiographies to map historical events. It captures social processes, highlighting gender/class differences, and even interprets the emotional nuances in a respondent's silence.

### Definitions
- **Focused Interview**: One-to-one, unstructured conversation where the researcher poses open-ended questions allowing extensive answers based on social trust.
- **Oral Histories**: Method giving space to respondents to share their life experiences and perceptions of processes and changes over time.
- **Life History**: Reconstructing an individual's history using memories and diaries to understand the historical context and social processes. ⭐ (exam-important)

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Unstructured formats require respondent trust.
> - Silence in life histories provides critical data points representing anxiety or intense emotion.

### Core Idea
Business economists use the case study method to study a firm or organization’s behavior holistically by focusing entirely on that single unit to draw detailed contexts.

> **In Simple Terms:** It's like doing a deep background check on a single company to study its DNA and behaviors from every angle.

## Section: Steps Involved in Action Research 🔴

### Core Idea
Action research follows a systematic process of six sequential steps — from identifying a problem through collecting data, analyzing it, and evaluating actions. The process can be conducted individually or collaboratively, and is always locally situated and practice-oriented.

> **In Simple Terms:** AR is like a doctor treating a patient — identify the illness, research possible causes, gather tests, analyse, prescribe, and then evaluate if the prescription worked before trying again.

### Mechanisms / Processes — Six Steps in Action Research ⭐

**Step 1: Identification of the Problem Area and Developing a Focus**
- Need arises from perceived dissatisfaction with an existing situation
- Focus questions: *What is the cause of the problem? Why is it happening? What can I do? Which steps can I take?*
- Choose a problem that is meaningful and solvable within stipulated time
- Prerequisites: Identifying the problem clearly before beginning

**Step 2: Formulating the Problem**
- Identify and state probable causes in concise, unambiguous terms
- Find causes underlying the problem and related issues

**Step 3: Stating Research Questions & Developing Propositions**
- State research questions clearly
- Develop a tentative theory (propositions) about the problem's genesis
- Establish conceptual and functional relationships to understand the situation

**Step 4: Data Collection** ⭐
- Most important step — determines what action is needed
- Sources of data:
  - Videotapes and audio tapes
  - Report cards
  - Attendance records, student work samples, projects, performances
  - Interviews with parents, students, teachers
  - Cumulative and anecdotal records
  - School diaries
  - Photos
  - Questionnaires
  - Focus group discussions
  - Checklists
  - Observation schedules
- Data is arranged by gender, classroom, grade level, school, etc.
- Purposive samples used for larger groups

**Step 5: Analysis and Interpretation of Data**
- Review qualitative data for common themes → summarize in table formats
- Analyze quantitative data with simple statistics: percentages, frequency tables, descriptive statistics
- Data is converted into **information** helpful for decision-making
- This stage requires maximum time
- Identifies important patterns and emerging trends

**Step 6: Discussion and Evaluating Actions**
- Review current literature relevant to the findings
- Three steps in literature review:
  1. Identify topics related to study area
  2. Gather research reports, books, and videotapes relating to the problem
  3. Organise materials to draw inferences in light of AR results
- Suggest one action at a time; observe its outcome before suggesting next
- Well-informed decision-making is the goal

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Suggesting multiple actions simultaneously in AR. → ✅ Correct: Suggest one action at a time to isolate and clearly observe its effects on the situation.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - 6 steps: Identify → Formulate → Research Questions → Data Collection → Analysis → Discussion/Evaluation
> - Data collection = most important step
> - Step 5 = maximum time needed
> - Suggest one action at a time!

## Section: Advantages and Disadvantages of Action Research 🔴

### Core Idea
Action research offers practical, participatory advantages over conventional research — but is significantly more demanding to execute, harder to generalize, and faces methodological scrutiny from mainstream researchers.

> **In Simple Terms:** AR is powerful for people who work *in* the system they're studying, but it demands much more from the researcher in terms of effort, justification, and methodological rigor.

### Advantages

| # | Advantage |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Workplace/community applicable** — Practitioners (agents of change) can use it as part of their normal activities |
| 2 | **Maximises conscious learning** — The AR cycle is also a learning cycle; systematic reflection is effective practitioner development |
| 3 | **Participative and ethical** — Partnership with clients; more ethically satisfying; occupationally relevant |
| 4 | **Helps determine policy** — Useful for developing policy related to identified problems |

### Disadvantages

| # | Disadvantage |
|---|---|
| 1 | **More difficult to execute** — Researcher responsible for change AND for research; more work to set up; no credit for setup work |
| 2 | **Counter-cultural** — Doesn't conform to expectations of some examiners; deliberately ignores some conventional research requirements |
| 3 | **Difficult to learn** — Researcher must learn AR methodology almost from scratch; limited exposure in mainstream research training |
| 4 | **More demanding library work** — Relevant literature is defined by the data collected, not known in advance; first collect data → then find literature |
| 5 | **Difficult to report** — Especially for thesis purposes; must justify overall AR approach, not just findings |

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: Thinking the literature review in AR is done before data collection. → ✅ Correct: In most forms of AR, you collect data *first* and then go to the literature to challenge your findings (Step 6).

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Key advantage: Practitioners learn consciously from their own practice cycles
> - Key disadvantage: Counter-cultural, harder to report, literature work is demanding
> - AR literature review = after data collection (unlike conventional research)

## Section: Key Words Glossary — Action Research 🟢

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Action Research** | Learning by doing — a group identifies a problem, does something to resolve it, evaluates success, and tries again |
| **Reflective Critical Analysis** | Becoming aware of one's own perceptual biases |
| **Dialectical Critical Analysis** | Understanding relationships between elements making up phenomena in context |
| **Collaborative Resource** | Everyone's view is a contribution to understanding the situation |
| **Risk** | Understanding taken-for-granted processes and submitting them to critique |
| **Plural Structure** | Developing various accounts and critiques rather than a single authoritative interpretation |
| **Reconnaissance** | Fact finding and explanation |
| **Planning** | Identifying the issue to be changed; developing questions and research methods for the specific environment |
| **Acting** | Trialling the change; collecting and compiling evidence |
| **Observing** | Analysing evidence; discussing with co-researchers; interpretation |
| **Reflecting** | Evaluating the first cycle; implementing findings or new strategy; revisiting the process |

## Section: Block 5 Summary — Qualitative Methods 🟡

### Core Idea
Block 5 of MEC-109 covers three qualitative research methods exclusively associated with the interpretative and critical theory paradigms: Participatory Method (Unit 18), Content Analysis (Unit 19), and Action Research (Unit 20). Together, they complement the quantitative methods of Blocks 3 and 4.

### Key Frameworks Summary

| Unit | Method | Paradigm | Core Technique |
|---|---|---|---|
| **18** | Participatory Method | Interpretative | EMIC view, observation, grounded theory, FGD |
| **19** | Content Analysis | Both (Quantitative + Qualitative) | Systematic coding and analysis of recorded materials |
| **20** | Action Research | Critical Theory | Spiral cycles of planning, acting, observing, reflecting |

### Connections Across Units
- All three methods accept and acknowledge **subjectivity** as part of the research process
- Both participatory research and action research emphasize **researcher–participant collaboration**
- Content analysis can be used *within* participatory research and action research as a data analysis tool
- All three reject purely positivist, detached, hypothesis-driven research design

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Block 5 = Qualitative Methods under Interpretative and Critical Theory paradigms
> - Participatory = data generation from inside; Content Analysis = systematic text analysis; Action Research = change through doing
> - All three: acknowledge subjectivity, use collaboration, produce practical knowledge

# Running Context

## Key Concepts Introduced
- **Data Generation**: Process of interactively generating varied responses rather than forcing structured data collection (Chunk 001)
- **Grounded Theory**: Inductive theorizing where theory emerges from empirical data during research (Chunk 002)
- **Constant Comparative Method**: Continuous comparing/contrasting of notes during qualitative analysis (Chunk 002)
- **Focus Group Discussion (FGD)**: Small group moderated discussion for obtaining perceptions on a defined topic (Chunk 002)
- **Reflexivity**: Researcher's self-awareness of biases during data collection and analysis (Chunk 002)
- **Bottom-up approach**: Planning starting from local community's felt needs rather than trickle-down expert planning (Chunk 002)
- **Ethnography/EMIC view**: Insider's perspective emphasizing respondents' own stories (Chunk 001)

## Definitions (⭐ exam-important)
- **EMIC view**: An insider's perspective where the people being studied tell their stories. (Chunk 001)
- **Data generation**: Interactive data procurement using flexible queries to generate plural responses. (Chunk 001)
- **Participant Observation**: Procedure where the investigator disguisedly records information as a group member without disturbing normal activities. (Chunk 001)
- **Quasi-Participant Observation**: Participating in some activities and observing others from outside to balance access and context. (Chunk 001)
- **Life History**: Reconstructing an individual's history using memories and diaries to understand history and social processes. (Chunk 001)
- **Case Study (Yin, 1984)**: Empirical inquiry investigating a contemporary phenomenon within its real-life context using multiple sources of evidence. (Chunk 002)
- **Grounded Theory (Glaser & Strauss, 1967)**: Good theory systematically discovered from and verified with data of social research. (Chunk 002)
- **Focus Group Discussion (Kruger, 1988)**: Carefully planned discussion to obtain perceptions on a defined area in a permissive, non-threatening environment. (Chunk 002)
- **Snowball Sampling**: Gathering subjects through an initial subject who provides names of others in the chain. (Chunk 002)
- **Reflexivity**: Researcher's ability to examine subjective involvement to shape interpretation of interview data. (Chunk 002)
- **Constant Comparative Method**: Qualitative analysis via constant comparing/contrasting of notes — who is saying what and in what context. (Chunk 002)
- **Content Analysis (Berelson)**: A research technique for the objective, systematic, and quantitative description of the manifest content of communication. (Chunk 003)
- **Content Analysis (Paisley)**: Phase of information processing transforming communication content through objective/systematic categorization into comparable data. (Chunk 003)
- **Manifest Content Analysis**: Counting words/phrases/surface features of text — reliable, quantitative. (Chunk 003)
- **Latent Content Analysis**: Interpreting underlying meaning of text — requires researcher skill to expose themes and cultural values. (Chunk 003)
- **Conceptual Content Analysis**: Tallying presence/frequency of a chosen concept — also called thematic analysis. (Chunk 003)
- **Relational Content Analysis**: Exploring relationships between identified concepts — also called semantic analysis. (Chunk 003)
- **Coding (Content Analysis)**: Systematically transforming raw data into units permitting precise description of content characteristics. (Chunk 003)

## Named Models / Laws / Theories
- **Grounded Theory** (Glaser & Strauss, 1967): Inductive theorizing — theory emerges from data not prior hypotheses. Two types: Substantive (specific) & Formal (generalizable). (Chunk 002)
- **Ethnography** (Bronislaw Malinowski): Participant observation-based qualitative method generating data from insider's perspective. (Chunk 001)
- **Kemmis & McTaggart Spiral Model**: Self-reflective cycles — Plan → Act → Observe → Reflect → Re-plan. (Chunk 005)
- **Kurt Lewin's Action Research Spiral**: Fact finding → Planning → Action → Evaluating → Amend plan → Second action step. (Chunk 005)

## Definitions (continued — Chunks 004–006)
- **Action Research (Kemmis & McTaggart, 1988)**: A form of collective self-reflective enquiry undertaken by participants in social situations to improve rationality and justice of their own practices. (Chunk 004)
- **Action Research (Dick, 2002)**: A flexible spiral process allowing action (change) and research (understanding) to be achieved at the same time. (Chunk 004)
- **Action Research (Frost, 2002)**: Process of systematic reflection, enquiry and action carried out by individuals about their own professional practice. (Chunk 004)
- **Reflective Critical Analysis**: Becoming aware of one's own perceptual biases; making interpretations, biases and assumptions explicit. (Chunk 005)
- **Dialectical Critical Analysis**: Understanding relationships between elements constituting a phenomenon in social context. (Chunk 005)
- **Collaborative Resource (AR)**: Each person's ideas are equally significant as resources; participants are co-researchers. (Chunk 005)
- **Plural Structure (AR)**: Developing multiple accounts and critiques rather than a single authoritative interpretation. (Chunk 005)

## Key Concepts Introduced
- **Aggregative analysis** (Chunk 001): macroeconomics studies economy-wide aggregates; behaviour at aggregate ≠ scaled-up individual.
- **Fallacy-of-composition rationale for macro** (Chunk 001): one firm hiring vs. all firms hiring shows micro logic fails at the aggregate level.
- **Schools of macroeconomic thought** (Chunk 001): groups sharing similar views on macro variable behaviour; macro lacks the consensus seen in micro.
- **Classical school** (Chunk 001): 18th–early 19th c.; flexible wages/prices/interest; markets self-clear at full employment.
- **Keynesian school** (Chunk 001): post-1930s; sticky wages/prices; output can fall below potential; activist fiscal/monetary policy.
- **Neoclassical Synthesis** (Chunk 001): integration of classical and Keynesian ideas; IS–LM is its canonical model.
- **Post-Keynesian growth extension** (Chunk 001): Harrod, Domar, Kaldor extended Keynesian ideas to long-run growth in the 1950s.
- **New Classical economics** (Chunk 001): post-1970s revival adding rational expectations and micro-foundations; perfect competition, flexible prices.
- **New Keynesian economics** (Chunk 001): response to New Classical; rational expectations + micro-foundations but with imperfect markets and sticky prices.
- **Heterodox economics** (Chunk 001): non-mainstream schools — Marxist, Austrian, institutional, feminist, social economics.
- **Stagflation (1970s)** (Chunk 001): simultaneous stagnation and inflation; key episode that discredited pure Keynesian policy.
- **Four points of Classical–Keynesian disagreement** (Chunk 001): role of D/S, price/wage flexibility, real–monetary dichotomy, neutrality of money.
- **Mercantilism (anti-thesis of classical)** (Chunk 002): pre-classical view that wealth = stock of bullion (gold/silver); promoted exports and curbed imports via subsidies and tariffs.
- **Seven features of classical theory** (Chunk 002): microeconomic focus, laissez faire, invisible hand, continuous market clearing, perfect competition, Say's Law, neutrality of money.
- **Smith's three duties of government** (Chunk 002): national defence, administration of justice, certain public works.
- **Three foundational assumptions of classical model** (Chunk 002): firms/workers are optimisers, have perfect knowledge, operate in perfectly competitive markets ⇒ wages and prices fully flexible.
- **Labour-market equilibrium (classical)** (Chunk 002): N^d = f(W/P) downward-sloping, N^s = g(W/P) upward-sloping; equilibrium real wage produces full-employment N₀.
- **Aggregate production function** (Chunk 002): Y = F(K, N); converts full-employment labour and given capital into full-employment output Y₀.
- **Vertical (classical) AS curve** (Chunk 002): AS fixed at full-employment output; shifts only with technology or capital stock changes.
- **Money market equilibrium** (Chunk 002): demand for money (PT) equals money supply (MV); monetary authority autonomously sets M in this unit.
- **Four functions of money** (Chunk 002): medium of exchange, standard of deferred payment, store of value, unit of account.
- **QTM as theory of price level** (Chunk 002): with V and Y fixed, P proportional to M; money supply determines price level.
- **Three theories of money demand** (Chunk 002): classical QTM, Keynesian theory, Friedman's restatement of classical QTM.
- **Say's Law income chain** (Chunk 003): Production price = rent + wages + profits ⇒ income generated equals output value ⇒ aggregate demand always matches aggregate supply.
- **Hoarding ruled out** (Chunk 003): Say's Law assumes money is never hoarded; expenditure stream MV stays neutral.
- **Modern caveat for Say's Law** (Chunk 003): valid in money economy only if savings are fully invested.
- **Real vs nominal variables split** (Chunk 003): real = physical (Y, K, N); nominal = monetary value (P, W, nominal i, nominal Y).
- **Real determinants of real variables** (Chunk 003): population, technology, capital stock, MPN, work-leisure preferences.
- **Four-panel Fig. 1.5 trace** (Chunk 003): ΔM → ΔAD → ΔP → momentary Δ(W/P) → ΔW (proportional) → W/P, N_F, Y_F unchanged.
- **Equiproportional wage adjustment** (Chunk 003): W₁/P₁ = W₀/P₀ when ΔP fully matched by ΔW; restores real wage and full employment.
- **Capital market neutrality** (Chunk 003): ΔM shifts nominal SS→S′S′ and II→I′I′ proportionally; real interest rate, real S, real I unchanged.
- **Money demanded only for transactions (classical)** (Chunk 003): ⇒ money market does not determine the interest rate; real interest rate set by S = I in real terms.
- **Inflation as evidence against neutrality** (Chunk 003): textbook concedes inflation has real costs (lower QoL, slower growth) ⇒ neutrality fails outside full-flexibility setting.
- **Long-run vs short-run AS** (Chunk 003): long-run AS vertical at Y* (potential output); short-run AS upward-sloping; classical = long-run model, Keynesian = short-run model.
- **Short-run effects of ΔM** (Chunk 003): shifts AD; both prices and income move (sticky prices); long run only P moves.
- **Comparative statics in Fig. 1.7** (Chunk 003): outward AS shift ⇒ Y↑, P↓; outward AD shift ⇒ Y↑, P↑.

## Definitions (⭐ exam-important)
- **School of macroeconomic thought** (Chunk 001): group of thinkers sharing similar views on the behaviour of macroeconomic variables.
- **Classical economics** (Chunk 001): school holding that market forces with flexible wages/prices/interest determine economic variables.
- **Keynesian economics** (Chunk 001): school holding wages/prices are sticky and that government must intervene to close output gaps.
- **Stagflation** (Chunk 001): simultaneous economic stagnation and inflation.
- **New Classical economics** (Chunk 001): retains classical core; adds rational expectations and micro-foundations; perfect competition.
- **New Keynesian economics** (Chunk 001): retains sticky wages/prices and imperfect competition; adds rational expectations and micro-foundations.
- **Economic theory** (Chunk 001): set of ideas/principles used to explain, analyse, and predict observable phenomena.
- **Mercantilism** (Chunk 002): pre-classical doctrine that national wealth depends on the stock of bullion; defended by export-promoting and import-restricting policies.
- **Laissez faire** (Chunk 002): French for "leave alone"; classical principle of minimal government intervention in business.
- **Invisible hand** (Chunk 002): Adam Smith's metaphor — self-interested individual action leads, as if guided, to general welfare.
- **Continuous market clearing** (Chunk 002): classical assumption that flexible wages and prices keep all markets continuously in equilibrium.
- **Say's Law of Market** (Chunk 002): "supply creates its own demand"; production generates the income that purchases the output.
- **Neutrality of money** (Chunk 002): changes in money supply alter only nominal variables; real variables (output, employment) remain unchanged.
- **Quantity Theory of Money (QTM)** (Chunk 002): classical theory that, with V and Y constant, the price level is proportional to money supply; theory of the price level.
- **Fisher's Equation of Exchange** (Chunk 002): MV = PT (transactions version) or MV = PY (income version).
- **Velocity of circulation (V)** (Chunk 002): number of times a unit of currency changes hands during a given period.
- **Marginal product of labour (MPN)** (Chunk 002): additional output from employing one more unit of labour; equal to the real wage W/P in classical equilibrium.
- **Real wage** (Chunk 002): nominal wage divided by price level (W/P); the determinant of labour demand and supply in the classical model.
- **Aggregate production function** (Chunk 002): Y = F(K, N); two-factor relationship between capital, labour, and output.
- **Classical Dichotomy** (Chunk 003): proposition that nominal variables do not influence real variables in the economy; rests on perfect competition + full flexibility of prices and wages. ⭐
- **Real variable** (Chunk 003): variable measured as physical quantity (real GDP, real capital stock, employment). ⭐
- **Nominal variable** (Chunk 003): variable measured in monetary value (price level, money wage, nominal interest rate, nominal income). ⭐
- **Hoarding** (Chunk 003): holding money idle rather than spending on consumption or investment; ruled out by Say's Law. ⭐
- **Capital market equilibrium (classical)** (Chunk 003): saving equals investment; determines the real interest rate independently of the money market.
- **Real interest rate** (Chunk 003): nominal interest rate adjusted for inflation; classical determinant of real S and real I.
- **Long-run aggregate supply (AS_LR)** (Chunk 003): vertical AS at potential output Y*; relevant when prices and wages are flexible.
- **Short-run aggregate supply (AS₀)** (Chunk 003): upward-sloping AS reflecting sticky prices; relevant in Keynesian short-run analysis.
- **Potential output (Y*)** (Chunk 003): full-employment level of real output; long-run anchor of AS.

- **Linear consumption function** (Chunk 004): C = a + bY (no govt) or C = a + b(Y − T) (with lump-sum tax); a > 0, 0 < b < 1.
- **MPC = b** (Chunk 004): ΔC/ΔY; constant; 0 < b < 1; saving out of ΔY is (1 − b)·ΔY.
- **APC = a/Y + b** (Chunk 004): C/Y; declines as Y rises; differs from MPC.
- **Autonomous vs Induced consumption** (Chunk 004): a = autonomous (financed by dis-saving at Y = 0); bY = induced; ↑a is parallel upward shift; trigger = consumer confidence.
- **Investment function specifications** (Chunk 004): I = Ī (autonomous), I = c + dY (induced; d = MPI), I = c + dY − ei (interest-sensitive).
- **Investment ≠ financial purchases** (Chunk 004): Keynesian I = real expenditure on capacity-building; excludes shares/bonds/FDs.
- **Inverse i–I relation via PV** (Chunk 004): P = ΣRt/(1+i)^t; ↑i ⇒ P↓ ⇒ projects with P < A dropped ⇒ I↓.
- **Three Keynesian assumptions** (Chunk 004): unemployed resources / excess capacity, sticky W and P, homogeneous output for both C and I ⇒ output is demand-determined.
- **Equilibrium output (three-sector, lump-sum)** (Chunk 004): Yₑ = (a − bT + Ī + G)/(1 − b); two-sector: Yₑ = (a + Ī)/(1 − b).
- **45° line / Keynesian Cross** (Chunk 004): OL is locus AD = Y; equilibrium = AD ∩ OL; unique and stable.
- **Inventory-driven self-correction** (Chunk 004): Y < Yₑ ⇒ AD > Y ⇒ inventories ↓ ⇒ Y ↑; Y > Yₑ ⇒ unplanned inventory accumulation ⇒ Y ↓.
- **Investment multiplier** (Chunk 004): ΔY/ΔI = 1/(1 − b); arises from convergent geometric series 1 + b + b² + … = 1/(1 − b).
- **Induced investment multiplier** (Chunk 004): with I = c + dY, multiplier = 1/[1 − (b + d)]; requires (b + d) < 1.
- **Multiplier prerequisites** (Chunk 004): excess capacity + demand-determined output; otherwise ΔAD raises P, not Y.
- **Lump-sum tax multipliers** (Chunk 005): ΔY/ΔG = ΔY/ΔI = 1/(1 − b); ΔY/ΔT = − b/(1 − b); tax is a leakage.
- **Balanced Budget Multiplier** (Chunk 005): ΔG = ΔT ⇒ ΔY/ΔG = 1 (income rises by exactly ΔG).
- **Proportional tax multiplier** (Chunk 005): 1/[1 − b(1 − t)]; smaller than 1/(1 − b) because fraction t leaks each round.
- **Open-economy multiplier** (Chunk 005): 1/(1 − b + m); smaller than closed because of import leakage m = MPM.
- **Three multiplier takeaways** (Chunk 005): larger with high b, d, low t, low m; symmetric for ↑/↓ AD; needs excess capacity.
- **Limitations of Keynesian model** (Chunk 005): supply bottlenecks ⇒ ↑P not ↑Y; full employment breaks model; short-run only.
- **Saving function** (Chunk 005): S = − a + (1 − b)Y; mirror of consumption; intercept − a; slope = MPS = 1 − MPC.
- **S = I equilibrium (two-sector)** (Chunk 005): closed economy without government clears at planned S = planned Ī.
- **Three-sector S–I identity** (Chunk 005): S + T = Ī + G ⇔ S + (T − G) = Ī ⇔ (S − I) = (G − T).
- **Public saving / deficit financing** (Chunk 005): T − G = public saving; if S > I then G > T; deficit financed by household borrowing.
- **Paradox of thrift (autonomous I)** (Chunk 005): ↑desired saving ⇒ ↓Y; aggregate S unchanged at original Ī.
- **Paradox of thrift (induced I)** (Chunk 005): ↑desired saving ⇒ ↓Y ⇒ ↓induced I ⇒ aggregate S falls below original.
- **Three motives for money demand** (Chunk 005): transactions (Y↑), precautionary (Y↑), speculative (i↓).
- **Speculative demand mechanism** (Chunk 005): high i ⇒ expect bond prices ↑ ⇒ buy bonds ⇒ ↓L; low i ⇒ expect prices ↓ ⇒ ↑L.
- **Opportunity-cost rationale for inverse i–L** (Chunk 005): ↑i ⇒ ↑forgone interest ⇒ ↓money demand (no speculative motive needed).
- **Money demand function** (Chunk 005): L = qY − ti; q, t > 0; demand for real balances.
- **Money market equilibrium (Keynesian)** (Chunk 005): L = M^s/P; ↑M^s ⇒ ↓i; central bank sets M^s.
- **Liquidity trap** (Chunk 005): at very low i*, L horizontal; t → ∞; conventional monetary policy ineffective; fiscal policy effective.
- **Liquidity-trap real episode** (Chunk 005): GFC 2007–08 — USA i = 0, Japan ≈ 0; led to unconventional monetary policy.
- **Keynesian role of government** (Chunk 005): activist fiscal policy; deficit budget OK; falling W worsens recession via ↓purchasing power.
- **Crowding out** (Chunk 005): high public borrowing ⇒ ↑i ⇒ ↓private I; cited concern about deficits.
- **Historical Keynesian episodes** (Chunk 005): 1929–33 Great Depression; 2007–08 GFC; 2020–21 COVID.

## Definitions added (Chunk 004)
- **Effective demand**: Keynesian concept of aggregate demand whose deficiency prevents full employment. ⭐
- **Aggregate demand (Keynesian)**: planned expenditure on goods and services by households, firms, and government. ⭐
- **Consumption function**: C = a + bY relating planned consumption to aggregate income; a > 0, 0 < b < 1. ⭐
- **Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC, b)**: ΔC/ΔY; fraction of incremental income spent on consumption; 0 < b < 1; constant. ⭐
- **Average Propensity to Consume (APC)**: C/Y = a/Y + b; falls as Y rises. ⭐
- **Autonomous consumption (a)**: consumption at zero income, financed by dis-saving; a > 0. ⭐
- **Dis-saving**: drawing down past saving to finance current consumption when income is low. ⭐
- **Investment (Keynesian)**: planned firm expenditure on new productive equipment; excludes purchases of bonds/shares/FDs. ⭐
- **Autonomous investment (Ī)**: investment independent of income; treated as fixed. ⭐
- **Induced investment (dY)**: portion of investment driven by income; via MPI = d. ⭐
- **Marginal Propensity to Invest (MPI, d)**: ΔI/ΔY in induced-investment specification; d > 0.
- **Present value (P)**: discounted value of expected future returns from a project; basis of investment decision (P − A ≥ 0).
- **Demand-determined output**: under excess capacity and price rigidity, Y is set by AD, not supply. ⭐
- **Wage and price rigidity**: short-run assumption that nominal W and P do not adjust to clear markets. ⭐
- **Equilibrium output (Yₑ)**: Y at which AD = Y; plans realised; no unplanned inventory change. ⭐
- **45° line (OL) / Keynesian Cross**: locus where vertical = horizontal distance; equilibrium at AD ∩ OL.
- **Unplanned inventory accumulation**: stocks rise because output exceeds AD; signals firms to cut production. ⭐
- **Investment multiplier**: ΔY/ΔI = 1/(1 − b); multiple by which Y rises in response to ΔĪ. ⭐

## Key Concepts Introduced (Chunk 006 — Unit 3 begins)
- **IS curve** (Chunk 006): locus of (Y, i) at which the real (goods) sector is in equilibrium (I = S, AD = Y). Downward-sloping.
- **IS algebra** (Chunk 006): Y = α_G(Ā − e·i); α_G = 1/(1 − b − d); Ā = a + c + Ḡ − bT̄.
- **IS slope** (Chunk 006): di/dY = − (1 − b − d)/e. Flatter when b, d, or e larger.
- **IS shifts** (Chunk 006): ↑Ḡ, ↑a, ↑c, ↓T̄ ⇒ shift up-right; ↓Ḡ, ↑T̄ ⇒ shift down-left. Slope changes only via b, d, e.
- **Disequilibrium in IS** (Chunk 006): above-right of IS = excess supply of goods; below-left = excess demand.
- **LM curve** (Chunk 006): locus of (Y, i) at which the money market clears. Upward-sloping.
- **LM money demand specification** (Chunk 006): L = kY − h·i; relabelled from L = qY − ti.
- **LM equilibrium** (Chunk 006): M̄/P̄ = kY − h·i; slope di/dY = k/h > 0.
- **LM limit cases** (Chunk 006): h → ∞ ⇒ horizontal (liquidity trap); h = 0 ⇒ vertical.
- **LM shifts** (Chunk 006): ↑M^s shifts LM right (↓i at every Y); ↓M^s shifts LM left.
- **Disequilibrium in LM** (Chunk 006): left of LM = excess supply of money; right of LM = excess demand for money.
- **Simultaneous IS-LM equilibrium** (Chunk 006): unique (Y, i) at IS ∩ LM.
- **IS-LM demand shocks** (Chunk 006): stock-market boom ⇒ IS right; ↓business confidence ⇒ IS left; ↑money demand (e.g., online fraud) ⇒ LM right.
- **Slope effect on policy** (Chunk 006): flatter LM ⇒ MP more effective; steeper LM ⇒ MP less effective.
- **IS-LM adjustment** (Chunk 006): off-equilibrium ⇒ Δi via bond market and ΔY via inventories ⇒ converges to E (Figs. 3.5–3.6).

## Definitions added (Chunk 010)
- **Imperfect capital mobility**: setting in which restrictions on cross-border flows or imperfect bond substitutability allow a persistent gap between domestic and foreign interest rates. ⭐
- **Pure float (Unit 5 operational definition)**: regime with no official intervention in currency markets, no role of forex reserves; BOP balance restored via e fluctuations. ⭐
- **Expansionary fiscal policy (open econ)**: ↑G or ↓t. ⭐
- **Fiscal ineffectiveness under flexible rates**: result that fiscal expansion fails to raise output in the short run when capital is perfectly mobile and e floats. ⭐
- **Expansionary monetary policy (open econ)**: ↑ nominal money supply via OMO, etc. ⭐
- **Monetary effectiveness under flexible rates**: result that monetary expansion is extremely effective at raising output under perfect capital mobility + pure float. ⭐
- **Fixed exchange rate regime (Unit 5 setup)**: regime in which e fluctuations are ruled out; CB defends fixed parity using reserves. ⭐
- **Fiscal effectiveness under fixed rates**: result that fiscal expansion raises output substantially with no crowding out, since money supply endogenously expands via reserve inflows. ⭐
- **No crowding out (in fixed-rate Mundell-Fleming)**: i held at i_f by capital flows ⇒ private investment unaffected by ↑G. ⭐
- **Accommodative monetary expansion**: passive ↑M^s via forex-reserve buildup that supports fiscal expansion under fixed rates. ⭐
- **Endogeneity of money supply**: under fixed ER + perfect capital mobility, M^s passively adjusts to forex-reserve changes as CB defends parity. ⭐
- **Monetary ineffectiveness under fixed rates (perfect mobility)**: ↑M^s leaves output unchanged in the short run because reserve drain restores M^s. ⭐
- **Independent monetary policy**: ability of CB to set M^s (or i) on its own without external constraint. ⭐
- **Policy trilemma (impossible trinity)**: a country cannot simultaneously have perfect capital mobility, independent monetary policy, and fixed exchange rates — at most two of the three. ⭐

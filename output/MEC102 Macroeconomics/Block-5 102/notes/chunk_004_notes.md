## Section: Significance of the Concept and Theory of Search Unemployment 🔴

### Core Idea
Search unemployment is not just a theoretical curiosity — it has **real policy significance**. Higher unemployment benefits raise the duration and incidence of search unemployment because workers wait longer for a better job. The 1990s US restructuring of unemployment benefits and the persistent EU-vs-US unemployment differential are both partly explained by this mechanism.

> **In Simple Terms:** If you're paid generously while unemployed, you can afford to be picky about which job to take. So generous benefits → more search unemployment. The US tightened benefits in the 1990s; the EU didn't, and EU unemployment stayed higher.

### Key Concepts

#### Why benefits matter
- The willingness to keep searching for a *better* job depends on income while unemployed.
- **Search unemployment**: arises when (a) someone quits to look for a better job, or (b) someone refuses an offer hoping for a better one.
- If all jobs were identical, no one would search — they'd take the first offer.
- Heterogeneity of jobs → search has option value → benefits raise that option value.

#### Higher benefits → higher search unemployment
- ↑unemployment benefits → ↑willingness to keep searching → ↑duration of unemployment.
- ↑benefits → ↑willingness to *quit* current job to search.
- Both effects raise the equilibrium unemployment rate.

#### US (1990s) vs European Union
- **1990s US**: Social security/benefits system restructured — benefits made harder to obtain.
- **Effect on US**: Lower duration of search unemployment.
- **EU**: Retained more generous benefits.
- **Result**: EU unemployment rates persistently higher than US in recent years — *partly* explained by benefits differential. (Not the whole story, but a major piece.)

### Definitions
- **Search unemployment**: unemployment that arises when (a) a worker quits to find a better job, or (b) an unemployed worker delays accepting a job in hopes of a better one. ⭐ (exam-important)

### Mechanisms / Processes
1. Worker faces dispersion in job quality
2. Generous benefits raise the option value of continued search
3. Worker either quits current job or rejects current offer to keep searching
4. Aggregate search-unemployment rate rises

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: "Higher unemployment benefits unambiguously increase welfare." → ✅ Correct: They cushion individual hardship but raise the equilibrium unemployment rate via longer search durations. There's a real trade-off.
- ❌ Mistake: "All EU-US unemployment differential is due to benefits." → ✅ Correct: Benefits are a *partial* explanation; many other factors (labour-market regulation, hiring/firing costs, taxes) also matter.

### Examples
**Example: US benefits restructuring (1990s)**
- Before: relatively long-lasting benefits.
- Restructured: harder to obtain, shorter duration.
- Effect: workers more likely to take an available job rather than continue searching.
- Outcome: US unemployment rates fell relative to EU through the 1990s and 2000s.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Generous unemployment benefits → ↑search unemployment.
> - US 1990s reform tightened benefits → reduced search unemployment.
> - EU-US unemployment gap is partly explained by this — but only partly.

## Section: Unit 14 Summary 🟢

### Core Idea
Unit 14 introduced **search and matching theory** as a Walrasian-compatible explanation for unemployment. The neoclassical model is theoretically elegant but doesn't accord with the observed reality of prolonged unemployment. Search theory plugs this gap by treating the labour market as a market for **heterogeneous** workers and jobs, with **costly matching** that produces unemployment as an **equilibrium phenomenon**.

### Key Concepts (consolidated)
- Origins trace back to **Pigou's frictional unemployment** in the inter-war period.
- Modern search models develop unemployment as an equilibrium outcome where firms hire by equating marginal benefit and marginal cost of hiring (with search costs).
- The equilibrium unemployment rate is **not socially optimal** because of (i) hiring externalities and (ii) bargaining wedges between private and social benefits of hiring.
- **Productivity shocks** drive variability of unemployment; **sectoral shocks** with frictions in inter-sector mobility (Lilien) provide an additional source.
- The **practical significance** of search unemployment lies in its role explaining the sensitivity of unemployment to social-security/unemployment-benefit policies.

### Connections
- Contrasts with: Unit 13 New Keynesian theories (which abandon Walrasian assumptions)
- Complements: Real Business Cycle theory (Unit 12)

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Search theory = equilibrium frictional unemployment in a Walrasian world.
> - Heterogeneity + matching costs do the work that price rigidity does in New Keynesian theory.
> - Equilibrium ≠ socially optimal: opposite-sign distortions from externalities and bargaining.
> - Sectoral shocks + intra-period labour immobility → unemployment (Lilien).
> - Higher unemployment benefits → more search unemployment.

## Section: Block 5 Synthesis — Unit 13 vs Unit 14 🔴

### Core Idea
Block 5 covers two complementary explanations for persistent unemployment: **New Keynesian** (Unit 13) and **Search & Matching** (Unit 14). They differ profoundly on whether markets are Walrasian, but both successfully reproduce the empirical facts of involuntary unemployment and mildly pro-cyclical real wages.

### Key Concepts

| Aspect | Unit 13 — New Keynesian | Unit 14 — Search & Matching |
|--------|-------------------------|----------------------------|
| Market structure | Imperfect competition | Walrasian / perfectly competitive |
| Source of unemployment | Wage/price rigidity (nominal & real) | Heterogeneity + matching frictions |
| Mechanism | Menu costs (Mankiw); efficiency wages; contracts | Costly search; bargaining over surplus |
| Equilibrium type | Non-Walrasian | Walrasian with frictions |
| Role of money | Money has real effects via nominal rigidity | Not a primary focus |
| Real wages cyclicality | Pro-cyclical (efficiency wage) or rigid | Pro-cyclical (productivity-driven) |
| Policy implication | Fix imperfections; demand management | Reduce matching frictions; tune benefits |

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- ❌ Mistake: "Search theory and New Keynesian theory contradict each other." → ✅ Correct: They explain *different aspects* of unemployment. Real economies likely contain both — rigidity *and* matching frictions.

> **Quick Recall:**
> - Two routes to involuntary unemployment: rigidity (New Keynesian) and frictions (search).
> - Both microfounded; both empirically reasonable.
> - The difference is whether the labour market is Walrasian — a deep methodological choice.

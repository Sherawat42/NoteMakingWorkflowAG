## Key Terms Glossary
| Term | Definition | First Appears |
|------|-----------|---------------|
| Business cycle | Recurrent alternating phases of expansion and contraction in a number of economic variables (employment, production, real income, sales). | Chunk 001 |
| Synchronic cycles | Contraction or expansion occurs simultaneously in most industries/sectors. | Chunk 001 |
| Contagion effect | International transmission of a cycle via capital flows and trade linkages. | Chunk 001 |
| Peak | Turning point: end of expansion, start of recession. | Chunk 001 |
| Trough | Turning point: end of depression, start of recovery. | Chunk 001 |
| Aggregate effective demand | Total demand for consumption goods + investment goods. | Chunk 001 |
| Multiplier | 1/(1−MPC); magnifies investment changes into income changes. | Chunk 001 |
| Accelerator (v) | Capital-output ratio; investment response to changes in income. | Chunk 001 |
| Induced investment (I_d) | Investment generated by income changes: I_d = v·(Y_(t−1) − Y_(t−2)). | Chunk 001 |
| Autonomous investment (Iₐ) | Investment independent of current income. | Chunk 001 |
| Political business cycle | Pre-election expansion + post-election restraint, producing recurring cycles tied to elections. | Chunk 002 |
| Recession (NBER) | Significant decline in economic activity spread across the economy, lasting more than a few months, visible in real GDP, real income, employment, industrial production, sales. | Chunk 002 |
| Expansion (NBER) | Substantial rise in economic activity spread across the economy, usually lasting several years. | Chunk 002 |
| Conformity | Consistency with which a series has conformed to business cycles. | Chunk 002 |
| Timing | Consistency with which a series' turning point has led, lagged, or coincided with reference data. | Chunk 002 |
| Leading indicator | Series whose turning points precede the cycle's (1–10 months on average). | Chunk 002 |
| Coincident indicator | Series whose turning points roughly coincide with the cycle's. | Chunk 002 |
| Lagging indicator | Series whose turning points follow the cycle's (1–12 months on average); confirms patterns. | Chunk 002 |
| H-P filter (Hodrick-Prescott) | Statistical detrending technique separating cyclical from trend components. | Chunk 002 |
| Animal spirit | Keynes' term for the human emotion driving fluctuations in investment. | Chunk 003 |
| Creative destruction | Schumpeter's process where entrepreneurial innovation disrupts existing production. | Chunk 003 |
| Rational expectations hypothesis | Agents form expectations using all available information and a correct model of the economy. | Chunk 003 |
| Continuous market clearing | Demand = supply at every point; flexible, instantaneous price/wage adjustment. | Chunk 003 |
| Lucas supply function | Output supply depends on actual minus expected price; signal-extraction problem. | Chunk 003 |
| Microfoundation | Macroeconomic phenomena explained via the optimising behaviour of micro agents. | Chunk 003 |
| DSGE | Dynamic Stochastic General Equilibrium model: inter-temporal, whole-economy, with random shocks. | Chunk 003 |
| Calibration | Choosing parameters so model output replicates empirical data; distinct from estimation. | Chunk 003 |
| Inter-temporal substitution between labour and leisure | Households shift work effort between time periods in response to relative wage and interest rate movements. | Chunk 003 |
| Real shock | Shock to production, real govt spending, or savings/consumption — affects the IS curve. | Chunk 003 |
| Nominal shock | Shock to money supply or money demand — affects the LM curve. | Chunk 003 |
| Supply / productivity shock | Real shock to the production function (technology, input quality, raw materials, weather, regulation). | Chunk 003 |
| Real Business Cycle (RBC) theory | Theory that real shocks (especially productivity) drive cycles in continuously clearing markets with rational agents. | Chunk 003 |
| Ricardian equivalence | Households neutralise fiscal deficit financing by anticipating future tax liabilities. | Chunk 003 |
| Walrasian model | Competitive general-equilibrium model without information, externality, or missing-market frictions. | Chunk 003 |
| Ramsey model | Baseline GE growth model with representative household and firm; converges to balanced growth path. | Chunk 003 |
| AR(1) process | Stochastic process: current value = ρ × previous value + white noise. | Chunk 003 |
| Cobb-Douglas (RBC) | Y = K^α · (AL)^(1−α); constant returns to scale. | Chunk 003 |
| White-noise error | Stochastic term with zero mean and zero correlation with other variables. | Chunk 003 |
| Two-period inter-temporal labour supply | Labour supply ratio (1−ℓ₁)/(1−ℓ₂) depends on relative wage and interest rate. | Chunk 004 |
| Elasticity of substitution between leisures | Equal to 1 under log utility. | Chunk 004 |
| Persistence (RBC) | Sustained effect of shocks via gradual capital and employment adjustment. | Chunk 004 |
| New-Keynesian economics | DSGE + microfoundation + rational expectations + sticky prices/wages + imperfect competition. | Chunk 004 |
| Menu costs | Adjustment costs of changing posted prices/wages. | Chunk 004 |
| Mark-up (price setting) | Wedge between price and marginal cost; measure of market imperfection. | Chunk 004 |
| Taylor Rule | Interest-rate policy rule used in new-Keynesian DSGE models. | Chunk 004 |

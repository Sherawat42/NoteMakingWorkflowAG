#### Q2 — Lucas Supply Curve
- Relies on a **price surprise** to explain why output deviates from the natural level of output.
- In the absence of perfect information, agents cannot anticipate future prices and wage rates accurately.
- Cannot distinguish nominal vs. real price increases due to the **signal extraction problem**.
- Therefore there is **no dichotomy** between real and nominal sectors and **money is not neutral in the short run**.
- Refer to Section 7.6.

#### Q3 — Policy Ineffectiveness Theorem
- Focuses on the **demand side** to explain deviations of output from the natural level.
- Deviations are explained by **price surprises caused by the non-anticipatable portion of money change**.
- Model has four equations: aggregate demand, aggregate supply, money-supply rule, rational-expectations equation.
- Use Fig. 7.2 and Fig. 7.3 to show the effect of increasing money supply.
- Use eqs (7.7), (7.8), (7.9), (7.10) to derive (7.15) and (7.16).
- Refer to Section 7.7.
